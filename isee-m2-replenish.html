Replenish

/rih-PLEN-ish/ — stress on PLEN · verb

NEW — first teaching (morpheme-only word)

Hear It
What It Means

To replenish something is to fill it back up again after it has been used or emptied.

Show it in context

Desert travelers plan each day's route around where they can replenish their water.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when replenish is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the power outage, the hospital rushed to replenish the backup generators with diesel fuel before another storm hit the city.

Re (again) + Latin plenus (full) + -ish (to make) = to make full again. The generators were empty after running all night, so replenish precisely describes restoring them to full functional capacity. A simpler word like 'refuel' could work, but replenish conveys the urgency and purpose of restoring critical resources to readiness.

Your Turn

The bookshelf in the library's children's section had a few missing titles, so a librarian added three new books to the shelves.

Is replenish the precise word for what the librarian did, or would 'stock' be more fitting?

stock

Correct — The librarian is adding books to maintain the collection, and stock is the broader word for that; it does not require the shelf to have run empty first.

replenish

Not quite — It is tempting because the librarian added books, but replenish means to make something full again after depletion (re plus plenus = full again). The shelf only had a few missing titles, not a real emptying.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
refill

To fill again with the same substance. More literal and narrow; replenish can imply restoring something to proper levels of readiness or capacity.

restock

To supply again with goods or materials. Used especially for commercial inventory; replenish emphasizes restoration to proper levels, especially for essentials.

supply

To provide or furnish. Broader in scope; doesn't necessarily imply 'again' or 'to full capacity.' Replenish is more specific about restoration.

Antonyms · opposites

deplete — To use up or reduce; the opposite of replenish. Depletion is what happens when supplies run low; replenish reverses it.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

re + plen + ish

re (again) + Latin plenus (full — think plenty) + -ish (a French verb ending, as in finish and nourish) = to make full again.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal-practical

Reach for replenish when describing the intentional restoration of something essential to its proper full level—it suggests both emptying and careful refilling. Common with supplies (water, fuel, oxygen), inventory (medical stock), and abstract resources (energy, resolve). It's more purposeful than 'refill' and implies preparation or recovery after depletion.
