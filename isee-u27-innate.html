Innate

/in-AYT/ — rhymes with create or fate · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Innate describes a quality or ability a person is born with, rather than one they learned.

Show it in context

Her innate sense of pitch showed before she ever took a lesson.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when innate is the exact word.

Worked Example

Her innate sense of melody allowed her to hum new songs in tune, even before learning to read music.

Innate is precise here because it shows the ability existed from birth. If we just said she had a 'natural sense' of melody, it would be less clear that this was inborn rather than something she picked up from listening to others.

Your Turn

After years of practice, the musician developed an excellent understanding of harmony.

Is 'innate' the right word, or would 'learned' be better?

innate

Not quite — An understanding built up over years of practice is developed, not inborn, so it is the direct opposite of innate, which means present from birth.

learned

Correct — Skill that grows through years of practice is learned by definition, which fits this musician's gradually acquired grasp of harmony.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
inborn

present from birth — nearly identical to innate, but inborn emphasizes the timing of birth more directly

inherent

built-in quality essential to something — inherent can apply to objects or ideas, not just people

natural

existing in or produced by nature — natural is broader and more casual, innate is more specific about being born-with

Antonyms · opposites

learned — Developed through practice and teaching — the opposite of innate

Build the Word

in- + natus Latin natus: 'born'

Innate literally means 'born into' — a quality or ability you're born with, not one you acquire or develop later.

Her natural gift for languages showed itself early, without any formal instruction.

Same root, new word — what does natural mean here?

carefully developed through long training and study

Not quite — This contradicts the sentence, which stresses that her gift appeared early and without any formal instruction, marking it as inborn rather than trained.

inborn; existing or produced by nature rather than learned

Correct — Correct. Natural comes from Latin natus (born), the same natus inside innate, so both point to something present from birth.

The root connection →

Root connection: innate and natural both grow from Latin natus (born). Innate adds in- (in, within) to mean born within, while natural keeps the plain sense of belonging to one's nature from birth.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Innate describes a quality or ability someone was born with, not something they learned or developed. Use innate when you want to emphasize that something is part of a person from the very beginning. For abilities developed through work, 'acquired' or 'developed' is clearer.
