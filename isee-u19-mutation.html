Mutation

/myoo-TAY-shun/ — rhymes with station · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A mutation is a change in an organism's genetic material that can produce a new or different trait.

Show it in context

A rare mutation gives these tulips their striped petals.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when mutation is the exact word.

Worked Example

In the laboratory, a scientist observes that a population of fruit flies has developed a new eye color never before seen; further testing reveals a change in their DNA that caused this entirely new trait.

Mutation is more precise than 'change' because it specifically refers to a genetic alteration in DNA that produces a new hereditary trait — that biological and hereditary component is essential to the meaning.

Your Turn

A baker accidentally uses expired ingredients and the cookies come out a different shade of brown than usual.

Is mutation the right word to describe what happened to the cookies?

Yes

Not quite — Tempting, because the cookies clearly changed. But mutation is reserved for genetic changes in living organisms, not an accidental change in the color of baked goods.

No

Correct — Correct. A mutation is a change in an organism's genes, so it does not fit expired ingredients altering a cookie's shade. It is more accurate to say the cookies simply turned out darker.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
variation

A difference in form or appearance. Mutation is specifically a genetic alteration that can be inherited.

modification

A change made to something. Mutation emphasizes the genetic cause and the resulting new trait.

adaptation

A trait an organism develops over time to survive better. Mutation is a genetic change; adaptation is how organisms use mutations.

Antonyms · opposites

stability — Staying the same without change — the opposite of the genetic shift that mutation creates.

How Strong Is It?

Build the Word

mutatus Latin: 'changed'

From the Latin root meaning 'to change,' mutation refers to change at the most fundamental molecular level — in the very genetic code of life.

Scientists hope to mutate the dangerous bacteria to make it harmless.

Same root, new word — what does mutate mean here?

to change or transform into a different form, especially in an organism's genes

Correct — Mutation and mutate both grow from Latin mutatus, 'changed.' To mutate is to undergo change, and a mutation is the changed result.

to multiply rapidly in number

Not quite — Tempting, because bacteria are known for multiplying fast. But mutate is about changing form, not increasing in quantity.

to make something completely harmless and safe

Not quite — Tempting, since the goal in the sentence is harmless bacteria. But that harmlessness is the hoped-for result; mutate itself just means to change form.

The root connection →

Whenever you meet mutatus, expect the idea of change: a mutation is a change that has already happened, while to mutate is the act of changing into something new.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

scientific / technical

Mutation is a precise scientific term for genetic changes that produce new traits in organisms. Use it when discussing heredity, evolution, or genetics. For everyday changes in appearance or form, 'alteration' or 'change' is more natural and appropriate.
