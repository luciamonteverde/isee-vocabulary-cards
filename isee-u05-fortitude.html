Fortitude

/FOR-ti-tood/ — rhymes with latitude · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Fortitude is the inner strength and courage that allows a person to endure pain, hardship, or difficulty without giving up.

Show it in context

It took real fortitude to finish the race on a sprained ankle.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when fortitude is the exact word.

Worked Example

Running the entire marathon on a badly sprained ankle, the runner refused to stop, pushing through searing pain mile after mile.

Fortitude is more precise than 'courage' because it names the specific inner strength needed to endure prolonged pain or hardship without giving up. Courage is momentary bravery; fortitude is sustained strength through suffering.

Your Turn

She quickly sprinted the final hundred meters of the race, giving everything she had to win.

Is 'fortitude' the right word here, or something else?

determination

Correct — A final all-out sprint is a short, intense burst of will to win, which 'determination' captures well.

fortitude

Not quite — It is tempting because she pushes hard, but 'fortitude' means sustained inner strength to endure hardship over a long stretch, not one quick burst of effort.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
courage

Bravery to face danger, but doesn't capture the specific strength to endure prolonged hardship without giving up that fortitude requires.

grit

Determination and toughness combined, but more colloquial and doesn't have fortitude's sense of dignified, noble perseverance.

perseverance

Continuing effort through difficulty, but emphasizes the act of keeping going more than the inner strength that fortitude names.

Antonyms · opposites

weakness — Lack of strength or will — the opposite of the inner strength fortitude requires to endure hardship.

How Strong Is It?
courage→fortitude→resilience→indomitable

Fortitude sits between courage (momentary bravery) and indomitable (absolutely unbreakable) — it's the inner strength to endure hardship steadily. (our word)

Build the Word

fortis Latin: 'fortis' = strong, brave

Fortitude comes directly from Latin 'fortis' (strong) — just as a fort is a stronghold built to withstand attacks, fortitude is the inner strength built to withstand hardship.

The fort stood on the hillside, its thick walls built to defend against invaders.

Same root, new word — what does fort mean here?

a small temporary campsite for hikers

Not quite — A blanket fort or a campsite borrows the word loosely, but the root fortis means 'strong,' so a true fort is a strong stronghold built to withstand attack.

a lookout tower used only for watching

Not quite — Forts may include watchtowers, but fortis points to overall strength, so a fort is a whole strong stronghold built to endure a siege, not just a place to watch from.

a strong stronghold built to resist attack and endure a siege

Correct — Fortitude and fort both come from the Latin fortis ('strong'). A fort is a strong structure built to withstand attack, and fortitude is that same strength built inside a person to withstand hardship.

The root connection →

Fortis ('strong') is the shared root: a fort stands strong against invaders, and fortitude is the inner strength that stands firm against hardship and pain.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Fortitude is more elevated and literary than everyday words like 'strength' or 'courage.' It suggests a kind of moral dignity and noble perseverance. Use it when you want to describe someone's dignified, sustained strength through real difficulty or pain. For simple toughness, the plainer words are better.
