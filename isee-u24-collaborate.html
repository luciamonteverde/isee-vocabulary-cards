Collaborate

/kuh-LAB-uh-rayt/ — stress on second syllable · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To collaborate is to work together with one or more other people toward a shared goal.

Show it in context

Two rival labs collaborated to map the virus in record time.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when collaborate is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the two rival scientists discovered a common problem, they set aside their competition and collaborated on research, combining their unique expertise to achieve a breakthrough.

Collaborate emphasizes the active teamwork and willingness to work together despite differences toward a shared goal. You could say 'work together,' but collaborate is more precise because it implies genuine partnership, mutual contribution, and a united purpose.

Your Turn

After the students divided the group project, each person worked on their assigned section separately at home.

Is collaborate the precise word to describe what the students did?

divided the work

Correct — They handed out separate sections and finished them independently, which is precisely dividing up labor rather than building something side by side.

collaborate

Not quite — Collaborate means actually working together, sharing ideas and effort in real time. These students split the project and each worked alone at home, so there was no joint teamwork to call collaboration.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
cooperate

Work together as a team, but less emphasis on the creative partnership and the shared creative goal that collaborate implies.

coordinate

Arrange and organize efforts together, but more about logistics and timing than about the creative, purposeful teamwork that collaborate emphasizes.

assist

Help someone else, but doesn't imply equal partnership or shared purpose—more one-way support than the mutual effort collaborate requires.

Antonyms · opposites

compete — Work against each other rather than together—the opposite of the unified teamwork and shared purpose that collaboration requires.

How Strong Is It?
assist→coordinate→partner→collaborate

Collaborate — the strongest of the group: work together creatively on a shared goal with equal contribution and mutual purpose. (our word)

Build the Word

col- + laborare Latin: 'col' (together) + 'laborare' (to work/toil)'

Collaborate literally means 'to work together'—the idea is that two or more people join their labor and effort toward a common purpose.

The laborious process of hand-stitching the quilt took months of careful work.

Same root, new word — what does laborious mean here?

involving hard, intensive work

Correct — Correct. 'Laborious' shares the laborare root ('to work, toil') with collaborate, so it describes something demanding a lot of hard work.

done quickly and with little effort

Not quite — That is the opposite of the laborare root ('to work, toil'). Hand-stitching a quilt for months is exhausting effort, so laborious means full of hard work, not effortless.

The root connection →

Collaborate comes from col- ('together') plus laborare ('to work'), meaning to work together. Laborious keeps that same laborare root, focusing on how much work something takes.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Collaborate is the precise word when two or more people actively work together on a shared creative goal, bringing their unique skills to one purpose. Use it when partnership and mutual creative effort are essential. For simpler helping or just organizing efforts together, the words 'assist' or 'coordinate' work fine.
