Chronology

/kruh-NOL-uh-jee/ — stress on NOL · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A chronology is the order in which a set of events happened, laid out from earliest to latest.

Show it in context

The museum wall shows a chronology of flight, from balloons to rockets.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when chronology is the exact word.

Worked Example

A detective interviews five witnesses to a car accident. Each person remembers the events in a different order—some say the traffic light failed first, others say the crash happened before that. She carefully creates a chronology, placing each event in the exact sequence from earliest to latest.

Chronology is more precise than "sequence" or "list" because it specifically names the *time-based order* of events. In an investigation, getting the chronology right is crucial because cause and effect depend on what happened when—not just any order, but events arranged from earliest to latest.

Your Turn

Before a trip, Sarah lists all the books she read last summer: *The Great Gatsby*, *To Kill a Mockingbird*, *Pride and Prejudice*.

Is chronology the precise word for what Sarah has written?

chronology

Not quite — Sarah only names three books she read; she never arranges anything from earliest to latest, and putting events in time order is exactly what a chronology requires.

list

Correct — A list just gathers items by name in any order, which is precisely what naming the three titles does.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
sequence

The order of items. Chronology is specifically the time-based order of events, not just any sequence.

timeline

A visual representation of events over time. Chronology is the actual order itself, not necessarily displayed.

record

Information about past events. Chronology emphasizes the temporal order, while a record simply documents what happened.

Antonyms · opposites

chaos — Complete disorder with no discernible order or time sequence. Chronology is events arranged precisely by when they occurred.

Build the Word

chronos + logos Greek: "time" + "word/study"

Chronology literally means "the logic or study of time"—it combines two Greek roots to suggest arranging events in their proper time order, revealing the sequence in which things actually happened.

The ancient chronicle described the kings' reigns in order from first to last.

Same root, new word — what does chronicle mean here?

a made-up tale about legendary heroes

Not quite — A chronicle is tied to the root chronos (time): it records real events in the order they happened, so an invented tale does not fit.

a historical account of events in time order

Correct — Correct. Chronicle shares the Greek root chronos (time) with chronology, so a chronicle records events in the order they occurred over time.

The root connection →

Chronology and chronicle both come from the Greek chronos, meaning time. A chronology arranges events by time, and a chronicle tells them in the time order they happened.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / academic

Use chronology in historical, investigative, or analytical contexts where the time-based order of events matters. For a simple list of items without emphasis on time, sequence or list is adequate.
