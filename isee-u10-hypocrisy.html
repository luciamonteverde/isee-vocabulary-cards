Hypocrisy

/hih-PAH-kruh-see/ — stress on second syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Hypocrisy is the act of claiming to hold certain beliefs or standards while actually behaving in a way that contradicts them.

Show it in context

It was pure hypocrisy for the litterer to lecture us about keeping the park clean.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when hypocrisy is the exact word.

Worked Example

The principal who constantly spoke about respect and kindness was caught mocking a struggling student in the hallway.

Hypocrisy is more precise than simply 'dishonesty' because it specifically names the contradiction between claimed beliefs and actual behavior—he preached values he didn't follow. A plain lie might hide the gap; hypocrisy puts it on display.

Your Turn

A friend says she doesn't like spicy food, so she orders mild curry at a restaurant.

Is this hypocrisy?

Yes

Not quite — Hypocrisy means publicly claiming a standard while secretly breaking it. Ordering mild curry after saying she dislikes spicy food is not that at all.

No

Correct — She is simply being honest about her tastes and acting on them. There is no gap between what she claims and what she does, so nothing here is hypocritical.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
deception

Lying or misleading someone—broader than hypocrisy. Hypocrisy specifically means claiming a standard you don't follow.

insincerity

Pretending to feel or believe something you don't. Hypocrisy is about claiming standards you don't actually practice.

phoniness

Being fake or inauthentic in general. Hypocrisy is specifically the gap between preached beliefs and actual actions.

Antonyms · opposites

integrity — Acting according to your stated values—the opposite of the contradiction at the heart of hypocrisy.

How Strong Is It?

Hypocrisy — the severest contradiction between claimed belief and actual behavior: you preach standards then openly break them. (our word)

Build the Word

hypokrites Greek: 'one who acts or plays a part' (hypo- = under, krinein = to judge/decide)

In ancient Greek theater, actors spoke lines and rendered judgments on stage that weren't their own beliefs—they were pretending. This image of false performance became the word for claiming principles you don't actually follow.

The scientist tested her hypothesis about how plants grow in darkness.

Same root, new word — what does hypothesis mean here?

a proven fact that has already been confirmed

Not quite — Tempting because scientists deal in established facts, but the prefix hypo- ('under') marks a hypothesis as an idea placed under examination, not yet confirmed. It is still being tested.

a tentative idea put forth for examination, set 'under' review before it is accepted (from hypo- 'under' + thesis 'proposition')

Correct — The Greek prefix hypo- means 'under.' A hypocrite keeps a truth hidden underneath a pretended one, and a hypothesis sits under examination, placed beneath the test before anyone accepts it.

a final conclusion drawn after the experiment

Not quite — Tempting because it involves an experiment, but hypo- ('under') signals the starting idea set beneath the test, not the ending result the test produces.

The root connection →

Hypocrisy and hypothesis do not share a whole root, only the Greek prefix hypo- ('under'). The krinein ('to judge') in hypocrisy and the thesis ('proposition') in hypothesis are different pieces; it is just the 'under' idea that they hold in common.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Hypocrisy is a serious word for a serious moral flaw—the contradiction between publicly claiming standards and secretly violating them. Reach for it when you want to name this specific gap between preached values and actual behavior. For simple dishonesty or lies, 'deception' or 'lie' works better.
