Bizarre

/biz-AR/ — stress on second syllable, rhymes with far · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something bizarre is so odd or out of the ordinary that it seems strange or hard to believe.

Show it in context

The garden was full of bizarre sculptures, including a giant fork stuck in the lawn.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when bizarre is the exact word.

Worked Example

The garden was full of bizarre sculptures, including a giant fork stuck in the lawn, rubber ducks painted neon pink, and a chandelier hanging from a tree branch.

Bizarre is more precise than 'strange' because it captures extreme and shocking oddness—so unusual it's hard to believe. 'Strange' just means surprising; bizarre means strangely extreme to the point of being unbelievable.

Your Turn

The magician performed a card trick I didn't understand.

Is bizarre the right word here, or would strange work better?

strange

Correct — The trick is confusing and surprising, and 'strange' captures that without exaggerating.

bizarre

Not quite — 'Bizarre' would only fit if the trick seemed to defy reality itself. A card trick you cannot follow is merely strange, not shocking or hard to believe.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
strange

Surprising and unusual, but not necessarily shocking or hard to believe.

weird

Eerie or unsettling oddness, but doesn't carry the same sense of extreme, shocking difference.

outlandish

Wildly exaggerated or outrageous, but emphasizes excess rather than the shockingness of strangeness itself.

Antonyms · opposites

ordinary — Boring and expected—the opposite of bizarre's shockingly strange and hard-to-believe quality.

How Strong Is It?
ordinary→strange→weird→bizarre

Build the Word

bizarro Spanish: uncertain origin, possibly from Italian or Basque

Bizarre traveled from Spanish through French into English, its meaning transforming from something striking and impressive into the shocking, hard-to-believe oddness we know today.

The musician performed her set bizarrely, mixing classical instruments with electronic beats in confusing ways.

Same root, new word — what does bizarrely mean here?

in a smooth, polished, professional way

Not quite — Tempting because it describes a live performance, but the phrase 'in confusing ways' signals oddness, not polish.

in a shockingly strange or surprising manner

Correct — Bizarrely is simply the adverb form of bizarre, so it carries the same meaning in action: doing something in a strikingly strange, hard-to-believe way. Bizarre's own origin is uncertain, so there is no separate root to trace here.

in a very loud, forceful way

Not quite — Tempting because concerts are often loud, but bizarrely points to strangeness and unpredictability, not volume.

The root connection →

Unlike most cards in this course, bizarrely does not introduce a new root; it is the same word as bizarre, just used to describe how an action is done. When you meet an -ly word built on a word you already know, its meaning usually stays the same and only its job in the sentence changes.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Use bizarre when describing things so shockingly odd they're hard to believe or classify. For mild strangeness or everyday surprises, 'strange' or 'weird' are simpler and often better.
