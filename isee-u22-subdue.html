Subdue

/sub-DYOO/ — rhymes with subdue verb

Hear It
What It Means

To subdue someone or something is to overpower it and bring it under control.

Show it in context

Rangers finally subdued the escaped bull with ropes and patience.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

The firefighters finally subdued the raging wildfire after three days.

Subdue is precise because it emphasizes overcoming active resistance and bringing the fire under control — not just managing something stable, but conquering what's wild and defiant. Plain 'controlled' would miss the sense of struggle and triumph.

Prefixsub-under, below (Latin)
Root-due (from dūcere)to lead, to draw (Latin)

Sub- means 'under' and the root traces to Latin dūcere, 'to lead' — so to subdue is literally to 'lead under': to draw someone or something down under your power. A second Latin verb, subdere ('to place under'), shaped the meaning too, which is why subduing means overpowering something and bringing it under control.

Use the root in a new word: conduct

A conductor leads an orchestra through a symphony — the same Latin root dūcere, 'to lead,' hides inside subdue, which literally means to lead something under your control.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Subdue is formal and suggests bringing something wild, rebellious, or resistant under control by force or strong effort. Use it when the struggle or the overcoming is the point. For calming a child or settling a minor issue, the plainer words 'calm' or 'quiet' are better and more natural.
