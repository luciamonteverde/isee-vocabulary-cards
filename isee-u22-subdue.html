What It Means

To subdue someone or something is to overpower it and bring it under control.

Show it in context

Rangers finally subdued the escaped bull with ropes and patience.

Here's how to decide when subdue is the exact word.

Worked Example

The firefighters finally subdued the raging wildfire after three days.

Subdue is precise because it emphasizes overcoming active resistance and bringing the fire under control — not just managing something stable, but conquering what's wild and defiant. Plain 'controlled' would miss the sense of struggle and triumph.

Your Turn

The mother asked her son to quiet down during the movie.

Is subdue the precise word here, or should it be something gentler?

Subdue

Not quite — Subdue suggests forcefully overpowering someone who is resisting. A mother politely asking her child to be quiet during a movie is a gentle everyday request, not a struggle to overcome resistance, so it is far too forceful here.

Quiet down

Correct — This matches the mild, polite tone of a parent's request during a movie. It asks the child to lower his noise without implying any force or battle of wills.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
control

To manage or direct. Subdue specifically implies conquering resistance or overcoming something defiant.

overcome

To defeat or get past something difficult. Subdue adds the sense of bringing under lasting control, not just winning one moment.

tame

To make docile and manageable. Subdue is more forceful and can suggest sudden overpowering, while taming is often gradual.

Antonyms · opposites

liberate — To set free — the opposite of subdue's control.

Build the Word

subduc- Latin: sub- 'under' + ducere 'to lead'

To subdue something is literally to lead it beneath your control — to put it under your authority.

The tour guide conducted us through the museum with expertise.

Same root, new word — what does conduct mean here?

to lead or guide

Correct — Correct. 'Conduct' keeps the ducere 'to lead' root of 'subdue' (sub- 'under' plus ducere 'to lead'): a guide who conducts a tour leads people through it.

to end or shut down

Not quite — Not quite. 'Conduct' shares the ducere 'to lead' root with 'subdue,' so it means to lead or guide people along, not to end or close something.

The root connection →

Both 'subdue' and 'conduct' come from the Latin ducere, 'to lead.' To subdue is literally to lead someone under your control, while to conduct is to lead a group along.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Subdue is formal and suggests bringing something wild, rebellious, or resistant under control by force or strong effort. Use it when the struggle or the overcoming is the point. For calming a child or settling a minor issue, the plainer words 'calm' or 'quiet' are better and more natural.
