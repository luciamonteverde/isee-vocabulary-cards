Pester

/PES-ter/ — rhymes with jester · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To pester someone is to bother them again and again with annoying requests, questions, or behavior.

Show it in context

The kids pestered their dad for ice cream until he finally gave in.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when pester is the exact word.

Worked Example

Maya's little brother keeps asking 'Are we there yet?' every five minutes during the long car ride, even though she's told him to stop.

The brother's behavior is relentless—he keeps doing the same annoying thing over and over. That repeated quality of the annoyance is what pester captures. 'Bother' could work, but pester is more precise because it specifically names the wearing-down effect of repetition.

Your Turn

A student forgets her math homework once and her teacher reminds her about it one time.

Is pester the right word, or should it say 'remind'?

remind

Correct — One reminder is not pestering; it is just a single notification, which is what remind names.

pester

Not quite — Pester requires repetition and a building sense of annoyance. A single reminder given one time does not have that repeated, wearing-down quality.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
bother

A general word for causing inconvenience. Bother doesn't emphasize the repetition that makes pestering so wearing.

bug

A casual word with the same meaning, but lighter and less forceful than pester.

nag

To nag emphasizes repetitive complaining and demanding—closer to pester, but with a whinier tone.

Antonyms · opposites

appease — To calm and satisfy someone — the opposite of pestering them with repeated demands.

Build the Word

pestis Latin: 'plague, disease, or nuisance'

A pest plagues you with constant trouble, just as plague afflicts you over and over — to pester is to plague someone with repeated annoyance.

The mosquitoes were a pest all summer, biting everyone in the backyard.

Same root, new word — what does pest mean here?

an annoying creature or person that causes repeated trouble

Correct — Pester and pest both grow from the Latin pestis, 'plague.' Summer mosquitoes plague the yard again and again, and to pester someone is to plague them with that same repeated annoyance.

a welcome, helpful visitor

Not quite — Pest and pester come from pestis, 'plague,' so a pest is unwelcome and troublesome, not helpful.

a creature seen only once and quickly gone

Not quite — Pestis ('plague') implies repeated, ongoing trouble, like mosquitoes all summer, not a single passing visit.

The root connection →

The shared root is pestis, 'plague': a pest afflicts you over and over like a plague, and pester is simply the verb for inflicting that repeated, plague-like nuisance on a person.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

informal / conversational

Use pester when someone's annoying behavior happens over and over — it's perfect for describing a kid who keeps asking for the same thing repeatedly. In formal writing, 'bother' or 'trouble' might be more appropriate, but pester captures the worn-down, accumulated annoyance that repetition creates.
