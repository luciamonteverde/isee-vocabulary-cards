Dialogue

/DY-uh-log/ — rhymes with analog · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A dialogue is a conversation between two or more people, especially one written into a play, book, or movie.

Show it in context

The movie's best dialogue is the rooftop argument between the two sisters.

Here's how to decide when dialogue is the exact word.

Worked Example

In the play, the characters' witty dialogue about love and war reveals their intelligence and playfulness.

Dialogue is precise because it means conversation, especially in a written work—not just any communication, but back-and-forth speech. It captures the give-and-take between characters in a way that 'conversation' alone doesn't emphasize the literary/performed aspect.

Your Turn

Two students text back and forth about homework assignments.

Is dialogue the right word for this exchange?

conversation

Correct — Two people trading homework questions and answers are simply having a conversation or exchanging messages. That fits exactly what is happening here without implying scripted or spoken speech.

dialogue

Not quite — The students are firing quick notes back and forth on a screen, which is casual texting. 'Dialogue' points to spoken conversation, especially the lines characters speak in a play or novel, so it overstates this informal text thread.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
conversation

General back-and-forth speech. Dialogue emphasizes the literary or performed context, while conversation is neutral.

monologue

One person speaking. Dialogue requires at least two speakers.

exchange

Back-and-forth of any kind, not necessarily spoken. Dialogue is specifically spoken conversation.

Antonyms · opposites

silence — The complete absence of conversation or speech. While dialogue is language flowing between people, silence is the void of any speech.

Build the Word

dia- (between) + logos (word) Greek: 'speech between' or 'conversation through'

Dialogue is words flowing between people—the 'dia' (between) and 'logos' (word) literally capture conversation.

The student's logic for why she deserved a better grade was sound and well-reasoned.

Same root, new word — what does logic mean here?

reasoning, the connection between ideas

Correct — Correct. 'Logic' comes from the Greek logos, 'word' or 'reason,' so it means reasoning and the way ideas connect.

a sudden burst of strong emotion

Not quite — This misses the root. 'Logic' carries the Greek logos, 'word' or 'reason,' so it names careful reasoning, not an emotional outburst.

The root connection →

Dialogue is dia- ('between') plus logos ('word'), literally 'words between people.' Logic keeps that same logos root, which is why it points to reasoned words and ideas.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / literary

Dialogue is the word for written or performed conversation, especially when it's important to the story. For casual chat, 'conversation' works fine. When you want to emphasize the back-and-forth and the literary context, reach for dialogue.
