Symbiosis

/sim-by-OH-sis/ — stress on third syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Symbiosis is a close, ongoing relationship between two different living things. Usually each one gets something it needs, like food or protection, from being near the other.

Show it in context

The clownfish and the anemone live in symbiosis, each protecting the other.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when symbiosis is the exact word.

Worked Example

The acacia tree and the ant colony live in symbiosis: the ants defend the tree from insects, while the tree provides them with food and shelter in its thorns.

Symbiosis captures the mutual benefit and deeply ongoing relationship precisely. You could say 'they help each other,' but symbiosis emphasizes that each living thing gains something essential and the relationship is permanent and interdependent, not temporary or one-sided.

Your Turn

A student borrowed a biology textbook from a friend. The student got to study for the test, and the friend got her book back in good condition.

Is symbiosis the precise word to describe this relationship?

symbiosis

Not quite — Symbiosis is a close, ongoing interdependence between living organisms. Borrowing a textbook once and returning it in good shape is a single favor between friends, not a lasting biological bond, so symbiosis overstates it.

an exchange

Correct — Each person gave and got one thing (the student got to study, the friend got her book back), which is exactly a one-time exchange rather than a permanent relationship.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
partnership

Mutual cooperation between parties, but doesn't emphasize the biological context or the specific benefits each living thing provides.

mutualism

Very similar to symbiosis—a mutually beneficial relationship—but more technical and specific to biology, whereas symbiosis is slightly broader.

interdependence

Mutual reliance, but doesn't specify what each party provides or gain—just that they need each other.

Antonyms · opposites

competition — Organisms fighting for the same resources rather than helping each other—the opposite of the mutual-benefit relationship symbiosis requires.

How Strong Is It?
coexistence→cooperation→partnership→symbiosis

Symbiosis — the strongest of the group: a deeply interdependent relationship where both living things are essential to each other's survival. (our word)

Build the Word

sym- + bios Greek: 'sym' (together) + 'bios' (life)'

Symbiosis literally means 'living together'—two organisms living in such close relationship that they're almost like one unit, their lives woven together.

Same root, new word — what does biography mean here?

the study of plant and animal life

Not quite — That definition matches 'biology,' not 'biography.' Both use the bios root ('life'), but the -graphy ending means 'writing,' so a biography is writing about a life, not the study of living things.

a written story of someone's life

Correct — Correct. 'Biography' shares the bios root ('life') with symbiosis; adding -graphy ('writing') makes it a written account of someone's life.

The root connection →

Symbiosis comes from sym- ('together') plus bios ('life'), meaning organisms living together. Biography reuses that same bios root, this time for writing about a person's life.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / scientific

Symbiosis is the precise word when describing living things that have a close, mutually beneficial relationship. Use it when both organisms depend on each other for something essential. For looser partnerships or cooperation, the simpler word 'partnership' works fine.
