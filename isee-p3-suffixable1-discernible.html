Discernible

dih-SUR-nuh-bul adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Discernible is able to be perceived, recognized, or made out clearly, especially with careful attention.

Show it in context

Only after the conservator cleaned three centuries of grime from the canvas did the artist's faint, original signature finally become discernible in the corner.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

To the untrained ear, the twins' voices sounded identical, but their music teacher insisted a discernible difference existed in how each one held a high note.

Discernible describes something that can be noticed or told apart, often only with careful attention -- it isn't always obvious at first glance.

Discernible comes from Latin dis- ('apart') + cernere ('to separate, sift, perceive') -- the same root behind ascertain, which you already met in Part 2 of this course. To discern something is to 'sift it apart' from everything around it; if you can do that, it's discernible. Notice the spelling, too: discern is already a real English word, yet it still takes -ible rather than -able -- another of this unit's exceptions.

Use the root in a new word: ascertain

Investigators worked for weeks to ascertain the exact cause of the fire.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Discernible often implies effort or careful attention -- a discernible change is one you might miss if you weren't looking closely, unlike something obvious or blatant.
