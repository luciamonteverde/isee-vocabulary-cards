Tentative

/TEN-uh-tiv/ — stress on first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Tentative describes something not yet fixed or certain, treated as a provisional first attempt rather than a final decision.

Show it in context

They set a tentative date for the picnic, weather permitting.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when tentative is the exact word.

Worked Example

The startup announced a tentative product launch for June, but warned investors that the date could shift if development fell behind.

Tentative is more precise than "unsure" because it emphasizes not personal doubt but rather a provisional arrangement—a first attempt that is deliberately left open to change. This is exactly what a business announcement requires.

Your Turn

Sarah made a tentative guess on the history test, marking an answer she wasn't confident about.

Is tentative the right word here?

uncertain

Correct — A one-time guess she is not confident about is simply an uncertain answer.

tentative

Not quite — Tentative means provisional and open to revision, like a tentative plan or schedule that may still change. A single test guess is just uncertain, not something being held for later revision.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
provisional

Temporary or conditional. Provisional is more official and bureaucratic; tentative suggests a casual first attempt.

uncertain

Not sure or confident. Uncertain describes feelings of doubt; tentative describes something put forward as a trial, not finalized.

hesitant

Reluctant or slow to act. Hesitant describes a reluctant person or action; tentative describes something provisional and open to change.

Antonyms · opposites

definite — Fixed and certain — the opposite of provisional and open to revision.

Tentative — put forward as a first attempt or trial, not yet final. (our word)

Build the Word

tentare Latin: to try, to attempt

The root image is a first try or attempt—something you test out before committing to it. Tentative preserves that sense: a first stab, open to revision.

She made three attempts at the painting before getting it right.

Same root, new word — what does attempt mean here?

a finished masterpiece

Not quite — Tempting because the sentence is about painting, but an attempt is a try, not a completed result.

a long break from work

Not quite — Tempting if you misread the sentence, but attempt means an effort to do something, not a rest.

a try or effort at doing something

Correct — Tentative and attempt both trace to Latin tentare, 'to try.' Something tentative is done as a trial, still open to change, and an attempt is a single try at doing something.

The root connection →

Both words carry the idea of trying built into tentare: tentative describes an approach held loosely and ready to be revised, while an attempt is one concrete try along the way.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Use tentative when you're putting forward a plan, schedule, or arrangement as a first draft that is likely to change or be finalized later. Use "provisional" for official temporary arrangements. Use "uncertain" or "unsure" for personal feelings of doubt.
