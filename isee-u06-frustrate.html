Frustrate

/frə-STRĀT/ — stress on second syllable, rhymes with gate · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To frustrate someone is to block them from getting what they want or expect, leaving them annoyed and disappointed.

Show it in context

The constant pop-up ads frustrate anyone trying to read the article.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when frustrate is the exact word.

Worked Example

During the championship soccer match, the referee called back a goal the team had just scored for a missed offside call, frustrating the entire team who thought they had won.

Frustrate captures exactly what happens: the team is actively blocked from their goal (the scored point) and left annoyed and disappointed. A milder word like 'disappoint' would miss the sense that they were prevented from what they expected.

Your Turn

Megan tried out for the school play but didn't get the role she wanted. She's been sad about it all week.

Is frustrate the precise word here, or something else?

disappointment

Correct — Megan is unhappy about not getting the role, which is what disappointment names. Nothing actively blocked her from something she expected to achieve.

frustrate

Not quite — Frustrate requires the sense of being actively blocked or prevented from a goal you were reaching for. Here there is just an unwanted outcome, not a blocked effort.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
annoy

To cause irritation; frustrate includes not just irritation but the sense of being blocked from a goal.

disappoint

To let someone down emotionally; frustrate adds the sense of active prevention or blocking.

thwart

To deliberately block or prevent; thwart is more forceful and formal than frustrate.

Antonyms · opposites

assist — To help or enable; frustrate is the opposite — preventing what someone wants.

How Strong Is It?
disappoint→irritate→exasperate→frustrate

Frustrate — the strongest of this group: actively blocked from a goal and left annoyed and disappointed. (our word)

Build the Word

frustrari Latin: 'to disappoint; to be ineffectual'

The Latin root captures the idea of being rendered null or ineffective — when someone is frustrated, their efforts and expectations come to nothing.

Despite every plan they tried, their frustration grew as each effort came to nothing.

Same root, new word — what does frustration mean here?

a feeling of calm contentment

Not quite — The root frustra means 'in vain,' so frustration is the sour feeling of effort wasted, the opposite of contentment.

eager excitement about something new

Not quite — Frustra ('in vain') points to efforts coming to nothing, which brings irritation, not excitement.

the sense of being blocked so that effort leads nowhere

Correct — Frustrate and frustration both come straight from the Latin frustra, 'in vain.' Frustration is simply the noun for that feeling of effort spent in vain, the same rendering-ineffective that frustrate names.

The root connection →

Because frustration is a direct derivative of frustrate, the root frustra ('in vain') is identical in both, not a surprising new cousin: each word names effort or hope that comes to nothing.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Frustrate goes beyond mere annoyance or disappointment — it specifically means being blocked from what you want or expect, then left annoyed and disappointed. Use annoyed for mild irritation; use frustrated when someone is actively prevented from reaching a goal.
