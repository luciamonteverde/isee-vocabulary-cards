What It Means

Penitent means feeling and showing sorrow for having done wrong, leaning toward confessing or making amends.

See the word in context

The penitent shoplifter returned to the store the next day to apologize and pay for everything he'd taken.

Standing penitent before the school board, the student admitted to copying his friend's homework and asked what he could do to make things right.

Penitent describes genuine remorse specifically tied to wrongdoing -- not just sadness, but sorrow over a mistake or a wrong committed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    remorseful: Remorseful centers on the gnawing feeling itself -- deep, private regret over a wrong. Penitent points outward as well as inward: a penitent person shows their sorrow and leans toward confessing or making amends, not just aching over the mistake.

    repentant: Repentant means having genuinely turned away from the wrongdoing -- the emphasis falls on change, on resolving never to do it again. Penitent stresses the sorrow being felt and shown right now; you can look penitent before any real turning has happened.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unrepentant: not feeling shame or guilt about one's actions

    defiant: boldly resistant or unwilling to comply, showing no regret

    Where it sits · how far regret turns into action

    apologetic → penitent → atone

    apologetic vs. penitent: A short, apologetic 'sorry' patches over a minor slip with little real reflection -- lighter than penitent, which carries genuine sorrow over the wrong itself.

    atone vs. penitent: This unit's own word atone goes further than penitent -- it describes taking real action to make up for a wrong, not just feeling sorry about it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Penitent describes the feeling of sorrow over wrongdoing; its sibling word in this unit, atone, describes the action of making up for it -- you can feel penitent without yet doing anything to atone.
