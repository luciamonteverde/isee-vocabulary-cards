What It Means

Subsistence is the minimal food, money, or resources needed to stay alive.

See the word in context

Anthropologists studied a community that had survived for centuries on subsistence fishing along the icy coastline.

During the siege, the city's remaining residents survived on a subsistence diet of rationed bread and whatever vegetables could be grown in bombed-out courtyards.

Subsistence emphasizes survival at the bare minimum -- just enough to get by, with little or nothing extra.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    survival: Survival is the bare fact of coming through alive — a shipwreck, an illness, a brutal winter. Subsistence is not about escaping danger: it is the minimal level of food, money, and resources a household lives on. Survival names an outcome; subsistence names a way of getting by with nothing to spare.

    bare minimum: The bare minimum is the smallest amount of anything that will do — the least effort, the least money, the fewest supplies. Subsistence applies that idea to staying alive: the minimal food and resources a person or family actually lives on. You can do the bare minimum of homework; subsistence is a way of living.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    abundance: a very large quantity; more than enough

    prosperity: the state of being successful and thriving, especially financially

    Where it sits · how close to having nothing

    modest → subsistence → destitution

    modest vs. subsistence: Modest means not large or extravagant, but not necessarily surviving on the bare minimum like subsistence.

    destitution vs. subsistence: Destitution describes an even more severe, complete lack of resources than mere subsistence.
