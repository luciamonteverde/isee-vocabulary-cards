Meaning

What It Means

Obtuse is annoyingly slow to understand what is obvious.

See the word in context

Marcus was being deliberately obtuse about whose turn it was to do the dishes, though the chore chart hung right beside him.

Everyone at the meeting has said, three different ways, that the deadline cannot be met. The obtuse new supervisor nods along, then asks brightly, 'So we're all set for Friday, then?'

He is annoyingly slow to grasp what is obvious to everyone else in the room. Obtuse can describe genuine dullness or -- very often -- a convenient, deliberate refusal to understand.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    oblivious: Oblivious means completely unaware that something is happening at all -- nothing has registered. An obtuse person is different: he has seen and heard the thing everyone else finds plain and is still annoyingly slow to take it in.

    clueless: Clueless means having no idea what is going on or how to proceed -- a clueless newcomer simply lacks the information. Obtuse is harsher: the obtuse listener has every clue in front of him, plain to everyone else, and still fails to catch on.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sharp: quick to notice and understand

    quick-witted: fast to grasp and respond

    Where it sits · how harsh the charge of slowness is

    dense → obtuse → dim-witted

    dense vs. obtuse: Dense is the everyday word for slow understanding, while obtuse adds the sting of annoyance -- an obtuse listener is missing something so obvious that everyone else is losing patience.

    dim-witted vs. obtuse: Dim-witted is the strongest and least kind: it declares the mind itself slow, while obtuse often accuses someone of choosing not to understand -- being deliberately obtuse is a strategy, not a limitation.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Obtuse is a criticism of understanding, and it often implies choice: 'deliberately obtuse' is one of the word's favorite pairings. Use it for people and their refusals to see the obvious -- its sting lands harder than dense, because it accuses the listener of failing at something everyone else found easy.
