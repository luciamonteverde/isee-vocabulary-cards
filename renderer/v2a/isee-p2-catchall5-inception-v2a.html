inception

in-SEP-shun — contains 'cept,' to take hold; at its inception, an idea first takes hold.

What It Means

Inception is the formal starting point at which an organization, project, or plan begins.

See the word in context

The club has kept a photo from every single meeting since its inception fifteen years ago.

A tiny lemonade stand run by two kids on a single summer weekend eventually grows into a chain of shops across the state. Years later, a reporter asks the founders to describe the company's inception — how it all first started.

The reporter wants the very first moment it began, before any of the later growth. Unlike 'history,' which covers the whole span of time, inception points specifically to the beginning.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    origin: The origin of something is where it came from, the source or root it grew out of, however informal or hazy. An inception is narrower: the formal moment an organization, project, or plan officially began. A tradition's origin may be murky; an inception has a date.

    founding: Founding is the deliberate act of establishing something: people found schools, towns, and companies. An inception is the point in time when something formally began, rather than the act of setting it up, and it also covers plans and projects that nobody 'founds.'

    onset: The onset is the start of something that arrives on its own and then runs its course: the onset of a storm, an illness, or winter. An inception is the formal beginning of something people create on purpose, like a club, a company, or a plan.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    conclusion: the end or final part of something

    termination: the act of ending something

    Where it sits · how grand and formal the beginning is

    start → inception → genesis

    start vs. inception: Start is a plainer, weaker word than inception — start can refer to any beginning, casual or formal, while inception specifically marks the formal beginning of something significant.

    genesis vs. inception: Genesis can feel even more significant than inception: both mark a beginning, but genesis often implies the very origin of something enormous or foundational.
