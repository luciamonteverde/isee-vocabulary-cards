ostensible

What It Means

Ostensible is stated or appearing to be true, but not necessarily so; put forward as the reason, though perhaps not the real one.

See the word in context

The store's ostensible closing time was 9 p.m., but the manager quietly let regular customers keep shopping well past ten.

When the celebrity chef closed her flagship restaurant, citing 'creative burnout' in a brief press release, insiders later revealed a far messier financial dispute with her business partner lurking behind that ostensible explanation.

Ostensible marks a stated reason or appearance that's presented to the world, while hinting that the real truth might be something else entirely.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    apparent: Apparent describes how things look from the evidence in front of you, and an apparent truth may well turn out to be real. Ostensible is more suspicious: it points at the surface story or stated reason while hinting that the real one is different.

    professed: Professed means openly declared by the person themselves -- a professed vegetarian has said so out loud, sincerely or not. Ostensible is broader and quieter: it covers surface stories no one ever announced, and it always hints that the real story may differ.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    genuine: real and true, without pretense

    actual: existing in fact, not merely claimed

    Where it sits · how deliberately the surface story misleads

    claimed → ostensible → deceptive

    claimed vs. ostensible: Claimed is weaker than ostensible: it just reports what someone said, while ostensible adds a raised eyebrow -- a hint that the stated version may not be the real one.

    deceptive vs. ostensible: Deceptive goes further than ostensible -- it means the false front was built on purpose, to intentionally mislead.

    Word Choice
