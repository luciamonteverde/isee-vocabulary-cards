decadent

DEK-uh-dunt -- Latin de- ('down') plus cadere ('to fall'): pleasure so heavy that standards fall down under it.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Decadent is self-indulgent to an excess far past what anyone actually needs, and sometimes morally corrupt.

See the word in context

The emperor's decadent banquets lasted three days and emptied the treasury.

A decadent weekend package includes a marble bath filled with rose petals, a private chef for every meal, and gold-flecked truffles delivered to the room at midnight.

Comfort and pleasure piled far past what anyone actually needs is what makes the package decadent. It can also carry a sharper edge: calling a society decadent suggests all that indulgence has left it soft and corrupt.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    luxurious: Luxurious means rich, comfortable, and expensive -- a luxurious hotel simply offers the best of everything, and nobody is criticized for it. Decadent adds a judgment: the comfort has slid past enjoyment into excess, and calling a person or an era decadent hints that all the indulgence has rotted their discipline.

    hedonistic: Hedonistic describes a person or way of life devoted to chasing pleasure -- pleasure is the goal that organizes everything else. Decadent looks at the excess itself and what it costs: a hedonistic prince pursues delight, while a decadent court has been softened and corrupted by years of it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    restrained: holding back from excess

    modest: plain and not extravagant

    Where it sits · how far pleasure slides into moral ruin

    indulgent → decadent → dissolute

    indulgent vs. decadent: Indulgent is the gentler word -- an indulgent dessert is just a treat, while decadent suggests indulgence taken to an eyebrow-raising extreme.

    dissolute vs. decadent: Dissolute is decadent at its harshest: decadent can be a playful compliment about a chocolate cake, while dissolute is a flat moral judgment about a wasted, pleasure-soaked life.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Decadent has two moods. About food it is nearly a compliment -- a decadent dessert is gloriously rich. About people, empires, or eras it is an accusation, suggesting that comfort has rotted away discipline and morals.
