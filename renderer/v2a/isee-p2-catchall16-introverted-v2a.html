introverted

IN-truh-vur-ted -- Latin intro- ('inward') + vertere ('to turn').

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Introverted means naturally drawn toward solitude and one's own thoughts rather than outward socializing.

See the word in context

As a deeply introverted kid, he'd rather spend recess reading alone than join the kickball game.

Her introverted roommate wasn't rude, exactly -- she just needed a full hour alone after class before she felt like talking to anyone.

That natural pull toward solitude and one's own thoughts, rather than outward socializing, is exactly what introverted describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    solitary: Solitary describes the situation -- being or working alone, like a lighthouse keeper on a months-long post. Introverted describes the person's wiring: where energy comes from. An introverted student can spend all day on a crowded team; solitary says only that someone is by themselves right now.

    private: A private person guards personal information -- they dislike sharing the details of their life. Introverted is about energy, not secrecy: an introverted friend may happily tell you everything about her week and still find a long party draining, needing quiet afterward.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    outgoing: friendly and socially confident

    gregarious: very sociable and fond of company

    Where it sits · how far the person turns inward

    reserved → introverted → withdrawn

    reserved vs. introverted: Reserved is milder than introverted: a reserved teammate is just quiet at first, while an introverted one is steadily turned inward, recharging in his own thoughts rather than in company.

    withdrawn vs. introverted: Withdrawn is stronger than introverted: an introverted player simply prefers quiet, but a withdrawn one pulls away entirely, even skipping meals with the rest of the team.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Introverted describes a natural preference for solitude and inner reflection over outward socializing -- it isn't the same as shyness caused by fear, though the two can look similar from the outside. Notice the intro- ('inward') prefix directly contrasts with extraneous, taught earlier in this same unit, whose extra- prefix points outward instead.
