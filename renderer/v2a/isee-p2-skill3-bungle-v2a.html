bungle

BUNG-guhl

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To handle a task so clumsily or carelessly that the task fails.

See the word in context

The new waiter bungled our order so thoroughly that the table beside us got three desserts and we got a bowl of soup nobody had asked for.

The magician's new assistant bungled the big reveal, opening the wrong box so that the 'vanished' rabbit sat in plain view before the trick had even begun.

Nobody sabotaged the show, and nothing broke on its own. The assistant had one job, handled it carelessly, and the trick collapsed because of that. That combination — a task, the doer's own clumsiness, and a task that fails as a result — is what makes this a bungle rather than plain bad luck.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spoil: to make something worse or no good, from any cause at all — rain spoils a picnic, one ringing phone spoils a concert. Bungle names a single cause: someone's own clumsy handling of a job. To tell them apart, ask whether the damage came from a person doing a task badly or from something else entirely.

    blunder: one careless, foolish mistake — the wrong turn, the wrong name, the wrong number. Bungle covers the whole job, not the single slip inside it. To tell them apart, ask whether the sentence is about one wrong move or a task that fell apart.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    accomplish: to carry a task through to a successful finish.

    pull off: to succeed at something tricky, against the odds.

    Where it sits · how much damage clumsy handling does

    fumble → bungle → wreck

    fumble vs. bungle: fumble is the clumsy moment — the dropped ball, the keys stuck in a pocket — and you can fumble and still recover. Bungle means the whole task is lost. Ask whether the sentence ends in failure or only in awkwardness.

    wreck vs. bungle: wreck is total destruction, and anything can do it — a storm, a crash, a careless person. Bungle is narrower: a person handling a task badly, and what is lost is the task, not necessarily any object. Ask whether something was destroyed outright or a job simply came to nothing.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Bungle is slightly informal, and it always blames the person doing the job: things do not bungle themselves, and weather or bad luck cannot bungle anything. Use it when someone had a task, handled it clumsily, and the task failed because of that. You bungle an attempt, a plan, a rescue, an introduction — the job, not the object. On the ISEE, the closest match usually offered for bungle is ruin: to bungle a plan is to ruin it, but specifically through your own fumbling.
