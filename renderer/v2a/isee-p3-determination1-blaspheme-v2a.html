Hear It

What It Means

To blaspheme is to speak with irreverence or disrespect about God or sacred things.

See the word in context

In the small mountain village, even a careless joke about the saints could be seen as blaspheming everything the townspeople held sacred.

The comedian's routine, which mocked several religious rituals on live television, led critics to accuse him of blaspheming openly for laughs.

Speaking disrespectfully about sacred beliefs or figures, especially in a way that shocks or offends, is exactly what blaspheme describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mock: To mock is to ridicule anyone or anything -- an accent, a haircut, a rule -- and it needs a target being made fun of. To blaspheme is narrower and graver: speaking with irreverence about God or sacred things, even carelessly, with no joke aimed at anyone.

    profane: To profane something sacred is to treat it with disrespect through action -- trampling a shrine, looting a temple, breaking holy vessels. To blaspheme is to do that kind of damage with words: irreverent speech about God or holy things.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    honor: to show great respect for

    worship: to show deep religious devotion

    Where it sits · how harsh the contempt is

    tease → blaspheme → scorn

    tease vs. blaspheme: Tease is far milder than blaspheme: teasing pokes light, often affectionate fun at someone, while to blaspheme is to disrespect what a whole community holds most sacred -- a much graver offense.

    scorn vs. blaspheme: Scorn is harsher than blaspheme: a careless joke can blaspheme without any hatred behind it, while scorn pours open, deliberate contempt on its target and means every word of it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Blaspheme specifically involves disrespect toward religious or sacred subjects -- it's a much narrower, more specific word than a general insult. Using it outside a religious context to mean simply 'to insult' would be a stretch most careful writers avoid.
