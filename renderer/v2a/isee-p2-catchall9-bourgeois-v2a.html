bourgeois

boor-ZHWAH -- borrowed directly from French, describing a member of the middle class.

Meaning

What It Means

Bourgeois is of, relating to, or typical of the comfortable middle class; sometimes used to suggest excessive concern with material comfort and respectability.

See the word in context

Critics dismissed the sprawling suburban development as a symbol of bourgeois comfort and conformity.

A family carefully arranges matching furniture, a perfectly trimmed lawn, and a new car in the driveway, all chosen mainly to signal to the neighbors just how comfortably bourgeois they have become.

That comfortable, status-conscious, middle-class conformity is what bourgeois describes. Unlike 'aristocratic,' which implies inherited noble status, bourgeois describes an earned, middle-class respectability.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    conventional: Conventional means following the accepted customs and norms -- a conventional wedding, a conventional answer -- in any area of life. Bourgeois ties that conformity to one social class in particular, the comfortable middle class, and often adds a mildly mocking edge about its safe, settled taste.

    conformist: Conformist describes someone who goes along with whatever the group expects, mainly from fear of standing out -- any group, any class. Bourgeois names one specific social world, the comfortable middle class, and often criticizes its love of material comfort along with its conformity.

    respectable: Respectable means meeting society's standards of decent, proper behavior -- dressing correctly, paying the bills, avoiding scandal. Bourgeois covers that same respectability but pins it to the middle class, and writers often use it to mock that respectability as smug, safe, and dull.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    proletarian: relating to the working class

    bohemian: unconventional, especially in artistic or social habits

    Where it sits · how sharply the label criticizes

    middle-class → bourgeois → materialistic

    middle-class vs. bourgeois: Middle-class is a fairly neutral, descriptive term -- it simply names a social and economic group. Bourgeois can describe the same group, but often carries a slightly critical edge, suggesting excessive comfort or conformity.

    materialistic vs. bourgeois: Materialistic can feel more pointed than bourgeois: bourgeois broadly describes a middle-class way of life, while materialistic specifically criticizes an excessive focus on possessions and status.
