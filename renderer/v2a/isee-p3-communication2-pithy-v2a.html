What It Means

Pithy is using few, but clever words.

See the word in context

Instead of a long explanation, the coach offered one pithy line before the game: 'Play for each other, not for the scoreboard.'

Her pithy reply -- 'good, fast, cheap: pick two' -- ended the budget argument in one sentence.

The line was short, but it was also clever enough to capture the whole argument at once -- both qualities together are what make a remark pithy, not just brevity alone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    succinct: Succinct praises how clearly an idea is packed into few words -- a succinct summary wastes nothing. Pithy goes further: a pithy remark is not just compact but clever, with a witty snap. Succinct is about efficiency; pithy is about efficiency plus wit.

    terse: Terse means short and clipped, sometimes so blunt it feels cold -- a terse reply can end a conversation without any warmth. Pithy is short too, but it is admired for its wit. Terse trims words to the bone; pithy sharpens them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    verbose: using far more words than necessary

    Where it sits · how much wit the shortness carries

    brief → pithy → razor-sharp

    brief vs. pithy: Brief just means short; pithy adds real cleverness and punch to that shortness.

    razor-sharp vs. pithy: A razor-sharp remark goes even further than pithy, suggesting a cutting wit as well as concision.
