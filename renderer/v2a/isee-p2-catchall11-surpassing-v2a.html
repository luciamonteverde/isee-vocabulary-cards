surpassing

sur-PASS-ing -- an adjective describing excellence, not the ordinary verb 'to surpass.'

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Surpassing is excelling beyond all others, standing above every direct comparison.

See the word in context

Critics agreed the young violinist played with a surpassing grace that few professionals ever achieve.

A chef prepares a signature dish of surpassing quality, so far beyond anything else on the menu that food critics travel across the country just to taste it once.

That distance above every ordinary comparison is what makes the dish's quality surpassing. Unlike 'decent,' which is simply acceptable, surpassing means truly extraordinary.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unrivaled: Unrivaled means having no rival -- no competitor currently matches it. Surpassing describes the quality itself as exceeding all others: a violinist of surpassing grace. Unrivaled ranks something against its actual competition, while surpassing praises how far the quality outshines every comparison.

    superb: Superb means excellent, first-rate -- strong, everyday praise for a fine meal or performance. Surpassing is rarer and more formal, a literary word reserved for qualities that stand above every comparison: surpassing grace, surpassing beauty, surpassing skill.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mediocre: of only ordinary or middling quality

    inferior: lower in quality or ability

    Where it sits · how far above all rivals

    exceptional → surpassing → peerless

    exceptional vs. surpassing: Exceptional is a bit milder than surpassing -- exceptional simply means unusually good, while surpassing specifically emphasizes standing above every direct comparison.

    peerless vs. surpassing: Peerless can feel even stronger than surpassing: surpassing means excelling beyond all others, while peerless suggests there is truly no equal to be found anywhere.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Surpassing is a formal, literary adjective usually paired with an admirable quality, as in 'surpassing talent,' 'surpassing beauty,' or 'surpassing skill' -- it does not describe an action the way the verb 'surpass' does.
