impact

IM-pakt

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impact is the strong force one thing has on another — a change that hits so hard that everyone can tell the thing has been changed by it, the way a crash leaves a mark on whatever it hits.

See the word in context

The new bridge had an immediate impact on the town: stores that had stood empty since 2019 were full again by the end of the month.

One letter about the school's broken roof had a bigger impact than a year of phone calls; within a week the district found the money to fix it.

The letter did not just get read and put away — it changed things, fast and in a big way, so that a year-old problem was suddenly being fixed. That hard, clear change is exactly what impact means: the force of one thing on another. Notice that a year of phone calls had produced some talk and some notes, so they had some small effect, but nobody would say they had an impact. The word is saved for the change everyone can see.

    Section 2

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    influence: Influence works slowly and from the background — a teacher, a book or a friend changing how someone thinks over years, often without anyone seeing it happen at the time. Impact hits all at once and is felt right away, and it can fall on things that do not think, like a town or a river. Ask whether the change came slowly and in the background to a person's way of thinking, or hit hard and fast on whatever was in its way.

    result: Result is what you end up with when something is over — the score, the answer, the grade. It is the thing that comes out at the end. Impact is the force that one thing puts on another along the way, not what comes out. Ask whether the sentence names what something turned into, or how hard one thing hit another.

    difference: Difference is the space between two things or between two moments — how far the before stands from the after. Impact is what made that space: the force of one thing acting on another. You measure a difference; you feel an impact. Ask whether the sentence is setting two things side by side, or telling you the force that changed one of them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cause: what starts a change, rather than the change itself.

    touch: a contact so light that it changes almost nothing.

    Where it sits · how hard one thing hits another

    effect → impact → blow

    effect vs. impact: Effect is the soft, wide end — anything at all that happens because of something else, a small change or a huge one, good or bad, quiet or loud. Every impact is an effect, but only the effects that hit hard and change things in a way people can see count as an impact. Ask whether the sentence shows the change as big and hard; if it could be small or quiet, effect is the better word.

    blow vs. impact: Blow is the far end — a hard hit that leaves damage, either to the body or to a person's plans: losing the job was a blow to the family. A blow is always bad news. An impact can be good, bad or mixed, and a program can have a positive impact. Ask whether the sentence is about something that did damage, or only about something that changed things in a big way.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Impact began as the word for a crash, one object running into another — the impact of the car against the wall, the plane was destroyed on impact — and it still has that feeling of a hit even when the change is not physical. That is why it goes with big, strong words: a huge impact, a major impact, a big impact on the world. A change that is small or slow is usually just an effect. The ISEE tests the figure-of-speech sense in items where one word is missing from a sentence, almost always in the frame 'had a ____ impact on'. Many people now also use impact as an action word ('the storm impacted travel'); the first use is the better choice in careful writing.
