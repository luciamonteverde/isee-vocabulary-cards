pragmatism

PRAG-muh-tiz-um -- from Greek pragma, 'a deed, a thing done': a belief in doing what works.

What It Means

Pragmatism is a belief in practical action and in what actually works.

See the word in context

Pragmatism, not idealism, is what keeps a hospital running through a long night.

With ten minutes left and no money for printing, a student council shows its pragmatism by dropping the plan for glossy posters and writing the announcement across the cafeteria whiteboard instead.

Choosing what will actually work over what would be ideal is the pragmatism in that decision. Unlike laziness, pragmatism still aims squarely at the goal -- it just takes the road that exists.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    practicality: Practicality is a focus on what can actually be done with the time, tools, and money at hand. Pragmatism is the larger belief behind it: that what works matters more than what would be ideal. A practical fix solves today's problem; pragmatism is the habit of choosing such fixes.

    common sense: Common sense is the everyday good judgment nearly everyone shares -- wear a coat in the cold, save before you spend. Pragmatism is a chosen outlook that prizes whatever actually works, even when the working answer is unusual or unpopular. Common sense follows the obvious; pragmatism weighs results.

    efficiency: Efficiency means getting a job done with the least waste of time, money, or effort. Pragmatism is a belief about which plans to choose in the first place -- the ones that will actually work. A method can be efficient and still serve a hopeless plan; pragmatism picks the plan.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    idealism: holding out for the perfect solution

    fantasy: a plan or hope with no basis in reality

    Where it sits · how far one goes to get results

    realism → pragmatism → expedience

    realism vs. pragmatism: Realism is quieter than pragmatism -- realism is simply seeing the situation clearly, while pragmatism takes the next step and acts on what will work.

    expedience vs. pragmatism: Expedience is pragmatism with the brakes off: pragmatism chooses the practical path, while expedience chooses whatever produces results fastest, even if principles get left behind.
