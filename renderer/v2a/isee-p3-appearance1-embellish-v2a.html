What It Means

To embellish is to make something more attractive by adding decorative details; also, to make an account or story more interesting by adding extra, often invented details.

See the word in context

The tailor embellished the plain black jacket with hand-stitched silver thread along the collar.

The birthday planner embellished the plain cardboard sign with glitter, ribbon, and paper flowers until it barely looked handmade.

The sign didn't start out fancy at all -- glitter, ribbon, and paper flowers were added to dress it up, which is exactly what it means to embellish something.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ornament: To ornament something is to attach physical decoration to it — beads, carving, gold trim — and it is only ever done to objects. To embellish reaches further: you can embellish a plain room, but you can just as easily embellish a story, adding extra and even invented details.

    dramatize: To dramatize is to tell or stage something in a more vivid, exciting way than it really was — the change is in the delivery. To embellish an account is to change its contents, stitching in extra, often invented, details. A dramatized tale is heightened; an embellished one is enlarged.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    simplify: to make plainer or less complicated

    understate: to describe something as smaller or less important than it really is

    Where it sits · how much extra gets added

    touch up → embellish → overdo

    touch up vs. embellish: Touching up is lighter than embellishing: a touch-up makes one small, quiet improvement, while to embellish is to layer on decorative detail after decorative detail.

    overdo vs. embellish: To overdo something means adding so much that it becomes too much, taking embellishment a step too far.
