invaluable

in-VAL-yuh-bul — despite starting with 'in-,' invaluable does not mean 'not valuable'; it means too valuable to measure.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Invaluable is extremely useful; so valuable that its worth cannot be measured.

See the word in context

The retired firefighter's calm advice proved invaluable to the rookies during their first real emergency.

A small town's only doctor works around the clock for forty years, delivering babies, treating the sick, and refusing to ever ask for extra pay, even during the worst emergencies — care the town comes to see as invaluable.

Her care is invaluable because it is so useful and irreplaceable that no amount of money could ever repay it. Unlike 'expensive,' which just means costly, invaluable means priceless — beyond any price at all.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    priceless: Priceless means too valuable to have a price, and it usually describes treasures — a painting, an heirloom, a medal. Invaluable points at usefulness instead: advice, experience, or help so important that no payment could match it. A vase is priceless; a mentor's guidance is invaluable.

    indispensable: Indispensable means absolutely necessary — the work simply cannot happen without it. Invaluable is praise for enormous usefulness, but it stops short of claiming necessity: invaluable help makes everything better, while an indispensable person's absence stops the job cold.

    precious: Precious means dearly cherished, often for rarity or sentiment — a precious photograph matters because of what it means to you. Invaluable measures usefulness rather than affection: invaluable advice earns its place by what it lets you do, not by how much you treasure it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    worthless: having no real value or use

    expendable: able to be sacrificed or done without

    Where it sits · how great its worth is

    useful → invaluable → irreplaceable

    useful vs. invaluable: Useful is weaker than invaluable — something useful simply helps, while invaluable means it is so valuable that it truly cannot be measured or replaced.

    irreplaceable vs. invaluable: Irreplaceable can feel even more absolute than invaluable: invaluable stresses immense worth, while irreplaceable stresses that nothing else could ever take its place.
