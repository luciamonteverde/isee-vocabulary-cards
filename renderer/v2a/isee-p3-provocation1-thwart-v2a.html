What It Means

To thwart is to prevent someone from accomplishing something; to foil a plan or attempt.

See the word in context

A last-second interception thwarted the opposing team's final scoring drive.

Undercover officers had been tracking the smugglers for weeks and thwarted the shipment just as it reached the harbor.

The officers didn't just slow the smugglers down -- they stopped the entire plan from succeeding at the last possible moment. That complete foiling of a specific attempt is exactly what thwart means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foil: To foil is to stop a scheme right in the act -- the word crime stories reach for when a robbery fails at the last second. To thwart covers wider ground: any plan or attempt, dramatic or ordinary, counts as thwarted the moment someone or something prevents it from succeeding.

    spoil: To spoil is to ruin the success or enjoyment of something, often accidentally -- rain can spoil a picnic without anyone intending harm. To thwart is more pointed: an obstacle or opponent prevents a plan from being accomplished at all, not just from going as pleasantly as hoped.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enable: to make something possible or give someone the means to do it

    further: to help advance or promote the success of something

    Where it sits · how completely the plan is stopped

    delay → thwart → crush

    delay vs. thwart: To delay a plan only postpones it, while thwarting a plan stops it from happening at all.

    crush vs. thwart: To crush a plan destroys it so completely there's nothing left to attempt again, an even more total defeat than being merely thwarted.
