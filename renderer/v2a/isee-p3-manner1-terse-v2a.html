What It Means

Terse is brief and to the point, often to the point of seeming abrupt.

See the word in context

His terse email -- just 'Approved. -J.' -- left the whole team wondering if he was upset.

Asked how the interview went, she gave a terse 'Fine,' grabbed her coffee, and left the room before anyone could ask a follow-up question.

Terse isn't just short -- it often carries a hint of coldness or impatience, as if the speaker doesn't want to waste any more words than necessary.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    curt: Curt means rudely brief -- an answer chopped so short that it feels like a slap. Terse is more neutral: a terse reply is stripped to the fewest words and can seem abrupt, but it is not necessarily rude. Every curt answer is terse; a terse one is not always curt.

    clipped: Clipped describes the sound of speech -- words bitten off short and fast, the way people talk when annoyed or in a hurry. Terse is about the message rather than the voice: a terse note or email is silent on the page and still feels abrupt.

    blunt: Blunt is about content, not length: a blunt person states a hard or unwelcome truth plainly, even when it stings. Terse is about form -- saying whatever you have to say in the fewest possible words. A terse message may contain no hard truth at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    verbose: using more words than needed

    rambling: lengthy and disorganized

    Where it sits · how cold the shortness feels

    brief → terse → brusque

    brief vs. terse: Brief simply means short, without terse's added hint of coldness or impatience.

    brusque vs. terse: Brusque pushes terse's abruptness further, into speech that feels gruff and almost rudely unfriendly.

    Word Choice

    Terse describes speech or writing that is brief to the point of feeling abrupt or unfriendly. This unit's succinct also means brief, but succinct is a compliment for clear, efficient communication, while terse usually suggests coldness or impatience.
