abrasive

uh-BRAY-siv -- shares a root with 'erase,' meaning to scrape.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Abrasive is harsh and biting in manner, scraping at people again and again until it wears them down.

See the word in context

New hires often struggled with the head chef's abrasive manner during even the calmest dinner service.

A customer asks a simple question at the return counter, and the clerk snaps back with an abrasive, mocking comment that leaves the entire line uncomfortable.

That harsh, wearing-down way of speaking is what makes the comment abrasive. Unlike 'blunt,' which is just direct, abrasive actively grates on the people around it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    harsh: Harsh means unpleasantly severe -- a harsh winter, a harsh punishment, a harsh voice. It is a broad word for anything hard to endure. Abrasive is narrower and more personal: a manner of dealing with people that grates on them and wears them down over time.

    cutting: A cutting remark wounds in one sharp stroke, like a blade -- brief, targeted, and painful. Abrasive describes something slower and rougher: a whole manner of speaking to people that scrapes at them again and again until they feel worn down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gentle: kind and mild in manner

    gracious: courteous and pleasant in manner

    Where it sits · how much the manner irritates others

    brusque → abrasive → grating

    brusque vs. abrasive: Brusque is a bit milder than abrasive -- brusque often just means abrupt and short with people, while abrasive suggests that manner actively wears on and irritates others over time.

    grating vs. abrasive: Grating can feel more persistent than abrasive: abrasive usually describes someone's overall harsh personality, while grating can describe any small, repeated irritation, even something as simple as a habit or sound.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Abrasive most often describes a person's harsh personality or manner, as in 'an abrasive management style,' though it can also describe a literally rough, scraping surface.
