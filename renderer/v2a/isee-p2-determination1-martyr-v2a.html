Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Martyr is a person who is killed for refusing to renounce their beliefs.

See the word in context

The cathedral was built over the tomb of a martyr executed centuries ago for refusing to renounce her faith.

Rather than deny the beliefs he had preached for decades, the elderly bishop calmly accepts the death sentence handed down by the tribunal, dying a martyr.

A person who is put to death rather than abandon their beliefs is a martyr. The word marks the ultimate cost of conviction -- a determination to a cause so complete that the person gives their life for it.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    casualty: A casualty is someone killed or injured in a war, accident, or disaster -- the word is neutral and says nothing about choice. A martyr's death is the opposite of random: a martyr is executed precisely because they refuse to renounce their beliefs.

    scapegoat: A scapegoat is a person unfairly blamed and punished for other people's failures. A martyr suffers for something they actually hold -- their own beliefs -- and accepts death rather than deny them; a scapegoat is handed someone else's guilt and rarely carries it willingly.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    apostate: one who abandons or renounces their beliefs

    turncoat: one who switches sides or betrays a cause

    Where it sits · how willingly the death is chosen

    victim → martyr → sacrifice

    victim vs. martyr: Victim is far broader and more neutral than martyr -- a victim may be harmed by accident or for no reason at all, while a martyr dies specifically because they refuse to abandon their beliefs.

    sacrifice vs. martyr: Sacrifice emphasizes the willing surrender even more starkly than martyr does on its own: calling a martyr 'a sacrifice to the cause' stresses that their death was the deliberate price of loyalty, chosen rather than merely suffered.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Reserve martyr for its serious historical, religious, or political sense: a person killed for refusing to renounce their beliefs. You may hear it used loosely for someone who complains about their burdens -- avoid that informal sense in essays and on the ISEE.
