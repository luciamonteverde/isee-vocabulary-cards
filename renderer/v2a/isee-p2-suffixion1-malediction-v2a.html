What It Means

Malediction is a formal, almost ritual curse -- a solemn spoken wish for harm or ruin to befall someone.

See the word in context

The dying pirate captain's malediction against the crew who had betrayed him was said to haunt the ship for decades.

Ancient court records describe a nobleman's malediction against the rival family he blamed for his son's death, sworn before witnesses in the city square.

A malediction isn't just an insult -- it's a formal, almost ritual curse meant to bring lasting harm.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    curse: A curse is any spoken wish for harm, from a muttered word in traffic to a solemn oath. A malediction is a curse in full ceremony -- formal, deliberate, almost ritual, the kind sworn in stories and old records. Every malediction is a curse, but only the gravest curses earn the name.

    hex: A hex is a spell -- harm worked through magic, cast with charms or rituals of witchcraft. A malediction needs no magic at all: it is harm wished aloud in words. A witch casts a hex; a furious rival pronounces a malediction.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    blessing: an expression of good wishes

    benediction: a formal blessing, especially at the end of a ceremony

    Where it sits · how severe the condemnation is

    criticism → malediction → damnation

    criticism vs. malediction: Criticism is only a negative comment or judgment on what someone has done -- much milder than a malediction, which wishes future ruin on a person.

    damnation vs. malediction: Damnation is condemnation to eternal punishment -- even more extreme than a malediction, which wishes ruin in this life rather than in the next.
