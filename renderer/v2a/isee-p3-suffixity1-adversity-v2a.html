What It Means

Adversity means a sustained stretch of difficult or unfortunate circumstances that a person must struggle through, not a single bad moment.

See the word in context

The biography spends its first three chapters on the adversity the inventor endured before a single one of his ideas ever worked.

The memoir describes how the family's years of adversity during the drought -- lost crops, lost income, and a burned-down barn -- only strengthened their bond.

Adversity is ongoing difficulty or misfortune -- the hard circumstances a person has to struggle through, not a single bad moment.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hardship: Hardship is the suffering itself -- the hunger, cold, or poverty that makes life painful. Adversity is the larger run of unlucky, difficult circumstances a person must struggle through. A family may face months of adversity, and going without heat is one hardship inside it.

    misfortune: Misfortune is a single unlucky stroke like a lost wallet or a rained-out fair, with no one to blame. Adversity is heavier and longer: a sustained stretch of serious trouble that tests you. One misfortune stings; adversity wears you down for months.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    prosperity: the state of being successful and thriving

    fortune: good luck; favorable circumstances

    Where it sits · how severe the trouble is

    inconvenience → adversity → calamity

    inconvenience vs. adversity: An inconvenience is a minor annoyance or difficulty, far milder than the serious, ongoing struggle of adversity.

    calamity vs. adversity: A calamity is an even more extreme, sudden disaster than ordinary adversity, often affecting many people at once.
