preposterous

prih-POSS-ter-us -- four syllables, stress on the second syllable, POSS.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Preposterous is contrary to common sense.

See the word in context

The suggestion that a house cat had opened the refrigerator seemed preposterous until they watched the video.

A witness offers a preposterous account: the truck was traveling three hundred miles an hour down a gravel road -- through a school zone, in the rain, towing a trailer.

Preposterous means running so far against common sense that nobody can take it seriously. It is stronger than merely wrong -- it is wrong in a way that invites laughter.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ridiculous: Ridiculous means deserving to be laughed at, and it can attach to anything -- a hat, a price, an excuse. Preposterous is narrower and heavier: it is reserved for ideas that fall apart against common sense, so a preposterous claim does not just look silly -- it fails as thinking.

    nonsensical: Nonsensical means making no sense at all -- the words never add up to a meaning you could even test. A preposterous claim is different: it makes perfect sense as a sentence; it is the idea behind it that defies common sense.

    outlandish: Outlandish means so strange and unfamiliar that it seems bizarre -- an outlandish costume, an outlandish scheme. A preposterous idea is not merely strange: an outlandish plan might still work, while a preposterous one cannot survive a moment's thought.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reasonable: fair and sensible

    plausible: believable on its face

    Where it sits · how badly it fails reason

    implausible → preposterous → absurd

    implausible vs. preposterous: An implausible story is much milder than a preposterous one: implausible says only that something is hard to believe -- it could still turn out true -- while a preposterous claim collapses against common sense and draws laughter on the spot.

    absurd vs. preposterous: Absurd is the coldest and most complete of the three: a preposterous plan defies common sense, but an absurd one defies logic itself, like a square with three sides.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Preposterous is a strong and slightly formal word: a preposterous claim, a preposterous excuse, utterly preposterous. Save it for ideas that collapse under a moment's thought, not for positions you merely disagree with -- calling a rival's careful argument preposterous will usually cost you the room.
