capitulate

kuh-PICH-uh-late

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To capitulate is to stop resisting and give in to the other side's demands, only after a real struggle first.

See the word in context

The kingdom's generals urged the king not to capitulate, insisting the siege could still be broken.

After the union threatened a citywide strike, the transit authority capitulated within a week and agreed to nearly every one of the workers' original demands.

Giving up resistance and agreeing to demands after holding out for a while is exactly what capitulate describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    acquiesce: To acquiesce is to accept something quietly, without ever putting up a fight — you may not even disagree. To capitulate is to give in only after real resistance; the word makes no sense unless there was a struggle first.

    concede: To concede is to admit a single point or give up one disputed thing — a candidate concedes an election, a debater concedes an argument. To capitulate is bigger: the whole resistance ends, not just one contested piece of it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    resist: to withstand the force or effect of

    persevere: to continue steadily despite difficulty

    Where it sits · how totally resistance ends

    yield → capitulate → surrender

    yield vs. capitulate: Yield is weaker than capitulate: you might yield on one small point in a negotiation, while to capitulate is to abandon resistance altogether and accept the other side's terms.

    surrender vs. capitulate: Surrender is stronger than capitulate: to capitulate is to stop resisting, often on negotiated terms, while to surrender is to give up everything -- the fort, the weapons, the fight itself.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Capitulate implies giving in after some period of resistance -- it's rarely used for someone who agrees right away, since the word's whole flavor comes from the earlier struggle.
