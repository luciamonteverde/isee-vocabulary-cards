Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Auspicious means serving as, or marked by, a good omen at the very start that the whole venture ahead will go well.

See the word in context

A record crowd on opening night was an auspicious sign for the theater's new season.

The exchange student's very first morning is auspicious: her host family greets her in her own language, the sun breaks a week of rain, and her new school assigns the one novel she already loves.

Each detail reads like a sign that the whole year ahead will go well. Auspicious belongs to beginnings, omens, and starts that point toward success.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    promising: Promising says a person or effort shows early signs of future success: a promising student, a promising first draft. Auspicious moves the promise outside the thing itself -- it is for signs and omens at a beginning, hinting that fortune favors the venture. Talent is promising; a good omen is auspicious.

    encouraging: Encouraging describes news that lifts your confidence while the work is under way -- rising scores, a coach's nod. Auspicious is saved for beginnings: a sign at the very start that the whole venture will go well. Midstream good news is encouraging; a perfect opening day is auspicious.

    opportune: Opportune praises timing: an opportune moment or an opportune pause arrives exactly when it is most useful. Auspicious praises what a sign seems to promise about the future. An opportune break helps you right now; an auspicious start hints at how everything ahead will unfold.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ominous: suggesting that something bad is coming

    inauspicious: boding ill; unpromising

    Where it sits · how strongly success seems guaranteed

    favorable → auspicious → propitious

    favorable vs. auspicious: Favorable is milder and more matter-of-fact -- favorable conditions simply help, while an auspicious start feels like an omen, hinting that fortune itself has taken your side.

    propitious vs. auspicious: Propitious presses the promise even further than auspicious: an auspicious sign suggests things will go well, while a propitious moment practically guarantees it -- the stars aligned, the timing perfect, success all but assured.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Auspicious clings to beginnings and signs: an auspicious start, an auspicious debut, an auspicious omen, an auspicious date for a wedding. It is odd to call a sandwich or a chair auspicious -- the word needs a future to point at. Its built-in opposite, inauspicious, marks the start that bodes ill.
