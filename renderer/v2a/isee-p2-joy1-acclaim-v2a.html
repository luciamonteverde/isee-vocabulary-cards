Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Acclaim is enthusiastic and public praise arriving from many directions at once.

See the word in context

The mural won instant acclaim from everyone who passed the schoolyard wall.

The tiny theater's production was supposed to run for one weekend. Then came the acclaim: the city paper called it 'the year's must-see show,' two more reviews agreed, and by Thursday the ticket line wrapped around the block.

Acclaim is exactly that: loud, public, widespread praise. It is a mass noun -- you cannot count acclaims -- because it names the collective roar of approval itself, rising from critics and crowds together.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    applause: Applause is approval you can hear in the room -- hands clapping at the end of a performance, while the performers stand right there. Acclaim needs no shared room: it is enthusiastic public praise that arrives from many directions at once, from people who may never meet.

    approval: Approval is the mildest judgment that something is acceptable -- a nod, a passing grade, a thumbs-up. Acclaim goes far beyond approving: it is loud, enthusiastic, and public, praise pouring in from crowds and critics together.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    criticism: statements pointing out faults

    condemnation: strong public disapproval

    Where it sits · how public and lasting the admiration is

    praise → acclaim → renown

    praise vs. acclaim: Praise is the everyday, weaker word -- one kind note from a teacher is praise -- while acclaim is praise gone public and loud, arriving from many directions at once.

    renown vs. acclaim: Renown is what sustained acclaim eventually builds: acclaim is the applause this season; renown is the lasting fame that remains after the clapping stops.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Acclaim is a mass noun for the roar itself: win acclaim, earn critical acclaim, receive wide acclaim -- never 'an acclaim' or 'three acclaims.' Its partner accolade (also in this unit) is the countable trophy or honor the roar produces. Acclaim is what you hear; accolades are what you shelve.
