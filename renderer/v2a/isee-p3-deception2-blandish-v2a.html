What It Means

To blandish is to charm someone with a sustained stream of flattery or kind words as an intentional tactic to soften them up before a request or unwelcome news.

See the word in context

Diplomats sometimes blandish a difficult foreign minister before raising the harder topics.

The car salesman tried to blandish the couple with compliments about their taste before mentioning the price.

Complimenting a couple's taste right before the price talk is exactly blandishing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flatter: To flatter is to praise someone more than they deserve in hopes of pleasing them. Flattery is the raw material, and to blandish is to put it to work -- a sustained stream of charm and kind words meant to soften a person up before a request.

    sweet-talk: To sweet-talk someone is to win them round with warm, pleasant words, and the word sounds light and playful. Blandish names the same move in a formal, literary register, and it usually signals that a request is coming right behind the kindness.

    butter up: To butter someone up is casual slang for softening a person with heavy compliments before asking for something. The tactic is the one blandish names, but butter up belongs to the lunchroom while blandish belongs to novels and formal prose.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    browbeat: pressure someone with harsh words

    Where it sits · how calculated the flattery is

    compliment → blandish → manipulate

    compliment vs. blandish: A compliment is a single, often sincere kind remark that asks for nothing; blandish goes further along this scale, naming an intentional strategy of flattery aimed at getting something in return.

    manipulate vs. blandish: To manipulate someone sits past blandish at the far end of calculation: blandishing softens a person up with kind words, while manipulating is a cold, fully planned campaign to control what they think and do.
