What It Means

Biased means showing an unfair preference for or against something, whether from personal opinion or simply from how something was put together.

See the word in context

Critics called the poll biased because it only surveyed people from one neighborhood.

A judge in a singing competition happens to be the best friend of one contestant's mother. Throughout the show, her scoring is plainly biased: she gives that contestant noticeably higher scores than performances of equal quality.

She favors one contestant unfairly because of a personal connection rather than judging performances equally. Unlike 'strict,' which just means demanding high standards for everyone, biased specifically means treating people unequally.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prejudiced: Prejudiced means holding an unfair opinion formed in advance, usually a hostile one aimed at a group of people. Biased is wider and can be entirely accidental: a poll or a report can be biased simply because of how it was put together, with no hostility behind it.

    partial: Partial means favoring one side unfairly, and it is usually said of a person who was supposed to stay neutral — a referee, a judge, an umpire. Biased covers people and things alike: a survey, a headline, or a sample can be biased with no single partial person behind it.

    slanted: Slanted describes how something is told: the presentation is angled so one view looks better. Biased is the broader fault underneath — an unfair preference that can live in what gets asked, sampled, or shown, not only in the telling. A slanted story is one visible symptom of a biased approach.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    impartial: treating all sides fairly and equally

    objective: based on facts rather than personal feelings

    Where it sits · how far unfairness turns into action

    opinionated → biased → discriminatory

    opinionated vs. biased: Opinionated is weaker than biased — having strong opinions doesn't necessarily mean you treat people unfairly, while biased means that leaning actually distorts your judgment.

    discriminatory vs. biased: Discriminatory is stronger than biased: biased describes an unfair leaning in judgment, but discriminatory describes actual unfair treatment or exclusion that results from it.
