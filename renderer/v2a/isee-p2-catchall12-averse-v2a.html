averse

uh-VURSS -- Latin ab- ('away') plus vertere ('to turn'): literally turned away from something.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Averse is having a strong, settled dislike of or opposition to something.

See the word in context

My grandfather is so averse to technology that he still refuses to carry a cell phone.

Offered a chance to double her savings on a risky stock, an investor averse to risk shakes her head immediately and moves her money into a plain savings account instead.

That instinctive turning away from something disliked is what being averse to risk looks like. Unlike 'afraid,' which centers on fear, averse centers on settled dislike or opposition.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disinclined: Disinclined is the mildest of these words: you simply would rather not do something, and a small push or a change of mood can turn you around. Averse is far more settled -- a strong, lasting dislike that makes a person resist the thing altogether.

    loath: Loath describes unwillingness in the moment -- someone loath to leave a warm fire still may leave it. Averse points to a standing dislike of the thing itself: a person averse to crowds avoids them on principle, not just today.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    eager: full of enthusiasm and desire

    keen: eagerly interested in something

    Where it sits · how far dislike becomes open resistance

    reluctant → averse → opposed

    reluctant vs. averse: Reluctant is milder than averse -- a reluctant person hesitates but may still go along, while an averse person has a settled dislike that makes them resist altogether.

    opposed vs. averse: Opposed is stronger and more public than averse: averse describes an inner dislike or distaste, while opposed suggests actively taking a stand against something.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Averse is almost always followed by 'to,' as in 'averse to risk' or 'not averse to a little adventure.' Take care not to confuse it with adverse, which means unfavorable, as in 'adverse weather.'
