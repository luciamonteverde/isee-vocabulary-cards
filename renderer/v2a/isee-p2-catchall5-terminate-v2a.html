What It Means

To terminate is to formally bring something to a complete and final end by decision.

See the word in context

The airline had to terminate the flight early and land at the nearest airport due to a mechanical issue.

A gym member keeps missing payments for six months despite several warning emails. Eventually, the gym's manager terminates the membership entirely and revokes the member's access to the building.

The membership is ended officially, completely, and with no continuation. Unlike 'pause,' which is temporary, terminate means bringing something to a full and final end.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    end: To end is the plainest word for bringing something to a stop, and it says nothing about how or why the stop happens. To terminate is the formal, deliberate version: a contract, job, or process is terminated by decision, often cut off before its natural finish.

    conclude: To conclude is to bring something to its natural, planned close — a meeting concludes when the agenda is done. To terminate is to end something by decision, often early: a terminated contract stops before the finish it was written to reach.

    cancel: To cancel is to call off something planned — a flight, a show, an order — often before it ever starts. To terminate is to shut down something already in motion: you cancel Saturday's picnic, but you terminate an agreement that has run for years.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    begin: to start something

    continue: to keep something going without stopping

    Where it sits · how final and sweeping the ending is

    pause → terminate → abolish

    pause vs. terminate: Pause is weaker than terminate — pausing something stops it only temporarily, while terminate means ending it completely and for good.

    abolish vs. terminate: Abolish can be stronger than terminate: terminate usually ends a single agreement or process, but abolish wipes out an entire system, law, or practice altogether.
