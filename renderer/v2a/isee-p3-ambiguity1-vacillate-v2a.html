What It Means

To vacillate is to be indecisive, wavering repeatedly between different opinions or actions.

See the word in context

The board vacillated for months over the merger, approving it one week and shelving it the next.

The senator vacillated publicly on the bill, voting for an early draft, then against a later one, then sitting out the final vote entirely.

Vacillate describes repeated wavering on something significant, not a single quick change of mind.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    waver: To waver is to feel your resolve loosen in the moment -- your voice wavers, your confidence wavers, you waver briefly before saying yes. To vacillate is a larger pattern: swinging between choices again and again, over days or months, without ever settling.

    oscillate: Oscillate is the technical word for swinging back and forth in a steady rhythm -- pendulums, fans, and signals oscillate, and none of them is confused. To vacillate is human and unflattering: your position keeps swinging because you cannot make up your mind.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    commit: to firmly decide on and stick with a course of action

    resolve: to settle something firmly and with total confidence

    Where it sits · how often the position changes

    hesitate → vacillate → flip-flop

    hesitate vs. vacillate: To hesitate is far milder than to vacillate: anyone might hesitate for a moment before a hard question, while someone who vacillates keeps swinging between choices for days.

    flip-flop vs. vacillate: Flip-flop is harsher than vacillate: someone who vacillates wavers back and forth privately, while a politician who flip-flops reverses positions so publicly that no one trusts a word.

    Word Choice

    Vacillate works best for a real, repeated back-and-forth on something that matters -- a policy, a major decision -- not a single quick change of heart.
