exorbitant

ig-ZOR-bih-tunt -- Latin ex- ('out of') plus orbita ('wheel track'): a price that has run right out of its lane.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exorbitant is unreasonably high, usually said of a price.

See the word in context

The plumber charged an exorbitant fee for twenty minutes of work.

A hotel near the stadium normally rents rooms for ninety dollars a night; on championship weekend it posts an exorbitant rate of nine hundred and refuses to bargain.

A price pushed that far past anything reasonable is what makes the rate exorbitant. Unlike 'expensive,' which may still be fair for what you get, exorbitant says the number itself is out of bounds.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    excessive: Excessive means beyond what is reasonable or necessary, and it stretches over almost anything -- excessive noise, excessive homework, excessive packaging. Exorbitant is the money specialist: unreasonably high, and nearly always said of a price, fee, or rent. Anything exorbitant is excessive, but only costs get called exorbitant.

    inflated: Inflated means raised above the true value -- a shop that quietly adds a modest markup has inflated its prices, even if only slightly. Exorbitant is far stronger: not merely above value but unreasonably high, a number so far past fair that customers walk out.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reasonable: fair for what is offered

    affordable: cheap enough to pay for easily

    Where it sits · how far past a fair price

    steep → exorbitant → extortionate

    steep vs. exorbitant: Steep is the mild, everyday complaint that exorbitant makes formal -- a steep price makes you wince, while an exorbitant price is so far past fair that people call it outrageous.

    extortionate vs. exorbitant: Extortionate is exorbitant with an accusation attached: exorbitant says the price is unreasonably high, while extortionate suggests the seller is squeezing you on purpose because you have no choice.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Exorbitant nearly always modifies a cost word -- an exorbitant price, fee, rent, or salary. If the sentence is not about money or amounts, exorbitant is probably the wrong choice.
