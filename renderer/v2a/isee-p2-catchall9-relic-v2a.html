What It Means

A relic is an object that has survived from the distant past and is valued for its historical age and significance.

See the word in context

The museum's oldest relic was a bronze coin dated to the fourth century.

A farmer plowing his field strikes something hard with his tractor blade and unearths a relic: a rusted sword that has been buried underground since a battle fought eight hundred years ago.

That surviving object from a much earlier time is exactly what a relic is. Unlike 'replica,' which is a modern copy, a relic is the genuine, original surviving item.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    heirloom: An heirloom is a valuable object passed down through the generations of one family -- a grandmother's ring, a christening gown. A relic belongs to history rather than to a household: it survives from the distant past and is prized for its age and meaning, not for the family line it traveled through.

    antique: An antique is an old object collected and traded for its age and craftsmanship -- furniture, clocks, silver. A relic is prized simply for having survived from the past: a rusted blade or a broken coin can be a relic of great importance while being worth nothing in an antiques shop.

    memento: A memento is a small keepsake saved to remember a person or an event you were part of -- a ticket stub, a photograph, a pressed flower. A relic reaches far beyond personal memory: it is an object that has survived from a distant past none of us ever saw.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    replica: an exact copy of something, especially on a smaller scale

    reproduction: a copy of an original work or object

    Where it sits · how much history the leftover carries

    remnant → relic → artifact

    remnant vs. relic: Remnant is broader than relic -- a remnant can be any small leftover piece of something, while relic specifically emphasizes a surviving object valued for its historical age and significance.

    artifact vs. relic: Artifact can feel more precise than relic in some contexts: relic often suggests something treasured or almost sacred from the past, while artifact is the more neutral, scientific term archaeologists use for any human-made object.
