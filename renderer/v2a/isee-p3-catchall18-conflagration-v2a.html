conflagration

kon-fluh-GRAY-shun -- from Latin conflagrare ('to burn up'), from com- (intensive) + flagrare ('to blaze').

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Conflagration is an extensive, destructive fire that consumes a large area of land or property.

See the word in context

A cigarette carelessly discarded behind the print shop sparked a conflagration that gutted three city blocks before dawn.

Investigators later determined that a faulty generator, left running overnight in the storage room, had sparked the conflagration that destroyed all six warehouses.

That scale -- a small spark growing into a fire that swallows an entire block of buildings -- is exactly what conflagration describes, not just any ordinary house fire.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wildfire: A wildfire burns across wild land — forest, brush, grassland — and takes its name from where it burns. A conflagration is measured instead by what it destroys: any fire, in a city or in open country, that consumes a huge area is a conflagration.

    firestorm: A firestorm is a fire so huge and hot that it creates its own winds, sucking in air and hurling embers for miles. A conflagration does not need to make its own weather — any vast fire that devours a wide area of buildings or land qualifies as a conflagration.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ember: a small piece of glowing coal or wood left after a fire has died down

    drizzle: a light, gentle rain

    Where it sits · how devastating the fire is

    blaze → conflagration → inferno

    blaze vs. conflagration: A blaze is milder than a conflagration: a blaze is a fierce, hungry fire that may still be confined to a single kitchen or building, while a conflagration devours whole blocks of a city at a time.

    inferno vs. conflagration: Inferno is more extreme than conflagration: a conflagration devours whole blocks of buildings, but an inferno — like the stairwell the trapped firefighters described — is fire at its fiercest, an inescapable furnace.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Conflagration is a formal, almost literary word -- reserve it for fires large enough to threaten whole buildings, blocks, or landscapes, not a candle or a campfire.
