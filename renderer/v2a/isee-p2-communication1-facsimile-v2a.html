facsimile

fak-SIM-uh-lee -- four syllables, stress on the second syllable, SIM; the ending is 'lee,' not 'mile.'

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Facsimile is an exact copy, especially of a document or printed material, carefully made to match the original in every detail.

See the word in context

The historical society sells a facsimile of the town's 1850 newspaper, complete with its original advertisements.

A film studio needs a famous painting for a heist movie, but the real one never leaves its gallery. So a specialist spends four months producing a facsimile -- matching every brushstroke, every hairline crack in the varnish -- until even the gallery's own curator has to lean in close to tell the two apart.

A facsimile is an exact copy, faithful to the original in every detail. A facsimile is honest about what it is -- unlike a forgery, it never pretends to be the original; its whole job is to match the original so the real thing can stay safe.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    reproduction: A reproduction is a copy of an artwork or image, usually printed in large numbers so the picture can hang anywhere. A facsimile makes a stricter promise: one careful copy of a particular document, exact enough to stand in for the original itself.

    photocopy: A photocopy is an instant machine copy -- one button, a few seconds, plain office paper. A facsimile of an old document is a slower, more painstaking production, matching the original's paper tone, its ink, and every one of its flaws.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    original: the real, first thing itself

    genuine article: the authentic item, not a copy

    Where it sits · how painstaking the copying is

    duplicate → facsimile → replica

    duplicate vs. facsimile: A duplicate makes a smaller claim than a facsimile: it says only that a second, identical copy exists -- a duplicate key, a duplicate file -- with no promise of the careful craftsmanship a facsimile is built with.

    replica vs. facsimile: A replica pushes the copying even further than a facsimile: a facsimile matches the surface of a page or painting, while a replica rebuilds the entire object -- its shape, weight, and materials -- so it convinces from every angle.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Facsimile lives among documents and artifacts: a facsimile of a manuscript, a map, a signature, a first edition. It promises exactness -- 'a reasonable facsimile' is the joking way to admit a copy falls short. It is also the word hiding inside 'fax': a faxed page is literally a facsimile. A facsimile openly declares itself a copy; the moment a copy is passed off as the original, the right word becomes forgery.
