accentuate

ak-SEN-choo-ayt

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To accentuate a feature is to make it stand out more sharply than before — usually by setting it against a contrast or adding to it — so that the feature itself looks or feels stronger, while staying true to its real size.

See the word in context

A thin line of dark eyeliner accentuated the green of her eyes.

The architect painted the hallway walls deep charcoal to accentuate the one white staircase, which now glows like a ribbon of light against the dark.

The staircase was always white; nothing about it changed. What the architect changed was everything around it, and the contrast makes the staircase stand out far more sharply than before. That is accentuate: a real feature made more noticeable — here by contrast — without being stretched into something it is not, and without anyone having to point at it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    emphasize: To emphasize is to give a point extra weight so that others know it matters — a teacher emphasizes a rule, a report emphasizes a risk — and it lives in speaking, writing, and argument. Accentuate belongs to features and qualities: it makes a color, a shape, a sound, or a difference show more sharply, and no one has to say anything at all. Ask whether someone is pressing a point on an audience, or a feature is actually being made to stand out.

    intensify: To intensify is to make something stronger or more extreme — heat, pain, a storm, a search — with no question of how it looks to anyone. Accentuate is about standing out: the feature becomes easier to notice, often because of what sits next to it, whether or not it grows stronger in itself. Ask whether the sentence is about how strong something is, or how clearly it shows.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    downplay: to treat a feature as smaller or less important than it is

    mask: to hide a feature so it cannot be noticed

    soften: to make an edge or feature less sharp and less noticeable

    Where it sits · how far a real feature is pushed forward

    highlight → accentuate → exaggerate

    highlight vs. accentuate: Highlight is the milder move — it points at a part so people notice it, the way a marker picks out one line on a page, and the part itself is left exactly as it was. Accentuate goes further: the feature itself is made to show more sharply, by contrast, by addition, or by being set off. Ask whether the thing was only pointed at, or actually made to stand out more than before.

    exaggerate vs. accentuate: Exaggerate is the stronger word — it stretches a feature past the truth, making it seem bigger, louder, or worse than it really is. Accentuate stays honest: the feature is made more noticeable but keeps its real size. Ask whether what you now see is still true to the thing itself.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Accentuate is a neutral, slightly formal word, at home in writing about clothes, design, makeup, photographs, and music — a belt accentuates a waist, side lighting accentuates a texture, a drum accentuates the beat. It can also describe a change that makes a difference or a problem sharper: a drought accentuates the gap between rich farms and poor ones. Look inside it for 'accent': to accentuate something is to put an accent on it.
