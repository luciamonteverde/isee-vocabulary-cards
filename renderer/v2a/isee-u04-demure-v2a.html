demure

dih-MYOOR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Demure describes a gentle, well-mannered way of carrying yourself that never shows off — a soft voice, hands folded, eyes lowered, nothing bold — and that looks calm, pleasant, and at home in the room rather than scared, uncomfortable, or closed off.

See the word in context

In the old photograph my great-grandmother looks demure, hands resting in her lap and eyes turned gently toward the floor.

At the awards banquet the champion was so demure — thanking her coaches in a soft voice and slipping straight back to her seat — that no one would have guessed she had just broken a national record.

The champion is not scared and not hiding; she has just won. What makes her demure is the manner: soft voice, no fuss, no reaching for the spotlight, pleasant and composed the whole time. Demure describes how a person carries themselves in company, not a lack of nerve — a demure person can be the strongest one in the room.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    timid: Timid means held back by fear and a lack of confidence — a timid person wants to speak or step forward and cannot make themselves do it. Demure is a manner, not a fear: the demure person is calm and at home, simply choosing soft and modest over bold. (On a test, timid is the closest single match for demure.) Ask whether the person is holding back because they are scared, or just behaving gently.

    shy: Shy means uncomfortable around people — blushing, looking away, wishing you were somewhere else. Demure is comfortable: the quietness is pleasant and composed, with no wish to escape. Ask whether the quietness looks like discomfort or like good manners.

    reserved: Reserved says only that a person keeps their thoughts and feelings to themselves, and a reserved person can be cold, proud, or stiff. Demure adds warmth and gentleness — a soft, pleasant, well-mannered presence. Ask whether the quiet is a wall or a courtesy.

    modest: Modest is about not bragging — a modest person plays down their own achievements, and can do it at the top of their voice, cheerfully, in the middle of a crowd. Demure covers the whole manner: voice, posture, and behavior all kept soft and small. Ask whether it is only about how they talk about themselves, or about how they carry themselves altogether.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bold: confident, forward, and glad to stand out

    flashy: dressed or acting to grab every eye in the room

    Where it sits · how much of yourself you hold back in company

    quiet → demure → meek

    quiet vs. demure: Quiet is the milder, plainer word — it only tells you someone is not talking much, and says nothing about their manners or why they are silent. Demure adds gentle, modest, well-mannered behavior to the quiet. Ask whether you know anything beyond the volume.

    meek vs. demure: Meek is the stronger word — a meek person gives way to everyone, accepting what others decide and never pushing back. Demure keeps its own mind: the demure person is soft-spoken and modest but can still hold firm opinions and win the argument politely. Ask whether the person actually gives in.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    slightly old-fashioned

    Demure has traditionally been used about girls and young women in older stories — the demure heroine with lowered eyes — so it can sound a little old-fashioned, though today it can describe anyone with that gentle, modest manner. It also describes clothing that covers up modestly: a demure dress. On the ISEE, expect it as a near match for timid or shy; the difference this card teaches is that demure is about behavior, not about being afraid.
