Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Conscientious is wishing to do what is right; careful, thorough, and principled.

See the word in context

The lab's most conscientious technician double-checked every measurement before signing off on the results.

A conscientious student who could easily copy a classmate's homework instead stays up late finishing every problem alone, determined to actually understand the material.

That inner drive to do things properly, even when no one is checking, is what makes the student conscientious. Unlike 'obedient,' which follows rules because told to, conscientious comes from a genuine wish to do right.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    meticulous: Meticulous is about precision in the details -- measured twice, labeled, double-checked. It describes how carefully work is done. Conscientious runs deeper: it names why -- a genuine wish to do right, so the care continues even when nobody is watching or checking.

    thorough: Thorough means complete -- every step finished, every corner covered, nothing skipped. But thoroughness only measures the work itself. Conscientious adds the moral engine behind the work: a person who takes care because doing things properly and rightly matters to them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    careless: not giving enough attention to avoid mistakes

    negligent: failing to take proper care

    Where it sits · how exacting the inner care becomes

    dutiful → conscientious → scrupulous

    dutiful vs. conscientious: Dutiful is a bit more about following expectations than conscientious -- dutiful can describe doing what's required out of obligation, while conscientious implies a genuine inner wish to do things well and rightly.

    scrupulous vs. conscientious: Scrupulous can feel even more exacting than conscientious: conscientious describes a general care to do what's right, while scrupulous emphasizes an almost obsessive attention to every single detail.
