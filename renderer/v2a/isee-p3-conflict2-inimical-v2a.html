inimical

ih-NIM-ih-kul

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Inimical means harmful in relation to something else, describing an effect that works against the thing it touches rather than a standalone quality or an angry attitude.

See the word in context

The new zoning law was seen as inimical to the neighborhood's small, family-owned shops.

Doctors warned that skipping meals entirely is inimical to long-term metabolic health.

Skipping meals doesn't just fail to help -- it actively works against the body's health, which is exactly inimical.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hostile: Hostile means actively unfriendly or aggressive -- a hostile crowd, a hostile question -- and it usually radiates from someone's attitude. Inimical is cooler and more formal: it names a harmful relationship, almost always with 'to', and nothing has to feel anger at all -- a law or a climate can be inimical to something.

    antagonistic: Antagonistic means showing active opposition -- rivals, debaters, and opposing sides are antagonistic because they push against each other on purpose. Inimical needs no purpose and no opponent: it describes anything whose effect works against something else, the way constant noise is inimical to concentration.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    favorable: helpful and encouraging toward something

    Where it sits · how much active harm it does

    unhelpful → inimical → destructive

    unhelpful vs. inimical: Something unhelpful simply fails to assist; something inimical actively works against the thing it touches.

    destructive vs. inimical: Inimical describes an ongoing harmful relationship between two things; destructive implies the harm has already caused serious damage.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Inimical almost always pairs with "to" (inimical to health, inimical to progress), describing a harmful relationship rather than a standalone quality, distinct from this unit's "baneful," which can describe a harmful thing on its own.
