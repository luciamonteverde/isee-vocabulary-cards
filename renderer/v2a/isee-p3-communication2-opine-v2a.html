What It Means

To opine is to hold and state as one's opinion.

See the word in context

Without waiting to be asked, the food critic opined that the new bistro's much-hyped soup was badly under-seasoned.

Without anyone asking, the passenger in seat 14B decided to opine loudly on exactly how the airline should be run.

No one requested his opinion, yet he stated it anyway and at volume -- that unprompted quality is typical of opine, though the word itself is neutral about tone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    remark: To remark is to drop a comment in passing — casual, low-stakes, quickly moved past. To opine is weightier: you are stating a judgment you actually hold, even if nobody asked to hear it. A remark brushes a topic; opining takes a position.

    assert: To assert is to state something confidently as fact — true, provable, end of discussion. To opine stays in the territory of opinion: the speaker offers a personal verdict, not settled proof. Asserting claims certainty; opining claims only a view.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    withhold: to hold back or refuse to give

    Where it sits · how forcefully the view is delivered

    mention → opine → pontificate

    mention vs. opine: To mention something is a light, passing comment; to opine is to actually state a judgment or opinion about it.

    pontificate vs. opine: To pontificate goes much further than to opine: both state a view, but pontificating is pompous and long-winded, delivered as if the speaker's opinion were beyond question.
