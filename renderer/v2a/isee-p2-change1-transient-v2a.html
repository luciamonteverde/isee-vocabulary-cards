Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Transient means lasting only a short time, passing through by its very nature rather than settling.

See the word in context

The desert bloom is transient, carpeting the dunes for a single week each spring before vanishing.

When silver is struck in the canyon, a transient town appears in months: hotels, laundries, two newspapers, a theater. Nobody plants trees. Nobody builds with stone. And when the vein runs out six years later, the whole population moves on, leaving the buildings to the wind.

Transient means lasting only a short time -- never meant to be permanent. The word is about impermanence: people, places, and states that pass through rather than settle. Nobody plants trees in a transient town, because nobody expects to see them grow.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impermanent: Impermanent states the plain fact that something will not last, and stops there. Transient goes further and paints the passing itself: people, pain, and fame that move through and are gone. Everything transient is impermanent, but impermanent carries none of transient's picture of passing through.

    provisional: Provisional means set up for now to hold things together until a permanent version arrives -- a provisional government, a provisional schedule. Transient things are not waiting to be replaced by anything; they simply pass through and end, like a desert bloom. Provisional looks ahead to something lasting; transient never does.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permanent: lasting indefinitely

    enduring: continuing steadily through time

    Where it sits · how quickly and naturally it ends

    temporary → transient → short-lived

    temporary vs. transient: Temporary usually comes with an end date someone planned -- a temporary fix, a temporary badge -- while transient describes what passes through by its very nature, on nobody's schedule.

    short-lived vs. transient: Short-lived is the strongest about brevity itself: a transient population cycles through over a season, but a short-lived truce collapses almost as soon as it is announced.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Transient stresses impermanence, and it loves people and states that pass through: transient workers, a transient population, transient pain, transient fame. Keep it apart from its unit-mate fleeting, which is about the sheer speed of a single moment. A related word-family note: you met the adjective transitory earlier in this course -- it is transient's close cousin, built on the same Latin 'go across.' Both mean not lasting; transient is the one that also describes people passing through a place.
