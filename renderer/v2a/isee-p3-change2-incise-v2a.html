What It Means

To incise is to cut a fine, precise line or design into a surface.

See the word in context

The engraver carefully incised the family crest into the silver ring.

Ancient scribes would incise their records into wet clay tablets, letting them harden into a permanent copy.

Incise describes a precise, controlled kind of cutting -- carving a mark, letter, or design into a surface, usually with a fine tool.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    carve: To carve is to cut shapes or designs into a material, often removing whole pieces as you go — carving a pumpkin, a statue, or a roast. To incise is narrower and neater: you incise by drawing one fine, clean line into a surface.

    engrave: To engrave is to cut letters or pictures into a hard surface such as metal or stone, almost always to decorate or label it. Incise is the more general precision word: a potter or a bookbinder can incise a clean line with no decoration in mind.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    smooth: make a surface even and unmarked

    erase: rub out a mark completely

    Where it sits · how deep the cutting goes

    scratch → incise → gouge

    scratch vs. incise: A scratch is far milder than an incision: it is a light, often accidental mark on the surface, while to incise is to cut a fine, purposeful line down into the material.

    gouge vs. incise: To gouge something is to tear out a deep, rough chunk of it, far more violent than a controlled incision.
