insolvent

in-SOL-vunt -- stress on the second syllable, SOL.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Insolvent means unable to pay the debts one owes, because they are beyond anything one will ever be able to pay back.

See the word in context

By December the toy company was insolvent, owing far more to its suppliers than its shops could ever bring in.

A family restaurant is insolvent: forty thousand dollars in unpaid bills, eight hundred dollars in the bank, and no way to pay this month's food delivery.

What it owes is simply beyond what it can pay. Now look at how the word is built -- solvent, which you already learned earlier in this course, means 'able to pay one's debts.' Put this unit's prefix in front of it and in-solvent flips straight into the opposite. This is the cleanest example of the whole unit: a real taught word, a real prefix, an exact reversal.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ruined: Ruined is the dramatic aftermath word: the money is gone, the business is wreckage, the fall is finished. Insolvent is a precise financial condition -- debts larger than any ability to pay -- and a company can be quietly insolvent for months before anyone would call it ruined.

    penniless: Penniless looks at what you have on hand: no cash, empty pockets, nothing to spend. Insolvent weighs debts against means -- a firm can hold cash today and still be insolvent, because what it owes is beyond anything it will ever be able to pay.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    solvent: able to pay one's debts -- the exact word this unit's prefix reverses, and one you already learned earlier in this course

    prosperous: financially successful and thriving

    Where it sits · how serious and official the debt is

    broke → insolvent → bankrupt

    broke vs. insolvent: Broke is the casual, temporary version -- a student is broke until payday -- while an insolvent business owes more than it can ever pay back.

    bankrupt vs. insolvent: Bankrupt is insolvency made official: insolvency is the condition of owing more than one can ever pay back, while bankruptcy is the legal declaration a court makes about it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Keep this pair together in your memory: solvent = able to pay, insolvent = unable to pay. They are the same word with this unit's prefix flipping the meaning, and the ISEE loves exactly that kind of pair. Insolvent belongs to businesses, estates, and banks more often than to people, and its noun is insolvency.
