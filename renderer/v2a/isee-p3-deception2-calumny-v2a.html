What It Means

Calumny is a false statement made to damage someone's reputation.

The biography spends a full chapter disproving decades-old calumny about the composer's private life.

The senator's career never recovered from the calumny his rival spread just weeks before the election.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slander: Slander is a false spoken statement that damages a reputation -- it is the everyday and courtroom word, and it applies only to speech. Calumny is the older, more literary term and is broader: a calumny can be spoken or written, and the word stresses the malice behind the lie.

    defamation: Defamation is the legal name for the wrong itself -- the act of damaging someone's reputation with false claims, the thing a person can be sued for. A calumny is the false tale; defamation is the offense that spreading it commits.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tribute: a statement of praise or respect

    Where it sits · how deliberate and damaging the falsehood

    gossip → calumny → libel

    gossip vs. calumny: Gossip is casual talk that may or may not be true and is often harmless; calumny sits far above it on this scale, being specifically false and intentionally damaging.

    libel vs. calumny: Libel pushes past calumny in deliberateness and reach: committing the falsehood to writing makes it last, travel farther, and carry legal consequences, while a calumny may be nothing more than spoken words.

    Word Choice
