What It Means

Nymph is a graceful nature spirit of myth and legend, bound to one particular place, such as a spring, a wood, or a mountain.

See the word in context

Local legend held that a nymph lived in the waterfall and blessed anyone who left flowers at its base.

A Renaissance painting shows a nymph: a graceful, otherworldly woman standing barefoot among the reeds of a quiet forest pool, clearly not entirely human.

In myth and legend, a nymph is exactly that kind of minor nature spirit, often tied to a particular forest, river, or mountain.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fairy: A fairy is a small winged magical being of storybooks and folklore, living in its own fairy realm. A nymph is different: in Greek myth she is a graceful, human-looking nature spirit bound to one real place -- a spring, a wood, a mountain.

    dryad: A dryad is one particular kind of nymph -- the tree nymph of Greek myth, whose life is tied to her forest. Nymph is the wider family name, covering the spirits of springs, mountains, and meadows as well as the woods.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mortal: an ordinary human being, as opposed to a god or spirit

    deity: a god or goddess, often more powerful than a nymph

    Where it sits · how narrowly the spirit type is defined

    sprite → nymph → naiad

    sprite vs. nymph: Sprite is a broader, more playful word -- almost any small magical being, mischievous or not, can be a sprite -- while nymph specifically names a graceful nature spirit tied to a particular place.

    naiad vs. nymph: Naiad is more specific than nymph -- it names only a water nymph, one particular kind of nymph among many (there are also nymphs of the mountains, trees, and meadows).

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Nymph most often appears in myth and literature for a nature spirit, but the same word is also a scientific term for an immature stage of certain insects, like dragonflies -- an unrelated but interesting double life for the same spelling.
