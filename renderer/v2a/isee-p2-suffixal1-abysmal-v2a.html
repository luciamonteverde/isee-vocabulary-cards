abysmal

uh-BIZ-mul -- from abysm, an old-fashioned spelling of abyss ('a deep, seemingly bottomless pit') + -al.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To be abysmal is to be extremely, shockingly bad, as if the quality had no floor.

See the word in context

The team's abysmal defense let in nine goals in a single game, the worst performance in the league's history.

Reviews called the film's special effects abysmal, noting that the supposedly terrifying monster looked more like a pile of green garbage bags.

Abysmal doesn't just mean "bad" -- it means bad on a shockingly deep, bottomless scale, as if the quality has no floor.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dreadful: Dreadful means extremely bad or unpleasant -- a strong, everyday complaint about weather, traffic, or a ruined picnic. Abysmal digs deeper: it says the quality is shockingly, bottomlessly bad, as though there were no floor beneath it. Most dreadful things never sink all the way to abysmal.

    dismal: Dismal means depressingly poor in quality -- dreary, gray, and disheartening, like a losing season played out in half-empty stands. Abysmal is far worse than dismal: not just gloomy but staggeringly bad, the kind of failure people talk about for years.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    superb: excellent; outstanding

    excellent: extremely good

    Where it sits · how severe the badness is

    poor → abysmal → catastrophic

    poor vs. abysmal: A poor effort is only a notch below average -- a mild, everyday disappointment nowhere near abysmal, which says the quality is shockingly bad with no floor at all.

    catastrophic vs. abysmal: Catastrophic is reserved for outright disasters, pushing even further than abysmal's bottomless low point.
