defamatory

dih-FAM-uh-tor-ee

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Defamatory is (of a statement) false and damaging to someone's reputation.

See the word in context

The talent agent threatened legal action after a blog post made defamatory claims that her client had faked his own charity work.

The school board launched an investigation after defamatory flyers wrongly claimed a beloved teacher had been cheating on standardized tests.

The flyers weren't just unkind -- they spread false claims that damaged a real person's reputation, exactly what defamatory describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    libelous: Libelous means containing a false statement in writing or print that damages someone's reputation. Defamatory is the umbrella term covering both written and spoken attacks: every libelous article is defamatory, but a defamatory remark shouted at a press conference is not libel -- nothing was printed.

    slanderous: Slanderous means containing a spoken false statement that damages someone's reputation -- the courtroom word for harmful lies said out loud. Defamatory covers the whole family, spoken or written; if the false claim is in print, defamatory still applies where slanderous no longer does.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    complimentary: expressing praise or admiration

    flattering: praising someone in a favorable, appealing way

    Where it sits · how unfair and hurtful the statement is

    critical → defamatory → vicious

    critical vs. defamatory: A critical statement is simply negative or fault-finding, which can still be true -- milder and more neutral than defamatory.

    vicious vs. defamatory: A vicious statement is cruel and deliberately harmful in tone, even more extreme in intent than defamatory.
