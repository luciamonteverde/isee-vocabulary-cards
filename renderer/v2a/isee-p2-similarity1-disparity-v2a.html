disparity

dis-PAIR-uh-tee

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A disparity is a large difference in amount, size or level between two people, groups or things that are being compared — one side has clearly more, or clearly less, than the other, and the difference usually feels unfair.

See the word in context

There was a striking disparity between the two teams' budgets: one traveled by chartered bus and slept in hotels, while the other shared rides and camped.

When the town counted its trees, it found a disparity between the north side and the south: the wealthier north had shade on nearly every street, while whole blocks in the south had none.

Two sides are set against each other — north and south — and something is measured on each: trees per street. One side has far more than the other, and the difference feels unfair, since shade is something every neighborhood needs. That is a disparity. Notice what it is not: both counts are correct, so there is no error to explain; it is one specific measured difference, not a general condition of the whole town; and it compares two separate sides rather than describing one thing whose parts are out of proportion.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    discrepancy: Discrepancy (a word from an earlier unit) is a gap between two records or facts that ought to match but fail to agree — the receipt total and the cash in the drawer. It can be tiny, and it points at an error waiting to be explained. A disparity is not an error at all: both amounts are correct, and one is simply much larger than the other. Ask whether the two figures were supposed to be the same.

    inequality: Inequality is the general condition of a society or a system in which people are not treated or provided for equally — a state of affairs that can last for generations. A disparity is one specific, measurable difference between two named sides: the shade on this side of town beside that side. Ask whether the sentence names two sides and the quantity that separates them, or describes an overall condition.

    imbalance: An imbalance is too much of one part and too little of another inside a single system — a diet, a budget, the chemicals in a body — so that the whole tips out of proportion. A disparity compares two separate sides against each other. Ask whether the sentence is about the parts of one whole that no longer balance, or about two sides, one of which has far more than the other.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    equality: the two sides having the same amount, so nothing separates them

    parity: an exact match in amount, each side with as much as the other

    Where it sits · how wide the difference between two sides is and how hard it would be to close

    gap → disparity → gulf

    gap vs. disparity: Gap is the everyday word for any opening or difference between two things, large or small — a gap in a fence, a gap in your knowledge, a two-point gap in the score. A disparity is a gap of one particular kind: large, in amount or level, between two sides being compared, and usually unfair-looking. Every disparity is a gap, so ask the other way round: does the sentence stress how large and how uneven the difference in amounts is?

    gulf vs. disparity: Gulf (a word from an earlier unit) goes past disparity: a disparity is a measurable difference in how much each side has, while a gulf is a separation so wide that the two sides can hardly reach or understand each other — and it need not be about amounts at all. Ask whether the sentence puts a number or a quantity on the difference, or stresses that the two sides can barely reach across it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Disparity is a formal word, common in news reports and studies — a disparity in wages, in health care, in funding — and it is almost always followed by 'in' or 'between'. It carries a quiet judgment: a disparity is not just a difference but an uneven one that someone ought to notice and perhaps fix. The adjective is disparate, which this unit also teaches, but its meaning has drifted: disparate things are simply very unlike one another, while a disparity is a difference in how much each side has.
