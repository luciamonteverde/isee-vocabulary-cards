Hear It

What It Means

To muster is to gather or pull together with purpose and effort, especially strength, support, or courage for a challenge.

See the word in context

The captain ordered the entire crew to muster on deck before sunrise for inspection.

Standing in the wings with shaking hands, the understudy takes three slow breaths, musters every scrap of confidence she has, and walks out onto the stage.

That deliberate gathering-up of courage before a daunting moment is exactly what it means to muster. You can muster troops, muster support, or muster your nerve -- in every case, you are pulling scattered resources together for a purpose.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assemble: To assemble is to bring people or parts together in an organized way -- a crew assembles, and furniture gets assembled. To muster adds effort and purpose: you muster troops for a battle or courage for a hard moment, scraping together what you need from what is scattered.

    rally: To rally is to bring people together and lift their spirits for a shared effort -- a leader rallies weary volunteers back into motion. To muster is quieter and more inward: it means collecting what is already there, whether soldiers, support, or your own nerve.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scatter: to send or fly off in all directions

    disband: to break up an organized group

    Where it sits · how much effort the collecting takes

    gather → muster → summon

    gather vs. muster: Gather is looser and more everyday than muster -- you can gather leaves or gather friends casually, while to muster implies purpose and effort, pulling things together for a challenge or a task.

    summon vs. muster: Summon can carry even more effort and formality than muster: you muster courage by collecting what you have, while summoning courage suggests calling it up from somewhere deep, almost commanding it to appear.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Muster shines in the pattern 'muster the courage to...' or 'muster the strength to...' -- gathering inner resources for a determined effort. It also keeps its old military sense: soldiers muster for inspection, and something that 'passes muster' is good enough to survive one.
