dispel

dih-SPEL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dispel means to make something that hangs over people — a fog, a gloomy mood, a fear, a doubt, or a rumor — scatter and disappear completely, usually by letting in facts, light, or good news.

See the word in context

A quick call from the airline dispelled the family's worry that their bags had been lost.

Half the class was sure the old science wing was haunted, so Mr. Reyes dispelled the idea in one afternoon by opening the vent where the moaning sound came from.

The haunting was never a thing anyone could hold — it was a belief hanging over the whole class. Mr. Reyes did not argue it away or order everyone to stop talking about it; he let in the facts, and the belief broke up and vanished on its own. That is dispel: the fear is gone, not just quieter, and it went because the truth came in.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disprove: To disprove something is to show, with evidence, that a claim is false — and the word stops there; it says nothing about whether people stop believing it. Dispel is about the belief or feeling itself going away from people's minds. Ask whether the sentence is about a statement being shown wrong or about a worry or rumor clearing out of the air.

    banish: To banish is to force something out by order or by an act of will — a prince from a kingdom, a thought from your head — and what is banished still exists, just somewhere else. What is dispelled fades to nothing, usually because facts or light came in. Ask whether the thing was pushed out or made to disappear.

    dismiss: To dismiss a worry or an idea is to wave it away as not worth your attention — a choice one person makes, and the worry may still be very real to everyone else. To dispel it is to make it actually go away, for everyone, usually with proof. Ask whether the doubt was truly gone afterward or just brushed aside.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    instill: to plant a feeling or idea in someone's mind

    confirm: to show that a belief or fear was right after all

    Where it sits · how much of the worry, doubt, or gloom is left afterward

    lessen → dispel

    lessen vs. dispel: Lessen is the milder word — a fear that is lessened is smaller but still there, and it can grow back. A fear that is dispelled is gone. Ask whether any of the worry remains once the sentence is over.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Use dispel for things that float: fog, gloom, doubts, fears, myths, rumors, suspicion. You cannot dispel a person, a pile of trash, or a broken chair. The word also carries a promise about the result — what is dispelled does not drift back a minute later; it is cleared away. It sounds a little formal, which is why it turns up in news reports and speeches.
