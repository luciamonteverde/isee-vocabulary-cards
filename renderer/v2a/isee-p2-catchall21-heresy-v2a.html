heresy

HAIR-uh-see

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Heresy is a belief or opinion, held by someone inside a religion — or inside any group that expects its members to accept a set of teachings — that directly contradicts what that group's authorities teach as true; the offense lies in the belief itself, not in any rude word or disrespectful act.

See the word in context

In a house where everyone had cheered for the Cubs since Great-Grandpa's day, Nadia's calm announcement that she liked the Cardinals better was treated as heresy.

In the 1500s, a printer who published pamphlets arguing that ordinary people did not need a priest to forgive their sins was put on trial for heresy.

The printer never damaged anything holy and never insulted anyone; the pamphlets were written in a serious, respectful tone. What put him on trial was the content of the belief: it contradicted what the church of his day taught as true, and he belonged to that church. That is the whole test for heresy — the offense is in what is believed, not in how it is said or in any deed. Had he stolen from the church instead, the charge would have been a different word.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sacrilege: Sacrilege is an act that treats something sacred with disrespect — stealing from a temple, using a holy book to hold a door open, joking through a funeral. The person may believe every teaching perfectly and still commit sacrilege. Heresy is not a deed at all; it is a belief that contradicts what the faith teaches. Sacrilege is the closest word the ISEE offers for heresy, so remember the one difference: ask whether the offense is something the person did or something the person believes.

    blasphemy: Blasphemy is disrespectful speech about God or sacred things — cursing by a holy name, mocking a prayer. It lives in the words and the tone. Heresy lives in the content of a belief, and it can be stated in the most polite and careful sentence imaginable. Ask whether the offense is insulting words about the sacred, or a belief that contradicts the teaching no matter how respectfully it is put.

    sin: A sin is an act or a thought that breaks a religion's rules of right and wrong — lying, stealing, cruelty, greed — and a sinner may accept every teaching of the faith while breaking its rules. Heresy is not bad behavior; it is denying something the faith teaches as true. Ask whether the person broke a rule of conduct or denied a teaching.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    orthodoxy: the set of beliefs a group's authorities accept as correct — the very teaching a heresy contradicts.

    conformity: going along with what everyone in the group is expected to believe and do.

    Where it sits · how deeply a view breaks with what a group's authorities teach

    disagreement → dissent → heresy

    disagreement vs. heresy: Disagreement is the mild end — any difference of opinion about anything, from which movie to watch to how to run a meeting, and nobody's place in the group is at stake. Heresy contradicts a teaching the group holds as true, so seriously that holding it can put a person outside the group. Ask whether the two sides simply see something differently, or whether one side has denied a core teaching.

    dissent vs. heresy: Dissent is the middle — openly refusing to go along with an official decision or position, like voting no, writing the minority opinion, or speaking against a policy, while remaining an accepted member. Heresy goes further: it denies what the group teaches as true, not merely what it has decided to do. Ask whether the person is saying no to a decision or denying a teaching.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Heresy is a formal, serious word from religion and law, and the person who holds a heresy is a heretic — another word in this course, whose card defines a heretic as a member of a faith condemned by its authorities because a belief strikes at its doctrine. You will also meet heresy stretched, often half-jokingly, to any group with strong shared beliefs: in a family of Cubs fans, a science club, a locker room, a kitchen with one sacred recipe, an unpopular opinion 'counts as heresy.' On the ISEE synonym section the closest match offered is sacrilege — but keep the difference in mind: sacrilege is a disrespectful act against something sacred, while heresy is a belief that goes against what is taught.
