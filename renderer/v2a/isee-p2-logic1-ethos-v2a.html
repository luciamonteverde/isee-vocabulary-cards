What It Means

Ethos is an appeal to one's own morals and character, asking the audience to trust you because of who you are.

See the word in context

The company's advertising leans entirely on ethos, promising that it has never sold a product it would not give its own children.

Two speakers argue for the same recycling program. The first cites tonnage figures. The second relies on ethos: 'I have picked up litter on this beach every Saturday for eleven years, and I would not stand here asking you for something I was not already doing myself.'

Ethos is an appeal to one's own morals and character -- asking the audience to trust you because of who you are and how you live. It does not prove the claim; it makes the person making the claim worth believing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    trustworthiness: Trustworthiness is the quality of deserving to be relied on -- a trait a person simply has. Ethos is not the trait but the appeal: a speaker uses ethos when she points to her own trustworthiness to persuade an audience to believe her.

    reputation: A reputation is the opinion other people already hold about you, good or bad, earned or not. Ethos is something a speaker actively builds inside an argument, showing her character so the audience will trust what she says next.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    deceit: lying or tricky behavior that betrays trust

    dishonor: loss of respect and moral standing

    Where it sits · from inner quality to earned trust

    character → ethos → credibility

    character vs. ethos: Character is what a person actually is; ethos is that character put to work in an argument -- you have character all the time, but you use ethos when you ask an audience to trust you because of it.

    credibility vs. ethos: Credibility is what ethos earns you: a speaker builds ethos by showing her honesty and experience, and the credibility she gains is the reason the audience believes her next sentence.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Ethos is one of the three classical rhetorical appeals, alongside "logos" (appeals to logic and reason -- taught earlier in this course) and "pathos" (appeals to emotion). Ethos, "logos," and "pathos" are related but distinct -- they are not synonyms of one another, and a test question will expect you to keep them apart. Ethos is the character appeal: 'trust me, because of who I am.' It also has a second everyday use -- 'the ethos of the school' means the spirit and values of a group.
