What It Means

Prosaic means dull, ordinary, and matter-of-fact, flat like everyday prose next to soaring poetry and missing any spark of beauty or imagination.

See the word in context

Compared to the fantasy novel's soaring battle scenes, the sequel's prosaic account of tax paperwork felt like a letdown.

The travel brochure promised adventure, but her travel diary turned out to be a prosaic string of delayed buses and forgettable meals.

Prosaic describes something plain and unremarkable -- the opposite of vivid or inspired -- like everyday prose next to soaring poetry.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mundane: Mundane (a word you met earlier in this course) points at the ordinary, everyday side of life — errands, laundry, paperwork. Prosaic judges the telling rather than the subject: a prosaic sentence can make even a shipwreck sound flat.

    humdrum: Humdrum is dullness that comes from sameness — the same route, the same tasks, week after week. Prosaic can strike in a single flat sentence, but humdrum needs a routine that repeats.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    poetic: having the beauty or expressiveness of poetry

    vivid: producing powerful, striking images

    Where it sits · how dull and unoriginal it is

    plain → prosaic → banal

    plain vs. prosaic: Plain describes something simple, not especially exciting or beautiful, milder than prosaic.

    banal vs. prosaic: Banal (a word you met earlier in this course) is so commonplace and boring it offers nothing new to anyone, even more extreme than prosaic.
