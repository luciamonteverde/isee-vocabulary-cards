What It Means

Idiosyncrasy is a peculiar habit or way of thinking distinctive to a particular person.

See the word in context

One of her idiosyncrasies was refusing to start any project on a Tuesday.

The famous chef's idiosyncrasies included tasting every dish with his eyes closed, no matter how many times he'd made it before.

This habit wasn't shared by other chefs or explained by any rule -- it was simply his own personal quirk, which is exactly an idiosyncrasy.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    quirk: A quirk is a peculiar personal habit noticed with a smile -- light, informal, and small. An idiosyncrasy is the same kind of one-person oddity in slightly more formal dress, and it can also be a whole way of thinking, not just a behavior.

    peculiarity: A peculiarity is any unusual or distinctive feature, and it can belong to anything -- a house, a dialect, a contract. An idiosyncrasy is narrower: a peculiarity of one particular person, usually showing up in their habits or thinking.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    convention: a widely accepted, ordinary way of doing something

    Where it sits · how tightly it grips the person

    habit → idiosyncrasy → obsession

    habit vs. idiosyncrasy: Any regular behavior, ordinary or not, is a habit; an idiosyncrasy is specifically a habit that is unusual and unique to one person.

    obsession vs. idiosyncrasy: An idiosyncrasy is a small, harmless personal quirk; an obsession is an all-consuming fixation that takes over someone's thinking.

    Idiosyncrasy always describes something specific to one particular person (or a small group), not a shared cultural tradition -- that distinction is what separates it from ordinary habits.
