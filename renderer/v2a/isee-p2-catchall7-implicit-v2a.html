implicit

im-PLIS-it — contains 'plic,' meaning to fold; something implicit is folded inside a statement, not unfolded and said aloud.

What It Means

Implicit means understood without being directly or plainly expressed, built into the whole situation rather than suggested in any single remark.

See the word in context

There was an implicit understanding among the roommates that whoever cooked dinner wouldn't have to do the dishes.

A veteran employee never officially trains new hires, yet every new hire on the team quietly ends up asking her for help first -- an implicit arrangement that everyone, including the manager, simply expects without it ever being written into anyone's job description.

That unstated but clearly understood expectation is what implicit means. Unlike 'explicit,' which is stated outright, implicit is built into the whole situation, something everyone senses without being told.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    implied: Implied describes a meaning a speaker deliberately suggests in one particular remark without saying it outright. Implicit is broader: an implicit rule or understanding lives in the whole situation, not in any single remark, and no one may ever have hinted at it at all.

    unwritten: Unwritten means never put down in writing -- an unwritten rule can still be talked about openly all the time. Implicit goes further: an implicit rule is not only unwritten but unexpressed, understood without anyone stating it aloud. Every implicit rule is unwritten, but not the reverse.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    explicit: stated clearly and in detail

    stated: expressed directly in words

    Where it sits · how deliberately the meaning is hinted at

    unspoken → implicit → tacit

    unspoken vs. implicit: Unspoken is a more casual, everyday word than implicit -- unspoken simply means not said aloud, while implicit more formally emphasizes that the meaning is folded into the situation itself.

    tacit vs. implicit: Tacit can feel even more deliberate than implicit: implicit often happens naturally, but tacit sometimes suggests a quiet, almost intentional agreement to not say something directly.
