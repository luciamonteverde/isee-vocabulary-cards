semblance

SEM-blunss -- Old French sembler, 'to seem,' from Latin similis, 'like': the same family as 'resemble.'

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Semblance is the outward form or appearance of something, suggesting that the look may be all there is, without the full reality behind it.

See the word in context

After the storm passed, the town slowly returned to a semblance of normal life.

Minutes before guests arrive, a family shoves laundry into closets and stacks dishes out of sight, giving the house a semblance of calm and order even though chaos hides behind every door.

That outward appearance of order without the full reality behind it is all a semblance is. Unlike 'reality,' a semblance may be only surface-deep.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    facade: A facade is a false front kept up on purpose, the way a building's grand stone face can hide plain brick behind it. A semblance is thinner and less deliberate -- the mere outward look of calm or order, however it came about.

    veneer: A veneer is a thin, attractive layer laid over something coarser -- polished manners over rudeness, fine wood glued over cheap board. A semblance is not a covering at all: just the outward look of a thing, with or without depth behind it.

    pretense: A pretense is a false show deliberately kept up so that others will believe something. A semblance needs no deceiver at all -- the outward look can simply be there, with nobody trying to fool anyone about what lies underneath.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reality: what actually exists

    substance: the real matter beneath appearances

    Where it sits · how deceptive the outward show is

    appearance → semblance → guise

    appearance vs. semblance: Appearance is the plain, neutral cousin of semblance -- everything has an appearance, while a semblance hints that the outward look may be all there is.

    guise vs. semblance: Guise is more deceptive than semblance: a semblance is a thin outward show of something, while a guise is a disguise-like false front deliberately put on.
