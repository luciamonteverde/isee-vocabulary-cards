bellicose

BEL-ih-kohs

What It Means

Bellicose means aggressive and openly eager for conflict or war, shown in present speech, threats, and posturing.

See the word in context

The union leader's bellicose rhetoric at the rally left little doubt that a strike was coming.

Though the two nations had been at peace for decades, the new prime minister's bellicose campaign speeches -- full of talk of 'reclaiming lost territory' -- alarmed diplomats across the region.

Bellicose describes an eagerness for conflict or war -- the prime minister isn't just tough-talking, he's actively signaling a readiness to fight.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    warlike: Warlike describes a standing character or tradition -- a warlike nation builds its identity around battle-readiness. Bellicose describes behavior you can hear right now: speeches, threats, posturing eager for a fight. A country can have a warlike past and nothing bellicose in today's speeches.

    hawkish: Hawkish describes a policy leaning -- favoring military strength and a hard line in disputes. A hawkish official may never want actual fighting; deterrence is often the point. Bellicose goes further: talk and posture openly eager for the fight itself.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    peaceable: inclined toward peace

    dovish: favoring a peaceful, cautious policy

    Where it sits · how ready to start a fight

    assertive → bellicose → warmongering

    assertive vs. bellicose: Assertive is far milder than bellicose -- an assertive speaker is only confident and direct about what she wants, with no appetite for a fight. Bellicose is openly spoiling for one.

    warmongering vs. bellicose: Warmongering describes actively pushing for war -- an even more extreme, active form of bellicose posturing.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Bellicose usually describes rhetoric, tone, or policy -- a leader's speech, a nation's posture -- rather than a single person's everyday mood. It's more eager for outright conflict than quarrelsome (petty bickering) or irascible (a short personal temper).
