Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Innocuous is not harmful or offensive.

See the word in context

The snake's bright bands look dangerous, but this particular species is completely innocuous.

A student worries all week about a rumor that the new principal is 'making changes.' The changes turn out to be entirely innocuous: a recycling bin in every classroom and a lunch period that starts fifteen minutes later.

They harm nobody and offend nobody. Innocuous is the word for the thing that could have been dangerous and simply is not -- a harmless bug, a harmless comment, a harmless new rule.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    harmless: Harmless makes one narrow promise about a thing: no damage will come from it. Innocuous stretches wider -- not harmful and not offensive either -- and it usually tells a small story: the thing looked like trouble and turned out to be nothing.

    unthreatening: Unthreatening is about how something comes across -- nothing in its look or manner makes you brace for trouble. Innocuous is about what a thing actually does: a creature can look terrifying and still be innocuous, because innocuous measures the harm itself, not the impression.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmful: causing damage or injury

    noxious: harmful, poisonous, or very unpleasant

    Where it sits · how far from hurting toward helping

    inoffensive → innocuous → benign

    inoffensive vs. innocuous: Inoffensive covers feelings only -- an inoffensive joke bothers nobody -- while innocuous covers real danger too: an innocuous snake will not hurt you at all.

    benign vs. innocuous: Benign adds a warmth that innocuous does not have: an innocuous remark simply does no harm, while a benign one is actively kind -- and in medicine, a benign growth is the reassuring, non-dangerous kind.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Do not let innocuous slide into innocent. They descend from the same Latin verb nocere ('to harm'), but modern English has split them cleanly: innocent means not guilty, innocuous means not harmful. An innocent suspect did not commit the crime; an innocuous spider will not bite you. Innocuous is also the standard word for something that seemed threatening and turned out to be nothing: 'an innocuous question,' 'an innocuous lump.'
