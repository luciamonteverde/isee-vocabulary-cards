cantankerous

kan-TANG-ker-us

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Cantankerous is habitually bad-tempered, argumentative, and difficult to work or get along with.

See the word in context

The cantankerous committee chair objected to every proposal at the meeting, no matter how small or reasonable.

Nobody wanted to sit near the cantankerous passenger who complained about the temperature, the snacks, and the flight attendant's tone, all within the first ten minutes.

Cantankerous describes an ongoing pattern of grumbling and picking fights, not just one bad mood -- it's a personality that's hard to satisfy.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    quarrelsome: Quarrelsome means prone to starting arguments and picking fights over small, unimportant things, at any age and in any mood. Cantankerous folds that argumentativeness into a lasting, grumbling bad temper -- and, unlike quarrelsome, it usually comes with a fond smile, the way you'd describe a beloved grumpy neighbor.

    grouchy: Grouchy is the everyday word for a grumbling, complaining mood -- sighing and muttering, but rarely arguing back. A cantankerous person goes further than grumbling: they actively contest, dispute, and object, making them genuinely difficult to work with.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    genial: warmly friendly and pleasant

    agreeable: pleasant and willing to cooperate

    Where it sits · how lasting and aggressive the crankiness

    irritable → cantankerous → hostile

    irritable vs. cantankerous: Irritable is milder and more temporary than cantankerous -- an irritable person may just be having a rough day, while a cantankerous one has made grumbling and arguing a lifelong habit.

    hostile vs. cantankerous: Hostile goes further than cantankerous, suggesting open unfriendliness or even aggression, not just grumbling and arguing.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Cantankerous almost always paints a fond, slightly humorous picture -- think of the classic grumpy-but-familiar neighbor or relative -- rather than describing someone genuinely dangerous or cruel.
