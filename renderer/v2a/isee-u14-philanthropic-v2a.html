philanthropic

fil-un-THRAH-pik

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Philanthropic describes a person, gift, or organization that gives away money or other resources — in a planned way and on a large scale — in order to make life better for other people or for society as a whole.

See the word in context

A retired dentist's philanthropic foundation has paid for a new library in every elementary school in the county.

The Okafor family's philanthropic trust has funded college scholarships for the children of farm workers for sixty years, and it plans to keep funding them for sixty more.

Money, given away on purpose, on a large scale, over a long time, to make other people's lives better — every part of philanthropic is here. The trust does not merely wish the students well or help one family through a hard month; it hands over real resources through an organized program built to last. That is the giving philanthropic describes.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    altruistic: Altruistic describes the motive: acting for someone else's good at real cost to yourself, with nothing to gain in return — the stranger who dives into a river, the volunteer who gives up her weekends. It says nothing about money or size. Philanthropic describes the activity — organized giving of money or resources at scale — and a philanthropic donor may enjoy the applause, the plaque, and the tax break. Ask whether the sentence is about what the giver sacrifices and gains, or about money given away on a large scale.

    benevolent: Benevolent describes a kindly attitude — a settled wish for people to do well, the way a benevolent ruler treats subjects gently — and a benevolent person may never give away anything at all. Philanthropic requires actual giving: money or resources handed over on a large scale. Ask whether anything was actually given, or only well-wished.

    humanitarian: Humanitarian describes work aimed at relieving human suffering — food after a flood, doctors in a war zone — and it can be done with time and skill rather than money. It always points at people in distress. Philanthropic gives money or resources, and the cause need not involve suffering at all: a philanthropic gift can build a concert hall or fund a telescope. Ask whether the sentence is about relieving suffering, or about money given to improve life in general.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stingy: unwilling to give or spend even a little

    self-serving: arranging everything, even gifts, for your own benefit

    Where it sits · how big and how organized the giving is

    generous → charitable → philanthropic

    generous vs. philanthropic: Generous is the widest and lowest rung — a generous person gives freely, of money, time, praise, or the last slice of pizza, in any amount and with no plan. Every philanthropic act is also generous, so generous is rarely wrong; philanthropic is the narrow, large-scale, organized case. Ask whether the giving is money or resources, on a scale far beyond one person's needs, through some planned program — if not, generous is the word.

    charitable vs. philanthropic: Charitable is the middle rung — charitable giving helps people in need, and any size counts: a can of soup for the food drive is charitable. Philanthropic goes bigger and more organized — foundations, funded programs, gifts that build things — and it does not have to aim at need: a philanthropic donor may pay for an orchestra. Ask whether the giving is one act of help for the needy, or an organized, large-scale program to improve life.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Philanthropic is the adjective that goes with philanthropy, a word taught in a later course; both come from Greek words meaning 'love of humankind.' In practice the word belongs to money and to scale: philanthropic foundations, philanthropic donors, a company's philanthropic arm. A person can be deeply kind, give away nothing, and never be called philanthropic; a billionaire can be cold in person and still be a philanthropic force. Elsewhere in this course you will meet altruistic, which describes why someone gives; philanthropic describes what is given and how much.
