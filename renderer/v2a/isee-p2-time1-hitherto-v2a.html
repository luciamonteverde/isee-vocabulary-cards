hitherto

hith-er-TOO -- from Middle English hider ('to this place') + to.

Section 1

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Hitherto means up until now, or up until a given moment in the past, and it always sets up a contrast with something that changes right after.

See the word in context

The lab's hitherto unpublished data finally appeared in a journal this spring.

The hitherto quiet neighborhood suddenly found itself in the news after the abandoned lot was chosen as the site for a new stadium.

Notice that hitherto marks a clear before-and-after: quiet up until now, and then suddenly not. It always signals that whatever comes next is a change from how things had been up to this exact moment.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    heretofore: Heretofore means exactly what hitherto means -- up until now -- but it lives in contracts, deeds, and courtroom filings. Hitherto is formal too, yet it still turns up in ordinary history writing and journalism; heretofore rarely escapes the fine print.

    to date: To date means up to the present day, and it is anchored to today -- reports and record-keeping use it for running totals. Hitherto can also look back from a moment in the past, right up to the instant everything changed.

    so far: So far is the casual, spoken way to say up to now -- 'no complaints so far' -- and, like to date, it counts from the present moment. Hitherto is its bookish cousin and can mark 'up until then' inside a story about the past.

    to this day: To this day insists a situation still holds right now -- the recipe is secret to this day. Hitherto points at the same stretch of time but says it is over: the situation held up until now, or until then, and is just about to change.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    henceforth: from this point onward, into the future

    hereafter: from now on, in the time still to come

    Where it sits · how much of the past it covers

    previously → hitherto

    previously vs. hitherto: Previously is weaker than hitherto: it points loosely to some earlier time, while hitherto stretches the claim over the entire span of time right up to the present moment -- or up to the very moment a story turns.
