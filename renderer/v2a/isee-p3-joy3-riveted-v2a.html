riveted

RIV-uh-tid

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Held so still by something you are watching, hearing, or reading that your eyes stay locked on it and your attention could not be pulled away if someone tried — pinned in place the way a metal rivet holds two plates together for good. A riveted person is wide awake and following every detail, not drifting; the hold is on this one thing, in this one moment; and what holds you can be tense or calm — a chase scene, a chess match, a slow speech.

See the word in context

Riveted by the tightrope walker inching across the canyon, the tourists let the sandwiches go warm in their laps.

The barbershop went quiet, every customer riveted to the little TV as the rescue crew lowered a rope toward the stranded hiker.

Eyes locked on one thing, a whole room gone still, and nobody able to look away while the rope inches down — the customers are held fast in this one moment, alert to every detail. That is riveted. Nothing here is charming them into staying, so it is not captivated; nobody is dreamy or dazed, so it is not spellbound; and nobody is busy with a task of their own, so it is not absorbed.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    absorbed: lost in something you are doing — sketching, coding, sorting coins — so deeply that the world fades and a tap on the shoulder makes you jump. Riveted is being held still by something in front of you. Ask whether the person's hands are busy with a task of their own; if so, the word is absorbed.

    fascinated: strongly drawn to a subject, and it can last for years — fascinated by volcanoes since third grade. Riveted is one moment, one thing, eyes locked. Ask whether the sentence describes a standing interest or a single scene where the person cannot look away; a lasting interest is fascinated.

    spellbound: held as if by magic, dreamy and dazed — the person drifts with the music and loses track of the room. Riveted is alert, following every detail. Ask whether the person is sharp and tracking what happens or floating in a daze; the daze is spellbound.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bored: attention has left entirely

    distracted: attention keeps sliding off to other things

    Where it sits · how firmly attention is held

    interested → captivated → riveted → transfixed

    interested vs. riveted: interested is mild — you follow along, you might ask a question, you could leave at any time. Riveted means you could not look away if you tried. Ask whether the person could turn away without effort; if so, it is interested.

    captivated vs. riveted: captivated is charmed — something lovely makes you want to stay, the way a good melody does. Riveted pins you in place, and what pins you need not be lovely at all: a dry safety report or a slow rescue will do. Ask whether the hold comes from charm and pleasure or from being unable to look away; charm is captivated.

    transfixed vs. riveted: transfixed goes further — the whole body freezes as if pinned through, and it is often shock or awe that does it: a deer in headlights, a crowd at a lightning strike. Riveted holds the eyes and the attention while the person stays able to move. Ask whether the person has frozen completely, perhaps in fear; if so, it is transfixed.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Riveted almost always comes with by or to — riveted by a speech, riveted to the screen — and it describes the person whose attention is held. Its partner riveting, taught earlier in this course, describes the thing that does the holding: a riveted crowd watches a riveting match. The picture behind both is mechanical — a rivet is a metal pin hammered flat so two plates can never come apart — so the word is far stronger than interested, and it is about stillness: a riveted audience goes quiet. It is not the word for a dreamy trance or for a lifelong hobby.
