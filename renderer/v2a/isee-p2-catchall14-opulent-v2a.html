opulent

OP-yoo-lunt -- from Latin opulentus, 'wealthy,' from ops, 'wealth, resources, power': an opulent place is overflowing with resources.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Opulent is overly rich, putting wealth on open display.

See the word in context

The sultan's palace was so opulent that even the doorknobs were carved from solid gold.

The community center's holiday party, usually just folding chairs and a fruit punch bowl, was suddenly opulent, featuring a chocolate fountain, a live orchestra, and ice sculptures of every guest's initials.

Opulent describes a level of luxury that goes well beyond what's normal or necessary -- a modest community party wouldn't normally have a chocolate fountain and ice sculptures, which is exactly what makes this year's version feel opulent.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sumptuous: Sumptuous describes rich, costly pleasure in the things you taste and touch -- a sumptuous banquet, sumptuous velvet. Opulent is bigger and more visual: it describes whole rooms, homes, or lifestyles that put wealth on open display, not just one fine meal or fabric.

    grand: Grand means impressive through sheer size, scale, or dignity -- a grand staircase, a grand old courthouse. Opulent adds wealth to the picture: gold, marble, and expensive display everywhere you look. A thing can be grand on scale alone, but nothing is opulent unless the money shows.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    plain: simple, without decoration

    austere: severely simple and stripped of comfort

    Where it sits · how far wealth spills into excess

    luxurious → opulent → lavish

    luxurious vs. opulent: Luxurious describes rich comfort in general, while opulent adds a sense of visible, almost showy display of that wealth.

    lavish vs. opulent: Lavish pushes even further than opulent, suggesting an almost excessive, overflowing generosity of spending, not just a rich appearance.
