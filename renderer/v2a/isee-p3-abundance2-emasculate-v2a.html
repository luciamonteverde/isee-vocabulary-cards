What It Means

To emasculate is to strip a law, plan, or institution of its real power or force, leaving it standing in form only.

See the word in context

Critics argued the last-minute amendments would emasculate the bill's original purpose entirely.

Years of budget cuts had gradually emasculated the agency's ability to enforce its own regulations.

The agency still technically existed, but it had lost the real power to act -- which is exactly what emasculate describes here.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    weaken: To weaken something is to lower its strength a step — the rule, the signal, or the argument still works, just less well than before. To emasculate goes much further: it strips a law, agency, or plan of its real power, leaving the shell standing with no force inside.

    cripple: To cripple something is to damage it so badly that it can barely function, though a struggling remainder survives. Emasculate names the outcome rather than the blow: an emasculated law still exists on paper but has lost the power that made it matter.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    empower: give someone or something greater strength or authority

    Where it sits · how much of its strength is removed

    soften → emasculate → gut

    soften vs. emasculate: Softening a rule makes it slightly less strict; emasculating it removes its real force almost entirely.

    gut vs. emasculate: To gut something goes even further than to emasculate it: an emasculated rule keeps its shape while losing its force, but a gutted one has its entire substance torn out, so nothing that mattered remains at all.
