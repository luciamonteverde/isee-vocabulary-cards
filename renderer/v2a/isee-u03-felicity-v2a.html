felicity

fuh-LIS-uh-tee

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Deep, lasting happiness — the state of a life, a marriage, or a stretch of years going well. It is a condition rather than a moment or a burst: the happiness of how things are, not of one thing that has just happened.

See the word in context

In the novel's last pages the once-stormy couple reaches a felicity that holds: a farm, four children, and thirty years of shared breakfasts.

The old king's final years were a time of felicity for the whole kingdom — full crops, quiet borders, and markets busy from dawn to dusk.

Nothing here is a single happy event; the sentence describes how things stood for years — the condition of a whole kingdom going well. That settled, deep happiness of how things are is felicity. If the sentence had described one perfect afternoon when nothing could be better, the word would be bliss; if the king's people had merely had no complaints, it would be contentment.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bliss: complete, perfect happiness in a moment, as if nothing in the world could be better right then — an afternoon on a warm beach. Felicity is the happiness of how a life or a long stretch of time is going. Ask whether the sentence is about one perfect moment (bliss) or about how things stand over months and years (felicity).

    joy: strong happiness that rises in you in response to something — a reunion, good news, music — and can burst out as a laugh or a shout. Felicity is not a response to one thing; it is the settled happiness of a situation. Ask whether something just happened to set the feeling off; if it did, the word is joy.

    delight: lively pleasure taken in one particular thing — a clever gift, a good meal, a fireworks show. Felicity belongs to a whole life or time, not to an object. Ask whether the sentence points to one thing the person is enjoying; if so, it is delight.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    misery: deep, lasting unhappiness

    sorrow: sadness over something lost

    Where it sits · how strong a happy state is

    contentment → felicity → ecstasy

    contentment vs. felicity: contentment is quiet satisfaction — 'this is fine,' nothing missing and nothing much wanted. Felicity is fuller: real, deep happiness, not just the absence of complaint. Ask whether the person is merely satisfied or truly happy about how life is going; satisfaction alone is contentment.

    ecstasy vs. felicity: ecstasy is happiness at explosive strength — leaping, shouting, swept off your feet — and it cannot last. Felicity is deep but steady, and it lasts. Ask whether the feeling is a peak that will pass in minutes; if so, the word is ecstasy.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Felicity is a formal, slightly old-fashioned word you will meet in novels, weddings, and speeches more than in conversation — domestic felicity, wished them every felicity. Use it for the happiness of a state of affairs, especially a life or a marriage, not for a happy jolt over one piece of news. Be aware that in writing about words, felicity can also mean a well-chosen, pleasing turn of phrase; this card teaches only the happiness sense, which is the one the entrance exam tests.
