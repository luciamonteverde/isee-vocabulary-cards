obstacles

OB-stuh-kulz

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Obstacles are the things that stand in your way and hold you where you are until you work out how to get over, around, or through them — a fallen tree across a trail, a rule that blocks a plan, a fear that keeps you from trying. The word names the thing in the path itself, not the effort of getting past it and not the bad spot it leaves you in.

See the word in context

The relay course was full of obstacles — a row of tires to step through, a wall to climb, and a net to crawl under.

The two biggest obstacles to opening the skate park were a city rule about noise and a shortage of money, and the students decided to tackle the rule first.

Both things stood between the students and the park they wanted: they could not simply go ahead until each one had been dealt with, by changing the rule or finding the money. That is what makes them obstacles. Notice that the word is used for the things in the way, not for the students' hard work — that would be the effort spent getting past them.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hurdles: A hurdle is a low check you clear in one quick step and leave behind — usually one of a row of them, like the forms, permits, and interviews a plan has to get through. An obstacle holds you where you are until you have worked out a way past it. Ask whether the thing is cleared and gone in a moment, or holds you up until you find a way past.

    barriers: A barrier exists to keep two sides apart or to keep something out — a fence around a field, a wall between two rooms, a language that two people do not share — and it faces both ways. An obstacle is seen from the traveler's side: it simply happens to sit in the path of someone trying to get somewhere. Ask whether the thing was put there, or exists, to separate or keep out, or whether it just stands in the way of someone's journey.

    setbacks: A setback is an event that pushes your progress backward — an injury, a failed test, a lost order — and it happens to you at a moment in time. An obstacle is a thing that sits in the path ahead, waiting to be got past. Ask whether the sentence is about something that happened and pushed progress back, or about something that stands in the way.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    openings: clear ways through that let you pass.

    aids: things that help you along instead of standing in the way.

    Where it sits · how completely the thing stops movement

    hindrances → obstacles → obstructions

    hindrances vs. obstacles: A hindrance makes your progress slower — a heavy pack, a slow computer, a friend who stops to chat every few steps — but you keep moving the whole time. An obstacle stops you where you are until you find a way over, around, or through. Ask whether the person is still moving, only more slowly, or is held up until a way past is found.

    obstructions vs. obstacles: An obstruction closes a passage off completely — a pipe packed solid, a wreck across all three lanes, a doorway sealed with concrete — and nothing gets through until it is cleared away. An obstacle can be got past: climbed over, walked around, argued through. Ask whether the way is shut entirely until the thing is removed, or whether there is some way past it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Obstacles is a plain, neutral word, used just as easily for a physical thing across a road as for an invisible one — a rule, a cost, a fear, a missing skill. It nearly always comes with a sense of a path or a goal: obstacles to a plan, obstacles in the way, an obstacle course. This unit also teaches two nearby words, and the three divide the work between them: obstacles are the things in the way, a plight is the bad spot a person is stuck in, and struggles are the fighting a person does to get through. Two earlier words in this course, hindrance and obstruct, sit on either side of it: a hindrance holds you back without stopping you, and to obstruct is to shut the way off entirely.
