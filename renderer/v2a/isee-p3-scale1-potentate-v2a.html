What It Means

A potentate is a ruler who holds sweeping, largely unchecked power, crowned or not.

See the word in context

The ambassador was granted a private audience with the potentate, who ruled the desert kingdom without any parliament to check his decisions.

Old travel journals describe European explorers being received by local potentates whose word was law across vast territories.

A potentate isn't just any ruler -- the word implies sweeping, largely unchecked power over a territory.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    monarch: A monarch is a king or queen — a ruler whose title comes from royal birth and a crown. A monarch can be nearly powerless under a modern constitution, while a potentate is defined by the sweeping power itself, crowned or not.

    sovereign: A sovereign is the recognized supreme authority of a state, usually a crowned ruler reigning by acknowledged right. Potentate stresses raw scope rather than right: a potentate's commands go unchecked, however the power was gained.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    subject: a person ruled by a monarch or state, without power of their own

    commoner: an ordinary person without noble rank

    Where it sits · how unchecked the power becomes

    ruler → potentate → tyrant

    ruler vs. potentate: Ruler is far milder than potentate: it is the neutral word for anyone in charge — a mayor, a president, a king — while a potentate holds sweeping, largely unchecked power.

    tyrant vs. potentate: A tyrant goes a step beyond a potentate: both hold near-absolute power, but the tyrant wields it cruelly and unjustly.
