What It Means

Acclaimed means having won a chorus of public praise from critics, judges, or official awards.

See the word in context

The acclaimed novelist was invited to speak at universities across the country.

A small indie film opens quietly with no big stars. Within weeks it is widely acclaimed: critics from major newspapers write glowing reviews, and it wins two festival awards. Strangers start recommending it to each other online.

The praise comes publicly, from critics and official awards. Unlike 'popular,' which just means many people like something, acclaimed specifically points to official or critical praise.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    celebrated: Celebrated means widely and publicly honored — the whole community joins in the praise. Acclaimed is narrower: the praise comes specifically from critics, judges, or official awards. A singer can be celebrated by millions of fans while critics ignore her work, so she is not yet acclaimed.

    lauded: Lauded means praised highly and formally — a speech, a review, or a citation delivers the praise out loud. Acclaimed describes the reputation such praise builds up: one glowing review means a film was lauded, but only a chorus of reviews and awards makes it acclaimed.

    esteemed: Esteemed means regarded with great respect, usually earned slowly through character and judgment, and the respect can stay private among those who know the person. Acclaimed requires a chorus of public praise from critics, judges, or official awards. A small-town lawyer can be esteemed by everyone who knows her work without ever being acclaimed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    criticized: judged unfavorably

    panned: harshly and publicly criticized

    Where it sits · how far the praise has grown

    liked → acclaimed → legendary

    liked vs. acclaimed: Liked is weaker than acclaimed — being liked just means people enjoy something casually, while acclaimed points to real public or critical praise.

    legendary vs. acclaimed: Legendary is stronger than acclaimed: acclaimed means praised now, but legendary means that praise has lasted and grown into near-mythic status.
