substantiate

sub-STAN-shee-ayt -- from Latin sub- ('under') + stare ('to stand'), via substantia ('substance').

What It Means

To substantiate is to supply solid evidence that supports a claim as true, even if it falls short of full proof.

See the word in context

Before running the expose, the network's lawyers demanded that every accusation be substantiated in writing.

The insurance company denied the claim at first, but once the homeowner substantiated the damage with dated photos and repair receipts, they approved it within a week.

Backing up a claim with real, solid proof -- not just an assertion -- is exactly what it means to substantiate something.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prove: To prove is to settle a question completely -- once something is proved, no honest doubt is left. To substantiate is one step earlier and humbler: you supply solid evidence that supports a claim, which may still fall short of full proof.

    back up: To back up a claim is the everyday, informal way of supporting it -- a receipt, a screenshot, a friend's word. To substantiate is the formal version: assembling solid evidence fit for a courtroom, a newsroom, or a thesis defense.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    refute: to prove that something is false

    discredit: to cause doubt about something's truth or reliability

    Where it sits · how thorough the proof must be

    confirm → substantiate → verify

    confirm vs. substantiate: Confirm is weaker than substantiate: one quick phone call can confirm a delivery address, while substantiating a claim demands solid evidence carefully assembled to support it as true.

    verify vs. substantiate: Verify goes further than substantiate: to substantiate a story is to lay solid supporting evidence behind it, while to verify it is to check every fact, line by line, until no doubt at all remains.
