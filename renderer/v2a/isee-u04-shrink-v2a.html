Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To shrink from something is to draw back from it — a step backward, a lean away, a hand that will not reach out — because you would rather not face it, whether it is a barking dog, a hard phone call, or a job that scares you. It is not a single sudden jerk of the body; it is the person holding back from the whole thing.

See the word in context

The toddler shrank from the department-store Santa, hiding her face in her father's coat.

Everyone in the office knew the new schedule was a mistake, but they all shrank from being the one to tell the boss.

Nothing sudden has happened, and no one's body has made a sudden jerk. The workers are drawing back from one whole thing — a hard conversation with the boss — because they would rather not face it, and each of them holds back and hopes someone else will do it. That pulling away from something you do not want to face is what shrink means here.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flinch: A flinch is one sudden, small jerk of the body — at a needle, a slap, a bang — that happens on its own and is over in an instant, and the person may go right ahead with what they were doing. To shrink is the person drawing back from a whole thing they would rather not face, and it lasts as long as the holding back does. Ask whether a sudden blow or bang set off one quick jerk, or whether the person is pulling back from something they do not want to do or meet.

    avoid: To avoid is to steer clear of something for any reason at all — you avoid the highway at rush hour, avoid sugar, avoid a boring neighbor — and it needs no fear. To shrink is drawing back because the thing itself is hard to face, and it shows: a step back, a lean away, a hand that stops. Ask whether the person is simply choosing a different route, or pulling back from something that scares or troubles them.

    retreat: To retreat is to move back a real distance and go somewhere else — an army can retreat, you can retreat to your room — and it can be planned, orderly, even wise. To shrink is smaller and closer in: the person stays where they are and pulls back from what is in front of them. Ask whether the person actually leaves the scene, or stays put and draws back.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tackle: to take on a hard job head-on

    volunteer: to step forward for a task nobody has forced on you

    Where it sits · how far fear pulls a person back from what they face

    hesitate → shrink → cower

    hesitate vs. shrink: To hesitate is the smallest step back — a pause, a moment of not being sure — and the person is still facing the thing and usually goes ahead a second later: a pause at the door, then a knock. To shrink is to pull back from it rather than go on. Ask whether the person paused and then went ahead, or drew back and held back.

    cower vs. shrink: To cower goes further — the whole body drops low and stays down, small and shaking, in open fear of a threat. To shrink is a lean or a step away, and the person is still on their feet. Ask whether the body has dropped low and stayed there, or only leaned or stepped back.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Shrink also has an everyday meaning you already know — to get smaller, the way a wool sweater shrinks in the dryer. On this card, and in the ISEE's word-matching questions (where the matching answer is shy away), shrink means drawing back from something you would rather not face, and in that sense it is nearly always followed by from or back: she shrank from the task, he shrank back from the edge. The past tense is shrank.
