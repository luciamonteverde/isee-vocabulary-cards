What It Means

To garble is to scramble a message so badly that its meaning is lost.

See the word in context

The garbled voicemail cut out twice, so all Priya could make out was something about "Tuesday" and "the second floor."

Static on the walkie-talkie garbled the coach's play call so badly that the quarterback just guessed and ran the wrong route.

Garble describes a message that comes out scrambled or mangled, not one that's simply quiet or brief.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scramble: To scramble is simply to mix things out of order -- eggs, letters, a seating chart -- and the pieces all survive the mixing. To garble is stricter: it applies to a message, and the mixing goes so far that the meaning itself is lost. Scrambled things can be put back; a garbled message often cannot.

    warp: To warp is to bend something out of its true shape, so the message arrives altered -- still saying something, just not the right thing. A garbled message is different: what arrives can no longer be pieced back into sense at all. Warp twists a meaning into a wrong one; garble destroys the meaning altogether.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clarify: to make something clear and easy to understand

    simplify: to make something easier to understand by removing complexity

    Where it sits · how much of the message survives

    mumble → garble → mangle

    mumble vs. garble: A mumble sits below garble: mumbled words are merely quiet and slurred, and a listener who leans in can usually still catch them, while a garbled message defeats even a careful listener.

    mangle vs. garble: Mangle goes beyond garble: a garbled message may still yield a stray word or two, but to mangle a message is to destroy it so completely that not even fragments make sense.
