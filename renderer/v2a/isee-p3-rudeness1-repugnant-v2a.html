repugnant

rih-PUG-nunt

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Repugnant means so distasteful or offensive that it feels morally unacceptable -- something you reject as wrong, not merely unpleasant.

See the word in context

News that the summer camp had been silently pocketing families' refund requests spread through the community as a repugnant betrayal of trust.

Most readers found the villain's casual cruelty toward animals more repugnant than any of his other crimes in the story.

Repugnant is a strong word -- it's not just dislike, it's the kind of reaction that makes you want to turn away or push back against something entirely.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    repulsive: Repulsive is what drives you back physically -- a smell, a sight, a slimy texture your body wants distance from. Repugnant lives one level up, in the conscience: it names what feels morally unacceptable, something you reject as wrong rather than merely gross.

    abhorrent: Abhorrent means inspiring hatred as well as disgust -- you loathe the thing and condemn it utterly. Repugnant is the slightly cooler judgment: strong distaste at something morally unacceptable, without abhorrent's note of active, personal hatred.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    appealing: attractive or pleasing

    admirable: deserving respect and approval

    Where it sits · how strong the disgust is

    distasteful → repugnant → revolting

    distasteful vs. repugnant: Distasteful is much milder -- it's simple disapproval, not the strong urge to reject something that repugnant carries.

    revolting vs. repugnant: Revolting pushes even further than repugnant, suggesting an almost physical reaction of disgust.
