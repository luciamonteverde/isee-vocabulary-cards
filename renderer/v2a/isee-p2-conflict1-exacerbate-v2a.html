exacerbate

eg-ZAS-er-bayt -- from Latin ex- ('thoroughly') + acerbus ('harsh, bitter').

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To exacerbate is to make a genuine, existing problem seriously worse, deepening what is already there.

See the word in context

Ignoring the small leak in the roof only exacerbated the water damage over the following winter.

Instead of resting the sprained ankle, the runner went back out for another practice the next morning, exacerbating the injury until, by evening, she could barely put any weight on it at all.

Taking an action that makes an already-bad situation even worse -- like running on a sprain instead of resting it -- is exactly what it means to exacerbate something.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    intensify: Intensify means to grow, or make something grow, more extreme -- and the something does not have to be bad: a storm intensifies, a search intensifies, training intensifies. It can even stand alone with no object at all. Exacerbate is narrower: it always takes an existing problem and makes that problem worse.

    compound: To compound a problem is to pile new trouble on top of it, so the mess grows layer by layer -- a lie compounded by a cover-up. To exacerbate is to make the original problem itself worse, deepening what is already there rather than stacking fresh troubles on top.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    alleviate: to noticeably lighten a problem or pain so it is easier to bear, without removing it

    soothe: to calm or gently ease pain or distress

    Where it sits · how severely the problem is worsened

    aggravate → exacerbate → inflame

    aggravate vs. exacerbate: Aggravate is the everyday, milder word for making something somewhat worse -- scratching aggravates a bug bite. Exacerbate is more serious and more formal: it deepens a genuine problem, the way a drought exacerbates a food shortage.

    inflame vs. exacerbate: Inflame goes further than exacerbate -- to exacerbate tensions is to make them worse, but to inflame them is to set them blazing, pushing a bad situation toward open crisis.
