quintessential

kwin-tuh-SEN-shul -- from quintessence ('the purest, most concentrated essence of something') + -ial.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To be quintessential is to be the single best or most perfect example of its kind.

See the word in context

With its red phone booths, black cabs, and steady drizzle, the scene captured the quintessential image of London.

Ordering a slice at a corner shop, cheese dripping off the edge as you eat it folded in half on the sidewalk, is the quintessential New York pizza experience.

Quintessential marks something as the single best, most representative example of its category -- not just a good example, but the example that everyone would picture first.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    classic: Classic means widely recognized as a standard example of its kind -- a classic mystery plot, a classic diner. There can be many classics of a category, but only one quintessential example: the single most perfect case that everyone would picture first.

    iconic: Iconic describes something famous enough to stand as a symbol -- its picture alone says the category. Fame is the test for iconic; quintessential is about fit, not fame, and an obscure corner shop can still be the quintessential neighborhood bakery.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    atypical: not representative of the usual type

    unrepresentative: not typical of the group or category

    Where it sits · how fully it represents its kind

    typical → quintessential → archetypal

    typical vs. quintessential: Typical sits at the bottom of this scale: it just means common and usual for its kind, with no praise attached. Quintessential is a much stronger claim -- not merely a normal example but the single most perfect one.

    archetypal vs. quintessential: Archetypal sits at the top of this scale, beyond quintessential: the quintessential example is the one everyone would picture first, but the archetypal one is the original pattern the whole category is measured against -- it does not just represent the kind, it defines it.

    Word Choice

    Quintessential should be reserved for the single best or most representative example of a category -- not used loosely for something that's merely 'pretty good' or 'somewhat typical.'
