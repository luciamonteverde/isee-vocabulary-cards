What It Means

Holistic is looking at or treating all the parts of something together, as one connected whole, rather than one part at a time.

See the word in context

Rather than drilling grammar alone, the teacher takes a holistic view of writing, weighing voice, ideas, and structure together.

The vet takes a holistic look at the limping dog: she examines his paw, but she also asks about his diet, his sleep, his exercise, and whether anything at home has been stressing him.

She treats the whole animal, not just the single injured part. Holistic thinking assumes the pieces of a system are connected, so you understand them best by looking at the whole.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    integrated: Integrated means combined so that separate parts work as one connected system -- an integrated sound system wires every speaker together. Holistic is not about how a thing is built but about how you look at it: a holistic review studies all the parts together to understand the whole.

    systemic: Systemic describes something that runs through an entire system -- a systemic failure spreads into every branch and level of it. Holistic is different in kind: it names a way of looking, the choice to study the parts together as one whole rather than one at a time.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    piecemeal: done one separate part at a time

    narrow: limited in range or scope

    Where it sits · how fully things are treated as one

    comprehensive → holistic → all-encompassing

    comprehensive vs. holistic: Comprehensive is broader but flatter than holistic -- a comprehensive report covers every item on the list, while a holistic one goes further and treats those items as one connected, living whole.

    all-encompassing vs. holistic: All-encompassing outreaches even holistic in sheer scope: holistic insists the parts be understood together as a whole, while all-encompassing sweeps in absolutely everything, leaving nothing outside its reach.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Holistic pairs naturally with approach, view, care, and education: a holistic approach to medicine, a holistic view of a problem. Whenever you see it, expect the opposite of piecemeal -- someone is choosing to study the whole rather than one part at a time.
