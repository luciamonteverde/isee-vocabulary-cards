ponderous

PON-duh-ruhs

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Ponderous is heavy, slow-moving, and lacking any grace or liveliness.

See the word in context

The committee's ponderous approval process meant that even simple requests took months to clear.

The old encyclopedia's ponderous, page-long definitions made even simple topics feel like a chore to read.

The writing wasn't just long -- it moved heavily and slowly, without any lightness, which is the clumsy weightiness that ponderous describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    plodding: Plodding describes a slow, heavy, one-step-at-a-time pace -- a plodding hike, a plodding investigation that inches forward. It is a complaint about speed. Ponderous is a wider complaint: a ponderous speech or machine is not only slow but weighty, graceless, and dull.

    cumbersome: Cumbersome means heavy and awkward to handle or use -- a cumbersome crate, a cumbersome sign-up process with too many steps. The fault is unwieldiness. Ponderous instead faults the way something moves or reads: heavily, slowly, without a spark of liveliness.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    brisk: quick and energetic

    graceful: smooth and elegant in movement or style

    Where it sits · how heavy and lifeless it feels

    sluggish → ponderous → leaden

    sluggish vs. ponderous: Sluggish is milder than ponderous: a sluggish reply is merely slow, while a ponderous one feels heavier still, dragging along without any grace or liveliness.

    leaden vs. ponderous: Leaden is heavier than ponderous: a ponderous speech moves slowly and gracelessly, but a leaden one goes further, so dull and lifeless it barely seems to move at all.

    Word Choice
