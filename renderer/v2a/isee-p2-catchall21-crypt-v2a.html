Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A crypt is a stone room built beneath the floor of a church or chapel, reached by going down steps, where the dead are laid to rest in coffins or carved stone boxes.

See the word in context

On the field trip, our class filed down a narrow stone staircase into the crypt beneath the old church, where the town's founders have lain for two hundred years.

Workers repairing the cathedral floor broke through into a forgotten crypt, where rows of dusty coffins had rested untouched since the 1600s.

Everything about the room fits: it is under the floor of a church, you reach it by going down, and it holds coffins. That combination — beneath a church, built for the dead — is exactly what crypt names. If the same underground room held the cathedral's silver instead of its dead, it would be a vault; if it stored the priest's firewood, it would only be a cellar.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vault: A vault is any room built strong — thick walls, a heavy door — to protect whatever is inside: a bank's cash, a library's rare books, sometimes a family's coffins. It can sit above or below ground and under any kind of building. A crypt is the burial room under a church and nothing else. Ask what the room protects and what building stands above it.

    tomb: A tomb is a resting place for the dead built to be seen — a stone monument in a cemetery, a carved chamber in a hillside, a pyramid. Most tombs stand above ground and hold one person or one family. A crypt is a room you walk down into beneath a church, often holding many. Ask whether you look at it from outside or go down steps into it.

    cellar: A cellar is an underground room too, but under a house or a shop, and it stores things the living use — potatoes, jars, wine, old furniture. Nobody is buried in it. A crypt is under a church and holds the dead. Ask what is kept in the room and what building sits on top of it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steeple: the tall tower rising above a church — the opposite end of the building from a crypt

    nursery: a bright room for the newly born, the far end of life from a room for the dead

    Where it sits · how much space is built to hold the dead

    grave → crypt → catacombs

    grave vs. crypt: A grave is the simplest resting place — a hole dug in the earth for one coffin, then filled in and marked with a stone. There is no room and no door; nobody goes inside. A crypt is a built room with a floor and walls that the living can enter. Ask whether you could stand up inside it.

    catacombs vs. crypt: Catacombs are the far end — a whole network of underground passages, usually beneath a city, lined with spaces for thousands of the dead. A crypt is one room, under one church, holding one community's dead. Ask whether it is a single chamber under a church or a maze of tunnels under a city.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Crypt comes from a Greek word meaning hidden, and it is a cousin of cryptic — a cryptic message hides its meaning the way a crypt hides its dead below the floor. Outside history books you will meet crypts in mystery stories, horror films and video games, always as a dim underground burial room. On the ISEE synonym section the closest match offered is vault, since both are strong enclosed rooms; keep in mind that a crypt is always the burial kind, and always under a church.
