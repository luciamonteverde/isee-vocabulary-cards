parable

PAIR-uh-bul -- Greek parabole, 'a placing beside': a story laid beside real life to reveal a moral.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A parable is a short story about ordinary human situations, told to teach a moral lesson.

See the word in context

The teacher ended the assembly with a parable about a farmer whose patience saved his whole village.

Instead of lecturing her grandchildren about greed, a grandmother tells them a parable: a brief tale of a merchant who loses the coins he has while snatching at a stranger's fuller purse.

A short story about an ordinary human situation, told to teach a moral lesson, is what makes her tale a parable. Unlike 'anecdote,' which simply recounts an amusing incident, a parable exists to make its listeners wiser.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fable: A fable is a brief tale that puts its lesson in the mouths of talking animals -- foxes, crows, tortoises -- and ends with the moral spelled out. A parable teaches the same way but keeps to ordinary human situations: a farmer, a traveler, a builder.

    tale: A tale is any short narrative told for interest -- scary tales, folk tales, tall tales. Parable is narrower: a tale only becomes a parable when its whole point is the moral lesson it plants in the listener.

    legend: A legend is a traditional story handed down over generations about heroes or famous places, told mostly to entertain and to remember. A parable is built for teaching: its little story exists to hand the listener a moral.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    chronicle: a factual record of events in order

    report: a factual account of something observed

    Where it sits · how large and layered the story grows

    anecdote → parable → allegory

    anecdote vs. parable: An anecdote is smaller and simpler than a parable -- it just recounts one brief incident for interest or laughs, while a parable shapes its story to carry a moral lesson.

    allegory vs. parable: Allegory is bigger and more elaborate than parable: a parable is a brief story with one clear moral, while an allegory can run the length of a whole book, with every character standing for something.
