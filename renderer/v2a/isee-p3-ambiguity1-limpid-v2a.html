What It Means

Limpid means completely clear and unclouded, praising the quality of pure clearness rather than just stating a physical fact.

See the word in context

Through the limpid glass of the aquarium tank, every scale on the koi shimmered in the light.

The travel brochure promised limpid Caribbean water so clear that snorkelers could count starfish thirty feet below.

Limpid describes something (often water, or clear writing) with no cloudiness at all -- total transparency.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    transparent: Transparent is the plain, physical fact that light passes through something so you can see what is behind it -- a sandwich bag is transparent. Limpid adds admiration: it praises water or writing for a pure, unclouded clearness. Transparent states a property, while limpid praises a quality.

    pellucid: Pellucid is used almost entirely for ideas and writing -- an explanation so pellucid lets its meaning shine straight through. Limpid can praise clear prose too, but it is equally at home describing physically clear water; pellucid rarely leaves the world of thought and style.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    murky: dark, unclear, or difficult to see through

    turbid: cloudy or muddy, especially of a liquid

    Where it sits · how perfectly see-through it is

    clear → limpid → crystalline

    clear vs. limpid: Clear is weaker than limpid: a clear window is easy to look through but may keep a faint tint or smudge, while limpid water lets nothing at all cloud the view.

    crystalline vs. limpid: Crystalline goes even further than limpid: limpid water is perfectly unclouded, while crystalline water sparkles with such glassy, flawless clarity it hardly looks real.
