visionary

VIZH-uh-ner-ee -- built on the root for 'to see.'

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Visionary is having or showing highly original and imaginative ideas about the future.

See the word in context

The architect's visionary designs imagined entire cities built around vertical farms.

Decades before anyone else believed it possible, a visionary engineer sketches plans for a device that lets ordinary people carry a computer, a camera, and a telephone all in one pocket-sized object.

That bold, far-ahead imagination is what makes the engineer visionary. Unlike 'practical,' which focuses on the present, visionary looks toward possibilities others haven't yet imagined.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inventive: Inventive emphasizes hands-on cleverness -- actually building or devising something new that works. Visionary is about the reach of the idea itself: a visionary thinker imagines a changed future, whether or not she ever builds anything.

    forward-thinking: Forward-thinking means planning sensibly for what is coming -- updating systems before they break, saving before retirement. Visionary is bolder: it imagines futures other people have not even pictured yet, not just prepares for the expected one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unimaginative: lacking creativity or original thought

    conventional: following accepted standards; ordinary

    Where it sits · how bold and large the ideas are

    imaginative → visionary → revolutionary

    imaginative vs. visionary: Imaginative is a broader word than visionary -- imaginative can describe anyone with a creative idea, while visionary specifically implies bold, large-scale ideas about the future.

    revolutionary vs. visionary: Revolutionary goes a step beyond visionary: a visionary plan imagines a bold new future, while a revolutionary one actually overturns how things are done, sweeping the old way aside.
