Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A gulf is a very wide separation between two people, groups, or ways of thinking — the two sides are so far apart that they can hardly reach or understand each other.

See the word in context

After the strike, a gulf opened between the factory workers and the managers that no meeting seemed able to close.

The survey showed a gulf between what parents believed their kids did online and what the kids were actually doing.

The parents and the kids are not a little off from each other — they are living in two different pictures of the same nights, so far apart that neither side can see the other's. That width is what makes it a gulf rather than a small difference. Notice that nothing was broken here; the two sides were simply never close.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rift: A rift is a crack — a split in something that used to be one piece, like a friendship that has broken or a team that has fallen out. A gulf is the wide open space between two sides, which may never have been joined at all. Ask whether the two sides were once one thing that broke apart.

    divide: A divide is the line that sorts people into two camps — one side and the other, with the line between them. A gulf is about how far apart the two camps stand. Ask whether the sentence points at the line between the sides or at the great space across.

    distance: Distance is any amount of space between two things, near or far, and it is a neutral measurement. A gulf is only the biggest kind of separation, one that is hard to cross. Wherever there is a gulf there is distance, so ask the other way round: does the sentence stress how far apart the sides are and how hard it would be to bring them together?

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bond: a close connection that holds two sides together

    agreement: the two sides thinking the same way

    Where it sits · how wide the separation between the two sides is

    gap → gulf

    gap vs. gulf: Gap is the smaller, everyday word — a gap can be any opening or difference, large or small, and is often easy to close. Gulf is saved for a separation so wide that the two sides can hardly reach each other. Ask whether the sentence treats the difference as huge and hard to cross.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Gulf comes from geography: a gulf is a huge arm of the sea reaching into the land, like the Gulf of Mexico. When you use it for people or ideas, that picture should still hold — a wide stretch of open water, with the land far away on either side. Save it for serious cases: a gulf between rich and poor, between what was promised and what was done. For a small or easily fixed difference, use a smaller word.
