clemency

KLEM-un-see -- three syllables, stress on the first syllable, KLEM.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Clemency is mercy granted by someone with the power to punish, officially softening a deserved punishment.

See the word in context

The convicted smuggler's lawyers petitioned the queen for clemency, pointing to his years of quiet charity.

The principal reviews the seniors' prank, notes that no one was hurt and that the students confessed at once, and shows clemency, reducing the punishment from suspension to a week of cleanup duty.

Softening a deserved punishment is exactly what clemency means: mercy granted by someone with the power to punish -- a judge, a ruler, a principal -- to someone who is at their mercy.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    leniency: Leniency is mildness in punishing as a habit -- the referee who lets small fouls go all season is lenient. Clemency is not a habit but a single official act of mercy: someone with the power to punish chooses, once, to soften a deserved penalty.

    forbearance: Forbearance is patient self-restraint -- holding back from punishing or striking back even when you have every right to. Clemency goes further and is official: not merely holding back, but a ruler or court formally reducing a punishment that has already been handed down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    severity: harshness in treatment or punishment

    ruthlessness: complete lack of pity

    Where it sits · how formal and official the compassion is

    mercy → clemency → pardon

    mercy vs. clemency: Mercy is the broad, everyday word; clemency is its formal, official cousin -- anyone can show mercy, but clemency comes from a bench, a throne, or a governor's desk.

    pardon vs. clemency: A pardon is even more official than clemency: clemency softens a punishment while the conviction stands, but a pardon is the signed legal act that wipes the conviction away altogether.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Clemency belongs to courts and rulers: a prisoner seeks clemency, a governor grants it, a judge shows it. Use it only where someone with authority softens a punishment -- everyday kindness is not clemency, and asking a friend for clemency over a missed birthday is a joke, not a real request.
