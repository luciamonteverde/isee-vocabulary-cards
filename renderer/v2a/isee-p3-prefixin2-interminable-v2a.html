interminable

in-TUR-mi-nuh-bul

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Interminable means seemingly endless: dragging on so long that anyone stuck in the middle of it feels it will never be over.

See the word in context

The awards ceremony felt interminable, with speech after speech dragging on long after midnight.

The line at the DMV was interminable; three hours in, the number on his ticket still hadn't been called.

Interminable is used for things that technically do end, but feel like they never will while you're stuck in the middle of them.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    endless: Endless is the plain everyday word for something that has, or seems to have, no end, and it carries no particular feeling. Interminable is its weary formal cousin: it adds the groan of somebody trapped inside the dragging thing, waiting for it to be over.

    drawn-out: Drawn-out describes something stretched to take longer than it needed to — a drawn-out goodbye, a drawn-out negotiation that could have been brief. Interminable goes further: the thing does not merely run long, it feels as though it will never end at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    brief: lasting only a short time

    momentary: lasting only a moment

    Where it sits · how much it seems to drag

    lengthy → interminable → unending

    lengthy vs. interminable: Lengthy is much weaker than interminable: a lengthy movie simply runs three hours, a neutral fact about the clock, while interminable adds the groan of an audience certain it will never end.

    unending vs. interminable: Unending goes further than interminable: an interminable ceremony does finally end, however badly it drags, while something unending truly never stops at all.
