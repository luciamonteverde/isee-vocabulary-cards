vexation

vek-SAY-shun

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Vexation is the worked-up, troubled state of being bothered by something that keeps at you and that you cannot settle, fix, or shake off — a question with no answer, a stain that will not lift, a neighbor's habit you can do nothing about. It needs no errand or task; the thing itself nags at your mind, so it stays with you and sours your mood.

See the word in context

The one sour note on the recording — a note she could hear every time but could never find in the score — became a nightly vexation for the conductor.

For weeks on end a faint tapping inside the bedroom wall kept Mr. Abernathy awake, and though he pulled up floorboards and checked every pipe, his vexation only grew as the sound refused to explain itself.

Mr. Abernathy is not running an errand, and nothing is blocking a goal — he simply cannot settle the tapping. He has tried everything he can think of, the sound is still there, and it has him stewing: bothered, a little worried, unable to leave it alone. That is vexation. If a pipe had rattled once and then stopped, he would have felt a moment's irritation; if the tapping had been a loud neighbor he could complain to, the feeling might have turned into resentment. Vexation is the bother that has nowhere to go.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    annoyance: Annoyance is the plain, all-purpose word for being bothered, and it is usually mild and soon over — a fly at the picnic, a song stuck in your head. It is true of every vexation, so the choice is a matter of degree and staying power: call it vexation only when the person is genuinely worked up over something that will not be settled and keeps coming back. Ask whether the person is merely bothered, or stewing over a thing they cannot fix.

    resentment: Resentment is the bitter, held-onto feeling of having been treated unfairly by someone — a smaller share, a broken promise, credit that went to the wrong person. Vexation needs no one to blame and no unfairness at all: a knot, a riddle, or a sound in the wall can vex you. Ask whether the person feels wronged by someone, or simply troubled by something they cannot settle.

    aggravation: Aggravation (also taught in this unit, and the word the ISEE pairs with vexation) is the wearing bother of hassle — delays, extra steps, things going wrong — while you are trying to get something done. Vexation needs no task: it is the stewing over a thing that will not be settled, and it sounds older and a shade more worried. Ask whether the person is being put through trouble on the way to something, or stewing over something that cannot be fixed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    calm: the untroubled state of having nothing preying on your mind

    contentment: the settled, satisfied feeling that all is well

    Where it sits · how far the bother has worked its way into a person

    irritation → vexation → exasperation

    irritation vs. vexation: Irritation is the scratchy first flicker of being bothered — one buzzing fly, one slow website — and it can come and go in a minute without leaving a mark. Vexation has settled in: the thing will not be fixed or shaken off, so the person keeps returning to it, worked up and a little worried. Ask whether the bother passed in a moment, or has stayed on the person's mind because it cannot be settled.

    exasperation vs. vexation: Exasperation is the point where patience gives out — the person throws up their hands, snaps, or quits: 'I give up.' Vexation is still wrestling: bothered and troubled, but not yet done. Ask whether the person is still turning the problem over, or has reached the end of their patience with it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Vexation is a formal, slightly old-fashioned word — you meet it in novels, letters, and exam passages more than in hallway talk. Its verb is vex ('the question vexed him') and its adjective is vexed; a 'vexed question' is one people have argued over for years without settling, which is the heart of the word. Like annoyance, vexation can also name the thing that causes the feeling: 'the leaking roof was a constant vexation.' On the ISEE, the closest answer choice is usually irritation — and if a sentence shows someone being blocked from a goal, this unit's own frustration is the sharper word.
