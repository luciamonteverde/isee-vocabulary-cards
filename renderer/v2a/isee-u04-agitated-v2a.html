agitated

AJ-ih-tay-tid

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Agitated describes someone so stirred up by worry, anger, or fear that they cannot settle down, and it shows on the outside — pacing, drumming fingers, shifting in a seat, snapping at people.

See the word in context

The horses grew agitated as the thunder rolled closer, stamping and tossing their heads in their stalls.

Waiting for the vet to come back with the X-rays, Mrs. Okafor grew agitated, circling the tiny room and twisting the leash between her fingers.

Mrs. Okafor is worried about her dog, and the worry does not stay inside: she circles the room, she twists the leash, she cannot sit down. That outward restless movement is what makes her agitated rather than simply concerned. Someone waiting silently with the same worry would be nervous; someone stiff and frozen in the chair would be tense.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nervous: Nervous names the uneasy, worried feeling itself, and a nervous person can hide it completely, sitting still with a calm face. Agitated means the feeling has broken through into restless motion or sharp words. To tell them apart, ask whether you could see it from across the room.

    upset: Upset means unhappy or troubled by something, and upset people are often quiet — tearful, silent, staring at the floor. Agitated adds motion: the person paces, drums their fingers, snaps at whoever is nearby. Ask whether the person is moving or still.

    tense: Tense means tight and braced, like a stretched rope — a tense person often goes stiff and silent, holding everything in. Agitated is the opposite kind of not-calm: the tension spills out into movement. Ask whether the person has gone rigid or cannot stop moving.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    calm: settled and steady, with nothing stirring

    composed: keeping firm control of yourself under pressure

    Where it sits · how far trouble is driving the restless movement

    restless → agitated → frantic

    restless vs. agitated: Restless is the milder word — a restless person cannot sit still, but only from boredom or spare energy, with nothing troubling them. Agitated adds a real cause: worry, anger, or fear is driving the movement. Ask whether something is actually wrong.

    frantic vs. agitated: Frantic is the stronger word — a frantic person is wild with worry, rushing in every direction and barely thinking. Agitated stops short of that: the person is visibly stirred up but still in control. Ask whether they can still stand in one place and talk sense.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Agitated is not about how scared or angry someone feels inside but about what the feeling does to their body and voice. Use it when a person cannot keep still or keep quiet. It also describes things that are physically stirred up — an agitated sea, the agitated water in a washing machine — and that picture is a good way to remember the human sense: churning, never at rest.
