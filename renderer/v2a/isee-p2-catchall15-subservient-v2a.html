subservient

sub-SUR-vee-uhnt -- from Latin sub- ('under') + servire ('to serve').

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Subservient means excessively, habitually obedient, placing one's own judgment beneath someone else's authority.

See the word in context

The assistant had grown so subservient that he apologized even when his boss was clearly in the wrong.

A new intern is so subservient that he agrees with everything the department head says in meetings, never once offering a differing opinion, even when asked directly for one.

Being subservient goes beyond ordinary politeness -- it is excessive, almost automatic obedience, giving up one's own judgment entirely.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    submissive: Submissive describes giving in to someone else's will in the moment -- you yield when another person pushes. Subservient goes further: it is obedience built into the relationship itself, a person who habitually places their own judgment beneath someone else's authority.

    meek: Meek describes a temperament -- quiet, gentle, and slow to stand up for yourself, no matter who you are with. Subservient describes a relationship to authority: a person can be bold among friends and still be completely subservient to a demanding boss.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    defiant: openly resisting authority

    domineering: excessively controlling of others

    Where it sits · how demeaning the obedience becomes

    obedient → subservient → servile

    obedient vs. subservient: Obedient is far milder than subservient -- an obedient student simply follows the rules while keeping their own judgment, but a subservient person hands that judgment over to someone else entirely.

    servile vs. subservient: Servile is stronger and more demeaning than subservient -- it suggests a groveling, slave-like obedience, while subservient can simply describe an employee's or subordinate's expected role.
