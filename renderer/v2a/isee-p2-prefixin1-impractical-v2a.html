Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impractical means not suited to the real conditions it must actually work in, however appealing it looks.

See the word in context

A white carpet in a house with three toddlers is an impractical choice, however lovely it looks in the showroom.

For a nine-mile uphill hike with everything on your back, one friend packs an impractical load: a cast-iron pan, four hardcover books, and a folding chair.

Impractical means not suited to the situation you are actually walking into. The word judges an idea by whether it survives real conditions, not by whether it sounds appealing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unrealistic: Unrealistic judges the hopes a plan is built on: it assumes people, money, or weather will behave in ways they will not. Impractical can rest on perfectly true assumptions and still fail, because the design does not suit real conditions. An unrealistic plan misreads the world; an impractical one just does not fit it.

    inconvenient: Inconvenient means annoying or requiring extra effort, while remaining entirely doable -- a detour that adds ten minutes is inconvenient. Impractical is a deeper verdict: the choice does not suit the real conditions it must live in, however appealing it looks. Inconvenient things still work; impractical ones fight the life around them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    practical: sensible and well suited to real use

    workable: able to be done successfully

    Where it sits · how close the plan is to impossible

    ill-advised → impractical → unworkable

    ill-advised vs. impractical: Ill-advised is the softer criticism -- an ill-advised plan is unwise but might still work -- while an impractical one simply does not fit the real conditions it has to survive.

    unworkable vs. impractical: Unworkable is the final verdict past impractical: an impractical plan is unwise and awkward in practice, while an unworkable one cannot be carried out at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Impractical criticizes plans, designs, and sometimes people ('brilliant, but hopelessly impractical'). Watch out for its close relative impracticable, which means flatly impossible to carry out; impractical is the everyday word -- unwise and unsuited to real life -- and it is the one the ISEE wants.
