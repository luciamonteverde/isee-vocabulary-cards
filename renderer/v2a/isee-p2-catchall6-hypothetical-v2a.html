hypothetical

hy-poh-THET-ih-kul — contains 'hypothesis'; a hypothetical situation is just a proposed idea, not yet proven real.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Hypothetical is existing only as an idea or concept; supposed rather than actual, posed as a what-if for the sake of discussion or argument.

See the word in context

The debate coach posed a hypothetical case to the students: what if the defendant had a solid alibi the whole time?

Before launching a new spacecraft, engineers spend months working through hypothetical failures -- what if an engine cuts out, what if a valve freezes -- purely on paper, long before any of those things ever actually occur.

Each of those imagined failures is hypothetical: a scenario considered purely as a possibility, without it necessarily being real. Unlike 'actual,' which describes something real and confirmed, hypothetical describes something only proposed or imagined.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    theoretical: Theoretical means based on theory rather than actual practice — a whole framework of ideas that has not been tested in the real world. Hypothetical is smaller and more pointed: a hypothetical is a single imagined case, a what-if posed for the sake of discussion.

    speculative: Speculative means based on guessing rather than known facts — a speculative claim is trying to say something true without the evidence. A hypothetical case makes no claim at all: everyone agrees it is not real, and it exists purely to be reasoned about.

    imagined: Imagined means existing only in the mind, not in reality — any daydream qualifies. Hypothetical is imagined with a job to do: a hypothetical scenario is deliberately proposed so people can test an idea or an argument against it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    actual: existing in fact; real

    factual: based on facts rather than ideas

    Where it sits · how fully it lives in thought alone

    possible → hypothetical → fantastical

    possible vs. hypothetical: Possible is a broader, weaker word than hypothetical — something possible simply could happen, while hypothetical specifically describes an idea proposed for the sake of discussion or argument, not a real prediction.

    fantastical vs. hypothetical: Fantastical goes even further than hypothetical — a hypothetical case is imagined but kept believable enough to reason about, while something fantastical has left reality behind entirely, the stuff of dragons and magic.
