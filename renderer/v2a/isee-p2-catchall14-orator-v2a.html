orator

OR-uh-tur -- from Latin orare, 'to speak, to plead,' from os, 'mouth': an orator speaks with the whole force of the mouth.

What It Means

An orator is a skilled public speaker whose powerful delivery moves and persuades an audience.

See the word in context

Frederick Douglass was such a powerful orator that audiences often fell completely silent within the first minute of his speeches.

Though she had never given a speech before, the exchange student stood at the podium and proved a natural orator, speaking so clearly and persuasively about her home country that the entire cafeteria stopped eating to listen.

An orator doesn't need years of formal training -- what matters is the ability to speak clearly and persuasively enough to hold a room's attention, exactly what the exchange student did on her very first try.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    lecturer: A lecturer delivers prepared talks meant to teach or inform, usually in a classroom or hall. An orator is defined by the power of the delivery itself -- a lecturer can drone through notes and still be a lecturer, but no one earns the name orator without moving the audience.

    spokesperson: A spokesperson is chosen to speak for a company, team, or cause, and the title says nothing about talent. Orator is a compliment about skill: it praises how powerfully someone speaks, not whom they speak for. A clumsy spokesperson is still a spokesperson; a clumsy orator is a contradiction.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    listener: someone who listens rather than speaks

    wallflower: a shy person who avoids public attention

    Where it sits · how skilled and studied the delivery is

    speaker → orator → rhetorician

    speaker vs. orator: Speaker is the plain, everyday word for anyone who talks in public, skilled or not -- calling someone an orator specifically praises their skill.

    rhetorician vs. orator: Rhetorician goes further than orator -- it emphasizes deliberate technique and persuasive craft, almost like a scholar of speech, while orator can simply mean someone who speaks powerfully.
