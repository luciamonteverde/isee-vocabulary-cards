Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To instill is to gradually establish something in someone's mind.

See the word in context

Scout leaders work for years to instill a love of the outdoors in every troop.

Nobody remembers Grandma ever lecturing about money. But every Sunday dinner ended the same way -- leftovers wrapped, coupons clipped, the last sliver of soap pressed onto the new bar -- and the thrift those dinners instilled runs so deep that forty years later, all four grandchildren still cannot throw away a usable thing.

A value was gradually established in their minds, drop by drop, through years of small repeated moments. Instill is never instant -- it builds character and attitude over time, the way water drips into stone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nurture: To nurture is to care for something already alive and help it grow, the way a gardener tends a seedling. To instill is to start earlier: it plants the value itself in someone's mind and builds it gradually, before there is anything to tend.

    ingrain: To ingrain is to fix a habit or attitude so deeply that it feels built in -- ingrained caution is already part of someone. To instill names the process at the start: gradually planting a value in someone's mind before it has taken hold.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    erase: to remove completely

    discourage: to make a feeling or effort weaker

    Where it sits · how deliberately the quality is grown

    foster → instill → cultivate

    foster vs. instill: Foster is the lightest, least deliberate rung: it encourages something already sprouting, giving a quality room to grow, while instill works with steadier intent -- planting the value itself and building it, drop by drop, until it takes root.

    cultivate vs. instill: Cultivate is the strongest and most deliberate of the three: instill quietly builds a value through repeated moments, while cultivate suggests a full program of tending -- planned, patient, and worked at like a garden.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Instill is about character, not facts: you instill discipline, confidence, honesty, or respect in someone, gradually and over time. Keep it apart from its two cluster neighbors in this unit -- impart hands over specific knowledge or wisdom ('the mentor imparted decades of advice'), and enlighten corrects ignorance with new insight. A coach instills grit, imparts technique, and enlightens the team about what went wrong.
