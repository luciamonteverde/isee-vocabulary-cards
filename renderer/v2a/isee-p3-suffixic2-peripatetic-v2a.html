peripatetic

per-ih-puh-TET-ik

What It Means

Peripatetic means traveling from place to place as a regular part of one's work or way of life, an ongoing pattern of movement rather than a single trip.

See the word in context

As a peripatetic session musician, she spent more nights in tour buses and hotel rooms than in her own apartment.

The peripatetic diplomat crisscrossed three continents in a single month, negotiating trade deals in one capital before flying to the next.

Peripatetic describes a lifestyle or job built around constant movement -- not a single trip, but an ongoing pattern of traveling from place to place.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    itinerant: Itinerant (a word you met earlier in this course) workers follow the work itself from town to town — harvest crews, circuit judges — staying in each place only while the job lasts. Peripatetic stresses the constant motion of the routine itself, and a peripatetic worker may keep a settled home base.

    nomadic: Nomadic means having no fixed home at all: the household itself moves with the seasons or the herds. Peripatetic is narrower — the working day keeps you moving, while a settled home may stay put behind you.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sedentary: staying in one place, especially seated (a word you met earlier in this course)

    stationary: not moving, fixed in place

    Where it sits · how constantly on the move

    well-traveled → peripatetic → vagabond

    well-traveled vs. peripatetic: Well-traveled is much milder than peripatetic: it counts the trips someone has taken over the years, while peripatetic describes a life or job built around constant motion.

    vagabond vs. peripatetic: A vagabond wanders with no fixed home at all, even more unmoored than someone merely peripatetic.
