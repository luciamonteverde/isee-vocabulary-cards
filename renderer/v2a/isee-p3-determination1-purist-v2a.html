What It Means

Purist is a person who insists on strict adherence to traditional rules, methods, or forms of a discipline.

See the word in context

A total purist about pizza, her uncle refuses to eat any pie that isn't made with San Marzano tomatoes and wood-fired dough.

Grammar purists at the newspaper still insist on never ending a sentence with a preposition, even when the alternative sounds stiff and awkward.

Being someone who insists on strict, traditional correctness in a particular field is exactly what purist describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    perfectionist: A perfectionist demands flawless results in whatever they do and is never satisfied with 'good enough.' A purist cares less about flawlessness than about faithfulness: doing a thing the traditional, correct way. A perfectionist happily adopts a new tool that improves the result; a purist often refuses it on principle.

    pedant: A pedant fusses over small points of learning -- dates, spellings, technicalities -- mostly to display how much they know. A purist's strictness serves the craft itself, protecting the traditional way a thing is done, rather than serving the pedant's wish to look clever.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    innovator: a person who introduces new methods or ideas

    pragmatist: a person who deals with things practically rather than by strict rules

    Where it sits · how strictly the rules are enforced

    traditionalist → purist → stickler

    traditionalist vs. purist: Traditionalist is milder than purist: a traditionalist prefers the old ways, hand-stitching every button out of habit and affection, while a purist insists the traditional method is the only correct one and refuses every shortcut.

    stickler vs. purist: Stickler goes further than purist: a purist insists on traditional methods and forms, while a stickler enforces every rule so fiercely he calls a foul over a half-inch violation.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Calling someone a purist can be a compliment (dedicated, principled) or a mild criticism (rigid, resistant to reasonable change) depending on tone -- context usually makes clear which one is meant.
