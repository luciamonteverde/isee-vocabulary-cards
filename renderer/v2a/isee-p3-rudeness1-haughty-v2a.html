Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Haughty is scornfully proud and superior in manner, carrying yourself as if everyone else were beneath notice.

See the word in context

The haughty museum donor insisted her name appear before every other patron's on the new wing's plaque, no matter their contribution.

Even after losing the championship, the haughty team captain refused to congratulate the winners, insisting his team was still clearly the better one.

Haughty describes an overall attitude of superiority -- it's less about one dismissive glance and more about a lasting sense that you're simply above everyone else.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    arrogant: Arrogant means having an inflated sense of your own importance -- loudly certain you are smarter, better, more deserving. Haughty is the colder cousin: a haughty person may boast about nothing at all, and simply carries themselves as if everyone else were beneath notice.

    lofty: Lofty (a word taught elsewhere in this course) literally means high up, and it often praises -- lofty goals, lofty ideals. Applied to a manner it keeps a dignified distance, while haughty is plainly scornful: a haughty person doesn't just stand above others, they look down on them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    humble: modest, not thinking oneself superior

    unpretentious: simple and modest, without airs

    Where it sits · how far above others they act

    proud → haughty → overbearing

    proud vs. haughty: Proud is far milder than haughty -- pride in your own accomplishments can be perfectly healthy and doesn't require looking down on anyone else.

    overbearing vs. haughty: Overbearing goes a step further than haughty: a haughty person looks down on you, while an overbearing one also tries to run you, pressing their weight into every choice you make.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Haughty describes a person's whole bearing or personality -- a standing sense of superiority -- while its cousin supercilious (also in this unit) usually points to one scornful look or remark in the moment.
