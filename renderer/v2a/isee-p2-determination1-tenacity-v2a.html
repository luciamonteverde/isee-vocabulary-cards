tenacity

tuh-NASS-ih-tee -- stress on the second syllable, NASS.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Tenacity is the refusal to let go of a goal, keeping a firm grip on it however many setbacks and difficulties try to pry it loose.

See the word in context

It took real tenacity to rebuild the team after it lost every single game of its first season.

A scientist's experiment fails forty times, and each morning she returns to the lab with the same tenacity, adjusts one variable, and tries again.

That refusal to let go of a goal, failure after failure, is tenacity. Unlike someone who gives up at the first setback, a person with tenacity keeps a firm grip on what they are trying to achieve.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    determination: Determination is firmness of purpose -- the strength of the decision you make to go after something, visible even before any obstacle appears. Tenacity shows itself later, under pressure: it is the refusal to loosen your grip once difficulty starts trying to pry it loose.

    stubbornness: Stubbornness is refusing to change your course or admit a mistake even when the evidence is against you. Tenacity is a grip on a worthwhile goal, and it can flex: a tenacious person will happily change methods as long as the goal itself survives.

    stamina: Stamina is the physical or mental energy to keep working through long stretches of effort, like an athlete's staying power. Tenacity is not about energy but about attitude -- the refusal to let a goal be pried away, however many setbacks arrive.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    irresolution: inability to commit firmly to a course of action

    half-heartedness: a lack of real effort or enthusiasm

    Where it sits · how tightly the goal is gripped

    persistence → tenacity → doggedness

    persistence vs. tenacity: Persistence is the broader, more everyday word -- it covers any kind of keeping-at-it, while tenacity suggests a tighter, almost unbreakable grip on a goal.

    doggedness vs. tenacity: Doggedness can sound even more grimly stubborn than tenacity: tenacity often carries a hint of admirable spirit, while doggedness pictures someone grinding forward with their head down, refusing to stop no matter what.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Tenacity is almost always a compliment -- it praises the determination and effort behind long struggles. You will often see it with verbs like 'show,' 'require,' or 'take': 'the climb took remarkable tenacity.'
