What It Means

To engross is to completely hold one's attention.

See the word in context

She was so engrossed in the mystery book that she missed her bus stop entirely.

He was so engrossed in restoring the old motorcycle that he forgot to eat lunch.

Engross describes a mentally absorbing focus on a task or activity, without necessarily any emotional excitement attached.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    absorb: To absorb someone is to take up their whole attention -- a good book absorbs its reader for an afternoon. Engross is the same idea at full strength: to engross is to hold attention so completely that everything else drops away and goes unnoticed. Absorb is the everyday word; engross is the total version.

    preoccupy: To preoccupy is to sit at the front of someone's mind while they try to do other things -- a worry that preoccupies you follows you through the whole day. To engross is the opposite direction: the task in front of you swallows your attention so fully that nothing outside it can break in.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bore: make weary through dullness

    distract: draw the attention away from

    Where it sits · how completely attention is captured

    interest → engross → obsess

    interest vs. engross: Interesting someone is a mild, passing reaction; engrossing them means they lose track of everything else.

    obsess vs. engross: To obsess over something is to fixate on it uncontrollably, a step beyond simply being engrossed in it.

    Word Choice

    Engross doesn't imply any particular emotion -- you can be engrossed in a thrilling story or in dull paperwork, as long as it holds your full attention.
