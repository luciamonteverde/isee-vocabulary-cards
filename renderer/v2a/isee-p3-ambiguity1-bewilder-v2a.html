What It Means

To bewilder is to overwhelm someone with so much at once that they lose their bearings in complete, disorienting confusion.

See the word in context

The airport's last-minute gate changes bewildered even seasoned travelers who thought they knew the terminal well.

The substitute teacher's contradictory instructions bewildered the class, and half the students ended up doing the wrong assignment entirely.

Bewilder describes overwhelming, disorienting confusion -- not mild puzzlement, but a real loss of bearings.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flummox: To flummox someone is to stop them cold with one baffling thing -- a question or problem they simply cannot answer. To bewilder is broader and more disorienting: a bewildered person has lost their bearings entirely, overwhelmed by too much happening at once rather than stumped by a single point.

    disorient: To disorient someone is to strip away their sense of where they are -- in a building, a schedule, or a plan. Bewilder includes that lostness and adds mental overload: a bewildered person cannot make sense of what is happening around them, not just where they stand.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    orient: to help someone understand a new situation clearly

    guide: to show someone the way clearly and confidently

    Where it sits · how completely the confusion takes over

    puzzle → bewilder → dumbfound

    puzzle vs. bewilder: Puzzle is milder than bewilder: a tricky crossword clue may puzzle you for a minute before it clicks, but to be bewildered is to be completely lost in confusion.

    dumbfound vs. bewilder: Dumbfound goes beyond bewilder: a bewildered person is lost in confusion but still hunting for sense, while someone dumbfounded is so astonished they are left utterly speechless.
