minimal

MIN-uh-mul

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Minimal describes an amount that is the least there could be — as small as it can be and still be there at all, and often the least that will do: minimal damage, minimal effort, a minimal fee. The word is about how much there is, and it puts the amount at the very bottom of the range. It does not say whether that amount matters, it does not judge how serious anything is, and it does not point to a lowest limit set by a rule.

See the word in context

The moving crew worked with minimal noise, sliding each box onto a rug rather than dragging it across the floor.

The new library was built with minimal decoration — plain white walls, one bench, and not a single picture — so that nothing would pull a reader's eye from the books.

The decoration is minimal because it has been kept to the least there could be: the walls carry nothing but paint, and the one bench is the only furniture. Notice what the sentence does not say — not that decoration does not matter, not that there is too little of it, and not that some rule set a lowest limit. It simply measures how much there is and finds almost none. That is minimal.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slight: Slight describes a faint degree of something — a slight cold, a slight breeze, a slight change in the score — and it is about how little of it you feel or notice; a slight fever simply happens to you. Minimal describes an amount that has been kept, or has come out, at the least there could be: minimal effort, minimal damage. Ask whether the sentence describes a faint degree of something felt or noticed, or an amount sitting at the very bottom of what there could be.

    minor: Minor is about how serious or important something is — a minor injury is better in days, a minor role has few lines, a minor mistake costs nothing. It ranks the thing as not serious. Minimal is about how much there is: the amount is the least there could be. A minor problem can still take a lot of work to fix; a minimal problem is almost no problem at all. Ask whether the sentence is judging how serious or important something is, or measuring how much of it there is.

    minimum: Minimum names a fixed lowest limit — a number or a rule that nothing may fall below: the minimum age is sixteen, the minimum wage, a minimum of two players. Minimal describes an actual amount as very small, with no set limit in sight: the team won with minimal help from its star. Ask whether the sentence points to a lowest limit set in advance, or describes how little there actually was.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    maximum: the greatest amount possible or allowed

    considerable: large enough to be noticed and to matter

    Where it sits · how little there is

    modest → minimal → negligible

    modest vs. minimal: Modest is the first step down from large — an amount that is not big but is clearly there: a modest crowd of two hundred, a modest raise, a modest house with three rooms. Minimal goes all the way down to the least there could be. Ask whether the amount is simply on the smaller side, or as small as it could possibly be.

    negligible vs. minimal: Negligible goes one step further than minimal: the amount is so small it can be ignored — a negligible fee is not worth mentioning, a negligible risk is not worth a second thought. Minimal only says the amount is the least there could be; it may still matter a great deal, like the minimal dose of medicine that still saves a patient. Ask whether the sentence treats the amount as something to brush aside, or just as very small.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Minimal is a cool, measuring word, common wherever an amount is kept low on purpose: minimal risk, minimal cost, minimal effort, with minimal delay. It is neutral and often a good thing to hear — minimal damage after a storm is good news. Watch two relatives: minimize, to make as small as possible, and minimum, the lowest amount allowed, which the compare panel keeps apart from minimal because students mix them up. On the ISEE the word sits near slight and negligible; the difference this card teaches is that minimal measures an amount as the least there could be and says nothing else about it.
