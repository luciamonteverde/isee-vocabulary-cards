Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To have craved something is to have felt a hunger for it — a gnawing, insistent pull that would not quiet down until the thing was in hand. A person can crave food, but also sleep, quiet, attention, or praise; what marks the word is the force of the pull, as if a real appetite were demanding to be fed right now.

See the word in context

After three weeks of camp food, Malik craved his grandmother's rice so much that he could almost smell it from his bunk.

Home from the hospital with a broken leg, the marathon runner craved movement; by the second day she was doing laps of the hallway on her crutches just to feel her heart pound.

She does not simply miss running or wish she could jog again someday. Her body is pulling at her like an empty stomach, and it will not leave her alone until she moves — so she gets up and moves, crutches and all. That insistent, appetite-like pull toward something is what craved means. If she had only sat by the window wishing her leg would heal so she could run again in the spring, the gentler word would be longed for.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    desired: Desired is the calm, formal word for wishing to have or reach something, at any strength — a desired outcome, the desired size — and it can name a plan rather than a feeling. Craved is never calm: it names an urgent pull that feels like hunger. Ask whether the sentence could swap in "wished for" without losing anything; if so, the word is desired.

    coveted: Coveted means wanting what belongs to someone else — a classmate's award, a neighbor's car — and the word carries a touch of envy. Craved says nothing about who owns the thing; it is about the force of the pull. Ask whether the thing is someone else's.

    needed: Needed means the person could not do without it — food to live, sleep to function, glasses to read. A need can be felt calmly or not felt at all. Craved is about the feeling, and a craving can be for something the person does not need in the least, like a third slice of cake. Ask whether the person would actually be harmed without the thing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    loathed: hated so much that it turned the stomach

    spurned: pushed away with scorn when it was offered

    Where it sits · how urgent and bodily the wanting is

    wanted → longed for → craved

    wanted vs. craved: Wanted is the plain, all-purpose word, and it covers everything from a passing wish for a snack to a lifelong dream. Craved is the top of that range: wanting with the force of hunger, a pull the person cannot ignore. Ask whether the person would have shrugged if they did not get it.

    longed for vs. craved: Longed for is a deep, stretched-out ache for something far away or out of reach — home, a friend who moved, a summer that is over — and it is patient because it has to be. Craved is sharper and closer: a pull toward something that could be had now, and the person cannot rest until it is. Ask whether the thing is out of reach or within reach.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Craved is the past tense of crave, and the word keeps the flavor of its most common use: food. Even when the thing is attention, approval, or peace and quiet, craved says the person felt it in the body, like a growl in the stomach. It is stronger than wanted and more urgent than longed for, and it usually points at something the person could actually get, so it pairs with satisfying: a craving is fed, not just felt. The noun is craving.
