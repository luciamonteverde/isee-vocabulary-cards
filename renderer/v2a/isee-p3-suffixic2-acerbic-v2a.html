What It Means

Acerbic is a habitually sharp, bitter tone, temper, or taste; sharply critical, stinging through wit and precision rather than plain cruelty.

See the word in context

The film critic's acerbic review dismissed the blockbuster as 'two hours of explosions with the emotional depth of a parking ticket.'

Known for his acerbic wit, the talk-show host could reduce even the most confident guest to stammering silence with a single well-aimed remark.

Acerbic describes a sharp, biting quality -- whether in someone's words, humor, or even a taste -- that stings rather than soothes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    caustic: Caustic criticism is corrosive -- it is meant to burn, and cruelty is part of the effect. Acerbic stings too, but through wit and precision: an acerbic line entertains even as it bites, where a caustic one only wounds.

    biting: Biting describes the sharp, cutting force of a single remark or gust of wind -- the immediate sting itself. Acerbic reaches wider: it names a whole tone, temper, or wit that is habitually sharp, the standing flavor of a person's speech rather than one moment's bite.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gentle: mild and kind

    diplomatic: tactful, avoiding giving offense

    Where it sits · how much sting the words carry

    blunt → acerbic → vitriolic

    blunt vs. acerbic: Blunt describes plainly honest speech, without necessarily being meant to wound the way acerbic can.

    vitriolic vs. acerbic: Vitriolic describes speech so venomously bitter it goes even further than acerbic's sharp bite.
