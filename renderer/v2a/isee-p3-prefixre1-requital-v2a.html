requital

rih-KWY-tul

Meaning

What It Means

Requital is something given in return for a favor, service, or wrong -- a repayment or a retaliation.

See the word in context

When the small country finally received foreign aid after decades of ignored pleas, commentators called it a small requital for years of diplomatic neglect.

After the veteran defenseman spent three seasons mentoring rookies from the bench, his teammates saw the championship ring as fitting requital for his patience.

Requital here means a fitting return -- something earned back after time spent giving without reward.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    repayment: A repayment settles a debt: you give back what you owed, usually money, and the account closes. Requital is broader and older-flavored -- the return a deed earns, which can be a reward for kindness or a punishment for a wrong, and it need not involve money at all.

    recompense: Recompense is a payment or reward that makes up for someone's effort, loss, or trouble -- it points in a kindly direction. Requital is two-sided: it is whatever a deed has earned in return, so a betrayal can meet its requital just as faithful service can.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ingratitude: failure to show thanks or repay a kindness

    neglect: failure to give someone what they are due

    Where it sits · how heavily a deed is repaid

    thanks → requital → vengeance

    thanks vs. requital: Thanks costs nothing but words, so it sits far below requital, which actually delivers what a deed has earned -- whether reward or penalty.

    vengeance vs. requital: Vengeance goes beyond requital: a requital repays a deed in fair measure, while vengeance answers a wrong with violent excess.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Requital can describe a reward or a punishment -- whichever a person is owed in return. Compare it with reprisal, which is narrower: a reprisal is always retaliation for harm, never a reward.
