Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Opprobrium is the harsh, widespread public disgrace that settles on a wrongdoer after shameful conduct, along with the storm of criticism that carries it.

See the word in context

The company faced widespread opprobrium after the safety cover-up came to light.

The senator resigned amid public opprobrium after the scandal broke on every news channel.

Harsh, widespread public disgrace, not just private disappointment, is exactly what opprobrium describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disgrace: Disgrace is the fallen state itself -- the loss of respect that follows shameful conduct, and it can be quiet and local, felt within one family or one office. Opprobrium is that fall on a public scale: harsh, widespread disgrace that settles on the wrongdoer, carried by a storm of criticism raining down.

    condemnation: Condemnation is strong disapproval spoken aloud -- statements, editorials, votes of protest; it names what people say. Opprobrium goes further than words: it is the public disgrace that settles on the wrongdoer, the shame that makes others keep their distance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    acclaim: enthusiastic public approval

    honor: public respect and esteem

    Where it sits · how public and lasting the disgrace

    criticism → opprobrium → infamy

    criticism vs. opprobrium: Criticism can be private or public, mild or strong; opprobrium is specifically harsh, public disgrace.

    infamy vs. opprobrium: Infamy goes even further than opprobrium, implying the shameful reputation becomes lasting and widely known.
