Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cautious means slowing down and holding back on purpose because you can see a chance of harm or a costly mistake ahead — reading every line before you sign, keeping well away from a strange dog, slowing down on a wet road — and choosing to check first rather than take the chance.

See the word in context

Cautious about the old rope bridge, Nadia pulled hard on the ropes at each side and then stepped onto one plank at a time.

Before lighting the grill on the dry slope above the lake, Theo cleared away the brown grass, set a bucket of water beside him, and kept the flame low — easily the most cautious cook at the picnic.

Theo can see a real chance of harm — one spark in dry grass could start a fire — and he answers it by holding back: clearing space, keeping water ready, keeping the flame small. He still cooks; he simply refuses to take the chance without checking first. That pairing of a seen risk with slowing down on purpose is cautious. If he had been too scared to light the grill at all, the word would be fearful.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wary: Wary is being on guard: your eyes are on a particular person, offer, or place that you suspect might turn out badly, and you keep your guard up. Cautious is the way you act when a risk is in view — slowly, with checks — and it needs no suspicion at all; a cautious driver in the rain does not suspect the road of anything. Ask whether the sentence is about watching a suspected threat or about doing something risky slowly and safely.

    hesitant: Hesitant means hanging back because you are unsure or unwilling — you hang back, and there may be no danger anywhere, only doubt. Cautious holds back for a reason you could name, a chance of harm or a mistake, and it usually goes ahead once the check is done. Ask whether the person is holding back from a risk or simply cannot make up their mind.

    prudent: Prudent praises the choice itself: a prudent decision is the sensible one that looks ahead to what could happen later, and it need not involve moving slowly at all — putting money aside for repairs is prudent. Cautious describes the manner of acting when harm is possible: slow, guarded, checking as you go. Ask whether the sentence is about a wise plan for the future or about a careful way of doing something risky.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reckless: taking a risk without a thought for what could go wrong

    rash: acting fast on impulse, before the danger has been weighed

    Where it sits · how far a person pulls back when there is a risk

    careful → cautious → fearful

    careful vs. cautious: Careful is the lighter of the two — attention to doing something right, which can be as small as coloring inside the lines, with nothing at stake. Cautious pulls back further: there is a real chance of harm, so the person slows down and checks before going on. Ask whether the sentence has a danger in it or only a task done well.

    fearful vs. cautious: Fearful is the strongest of the three — so afraid that the person will not act at all, and often the fear is far bigger than the danger. Cautious weighs a real risk and then goes ahead, slowly and with checks. Ask whether the person still does the thing or backs away from it entirely.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Cautious is a mild compliment — a cautious driver, a cautious investor, a cautious first step — and it describes behavior in the face of a risk, not a weakness of character. Say cautious about or cautious of something. The noun is caution, and cautiously means in a cautious way. Because the word always has a possible harm in view, it sounds odd when nothing could go wrong: a tidy desk is careful work, not cautious work.
