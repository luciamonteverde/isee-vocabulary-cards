What It Means

To aggrandize is to magnify someone's reputation, power, or importance beyond the truth.

See the word in context

The retired general spent his later years trying to aggrandize his own role in the campaign.

Every story in the memoir seemed engineered to aggrandize the author at the expense of his old colleagues.

The stories weren't simply proud -- they were specifically shaped to inflate his own importance beyond what actually happened, which is exactly aggrandize.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    magnify: To magnify something is to make it look bigger, stronger, or more serious than it really is — you magnify a problem, a risk, or a detail. It works on things and facts. Aggrandize is narrower: it puffs up a person's reputation, power, or role, usually the speaker's own.

    inflate: To inflate something is to pump a figure or claim up beyond its true size — inflated numbers, an inflated bill, inflated praise. It needs something measurable to swell. Aggrandize aims at a person instead: it swells someone's reputation, power, or standing rather than a quantity.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    diminish: make something seem smaller or less important

    Where it sits · how much the truth gets inflated

    brag → aggrandize → glorify

    brag vs. aggrandize: Bragging is simply talking proudly about oneself; aggrandizing specifically warps the truth to seem more important.

    glorify vs. aggrandize: Glorify goes a step beyond aggrandize: aggrandizing inflates a person's rank, role, or importance, while glorifying dresses its subject up as wholly splendid and heroic — and it usually praises someone or something else, not oneself.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Aggrandize almost always describes someone inflating their own reputation or role, often phrased as "self-aggrandizing," distinct from this unit's "dramatize," which exaggerates the importance of an event rather than a person.
