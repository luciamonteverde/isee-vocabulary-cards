What It Means

A plethora is an oversized amount facing you all at once -- more than anyone actually needs or can use.

See the word in context

The festival offered a plethora of food stalls -- more than any visitor could sample in a weekend.

Searching for one simple phone case, the shopper scrolls past a plethora of results -- four thousand of them -- and after an hour she gives up without choosing any.

Four thousand options for one small purchase is an amount so large it overwhelms. A plethora is an oversized amount facing you all at once, more than anyone actually needs or can use.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    surplus: A surplus is what remains after every need has been met -- extra bushels once the granary is full, money left when the bills are paid. A plethora is an oversized amount facing you all at once, more than anyone can use. A surplus sits quietly left over; a plethora overwhelms.

    glut: A glut is a market-wide oversupply: so much of one product, across so many sellers, that buyers cannot absorb it and prices sink. A plethora is any oversized amount of anything -- choices, excuses, apps. A glut is a whole market drowning in a product; a plethora is one overwhelming heap.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    shortage: an amount less than what is needed

    dearth: a serious scarcity or shortage

    Where it sits · how far past enough it goes

    abundance → plethora → excess

    abundance vs. plethora: Abundance is warmer and weaker than plethora -- an abundance is happily plenty, while a plethora tips past plenty toward more than anyone can use.

    excess vs. plethora: Excess is more disapproving than plethora: a plethora of choices may merely overwhelm, while an excess of anything has clearly crossed the line into too much.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Plethora takes 'of': a plethora of options, a plethora of excuses, a plethora of apps. It means an overabundance -- more than anyone actually needs or can use. Treat it as singular: 'there is a plethora of reasons,' not 'there are.'
