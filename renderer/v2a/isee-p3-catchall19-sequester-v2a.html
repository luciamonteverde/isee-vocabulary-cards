sequester

sih-KWES-ter

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To sequester is to set apart and hold by authority: shutting a person away from others or from outside influence, or holding disputed property in safekeeping until a matter is resolved.

See the word in context

During the outbreak, health officials moved to sequester anyone showing symptoms in a separate wing of the hospital.

To protect the funds during the lawsuit, the court ordered the disputed inheritance sequestered in a separate account until a judge could decide who it belonged to.

Setting something apart and holding it in safekeeping (or under legal control) until a matter is resolved is exactly what sequester describes -- one of its two closely related senses, along with keeping a person isolated from outside influence.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    confiscate: To confiscate is to seize someone's property by legal authority -- a phone, a shipment, a fake ID -- and the point is taking it away from them. To sequester is to set apart and hold: a court sequesters disputed funds to keep them safe and untouched, not to claim them.

    seclude: Seclude means to tuck yourself away somewhere quiet and private, and it is usually chosen -- a writer secludes herself in a cabin to finish a book. Sequester is imposed from outside by an authority or a formal procedure, and it happens whether the person wants it or not.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    release: to set free or let go

    distribute: to give out to multiple people

    Where it sits · how completely cut off it is

    separate → sequester → isolate

    separate vs. sequester: Separate is weaker than sequester: roommates can be separated into different rooms for an afternoon, while to sequester someone is to shut them away completely, like a jury cut off from the world.

    isolate vs. sequester: Isolate is stronger than sequester: a sequestered patient is kept apart from others, but a fully isolated one is sealed off entirely, with no visitors allowed at all.

    Word Choice

    Sequester carries two related senses -- setting a person apart from outside influence (like a sequestered jury) and formally seizing property or funds (like sequestered assets) -- context makes clear which one is meant.
