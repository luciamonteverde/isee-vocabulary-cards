stratum

STRAT-um

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A stratum is one level in a structure whose levels stand one above another in order of standing — most often a society, where a stratum is all the people who share roughly the same wealth, schooling and position, with a level above them and a level below.

See the word in context

In the old kingdom the merchants formed a stratum of their own, richer than the farmers but never allowed to marry into noble families.

The survey divided the city into five strata by income, and the researchers found that children in the lowest stratum changed schools twice as often as those in the highest.

Each stratum here is one level in a ranking: the five income groups sit one above another, and the lowest is a whole group of people, not one person's position. That is what makes stratum the right word rather than a word for any single sheet of something, or for where one person stands. A stratum is defined by where the level sits — above some, below others — and by the people who share it.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    class: Class is the everyday word for a group of people at about the same social level — the working class, the middle class — and it also means a school group or a kind of thing, like a class of medicines. It names the group by what its members are. Stratum names a position: one level in a ranked stack, and the stack can be a company, an army or a city as well as a whole society. Ask whether the sentence names a group of people as a kind, or points at one level sitting above some and below others.

    rank: Rank is one person's position on a scale of authority — sergeant, captain, general. It belongs to an individual and it comes with a title. A stratum is a whole level made of many people at once, and nobody is given a stratum as a title. Ask whether the sentence points at where one person stands, or at an entire layer of people.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    whole: the entire structure taken together, not one level of it

    individual: a single person, rather than a whole group at one level

    Where it sits · how firmly the level fixes a person's standing

    layer → tier → stratum → caste

    layer vs. stratum: Layer is the plain, all-purpose word — one sheet of anything lying on top of another: frosting on a cake, paint on a wall, a layer of clouds. Nothing is higher in standing; the layers are only stacked. A stratum is a layer in an order of standing, where higher means higher position. Ask whether the sentence only shows things piled in order, or shows a level that stands above the ones below it.

    tier vs. stratum: Tier is a step up from layer — tiers are stacked levels that are numbered or graded: the top tier of a stadium, a top-tier team, the middle tier of a phone plan. The word grades things, teams and prices. A stratum grades people by their standing in a society or an organization. Ask whether the levels hold things or plans, or hold groups of people ranked by standing.

    caste vs. stratum: Caste goes beyond stratum — a caste is a level of society that a person is born into and cannot leave, with rules about whom they may marry and what work they may do. A stratum can be climbed out of; people move between strata all the time. Ask whether the sentence shows a level fixed for life by birth, or one a person can rise above or fall below.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Stratum is a formal, somewhat scientific word. Its plural is strata, and you will see the plural more often: 'every stratum of society' but 'all strata of society.' The word began in geology, where a stratum is one layer of rock or soil laid down on top of the last, and geologists still use it that way; this card teaches the social sense, where the layers are groups of people ranked by wealth, schooling or standing. Either way the picture is the same — flat layers stacked in order. Compare hierarchy, taught in this same unit: a hierarchy is the whole ranked system from top to bottom; a stratum is one level within it.
