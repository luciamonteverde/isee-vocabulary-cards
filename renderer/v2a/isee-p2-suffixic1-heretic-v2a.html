What It Means

Heretic is a person within a faith who is formally condemned by its authorities because his or her beliefs strike at its established doctrine.

See the word in context

In 1633, the astronomer was tried as a heretic for defending the idea that the Earth moves around the Sun.

In the history reading, a sixteenth-century printer is hauled before a tribunal as a heretic for publishing pamphlets that contradict the church's official teachings.

He holds and spreads beliefs that strike at the established doctrine of his own faith, and its authorities formally condemn him for it. The word names a person -- usually one who believes deeply, just not what the officials say he must.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nonconformist: A nonconformist refuses to follow accepted customs -- dressing, living, or thinking differently from the crowd, in any walk of life, with no court involved. A heretic is sharper and more specific: a member of a faith formally condemned because a belief strikes at official doctrine.

    freethinker: A freethinker works out beliefs by reason instead of accepting an authority's teaching, and the name is usually worn with pride. Heretic is a label the authorities stamp on someone: it marks a believer inside a faith whose convictions clash with official doctrine, and it invites punishment.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    believer: one who accepts and holds a faith or doctrine

    conformist: one who follows established beliefs and customs

    Where it sits · how deep the break with official teaching

    dissenter → heretic → apostate

    dissenter vs. heretic: Dissenter is milder and broader than heretic -- a dissenter disagrees with an official position on some point, while a heretic is formally condemned for beliefs that strike at the established doctrine itself.

    apostate vs. heretic: Apostate goes a step beyond heretic in the eyes of the authorities: a heretic believes the 'wrong' version of the faith, while an apostate has abandoned the faith altogether and walked away from it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Heretic is a noun for a person: someone is 'branded a heretic' or 'tried as a heretic.' The matching adjective is 'heretical' -- heretical ideas, never 'heretic ideas.' Keep it distinct from atheist (a person who believes in no higher power at all): a heretic usually believes deeply, just differently from the official doctrine.
