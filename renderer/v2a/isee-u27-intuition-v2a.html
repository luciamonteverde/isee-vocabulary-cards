Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Intuition is the ability to know or understand something at once, as a feeling that arrives whole, without working through any steps or being able to point to the evidence — a sense built up from years of experience rather than one you were born with.

See the word in context

After twenty years of driving the same school bus route, Mr. Ferreira had an intuition that the silent boy in the back row was upset about something, and he found an excuse to stop and chat.

The pastry chef could not have said what was wrong with the batter, but her intuition told her to add one more egg, and the cake rose perfectly.

The chef does not measure anything or follow a rule in the recipe; the knowing simply arrives, and it arrives because she has mixed thousands of bowls of batter before. That is intuition: sure, immediate, and impossible to explain step by step, yet resting on long experience. A newborn could not have it, so it is not instinct; she could not have written out her reasons, so it is not reasoning; and she was confident and right, which is not what a guess feels like.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    instinct: Instinct is a built-in urge or reaction a creature is born with — a baby duck swimming the first time it touches water, a hand jerking back from a hot pan. It needs no experience at all and usually moves the body. Intuition is learned the slow way: years of noticing add up until the mind knows something at once. The ISEE accepts instinct as a correct answer for intuition, so when you must choose between them, ask whether a newborn could have it and whether it moves the body or informs a judgment.

    insight: Insight is a deep, clear understanding of why something is the way it is — the understanding itself, however it was reached, whether by hours of study or in a flash. Intuition is about how a piece of knowing arrives: at once, with no steps to show. Ask whether the sentence praises how deep the understanding goes or how it came without anything being worked out.

    perception: Perception is noticing what is in front of you — a glance, a pause, a change in someone's voice — and reading what it means; the clue is there and could be pointed to afterward. Intuition arrives with no clue the person could name. Ask whether the person could say what they noticed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    analysis: taking a problem apart piece by piece and studying each part before deciding

    proof: evidence laid out so plainly that anyone else can check the answer

    Where it sits · how much a person could explain about how they reached a conclusion

    guess → intuition → reasoning

    guess vs. intuition: A guess is a pick made with nothing behind it — flip a coin and you would do about as well — and the person guessing knows it may be wrong. Intuition feels like knowing, and it has years of experience packed behind it even though none of that can be laid out. Ask whether the person would be surprised to turn out right.

    reasoning vs. intuition: Reasoning reaches a conclusion by steps — first this, then that, therefore — that could be written down and checked by someone else. Intuition skips the steps entirely and lands on the answer. Ask whether the person could show their work.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Intuition is a noun for both the ability and a single moment of it: 'she has good intuition' and 'I had an intuition that something was wrong.' The everyday phrase for the same thing is a gut feeling. It is less a compliment to how clever someone is than to their experience — the people we credit with intuition are usually the ones who have seen a thing a thousand times. The ISEE keys intuition to instinct, so on the test treat the two as a match while keeping the difference this card teaches in mind. The adjective is intuitive, which this course teaches elsewhere for a design anyone can use correctly on the first try — the same idea of understanding that arrives without instructions.
