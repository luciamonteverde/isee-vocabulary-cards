filament

FIL-uh-munt -- Latin filum, 'thread': a filament is a fine thread of something.

Meaning

What It Means

Filament is a single, especially fine and delicate threadlike object.

See the word in context

Inside the old light bulb, a tiny tungsten filament glowed white-hot.

Under a microscope, a single filament of spider silk looks like a shining wire thinner than a human hair, yet strong enough to hold a struggling insect.

That fine, threadlike strand is precisely what makes it a filament. Unlike 'cable,' which bundles many strands into something thick, a filament is a single delicate thread.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    strand: A strand is the everyday word for any single thin length -- a strand of hair, of spaghetti, of yarn. A filament is the precise, technical cousin: an especially fine, delicate thread, the word a scientist or an engineer reaches for.

    fiber: Fiber names the threadlike material things are made of -- cotton fiber, muscle fiber, fiber in food. A filament is not the material but one specific slender thread of it, like the single glowing wire inside a bulb.

    wire: A wire is a thin length of metal, stiff enough to hold its shape and carry a current. A filament need not be metal at all -- silk, glass, or plastic can form one -- and the word stresses how fine and threadlike it is.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cable: a thick bundle of wires or rope

    slab: a thick, flat block of solid material

    Where it sits · how fine and specific the thread is

    thread → filament → gossamer

    thread vs. filament: A sewing thread is far less fine than a filament -- it is spun thick enough to handle and stitch -- while a filament is a more delicate, hair-thin line of material, like the wire inside a bulb.

    gossamer vs. filament: Gossamer goes further than a filament on the same scale: a filament is a fine thread you can still see and handle, while gossamer is so fine it drifts on the air, like spider silk catching the light.
