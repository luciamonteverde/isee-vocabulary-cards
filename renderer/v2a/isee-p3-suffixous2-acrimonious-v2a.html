acrimonious

ak-rih-MOH-nee-uhs

What It Means

Acrimonious means marked by open bitterness, anger, and ill will that boils out in speech.

See the word in context

The neighbors' acrimonious feud over the shared fence eventually landed in small claims court.

What began as a friendly rivalry between the two chess grandmasters turned acrimonious after one accused the other of cheating.

The relationship didn't just cool off -- it became openly bitter and hostile, which is the sharper edge that acrimonious adds beyond simple disagreement.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bitter: Bitter names the feeling itself -- the resentment a person carries inside after being hurt or wronged. Acrimonious describes how a dispute sounds from the outside: openly angry, sharp-tongued, full of ill will. One person can be silently bitter; it takes an open clash to be acrimonious.

    rancorous: Rancorous points to deep-seated resentment -- a grudge nursed so long it colors everything a person does. Acrimonious needs no such history: a dispute between friendly partners can turn acrimonious in a single afternoon. Rancorous measures how old and deep the ill will runs; acrimonious measures how openly it boils out.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cordial: warm and friendly

    amicable: characterized by friendliness and absence of conflict

    Where it sits · how bitter the exchange turns

    tense → acrimonious → vitriolic

    tense vs. acrimonious: Tense is weaker than acrimonious: a tense meeting is merely strained and uneasy, while an acrimonious one boils over into open bitterness and angry words.

    vitriolic vs. acrimonious: Vitriolic is harsher than acrimonious: an acrimonious dispute is bitter and angry, but vitriolic attacks go further, dripping with venomous, scathing contempt.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Acrimonious is almost always used for disputes, breakups, or negotiations -- reserve it for conflicts that have turned openly bitter, not simple disagreements.
