What It Means

Tenuous means so thin that it barely exists, as of a connection, argument, or claim hanging by a single thread.

See the word in context

The prosecution's case rested on a tenuous chain of coincidences rather than any direct evidence.

His claim to the family fortune was tenuous at best, based on a rumor about a great-great-uncle no one could confirm existed.

Tenuous fits a connection, argument, or claim so thin that it could fall apart under the slightest scrutiny.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flimsy: Flimsy describes something weakly made that fails under the slightest pressure -- a flimsy excuse, a flimsy cardboard shelf. Tenuous is about thinness rather than poor construction: a tenuous link or claim is stretched so thin it barely exists, even before anyone pushes on it.

    shaky: Shaky means unstable -- a shaky ladder or a shaky alliance wobbles and looks likely to give way. Tenuous points at how little substance something has, not how unsteady it is: a tenuous connection may sit quietly for years, yet it is only a single thin thread.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    solid: strong, firm, and dependable

    unshakable: so strong and firm that it cannot be disrupted

    Where it sits · how little evidence holds it up

    uncertain → tenuous → baseless

    uncertain vs. tenuous: Uncertain is milder than tenuous: uncertain plans may still firm up once the tickets are booked, but a tenuous connection is already so thin it could snap.

    baseless vs. tenuous: Baseless goes even further than tenuous: a tenuous claim still hangs by one thin thread of evidence, while a baseless claim has nothing under it at all.
