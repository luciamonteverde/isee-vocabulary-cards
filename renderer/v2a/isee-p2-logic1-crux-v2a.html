What It Means

The crux is the single decisive point on which the whole matter turns.

See the word in context

The crux of the argument was not the cost of the repairs but who had promised to pay for them.

The town meeting runs three hours -- parking spaces, paint colors, the shape of the new sign -- but every road leads back to the crux of it all: is there enough money to keep the library open past six?

The crux is the one point everything else depends on -- not merely an important detail, but the place where the whole issue is settled.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    heart: The heart of a matter is its central, most human part -- what the whole thing is really about. The crux is sharper: the single point on which the outcome actually turns. A meeting can circle the heart of an issue for hours and only end when someone names the crux.

    essence: Essence is what something fundamentally is -- the core nature that makes it itself. A crux is not what a dispute is but the one point that will decide it. Describe an argument and you have given its essence; settle it and you have found the crux.

    kernel: A kernel is the small, solid center inside a bigger, messier issue -- what is left when the padding is stripped away. The crux adds decisiveness: it is the point where the matter is won or lost, not merely its compact middle.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sideshow: a minor distraction away from the main issue

    footnote: a small remark of little importance

    Where it sits · how much hangs on that point

    gist → crux → linchpin

    gist vs. crux: Gist is the gentler word -- the gist is just the general idea of what an argument is about, while the crux is the one decisive point the whole argument turns on.

    linchpin vs. crux: Linchpin goes further than crux: the crux is the decisive point the whole debate turns on, but a linchpin is the single piece holding the entire plan together -- pull it out and everything collapses.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Crux almost always arrives in the same frame: the crux of the matter, the crux of the argument, the crux of the problem. Use it for the single point on which everything turns, not for any old important detail -- an issue can have many important points but only one crux.
