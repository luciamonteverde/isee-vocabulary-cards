shuh-GRIN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Chagrin is the sharp, uncomfortable sting you feel when you fail at something or are proved wrong — especially after you expected to do well, or said out loud that you would. It is part irritation at yourself and part wishing that no one had been there to see it. The failure is usually small, but it lands on your pride, because you had counted on getting it right.

See the word in context

Much to his chagrin, the fast route Devon had bragged about all morning led the whole carpool straight to a closed bridge.

Priya had spent the ride to the museum correcting her little cousin on how to say the dinosaur names, so when the guide said one of them the cousin's way, her cheeks went hot with chagrin.

Priya has not been hurt or insulted, and nothing terrible has happened. She set herself up as the expert, and then, in front of a small audience, she turned out to be the one who was wrong. That mix — annoyed with herself, wishing she had kept quiet, wanting to disappear for a moment — is chagrin. If she had simply dropped her ticket in a puddle, she might be embarrassed but not chagrined, because she would not have failed at anything she had claimed to be good at.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    distress: Distress is the broad word for being upset — worried, frightened, or in pain — and it can come from anything at all: a lost pet, a scary diagnosis, money trouble. Chagrin is one narrow, milder kind: the sting of your own flop, of having been wrong after expecting to be right. Ask whether the trouble came from outside the person or from their own mistake.

    embarrassment: Embarrassment is the hot, self-conscious feeling of being looked at awkwardly, and it needs no failure at all — you can be embarrassed by a compliment, a hiccup, or a relative's loud singing. Chagrin requires that you got something wrong or fell short, usually after being confident about it. Ask whether the person actually failed at something, or just felt eyes on them.

    annoyance: Annoyance is irritation at something outside you — a buzzing fly, a slow line, a friend who is late — and it comes with no red face. The irritation in chagrin is aimed at yourself, and it comes with the wish that no one had seen. Ask whether the person is bothered by something else or by their own mistake.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    satisfaction: the pleased feeling of having got something right

    triumph: the glow of winning, or of being proved right after all

    Where it sits · how badly a failure wounds a person's pride

    disappointment → chagrin → humiliation

    disappointment vs. chagrin: Disappointment is the letdown of hoping for something and not getting it — rain on the picnic, a gift that was not the one you wanted — and the person's pride is untouched, because they did nothing wrong. Chagrin adds the sting of having been wrong or having fallen short yourself, after you expected better. Ask whether the person merely lost out or actually got something wrong.

    humiliation vs. chagrin: Humiliation is far heavier: a person's pride is crushed in front of others, often on purpose by someone else, and it can take a long time to recover from. Chagrin is a sting, not a wound — small enough that a laugh at yourself can fix it. Ask whether the person was made to feel worthless or simply caught out in a mistake.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Chagrin is a noun and almost always arrives in the phrase "to my chagrin" or "much to her chagrin," announcing that things did not go the way the person expected. The adjective is chagrined: a chagrined coach, a chagrined little smile. It is a fairly formal, slightly dry word, often used with a touch of humor about small failures rather than about real tragedy. On the ISEE the closest one-word match is usually distress, but remember what makes chagrin special: the trouble is your own mistake, and your pride took the hit.
