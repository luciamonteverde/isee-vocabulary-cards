malevolent

muh-LEV-uh-lunt -- four syllables, stress on the second syllable, LEV.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Malevolent is having a wish to do evil to others.

See the word in context

The malevolent stepmother in the tale smiled sweetly in public while plotting the heroine's downfall.

The chess club's malevolent rival doesn't just want to win -- he hides the other team's clocks, spreads rumors about their coach, and grins whenever one of them stumbles.

A rival who actively wants bad things to happen to others is malevolent. Malevolent describes the wish or intent to do evil -- the ill will itself, whether or not any harm has actually been done yet.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    malicious: Malicious means intending to cause harm in a particular act -- a malicious rumor, a malicious prank, each one built to hurt. Malevolent sits beneath such acts: it names the standing wish for evil itself, which can smolder in a person who never acts at all.

    vindictive: Vindictive means driven to pay back a wrong -- all the ill will aimed at the one person who caused the injury. Malevolent is broader and needs no injury at all: a malevolent character simply wishes harm on others, provoked or not.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    benevolent: wishing to do good to others

    kindhearted: warm and caring toward others

    Where it sits · how deep the wish to harm runs

    spiteful → malevolent → murderous

    spiteful vs. malevolent: Spiteful is smaller and pettier than malevolent -- a spiteful remark wants to sting once, while a malevolent will wishes lasting evil on its target.

    murderous vs. malevolent: Murderous goes further than malevolent: a malevolent rival wishes you ill, while a murderous hatred wants nothing less than a person's destruction -- the wish to harm at its absolute limit.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Malevolent describes intent, not results: a malevolent glare, a malevolent scheme, malevolent forces in a story. Someone can be malevolent without ever succeeding at harm -- the wish alone earns the word. For harm actually done, reach for 'destructive' or 'harmful' instead.
