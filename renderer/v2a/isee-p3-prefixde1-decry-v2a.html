Hear It

What It Means

To decry is to publicly and forcefully condemn something.

See the word in context

Human rights activists decried the company's use of child labor overseas, calling on shoppers to boycott its products.

Newspaper columnists decried the city's decision to demolish the historic library, arguing that no parking garage was worth the loss.

The columnists weren't mocking anyone personally -- they were publicly condemning a decision, which is exactly what decry describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    denounce: To denounce is to publicly declare something or someone wrong or guilty, often in a formal accusation that names a specific target. Decry is aimed less at a culprit and more at a practice, policy, or trend -- you decry rising rents; you denounce the official who took the bribe.

    condemn: To condemn is to express strong disapproval of something, and it can happen anywhere -- in private, in a mutter, in an official ruling. Decry is narrower: the condemnation is public and forceful by definition, made out loud for others to hear.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    applaud: to express strong approval of

    endorse: to publicly declare support for

    Where it sits · how fierce the disapproval grows

    question → decry → excoriate

    question vs. decry: To question something is to express mild doubt about it, gentler than decry.

    excoriate vs. decry: To excoriate something is to criticize it with harsh, scathing severity, even stronger than decry.

    Word Choice

    Decry is a formal word for publicly condemning something, often a policy, practice, or injustice -- compare it to deride, which mocks a person or idea with scorn, and denigrate, which unfairly attacks someone's character.
