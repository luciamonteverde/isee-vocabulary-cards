imminent

IM-ih-nent — starts like 'immediate'; an imminent event is about to happen immediately.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Imminent is about to happen very soon.

See the word in context

Forecasters warned residents that a dangerous flood was imminent along the riverbanks.

A hiker notices the sky darkening rapidly, hears distant thunder growing louder every minute, and feels the wind suddenly pick up speed. The storm is imminent — she has only minutes before it actually arrives.

The storm is not a distant maybe; it is about to break at any moment. Unlike 'possible,' which just means something could happen eventually, imminent means it is right on the verge of happening now.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impending: Impending means approaching and already hanging over you, often with a sense of threat: an impending deadline weighs on the mind days ahead. Imminent is tighter about timing; an imminent event is about to happen at any moment, not merely drawing closer.

    forthcoming: Forthcoming describes something expected soon, usually because it is already scheduled or announced: a forthcoming report, album, or election. Imminent carries urgency instead of a calendar; it means the event is right on the verge of happening now.

    looming: Looming describes something large or threatening that seems to rise into view as it draws near: a looming storm, a looming exam. Imminent is purely about time; an imminent event is moments away, whether or not it feels big or frightening.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    distant: far off in time or space

    remote: unlikely or far from happening soon

    Where it sits · how near and sure it is

    possible → imminent → inevitable

    possible vs. imminent: Possible is much weaker than imminent — something possible might happen eventually, while something imminent is about to happen at any moment.

    inevitable vs. imminent: Inevitable can feel stronger than imminent: imminent describes timing — happening very soon — while inevitable means it is certain to happen no matter what, regardless of when.
