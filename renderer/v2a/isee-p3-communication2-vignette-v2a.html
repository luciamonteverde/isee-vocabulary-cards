What It Means

A vignette is a brief, carefully composed scene or story that vividly captures a mood or moment.

See the word in context

The novel opens with a quiet vignette of a boy watching the first snow fall outside his grandmother's kitchen window.

The documentary opens with a quiet vignette of an old fisherman mending his nets at dawn.

The scene is short and doesn't advance a full plot, but it captures a mood and a moment vividly -- exactly what defines a vignette.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sketch: A sketch is a short, simple depiction -- a few quick strokes that outline a scene or character without much finish. A vignette is just as brief but far more polished: it is carefully composed for atmosphere. A sketch is rough and fast; a vignette is small and finished.

    snapshot: A snapshot is a quick, unposed glimpse -- a moment caught in passing, like a photo grabbed from a moving car. A vignette is composed, not caught: the writer arranges the scene deliberately for its atmosphere. A snapshot happens by luck; a vignette is built with care.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    epic: a long, sweeping story covering great events

    Where it sits · how crafted and developed the story

    anecdote → vignette → novel

    anecdote vs. vignette: An anecdote is usually a spoken, casual little story; a vignette is more deliberately crafted and often written, aiming for a vivid mood.

    novel vs. vignette: A novel takes the idea of storytelling far beyond a vignette, developing full plots and characters across many chapters rather than one brief scene.
