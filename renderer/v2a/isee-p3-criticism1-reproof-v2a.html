What It Means

Reproof is an expression of blame or disapproval.

See the word in context

A single reproof from the coach was enough to make the rookie change his attitude.

The gentle reproof in her father's voice was enough to make her put the phone away at the table.

A mild, spoken expression of disapproval -- not a shout or punishment -- is exactly what a reproof is.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    admonishment: An admonishment is a firm warning aimed at what might happen next -- 'don't let it happen again.' A reproof looks backward instead: it expresses blame or disappointment over what was already done, often in the mildest, quietest of words.

    correction: A correction simply fixes a mistake -- it points out the error and supplies the right answer, with no feeling attached. A reproof carries feeling: it tells you, however gently, that your conduct disappointed someone, not merely that you got something wrong.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: an expression of approval

    commendation: formal praise

    Where it sits · how strong the disapproval is

    comment → reproof → rebuke

    comment vs. reproof: A comment can be neutral; a reproof is specifically an expression of disapproval or blame.

    rebuke vs. reproof: A rebuke is sharper and more direct than a reproof, often delivered bluntly and without softening.
