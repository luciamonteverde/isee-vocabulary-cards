procure

proh-KYOOR -- shares a root with 'curator,' meaning to care for.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To procure is to obtain something through care or effort.

See the word in context

The quartermaster spent weeks trying to procure enough tents for the entire battalion before winter.

A restaurant owner calls a dozen different farms, compares prices, and finally manages to procure a delivery of a rare imported cheese in time for opening night.

That deliberate effort to track down and obtain the cheese is why procure is the right word. Unlike 'find,' which can happen by accident, procure implies real work went into getting it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    acquire: To acquire is simply to come to have something, whether you bought it, inherited it, or picked it up over time. To procure stresses the legwork: deliberate searching, arranging, or negotiating to get a specific thing you set out to find.

    requisition: To requisition is to demand something through official channels, claiming it by authority -- an army requisitions trucks and fuel. To procure is to get something through your own care and effort, by searching or negotiating rather than by ordering it handed over.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forfeit: to lose or give up something as a penalty

    surrender: to give something up or hand it over

    Where it sits · how hard you work to get it

    obtain → procure → secure

    obtain vs. procure: Obtain is a more everyday word than procure -- obtain can describe getting almost anything easily, while procure often implies a deliberate search or negotiation was involved.

    secure vs. procure: Secure can feel like an even harder-won version of procure: procure simply means to get something through effort, while secure often implies getting it despite real competition or difficulty.
