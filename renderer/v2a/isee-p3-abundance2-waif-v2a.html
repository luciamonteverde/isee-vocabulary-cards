What It Means

A waif is a frail child left abandoned and unclaimed, with no one caring for them, or a thin, delicate-looking person.

See the word in context

The old photograph showed a small waif waiting barefoot outside the train station.

The novel's heroine begins as a waif wandering the streets before she's finally taken in by a kindly baker.

She wasn't simply young -- she was homeless, alone, and unclaimed by anyone, which is exactly a waif.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    orphan: An orphan is a child whose parents have died; the word says nothing about where the child lives now — many orphans are housed and cared for by relatives. A waif has been abandoned or left unclaimed, with no one caring for the child at all.

    urchin: An urchin is a scruffy child who lives or plays in the streets, often mischievous and begging or stealing to get by. A waif is defined by abandonment and frailty — a waif stirs pity, while an urchin suggests grubby, streetwise energy.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    heir: a person legally entitled to inherit wealth or a position

    Where it sits · how completely the family is gone

    child → waif → foundling

    child vs. waif: Any young person is simply a child; a waif is specifically one who is homeless, abandoned, or unclaimed.

    foundling vs. waif: A waif has been abandoned and left unclaimed, though someone may still know the child's name or where the family went; a foundling's family is even more completely gone — the baby is discovered with no name, no history, and no one to ask.
