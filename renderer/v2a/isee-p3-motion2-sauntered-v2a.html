Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To saunter is to walk toward or into a place at a slow, easy, unhurried pace, with an easy confidence — a touch of cool — as if no one waiting or watching could make you hurry. Sauntered is the past tense. The word is about the manner of the walk, not the walk as an outing: a person saunters up to a counter, into a room, or past a line of people, looking as if nothing in the world could rush them.

See the word in context

The cat sauntered across the kitchen counter, ignoring the three people telling it to get down.

While the rest of the team sprinted for the bus, the goalkeeper sauntered across the parking lot, sipping a sports drink and waving to the fans.

Everyone else is hurrying, and he is not. He has somewhere to be — the bus — so he is not roaming with no destination, and this is no pleasant walk taken for its own sake. He is not lagging behind because he lost track of time, either: he is enjoying every slow step, drink in hand, waving. That easy, unhurried, slightly cool way of moving toward a place, with no worry about who is waiting, is sauntering.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    stroll: To stroll is to take a slow, pleasant walk for its own sake — along a beach, through a park, around the block — with the walk itself as the point and nowhere you have to be. To saunter is about the easy, unhurried way a person moves toward or into a place — up to a counter, into class, past a waiting line — often where others might expect some hurry. Ask whether the sentence is about a relaxed walk taken for enjoyment, or about the cool, unhurried way someone arrives somewhere.

    dawdle: To dawdle is to be slow because you are wasting time — lagging behind the group, poking at your dinner, taking forever to tie your shoes — and it usually annoys someone who is waiting. To saunter is slow on purpose and at ease: the walker is not behind or lost in something else, just in no rush and comfortable about it. Ask whether the slowness is time-wasting that holds things up, or a relaxed, confident pace the person has chosen.

    wander: To wander is to move about with no fixed destination — you can wander fast or slow, lost or curious, through a city or a museum. To saunter is to head somewhere in particular, just at an easy, unbothered pace. Ask whether the person has nowhere in particular to go, or is moving toward a place without any hurry.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rush: to move as fast as you can because time is short

    march: to walk fast and stiffly, with a purpose

    Where it sits · how much the walk is meant to be noticed

    amble → sauntered → swagger

    amble vs. sauntered: To amble is to walk slowly and easily with no message in it at all — a tired hiker heading back to camp, an old dog crossing the yard, a horse on a trail — pure comfort, and no thought of who is watching. To saunter is the same easy pace with a touch of cool in it: the walker looks completely at ease and in no hurry to please anyone. Ask whether the slow walk is simply comfortable, or carries an air of unbothered confidence.

    swagger vs. sauntered: To swagger goes further — a bold, rolling, chest-out walk meant to be seen, with a proud or arrogant edge; its own card calls it 'a confident and arrogant way of walking.' To saunter is looser and quieter: the confidence is in not hurrying, not in putting on a show. Ask whether the walker is performing for the people watching, or simply looks relaxed and unhurried.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Saunter carries a hint of attitude. It is not a rude word, but it often turns up when someone is in less of a hurry than the people around them think they should be, and the writer wants you to notice how relaxed — even a little smug — the walker looks. Cats saunter; so do people who know they are being watched and do not mind. The past tense is sauntered, and it is usually followed by a direction: sauntered in, sauntered over, sauntered up to the counter.
