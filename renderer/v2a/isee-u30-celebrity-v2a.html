celebrity

suh-LEB-rih-tee

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A celebrity is someone whom a great many strangers know by name and face, usually through television, movies, music, sports or the news — and the word says only that they are known, not that they have earned respect or done anything to deserve it.

See the word in context

The chef became a celebrity after her cooking show took off, and now strangers stop her in grocery stores to ask about recipes.

Nobody could say what the man on the magazine cover had actually done, but he was a celebrity all the same — people recognized him in every train station he passed through.

He is a celebrity because a large public knows his face, and that is the whole of what the word claims. It does not say he is talented or respected, or that he earned anything; being recognized in every station makes him one on its own. That is the line between celebrity and the words that praise a person: it measures how many people know you, not why they know you or whether they think well of you.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    star: A star is the leading player at the top of the show — the lead in the play, the striker who scores, the singer whose name sells the tickets. The word is about having a leading part and the talent to hold it, and a person can be the star of a school play without a single stranger knowing them. A celebrity needs no part and no talent, only to be known by the public. Ask whether the sentence is about a leading part on stage or on the field, or about being recognized by strangers.

    idol: An idol is a person someone looks up to and wants to be like — a fan's idol, a young player's idol. The word measures the feeling of the person looking up, and an idol can be known to very few people, like a small-town coach a child copies in everything. A celebrity is measured by how many people know them, and nobody has to look up to them at all. Ask whether the sentence shows someone deeply looked up to by one person, or widely recognized by many.

    legend: A legend is a person whose achievements are so great that people keep telling the stories long after — a legend of the game, a jazz legend. The fame has to be earned and it has to last. Celebrity can arrive in a week and disappear in a month, earned or not. Ask whether the sentence shows lasting fame built on what the person did; if it shows only that people know the face right now, the word is celebrity.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    nobody: a person no one has heard of

    unknown: someone the public does not recognize

    Where it sits · how widely a person is known

    personality → celebrity → icon

    personality vs. celebrity: Personality is the lighter word — a television personality or a radio personality is known to the audience of one show or one station, and known for doing that job on the air. Step outside that audience and the recognition stops. A celebrity is known well beyond any one show or town. Ask whether the sentence shows someone known through one program's audience, or known by the public at large.

    icon vs. celebrity: Icon goes further than celebrity — an icon has come, usually over many years, to stand for something bigger than themselves: an era, a style, a whole sport. Every icon is known to the public, but most celebrities never come to stand for anything. Ask whether the sentence shows a person who represents something larger over time, or simply someone the public knows.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Celebrity is the neutral word for someone the public knows; it does not praise. On this card it names the person — a celebrity — but the same word also names the condition: 'the sudden celebrity that followed the film' means the state of being famous, and you will meet that use in what you read. Keep it apart from dignitaries, taught in this same unit: dignitaries are important because of the offices they hold, while a celebrity matters only because people know who they are. The word comes from celebrate, but a celebrity need not have done anything worth celebrating.
