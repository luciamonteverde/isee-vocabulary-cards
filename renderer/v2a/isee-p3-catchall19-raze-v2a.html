What It Means

To raze is to completely destroy something, down to the ground.

See the word in context

Fire crews could only watch as the wildfire razed three mountainside cabins in under an hour.

Rather than restore the fire-damaged theater, the city council voted to raze it entirely and build a public park in its place.

Tearing a structure down completely, leaving nothing standing, is exactly what raze describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    demolish: Demolish means to deliberately knock a structure down, usually as a planned job with permits, crews, and machinery. Raze stresses the result instead: the site left completely clear, as if the building had never stood -- and a fire or an army can raze a block just as surely as a wrecking crew.

    level: To level a structure is to knock it down completely, so that the ground lies flat where it stood. Raze means the same total destruction but is the harsher, more final word -- a razed building is not merely flattened but erased.

    gut: To gut a building is to destroy everything inside it -- floors, wiring, walls -- while the outer shell keeps standing. To raze goes all the way: nothing is left standing at all, inside or out.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    build: to construct something new

    restore: to bring back to an earlier good condition

    Where it sits · how complete the destruction is

    damage → raze

    damage vs. raze: Damage is far milder than raze: a damaged building has taken real harm -- scorched beams, a flooded basement -- but it still stands and can be repaired, while to raze a building is to leave nothing of it standing at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Raze applies almost exclusively to buildings and structures -- you raze a building to the ground, but you wouldn't usually raze a plan or an argument.
