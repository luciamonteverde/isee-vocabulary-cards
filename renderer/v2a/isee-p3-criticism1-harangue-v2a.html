What It Means

Harangue is a lengthy, aggressive speech of criticism or lecture delivered at its listeners.

See the word in context

The customer launched into a ten-minute harangue about the delayed flight.

The coach's postgame harangue lasted nearly twenty minutes, covering every mistake the team had made that season.

A long, forceful, one-sided speech of criticism -- not a quick word -- is exactly what a harangue is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    lecture: A lecture is a long, one-sided talk meant to teach or improve you -- it can be dry or preachy, but it stays calm. A harangue is a verbal attack: loud, aggressive criticism fired at its listeners until they wilt.

    sermon: A sermon presses a moral lesson -- how you ought to live -- and it can be patient, even warm. A harangue has no warmth in it: an aggressive, hectoring speech that batters its listeners with criticism.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    compliment: an expression of praise

    tribute: a statement of respect or admiration

    Where it sits · how forceful and unrestrained the speech

    complaint → harangue → tirade

    complaint vs. harangue: A complaint voices one specific grievance and can be brief and mild; a harangue is a long, forceful, aggressive speech of criticism aimed at its listeners.

    tirade vs. harangue: Tirade is close to harangue in intensity, though it can be even more emotionally out-of-control and less structured.
