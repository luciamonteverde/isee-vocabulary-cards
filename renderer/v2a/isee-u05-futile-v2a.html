futile

FYOO-tul

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Futile describes an effort or action that cannot produce the result it is aimed at, no matter how hard or how long anyone tries — trying to empty a boat with a hole the size of a door, arguing with a recorded message. The result was decided against the effort before it began; the trying changes nothing. Futile is about attempts, not about objects: a search, a plea, a struggle can be futile.

See the word in context

Once the ferry had pulled away from the dock, chasing it down the pier was futile, so Dana sat on her suitcase to wait for the next one.

Shouting directions at the runaway shopping cart was futile — it rolled down the parking lot toward the fountain no matter what anyone yelled.

The cart cannot hear, so no amount of shouting — louder, longer, in any words — could ever have stopped it. That is what makes the shouting futile rather than merely unsuccessful: the result was fixed before the first yell. Notice that the word describes the effort, the shouting, and not the cart.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    useless: Useless describes a thing that is of no use — a broken clock, a map of the wrong city, advice that does not apply. It says the thing has no value or serves no purpose. Futile describes an attempt: the trying itself cannot reach its goal. A useless flashlight has a dead battery; shaking it for an hour is futile. Ask whether the word is attached to a thing that serves no purpose, or to an effort that cannot succeed.

    pointless: Pointless means there is no good reason to do the thing at all — the goal is not worth having, or there is no goal: a pointless argument about a show nobody watched. Futile is different: the goal is real and wanted, but it cannot be reached. Moving the deck chairs around is pointless if nobody will sit in them, and futile if the ship is sinking. Ask whether the sentence says the aim was not worth pursuing, or that it could not be reached.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    effective: producing the result it was meant to produce

    worthwhile: worth the effort it takes

    Where it sits · how little chance an effort has

    ineffective → futile → hopeless

    ineffective vs. futile: Ineffective is the milder word — the effort was tried and did not do the job well enough: an ineffective cough syrup takes the edge off and nothing more. A bigger dose or a different brand might have worked. Futile means nothing of the kind could ever work; the result was out of reach from the start. Ask whether the sentence shows an effort that fell short, or one that never had a chance.

    hopeless vs. futile: Hopeless is the bigger word — it says the whole situation is lost, so that not just this effort but every effort is ruled out: a hopeless case, a hopeless position. Futile judges one attempt at a time; a different approach might still succeed. Ask whether the sentence rules out one attempt, or every attempt anyone could make.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Futile is a plain, slightly formal word, and it is always a judgment on an effort, an attempt, a gesture — a futile search, a futile protest, futile resistance. It is often used after the fact, once the trying has failed: the rescue proved futile. Either way the point is the same: the goal was out of reach, so no amount of effort could have changed the outcome. On the ISEE, futile most often appears as the opposite of words like fruitful and effective, both taught in this course — an effort those words praise is one futile says could never have paid off.
