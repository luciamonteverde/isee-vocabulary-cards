What It Means

Umbrage is offense taken at a perceived insult or slight to yourself (often 'take umbrage at').

See the word in context

The senator took umbrage at the reporter's question, calling it a deliberate attempt to embarrass him.

When her coworker joked that her presentation ran 'a little long,' Priya took real umbrage, convinced he was mocking her in front of the entire team.

Umbrage is the feeling of being offended or insulted -- Priya isn't just mildly bothered, she feels genuinely slighted by the remark.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    offense: Offense is the general feeling of being hurt or angered by what someone said or did. Umbrage is a sharper, more particular version: taking umbrage means bristling at a perceived insult or slight, often one aimed at your dignity. All umbrage is offense; not every offense rises to umbrage.

    indignation: Indignation is anger provoked by something unjust -- cheating, cruelty, an unfair rule -- and it points outward at the wrong itself. Umbrage is more personal: you take umbrage when you feel you yourself have been insulted or slighted, whether or not any real injustice occurred.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gratitude: a feeling of thankfulness

    delight: great pleasure

    Where it sits · how strongly the insult stings

    annoyance → umbrage → outrage

    annoyance vs. umbrage: Annoyance is milder than umbrage -- ordinary irritation at a nuisance, with no sense of being personally insulted; umbrage always carries the sting of a perceived slight.

    outrage vs. umbrage: Outrage is a much stronger, more public reaction of anger and shock -- beyond the more personal sting of umbrage.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Umbrage names the feeling of being insulted or slighted, usually over something said -- more personal and reactive than rancor (a deep, long-lasting bitterness), and it doesn't necessarily involve any outward hostility, unlike antagonize or accost.
