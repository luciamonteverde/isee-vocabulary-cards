abstain

ab-STAYN -- the second syllable rhymes with 'rain'.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To abstain is to firmly and deliberately hold oneself back from doing or enjoying something, as a determined act of self-denial.

See the word in context

The councilwoman chose to abstain from the vote because her family owned land near the proposed construction site.

Dessert is passed around the table, but the marathon runner chooses to abstain, politely waving it away and sticking to the strict diet she follows before every race.

Deliberately holding herself back from something tempting is exactly what it means to abstain. Unlike 'indulge,' which means giving in to a desire, to abstain means to refuse it on purpose.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    forgo: To forgo something is to give it up or do without it, usually letting a particular chance or claim pass — you might forgo your turn or forgo a bonus. To abstain is firmer and more inward: deliberately holding yourself back from an indulgence itself.

    forbear: To forbear is to hold yourself back with patient self-control, usually from a sharp word or rash act in the heat of a moment. To abstain is less about patience under provocation and more about denial: deliberately going without something you would enjoy.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    indulge: to give in to a desire or pleasure

    partake: to take part in something or have a share of it

    Where it sits · how firm the self-denial is

    refrain → abstain → desist

    refrain vs. abstain: Refrain is lighter than abstain — you refrain when you hold yourself back from starting something, like refraining from talking during a movie, while to abstain is a firmer, more deliberate act of self-denial.

    desist vs. abstain: Desist can sound stronger and more official than abstain: to abstain is a personal choice to hold back, while desist often means stopping something already underway, sometimes because an authority demands it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Abstain is almost always followed by 'from,' as in 'abstain from sweets' or 'abstain from arguing.' It is also the official word for choosing not to cast a vote at all -- a determined act of self-restraint rather than simple forgetfulness.
