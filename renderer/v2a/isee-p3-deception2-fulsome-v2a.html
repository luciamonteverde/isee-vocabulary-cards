What It Means

Fulsome is expressing praise or flattery to an excessive, insincere degree.

See the word in context

The obituary's fulsome tribute struck several old colleagues as more strategic than sincere.

The critic's fulsome praise for the mediocre film made readers wonder if he'd been paid to write it.

Praise so over-the-top for a mediocre film that readers suspect a bribe is exactly fulsome.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gushing: Gushing praise bubbles over with excitement -- a fan going on and on about a favorite book -- and the speaker usually means every word of it. Fulsome praise is different: so heavy and overdone that it stops feeling sincere and starts feeling calculated.

    effusive: Effusive describes feelings poured out without restraint -- long hugs, endless thanks -- and says nothing about whether they are honest. Fulsome adds the judgment: the praise is not just unrestrained but excessive to the point of seeming insincere or self-serving.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    understated: expressed with restraint, less than fully

    Where it sits · how overdone the praise feels

    generous → fulsome → obsequious

    generous vs. fulsome: Generous praise is warm and kind, often perfectly sincere; fulsome sits further up this scale, implying the praise has gone too far to feel genuine.

    obsequious vs. fulsome: Obsequious (taught earlier in this course) is a step more overdone still: fulsome describes praise or language that has tipped into excess, while an obsequious person turns their entire manner -- every word, nod, and gesture -- into eager-to-please flattery.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Fulsome describes praise, apology, or language so excessive that it starts to feel insincere or self-serving -- it is almost never a compliment to call something fulsome.
