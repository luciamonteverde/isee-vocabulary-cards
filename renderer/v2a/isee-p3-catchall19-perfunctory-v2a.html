perfunctory

per-FUNK-tuh-ree

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Perfunctory is casually and hastily completed, without real care.

See the word in context

The manager's perfunctory 'great job, everyone' at the end of the shift felt more like a formality than real praise.

The building inspector walked through the apartment in under two minutes, gave a perfunctory nod, and signed off without checking a single outlet.

Going through the motions quickly, without any real attention or care, is exactly the hollow, dutiful minimalism perfunctory describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    halfhearted: Halfhearted effort is real effort with only half the will behind it -- the player still wants to win, just not badly. Perfunctory effort is different in kind: the person has stopped caring altogether and performs the routine only because the routine is required.

    token: A token action is the smallest possible gesture, made mostly to be seen -- one symbolic donation, one minute of silence. Perfunctory describes a fuller performance: the whole task actually gets completed, but on autopilot, with no genuine care invested anywhere in it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thorough: complete, with great attention to detail

    diligent: showing careful and persistent effort

    Where it sits · how little care goes in

    cursory → perfunctory → negligent

    cursory vs. perfunctory: Cursory is milder than perfunctory: a cursory review is genuinely trying to check, just quickly, so it catches the big errors and skips the fine detail -- while a perfunctory review is pure going-through-the-motions, with no real attention behind it at all.

    negligent vs. perfunctory: Negligent is stronger than perfunctory: a perfunctory worker still completes every required step, only without care, while a negligent one abandons the duty itself -- checks skipped, hazards ignored, and real harm left possible.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Perfunctory criticizes the lack of genuine care or interest behind an action, even when the action itself gets technically completed -- contrast it with vapid, which describes something empty of substance rather than done half-heartedly.
