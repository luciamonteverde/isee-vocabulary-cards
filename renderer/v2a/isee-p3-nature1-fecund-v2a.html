What It Means

Fecund is highly fertile or productive, capable of producing abundant results, offspring, or growth.

See the word in context

Marine biologists were amazed by how fecund the coral reef was, teeming with more new fish hatchlings each season than any other reef in the region.

The old orchard, though neglected for years, proved surprisingly fecund once it was pruned, producing baskets of apples from branches everyone had assumed were dead.

Fecund describes something exceptionally capable of producing abundant results -- crops, offspring, or ideas -- here, the orchard turned out to be far more productive than expected.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fertile: Fertile means able to produce -- fertile soil grows heavy crops, a fertile pair of rabbits fills a hutch. Fecund is the same idea taken to an extreme: not merely able to produce, but producing in astonishing abundance. Fertile is the everyday word; fecund marks the remarkable case.

    prolific: Prolific describes someone or something that turns out a large quantity -- a prolific writer, a prolific apple tree. Prolific counts the finished output, while fecund praises the rich capacity behind it: the deep fertility of the soil, mind, or reef that keeps producing more.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    barren: incapable of producing crops, growth, or offspring

    sterile: unable to produce offspring or results

    Where it sits · how much new life it yields

    productive → fecund → teeming

    productive vs. fecund: To be productive is simply to get good results or output, a much more everyday word than fecund's specific sense of abundant fertility.

    teeming vs. fecund: Something teeming is so overflowing with life or activity that it seems to burst at the seams, an even more intense image than fecund.
