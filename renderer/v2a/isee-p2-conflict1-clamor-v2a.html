clamor

KLAM-er -- from Latin clamor, 'a shouting, outcry.'

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To clamor is to shout loudly and insistently as a group making a demand.

See the word in context

Ticket-holders began to clamor at the gate the instant the doors opened forty minutes late.

Hundreds of fans pressed against the barricade outside the stadium, clamoring -- shouting the team's name over and over -- until security finally let the gates open.

That kind of loud, insistent, collective shouting -- demanding something right now -- is exactly what it means to clamor.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vociferate: To vociferate is to voice an opinion loudly and vehemently, and it is a rare, formal word usually describing a single insistent speaker. To clamor is a group act: many voices shouting together because they want something now. One senator vociferates; a crowd outside clamors.

    holler: To holler is to give a single, casual yell -- you holler across a backyard to get one person's attention, and then it is over. To clamor is collective and sustained: a group shouting the same demand again and again until someone answers it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    whisper: to speak very softly

    acquiesce: to comply or agree quietly, without protest

    Where it sits · how insistent the shared demand is

    shout → clamor → howl

    shout vs. clamor: To shout is simply to speak very loudly, with no particular urgency or shared demand behind it. To clamor is more insistent -- a whole group raising the same cry because it wants something right now.

    howl vs. clamor: A crowd that howls has pushed past clamoring -- the same demand, but louder and angrier, screamed with such fury that no answer could even be heard.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Clamor is used for a crowd or group making noise together, as in 'clamor for change' or 'clamor at the gates' -- it can also work as a noun for the noise itself.
