What It Means

Arson is the criminal act of deliberately setting fire to property.

See the word in context

Investigators found traces of gasoline in the warehouse ruins and reclassified the fire as arson.

Security footage shows the arson in progress: a figure splashing liquid along the shop's back wall at midnight, striking a match, and running as the flames leap up.

Deliberately setting fire to property is the crime of arson. What separates arson from an accidental blaze is intent -- this fire was started on purpose, and that intent makes it a crime.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fire-setting: Fire-setting is the plain, clinical name for deliberately starting fires -- the phrase psychologists and safety reports use to describe the behavior itself. Arson is the crime: once prosecutors are involved, deliberate fire-setting is charged as arson, with a trial and a sentence attached.

    fire-raising: Fire-raising is the British legal name for the same offense -- a Scottish court charges fire-raising where an American court charges arson. Both words name one crime: deliberately burning property. Arson is the standard term in the United States, the one you will meet in American news and courtrooms.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    firefighting: the work of extinguishing fires

    fireproofing: the work of protecting property against fire

    Where it sits · how official and widespread the burning is

    torching → arson → incendiarism

    torching vs. arson: Torching is casual slang next to arson -- people say a car was torched, but arson is the formal criminal charge, with courts, evidence, and prison terms attached.

    incendiarism vs. arson: Incendiarism can sweep even wider than arson: arson names the specific crime of deliberately burning property, while incendiarism suggests a whole pattern or campaign of malicious fire-setting.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Arson is a precise legal term: the crime is arson, and the criminal is an arsonist. It appears in phrases like 'committed arson' and 'charged with arson.' Never use it for accidental fires -- the word requires deliberate intent to burn property.
