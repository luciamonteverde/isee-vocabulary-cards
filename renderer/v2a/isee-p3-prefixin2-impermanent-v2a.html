Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impermanent means not built to last: by its very nature, the thing cannot last, whatever anyone intends.

See the word in context

Sand sculptures are deliberately impermanent -- the artist knows the tide will erase the whole piece by morning.

The temporary tattoo was impermanent by design, fading completely within a week of swimming and showering.

Impermanent describes something built or made without any expectation of lasting -- its short life is often intentional, not a flaw.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    transient: Transient means passing through -- staying briefly and then moving on, like guests in a hotel or workers who follow the harvest. Impermanent points at the thing itself rather than its travels: something impermanent stays put but is built, by nature, to fade or fall.

    temporary: Temporary is the everyday word for something meant to last only a while -- a plan with an end date, like a temporary job or a temporary fix. Impermanent is the more formal word and says something deeper: by its very nature, the thing cannot last, whatever anyone intends.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permanent: lasting forever or for a very long time

    enduring: lasting over a long period

    Where it sits · how certain it is to vanish

    brief → impermanent → fleeting

    brief vs. impermanent: Brief is milder than impermanent: a quick summer shower is merely brief, while calling something impermanent goes further, saying that by its very nature it cannot last at all.

    fleeting vs. impermanent: Fleeting goes a step beyond impermanent: an impermanent thing is built with a short lifespan but may stand for weeks or seasons, while a fleeting one -- a glance, a smile, a smell of rain -- is gone almost as soon as it appears.
