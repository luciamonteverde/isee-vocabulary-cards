What It Means

To allege is to claim wrongdoing without proof.

See the word in context

The lawsuit alleges that the company knew about the faulty brakes for years.

The school paper's big story says a rival school 'took' the missing mascot costume. The journalism adviser circles the word and hands back the draft: 'You don't know that yet. Write that students allege the costume was taken -- until there's proof, it is an accusation, not a fact.'

The adviser is teaching the exact job of allege: to report a claim of wrongdoing that has not been proven. Newspapers and courts lean on this word -- 'the suspect allegedly...' -- because it draws a bright line between what someone accuses and what anyone has established as true.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    contend: To contend is to argue that something is true, pressing your side of a question with reasons. Contending covers any argued position at all, while to allege is narrower: it raises a specific claim of wrongdoing and openly flags it as unproven.

    accuse: To accuse is to blame someone for a wrong directly and openly, with no built-in caution about proof. To allege is the careful version: it raises the same accusation while signaling, in the word itself, that nothing has been proven yet.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    prove: to establish as true with evidence

    retract: to take back a statement

    Where it sits · how official the accusation becomes

    claim → allege → charge

    claim vs. allege: Claim sits below allege: a claim is the everyday word for any unproven statement, wrongdoing or not -- you can claim you finished your homework -- while to allege is to raise unproven wrongdoing specifically, with a legal edge to it.

    charge vs. allege: Charge is the strongest and most formal: to allege is to raise an unproven accusation, but to charge someone is to accuse them officially, with paperwork and consequences attached.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Allege lives in legal and journalistic writing, and it always signals an unproven accusation: reports allege, lawsuits allege, witnesses allege. You met its noun cousin earlier in this course -- "allegations," from the Part 2 catch-all unit: an allegation is exactly what someone who alleges produces. And keep allege apart from its unit-mate affirm: to affirm is to state a fact you stand behind; to allege is to raise a claim no one has yet proven.
