Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

What It Means

To coalesce is for separate, scattered things to gradually come together and grow into one unified whole.

See the word in context

Over the course of the meeting, a dozen scattered proposals slowly began to coalesce into one clear plan.

The scattered protest groups eventually coalesced into a single, well-organized movement with shared leadership.

Separate groups merging over time into one unified whole is exactly the process coalesce describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    merge: To merge is to combine into a single unit -- two companies, two lanes, two files become one, often at a decided moment. To coalesce is slower and more organic: many scattered pieces gradually grow together into one whole. Mergers are arranged; things coalesce almost on their own.

    converge: To converge is to come together from different directions toward one meeting point -- roads, marchers, lines of evidence. The things that converge can stay separate once they meet. When the parts actually grow into a single whole, that is when coalesce is the right word.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    disperse: spread out or scatter

    fragment: break into smaller pieces

    Where it sits · how tightly things join into one

    combine → coalesce → fuse

    combine vs. coalesce: To combine things is simply to join them; to coalesce is for many separate, scattered things to gradually grow into one unified whole.

    fuse vs. coalesce: Fuse suggests an even tighter, often permanent joining than coalesce, sometimes implying heat or pressure rather than gradual growth.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Coalesce implies a genuinely unified whole forming from separate parts -- not just an agreement or partnership between things that stay separate. When separation is only partly overcome, 'merge' or 'combine' often fits more precisely.
