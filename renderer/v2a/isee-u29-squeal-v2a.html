What It Means

To squeal is to let out a long, thin, high-pitched sound that is held for a moment rather than clipped short.

See the word in context

The toddler squealed with delight when the puppy licked her nose.

Worn brake pads made the old van squeal at every red light.

The van's brakes gave off a thin, high note that dragged on for a moment -- machines as well as people and animals can squeal.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shriek: to let out a sudden, full-voiced scream of terror, pain, or wild excitement -- a burst at full volume; a squeal is thinner and is a held note rather than a burst.

    screech: to make a harsh, grating high sound, like metal scraping -- ask whether the sound has a rough, scraping edge; a squeal is thin and clean.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    murmur: to make a soft, low, continuous sound

    rumble: to make a deep, low rolling sound

    Where it sits

    squeak → squeal → shriek

    squeak vs. squeal: A squeak is small and brief, like a mouse or a floorboard -- a squeal is longer, louder, and sharper.

    shriek vs. squeal: A shriek is even wilder than a squeal -- a piercing scream of terror, pain, or excitement.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Squeal covers happy, scared, and mechanical sounds alike -- children, pigs, tires, brakes. Informally, 'to squeal on someone' means to inform on them ('the getaway driver squealed to the police'), but the sound sense is the one tested here.
