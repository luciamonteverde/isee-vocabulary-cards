What It Means

Utmost is the greatest or most extreme possible degree of a quality, such as care or caution.

See the word in context

The surgeon operated with the utmost precision, knowing there was no room for even the smallest error.

A lifeguard spots a swimmer struggling far from shore. She swims with the utmost speed, pulling every last bit of strength from her body to reach him in time.

She gives everything she possibly has, with nothing held back. Unlike 'sufficient,' which just means enough to get by, utmost means the highest degree possible.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    greatest: Greatest is the everyday superlative — the largest amount or degree of anything, from noise to talent. Utmost is more formal and more forceful: it names the very limit of a quality, and it clings to nouns like care, caution, and respect.

    maximum: Maximum is the highest amount something can reach, and it belongs with numbers and measures — a maximum speed, a maximum load, a maximum dose. Utmost applies the same idea to qualities that cannot be counted: you show a guest the utmost respect.

    supreme: Supreme means highest in rank or quality, standing above all rivals — a supreme court, a supreme achievement. It ranks a thing against others, while utmost measures how much of a quality is given: the utmost respect is as much respect as anyone could show.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    minimal: the smallest possible amount

    slight: small in degree; barely noticeable

    Where it sits · how near the maximum it reaches

    considerable → utmost → absolute

    considerable vs. utmost: Considerable is weaker than utmost — a considerable effort is a large one, but utmost means giving every last bit you possibly have.

    absolute vs. utmost: Absolute can be stronger than utmost: utmost means the greatest degree you're capable of, but absolute allows no exceptions or limits whatsoever.
