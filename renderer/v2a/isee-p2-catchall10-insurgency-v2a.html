in-SUR-jen-see -- shares a root with 'surge,' meaning to rise up suddenly.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Insurgency is an organized, sustained rebellion against an established authority, a drawn-out campaign carried on over time rather than a single uprising.

See the word in context

The government struggled for years to put down the armed insurgency in the mountainous region.

A small group of villagers, angry over years of unfair treatment, arms itself and begins an insurgency, launching coordinated attacks against government outposts across the countryside.

That organized, armed campaign against those in power is exactly what an insurgency is. Unlike 'protest,' which can be entirely peaceful, insurgency implies an organized, often violent rebellion.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rebellion: A rebellion is armed resistance against a government, named for the resistance itself, whatever its shape or length. An insurgency is a particular way of rebelling: an organized, drawn-out campaign, often fought from the countryside in scattered engagements rather than open battles.

    revolt: A revolt is a sudden, forceful rejection of authority -- a sharp burst of open defiance. An insurgency unfolds differently: instead of one dramatic explosion, it is a sustained, organized struggle that can grind on against a government for years.

    insurrection: An insurrection is a single violent uprising against authority -- one dramatic event, like the storming of a palace, that begins and ends quickly. An insurgency shares the same root but stretches out: a long, organized campaign rather than one eruption.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    compliance: acting according to established rules or authority

    allegiance: loyal support for a person, group, or cause

    Where it sits · how organized and lasting the resistance is

    uprising → insurgency → revolution

    revolution vs. insurgency: A revolution is larger and more sweeping than an insurgency: an insurgency fights a sustained campaign against those in power, while a revolution aims to overthrow and replace the whole system of government.

    uprising vs. insurgency: Uprising is a bit broader than insurgency -- an uprising can be a single, sudden burst of resistance, while insurgency implies a sustained, organized rebellion carried out over time.
