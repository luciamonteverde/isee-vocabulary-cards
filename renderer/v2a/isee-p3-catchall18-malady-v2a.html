malady

MAL-uh-dee -- from Old French maladie, from Latin male habitus ('badly kept, in poor condition').

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Malady is a formal word for a serious, often long-lasting disease or disorder, sometimes used figuratively for a deep-rooted social or economic problem.

See the word in context

The clinic specialized in treating rare tropical maladies that most doctors had never encountered.

Economists described the small town's shuttered shops and shrinking population as symptoms of a deeper economic malady rather than just bad luck.

Using malady for a long-running economic problem, not just a physical illness, shows how the word can describe any serious, deep-seated disorder.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sickness: Sickness is the everyday, plain word for being unwell, and it usually points to something short-term like a cold or a stomach bug. A malady is a more formal word for a disease or disorder, often long-lasting, and it can even name a deep social problem.

    complaint: A complaint is a minor health problem a person mentions to a doctor -- a stiff back, an itchy rash. A malady is weightier: a genuine disease or disorder, and sometimes a deep-rooted figurative one, not a small bother someone reports.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    vitality: the state of being strong, healthy, and full of energy

    wellness: the state of being in good health

    Where it sits · how serious and lasting it is

    illness → malady → affliction

    illness vs. malady: An illness is usually milder than a malady -- a sore throat or a cold that passes in days -- while a malady is a more serious, longer-lasting disease or disorder.

    affliction vs. malady: An affliction is graver than a malady: a malady is a disease that treatment may cure, while an affliction is suffering that settles in for years and can shadow a whole life.
