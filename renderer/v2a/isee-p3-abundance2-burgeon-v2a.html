What It Means

To burgeon is to grow or increase rapidly, in a flourishing, organic way rather than with loud fanfare.

See the word in context

Interest in the small pottery studio began to burgeon after a single viral video.

What started as a two-person startup burgeoned into a company with hundreds of employees within five years.

The growth wasn't slow or steady -- it happened quickly and dramatically, which is exactly burgeon.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    boom: To boom is to surge with loud, unmistakable success — a booming economy, a booming resort town — sudden, vigorous, and impossible to miss. To burgeon is quieter and more organic: rapid, flourishing growth that builds fast without the fanfare a boom implies.

    prosper: To prosper is to do well — steady health, profit, and good fortune over time, at whatever pace. To burgeon is specifically about speed and size: a burgeoning town is adding streets faster than maps can keep up. Something can prosper for decades without ever burgeoning.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dwindle: gradually decrease in size or number

    Where it sits · how fast the increase happens

    grow → burgeon → explode

    grow vs. burgeon: Growing can happen at any pace, slow or fast; burgeoning specifically means growing quickly and noticeably.

    explode vs. burgeon: Burgeon describes strong, healthy rapid growth; explode implies an even more sudden, dramatic surge, often past a tipping point.
