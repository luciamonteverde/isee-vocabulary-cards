DEFT

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Handling something with quick, light, exact movements, so that a tricky job gets done neatly and no motion is wasted.

See the word in context

With a few deft folds, the street vendor turned a square of paper into a crane before the child had finished counting out her coins.

The librarian's deft twist freed the jammed page from the copier without leaving a single crease on it.

The page was stuck, and pulling at it would have torn it. One quick, light, exactly placed twist gets it out clean — no crease, no second try. That is deft: not that the librarian is good with machines in general, but that this one motion was fast, light and precise, and the awkward little job came out neat.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    adept: very good at a particular activity, usually from long practice — adept at chess, an adept swimmer. It rates the person's overall ability and says nothing about how any single movement looked. Deft describes the handling itself: quick, light and exact. To tell them apart, ask whether the sentence rates how good someone is at something or describes how neatly one particular thing was done.

    nimble: quick and light in movement — nimble feet on a rocky path, nimble fingers on a keyboard — whether or not anything gets done. Deft adds a result: the quick, light motion lands exactly where it was aimed and finishes a tricky job. To tell them apart, ask whether the sentence is only about moving quickly and lightly or about a job being done neatly by that movement.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clumsy: awkward in movement, knocking into things and dropping what it holds.

    fumbling: groping awkwardly for a grip — the clumsy moment itself, even if the job is saved afterward.

    Where it sits · how much skill shows in the way a task is handled

    capable → skillful → deft → masterful

    capable vs. deft: capable means the person can get the job done — the ability is there — and says nothing about how the doing looked. Deft is about the doing: quick, light and exact. Ask whether the sentence tells you the job could be done or shows you how neatly it was done.

    skillful vs. deft: skillful is the broad word for doing something well, and a skillful job can be slow, heavy and careful. Deft is one particular kind of skill — fast and light, with no wasted motion. Ask whether the sentence praises the speed and lightness of the work or only that it was done well.

    masterful vs. deft: masterful means complete command of a whole craft, built up over years — a masterful chef, a masterful violinist. Deft can be one quick, neat move by anyone. Ask whether the sentence is about command of the whole craft or about one thing handled neatly.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Deft is a compliment for the way something is done, not for a person's whole ability: you praise a deft touch, a deft move, deft fingers. It starts with the hands, but it stretches to anything handled with the same quick, light touch — a deft reply that turns an awkward question aside, the deft handling of a crisis. It never means careful and slow; a deft job is over before you expected it to be. On the ISEE, the closest match usually offered for deft is adept.
