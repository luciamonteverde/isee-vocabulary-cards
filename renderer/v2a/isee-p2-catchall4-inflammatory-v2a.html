inflammatory

in-FLAM-uh-tor-ee — contains 'flame'; inflammatory words are meant to set tempers on fire.

Meaning

What It Means

Inflammatory is intended or likely to provoke anger, outrage, or violence.

See the word in context

The senator's inflammatory speech was blamed for turning a calm debate into a shouting match.

A commentator posts an inflammatory article calling an entire group of people lazy and dishonest, with no evidence to back it up. Within hours, furious comments flood in and the post is shared thousands of times.

The language seems designed to make people furious. Unlike 'blunt,' which just means direct, inflammatory language is intended or likely to provoke outrage rather than simply state a fact plainly.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    provocative: Provocative means intended to provoke a strong reaction — but the reaction can be thought, debate, or shock as easily as rage, and a provocative essay can be admired. Inflammatory is narrower and always hostile: inflammatory words stir up anger, outrage, or even violence, never mere lively discussion.

    incendiary: Incendiary literally means fire-starting, and applied to words it is inflammatory's closest twin: both ignite anger. Incendiary runs slightly hotter — it suggests the speaker is deliberately throwing a match into dry tinder, courting real conflict, while inflammatory covers anything likely to provoke outrage, deliberate or not.

    volatile: Volatile describes a person, mood, or situation liable to erupt suddenly — a volatile temper, a volatile market. It points at the thing that might explode. Inflammatory points at what lights the fuse: language intended or likely to make other people erupt.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soothing: calming, easing tension

    diplomatic: tactful and careful not to offend

    Where it sits · how strongly the words stir people up

    blunt → inflammatory → seditious

    blunt vs. inflammatory: Blunt is weaker than inflammatory — blunt speech is simply direct and may upset people accidentally, while inflammatory speech is intended or likely to provoke outrage.

    seditious vs. inflammatory: Seditious is stronger than inflammatory: inflammatory language stirs up anger, but seditious language goes further, actively urging people to rebel against authority.
