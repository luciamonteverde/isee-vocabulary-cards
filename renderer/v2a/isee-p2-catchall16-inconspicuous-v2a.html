inconspicuous

in-kun-SPIK-yoo-us -- Latin in- ('not') + conspicuous (con- 'together' + specere 'to look').

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Inconspicuous is not easily noticed; blending in rather than standing out.

See the word in context

The undercover officer chose an inconspicuous gray sedan instead of anything that might draw attention.

Hoping nobody would notice she'd arrived twenty minutes late, she slipped into the back row and tried to stay inconspicuous for the rest of the assembly.

That effort to blend in and avoid drawing any attention at all is exactly what it means to be inconspicuous.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    understated: Understated means deliberately restrained — kept simple on purpose, with no flash. Inconspicuous is about escaping notice altogether: an inconspicuous outfit keeps you from being spotted at all. Something understated is still meant to be looked at; it just refuses to shout.

    nondescript: Nondescript means so ordinary and featureless that there is nothing to describe — a beige building, a plain cardboard box. Inconspicuous is about attention rather than features: an inconspicuous thing escapes notice. A nondescript van may still get noticed; it just leaves nothing memorable behind.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    prominent: very noticeable or standing out clearly

    flashy: showy and deliberately attention-grabbing

    Where it sits · how hard it is to spot

    unnoticeable → inconspicuous → camouflaged

    unnoticeable vs. inconspicuous: Unnoticeable is milder than inconspicuous: a tiny sidewalk crack is simply easy to miss, while something inconspicuous blends into its surroundings so thoroughly that attention slides right past.

    camouflaged vs. inconspicuous: Camouflaged is stronger than inconspicuous: an inconspicuous moth simply avoids attention, but a camouflaged one matches the bark so completely that even a hunting bird flies past it.

    Word Choice

    Inconspicuous usually describes something that simply doesn't draw the eye, whether by accident or on purpose -- it's a slightly more formal word than 'plain' or 'unnoticeable.'
