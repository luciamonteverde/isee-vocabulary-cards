What It Means

To venerate is to regard with great, solemn respect.

See the word in context

Villagers still venerate the elderly healer who guided them through the epidemic decades ago.

Members of the tribe continued to venerate the ancient burial grounds their ancestors had chosen.

Venerate describes deep, solemn respect, often for something old, wise, or sacred -- elders, traditions, or holy sites.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    honor: To honor someone is to show them respect in a recognized, public way — an award, a toast, a moment of silence — usually on a particular occasion. To venerate goes deeper and lasts longer: a sustained, almost sacred reverence, typically for someone or something old, wise, or holy.

    hold dear: To hold something dear is to treasure it with warm, private affection — a family recipe, a childhood friend, a keepsake. To venerate is cooler and more solemn: grave, ceremonial respect for something almost sacred, kept at a respectful distance rather than held close.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scorn: regard with contempt

    mock: treat with ridicule

    Where it sits · how deep the reverence goes

    respect → venerate → deify

    respect vs. venerate: Respecting someone is a common, everyday courtesy; venerating them is a deeper, more solemn form of reverence.

    deify vs. venerate: To deify someone is to treat them as an actual god, a step beyond simply venerating them.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Venerate is a close, already-taught synonym you may recognize: "revere" (Part 1, this same theme's own first installment) means almost exactly the same thing. Venerate is also distinct from this unit's own "idolize" -- venerating something is solemn and respectful, while idolizing someone is more like starstruck hero-worship.
