What It Means

To sate is to satisfy fully, especially an appetite or desire.

See the word in context

A single bowl of the warm stew was enough to sate even the hungriest hiker.

No amount of applause seemed able to sate the young performer's hunger for attention.

This wasn't ordinary satisfaction -- the desire ran so deep that nothing seemed to fully quench it, which is exactly what sate describes (or fails to, here).

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    satisfy: To satisfy is the broad word for meeting a need or wish — a snack can satisfy you for an hour. To sate goes further: an appetite so completely filled that nothing more is wanted at all.

    quench: To quench is to put out a burning need the way water puts out fire — and it is always the thirst or craving itself that gets quenched, never the person. To sate is to fill someone up completely, appetite and all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    whet: sharpen or awaken an appetite or desire

    Where it sits · how fully the craving is filled

    please → sate → glut

    please vs. sate: Pleasing someone only means making them happy in some general way; sating them is stronger and more specific — filling an appetite or craving so completely that nothing more is wanted.

    glut vs. sate: Sate describes fully satisfying a desire; to glut someone goes further, filling it to the point of excess or discomfort.
