multiplicity

mul-tih-PLIS-ih-tee -- from Latin multi- ('many') + plicare ('to fold').

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Multiplicity is a large number and variety of different things.

See the word in context

The conference schedule offered a multiplicity of workshops, so no two attendees ended up with the same day.

Instead of one single design, the architects presented a multiplicity of possible layouts, from a compact studio to a sprawling open floor plan.

That sheer number of different options, all available at once, is exactly what multiplicity describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    abundance: An abundance is a very large quantity -- a garden can produce an abundance of one single crop. Multiplicity is about how many different kinds there are at once: a multiplicity of tomatoes would mean dozens of distinct varieties, not baskets of one.

    assortment: An assortment is a mix of different kinds deliberately put together, like a sampler box, and it stays small enough to take in at a glance. A multiplicity is not curated at all -- it is a sheer, almost overwhelming number of different things.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scarcity: a very small, insufficient supply

    uniformity: the quality of being all the same

    Where it sits · how many different kinds pile up

    variety → multiplicity → jumble

    variety vs. multiplicity: Variety is weaker than multiplicity: a cafeteria's variety means a decent range of side dishes, while a multiplicity is a far larger, almost overwhelming number of different options.

    jumble vs. multiplicity: A jumble goes further than a multiplicity: a multiplicity of exhibits is a huge range a visitor can still walk through, while a jumble piles up so many unrelated kinds that no one can sort them at all.
