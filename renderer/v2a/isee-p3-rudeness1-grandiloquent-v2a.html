What It Means

Grandiloquent is using language that is extravagant, self-important, and meant to impress.

See the word in context

The bakery's menu described a simple loaf of bread as 'an ode to humankind's oldest culinary invention,' a grandiloquent turn of phrase that made regulars chuckle.

Rather than a plain out-of-office reply, his email auto-response declared he was 'embarking on a sacred quest toward inbox zero,' a grandiloquent way to describe a two-day vacation.

Grandiloquent language reaches for big, showy words even when a simple sentence would do -- it's about sounding impressive more than saying something clearly.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    overblown: Overblown means inflated beyond what the facts support -- an overblown reaction, an overblown claim of danger. It is about substance: the thing is made out to be bigger than it truly is. Grandiloquent is about style: the wording is grand even when the claim underneath is modest and true.

    highfalutin: Highfalutin is the playful, spoken-aloud word for talk that is fancier than it needs to be -- 'enough of the highfalutin speeches.' Grandiloquent names the same puffed-up, meant-to-impress language, but it is the formal, written-review way of saying so.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    modest: not exaggerated; simple and unassuming

    succinct: brief and clearly expressed (a word taught earlier in this course)

    Where it sits · how showy the language gets

    flowery → grandiloquent → bombastic

    flowery vs. grandiloquent: Flowery language is just mildly ornate and decorative -- much milder than grandiloquent's showy excess.

    bombastic vs. grandiloquent: Bombastic language is even more extreme than grandiloquent -- it sounds impressive but is almost entirely empty of real meaning (a word you met earlier in this course).
