VAIR-ee-uh-gayt

What It Means

To variegate is to mark or diversify with different colors, patterns, or elements, making something more varied and visually interesting.

See the word in context

The botanist pointed out that the ivy's leaves were naturally variegated, each one bordered in cream while the center stayed a deep forest green.

To keep the office lobby from looking dull, the designer chose to variegate the tile floor with alternating bands of slate gray and warm terracotta.

Variegate means to add visual interest by mixing in different colors, patterns, or elements instead of using just one -- here, alternating two tile colors instead of a single flat one.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    diversify: To diversify is to add variety of any kind — a menu, an investment portfolio, a team can all be diversified. To variegate is narrower and visual: you variegate a surface by working different colors or patterns into it. A committee can be diversified, but only something you can see can be variegated.

    mottle: To mottle is to mark a surface with irregular, blotchy spots or patches of color, the way rust mottles old metal or shade mottles a path. To variegate suggests a more deliberate, orderly mixing — chosen bands, borders, or repeating patterns — rather than random speckling.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    monochrome: having or using only one color

    plain: without any pattern or decoration

    Where it sits · how thoroughly the colors are worked in

    tinge → variegate → marble

    tinge vs. variegate: To tinge something is to add just a faint trace of color, a much lighter touch than fully variegating it.

    marble vs. variegate: To marble something is to swirl multiple colors together so thoroughly that they blend into dramatic streaks, an even more intense effect than simply variegating it.

    Word Choice

    Variegate is most often used to describe patterns of color -- on leaves, fabric, tile, or artwork -- rather than abstract variety in general; you'd variegate a garden bed with different colored flowers, but you wouldn't usually say a schedule is 'variegated.'
