What It Means

To reprove is to express serious disapproval of someone in a deliberately mild, gentle way, meant to correct their behavior.

See the word in context

The librarian reproved the students softly, reminding them to lower their voices.

The coach reproved the young player quietly on the sideline, telling him to keep his temper in check next time.

A calm, private word of correction -- not a harsh public scolding -- is exactly what reprove describes.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scold: To scold is to tell someone off angrily, voice raised and heat showing. To reprove is deliberately gentler: a calm, often private word of disapproval meant to correct the behavior without wounding the person.

    chide: To chide is to express disapproval lightly, sometimes half-teasingly -- 'you know better than that.' To reprove is a shade more serious and sober, though both stay gentle; neither rises to open anger.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: express approval or admiration

    commend: express approval formally

    Where it sits · how sharp and direct the disapproval

    correct → reprove → rebuke

    correct vs. reprove: To correct someone is simply pointing out a mistake; to reprove them is to gently express disapproval of their behavior, not just the error itself.

    rebuke vs. reprove: Rebuke pushes further than reprove, implying a sharper, more direct expression of disapproval, often in front of others.
