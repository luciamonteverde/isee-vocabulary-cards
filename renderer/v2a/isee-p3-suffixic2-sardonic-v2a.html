What It Means

Sardonic means grimly mocking or cynical in tone or expression, a dry, scornfully amused delivery that can sound almost polite while the tone does the sneering.

See the word in context

He gave a sardonic smile when his boss praised the 'innovative' new policy that everyone knew was a disaster.

'Oh, wonderful, another mandatory meeting,' she said with a sardonic laugh, rolling her eyes as she grabbed her notebook.

Sardonic describes a mocking, scornfully amused tone -- often through a smile, laugh, or dry remark that says the opposite of what it seems to.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    derisive: Derisive means openly ridiculing something to make it look worthless — jeers, snickers, laughter used as a weapon. Sardonic mockery is drier and quieter: a sardonic remark can sound almost polite while its tone does the sneering.

    scornful: Scornful means showing open contempt — plainly treating someone or something as beneath respect. Sardonic wraps that contempt in dark humor: a sardonic smile mocks without ever saying so, while scorn skips the joke entirely.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sincere: genuine, without pretense

    earnest: serious and heartfelt

    Where it sits · how much scorn the tone carries

    wry → sardonic → contemptuous

    wry vs. sardonic: Wry describes a dry, quietly amused tone, without necessarily being mocking or cynical the way sardonic is.

    contemptuous vs. sardonic: Contemptuous openly shows that someone or something deserves no respect at all -- harsher than sardonic's mocking edge.
