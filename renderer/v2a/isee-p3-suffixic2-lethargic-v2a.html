What It Means

Lethargic is weighed down by a heavy, draining lack of energy, so that even simple movements and everyday tasks feel slow and difficult.

See the word in context

After the long flight, the whole family felt too lethargic to do anything but collapse onto the couch.

The heat wave left the entire office lethargic, with everyone moving slowly and struggling to focus past noon.

Lethargic describes a heavy, draining lack of energy -- physical, mental, or both -- that makes even simple tasks feel like a struggle.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    listless: Listless is a lack of interest and spirit -- nothing appeals, nothing seems worth doing, even when moving would be easy. Lethargic is the body's heaviness: energy so drained that moving at all feels like work, whatever the mood. A listless person won't; a lethargic one can hardly.

    sluggish: Sluggish measures pace -- slow to move, slow to respond -- and applies to traffic, economies, and engines as easily as to people. Lethargic belongs to living beings and names the drained, heavy feeling behind the slowness, not just the reduced speed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    energetic: full of energy and enthusiasm

    vigorous: strong, full of physical energy

    Where it sits · how severe the lack of energy

    tired → lethargic → comatose

    tired vs. lethargic: Tired is a mild, temporary dip -- everyone's ordinary end-of-day feeling, gone after a night's sleep -- while lethargic is a heavier drain that lingers and makes even small tasks feel large.

    comatose vs. lethargic: Comatose, used loosely, means so drained of energy it's as if completely unconscious -- far beyond ordinary lethargic tiredness.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Lethargic covers both a temporary slump (after a big meal, in the afternoon heat) and a longer-lasting pattern of low energy -- context tells you which.
