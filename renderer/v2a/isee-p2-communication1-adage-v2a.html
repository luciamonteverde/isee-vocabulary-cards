What It Means

Adage is a traditional saying that expresses a general truth.

See the word in context

Her writing teacher's favorite adage, 'show, don't tell,' was printed at the top of every assignment sheet.

All week, Marcus tells everyone he has won the science fair -- the judges seemed to love his volcano. At the ceremony, first place goes to a quiet girl with a solar oven. His grandmother pats his shoulder and offers only the old adage: 'Don't count your chickens before they hatch.'

An adage is a traditional saying that expresses a general truth. Adages are old, short, and battle-tested -- generations kept repeating them because they kept turning out to be right. Marcus just learned this one the hard way.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    byword: A byword is a phrase a group has repeated so long that outsiders use it to describe them. An adage carries a general truth anyone can quote; a byword belongs to one circle, and it is earned over years of use rather than picked in a moment.

    motto: A motto is a short phrase a person, team, or family chooses on purpose to guide how they act -- 'never give up.' It points forward, steering future behavior, while an adage points backward, repeating a truth that generations have already tested.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    falsehood: a statement that is untrue

    absurdity: a claim with no sense or truth in it

    Where it sits · how much tested wisdom it carries

    saying → adage → proverb

    saying vs. adage: Saying is the loosest of the three -- any short expression people repeat counts -- while an adage must carry a general truth inside it.

    proverb vs. adage: Proverb is the strongest and most official: an adage may just be a familiar old line, but a proverb has been polished by so many generations that it reads like folk law.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    An adage is a saying with mileage on it -- old, traditional, handed down: 'haste makes waste,' 'actions speak louder than words.' You met the very similar word maxim earlier in this course: a maxim is any short statement of a general truth, while an adage is specifically the old, traditional one that has been passed along for generations. Every adage could be called a maxim, but only the time-worn ones earn the name adage.
