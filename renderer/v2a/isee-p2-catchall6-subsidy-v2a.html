subsidy

SUB-sih-dee — think of support sitting 'under' (sub-) a struggling business, propping it up.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Subsidy is money granted, especially by a government, as ongoing support to keep a business, service, or cause running that could not survive on its own.

See the word in context

Without the annual subsidy, the small theater company would never have been able to afford its own building.

A struggling public bus line can't afford to keep running on such a small number of daily fares. Rather than let the buses stop entirely, the city grants it an annual subsidy, setting aside part of its budget every year specifically to keep the fares low and the buses running for the neighborhoods that depend on them.

That set-aside money, given specifically to help something survive that couldn't survive on its own, is what makes it a subsidy. Unlike 'loan,' which must be paid back, a subsidy is granted, not borrowed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    grant: A grant is a sum of money given once to fund a defined project — a study, a mural, a restoration — awarded in advance and spent to completion. A subsidy is ongoing support: money paid again and again to keep something running that could not survive alone.

    subvention: Subvention is the formal, technical term for a grant of money from a government — the word appears mostly in policy documents. Subsidy is the everyday word, and it stresses the purpose: propping up prices, fares, or farms so they stay affordable and alive.

    stipend: A stipend is a fixed, regular payment made to a person — an intern, a scholar, an apprentice — to cover living costs while they work or study. A subsidy supports an enterprise or service instead: a government props up farms, fares, or housing to keep prices bearable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tax: money required to be paid to a government

    penalty: a punishment, often a fine, for breaking a rule

    Where it sits · how targeted and urgent the help is

    aid → subsidy → bailout

    aid vs. subsidy: Aid is a broader, weaker term than subsidy — aid can mean any kind of help, while a subsidy specifically means money granted, often by a government, as ongoing support to keep something running.

    bailout vs. subsidy: A bailout can be more dramatic than a subsidy: a subsidy is regular, ongoing support, but a bailout is an emergency rescue, usually to prevent a business or bank from collapsing entirely.
