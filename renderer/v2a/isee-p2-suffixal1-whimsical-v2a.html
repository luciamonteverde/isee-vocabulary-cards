whimsical

WIM-zih-kul -- from whimsy ('a playful or fanciful idea') + -ical.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To be whimsical is to be playfully imaginative -- light-hearted unpredictability in ideas or style, never a negative or unsettling quality.

See the word in context

The children's book illustrator was known for her whimsical drawings of teacups that sprouted legs and walked around the kitchen.

The garden was full of whimsical touches -- a gnome riding a snail, a birdhouse shaped like a teapot, wind chimes made of old spoons.

Whimsical describes playful, imaginative, light-hearted unpredictability in ideas or style -- never a negative or unsettling quality, unlike mercurial, which describes a person's unpredictable, sometimes troubling mood.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    quirky: Quirky means marked by odd, unexpected habits or details -- the oddness itself is the point, and it can be charming or just strange. Whimsical needs more than oddness: playful imagination, a fanciful idea given form, like teacups sprouting legs.

    quaint: Quaint describes old-fashioned charm -- a cobbled lane, a village tearoom -- where the appeal comes from age and bygone style. Whimsical charm comes from playful imagination instead, and something brand-new can be whimsical.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    somber: dark, serious, and gloomy in tone

    serious: solemn; without humor or playfulness

    Where it sits · how far imagination takes over

    playful → whimsical → fanciful

    playful vs. whimsical: Playful is the broadest, mildest word on this scale: any light, fun-loving mood or tone counts -- a wink, a joke -- with no imagination required. Whimsical asks for more: the fun must come from a fanciful, inventive idea.

    fanciful vs. whimsical: Fanciful pushes further than whimsical, into the realm of imaginative, almost dreamlike ideas.
