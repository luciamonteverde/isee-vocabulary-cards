What It Means

To remunerate is to pay someone for work, services, or a loss they have suffered.

See the word in context

Nonprofit boards sometimes debate whether to remunerate volunteer directors at all, since many serve purely out of commitment to the cause.

The law firm's partners voted to remunerate the paralegal who had uncovered the filing error, awarding her a bonus equal to a full month's pay.

Here, remunerate means to pay someone, often generously, in recognition of valuable work.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    compensate: To compensate is to make up for a loss, effort, or inconvenience, and it does not have to involve money -- extra days off can compensate for overtime. To remunerate is always about payment: actual money handed over for work, a service, or a loss. If nothing is paid, no one has been remunerated.

    pay: To pay is the plain, everyday word for handing over money that is owed, for anything from a sandwich to a salary. Remunerate is its formal cousin, used almost only of paying a person for work, services, or a loss -- you pay a bill, but a firm remunerates its consultants.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    underpay: to pay someone less than they deserve

    shortchange: to give someone less than what they are fairly owed

    Where it sits · how much is given in return

    thank → remunerate → overpay

    thank vs. remunerate: To thank someone is to offer words of gratitude and nothing else, so it sits well below remunerate, which always puts real money in the person's hands.

    overpay vs. remunerate: To overpay goes a step beyond remunerate: remunerating gives fair payment for what was done, while overpaying hands over far more than the work was worth.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Remunerate is a formal word for paying someone directly for work or services. Compare it with remittance, the noun for money sent somewhere else entirely -- often support sent home to family, not payment for a service rendered.
