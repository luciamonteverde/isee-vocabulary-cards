What It Means

Mawkish is sentimental in a feeble, cloying, and insincere way.

See the word in context

Critics panned the film's ending as mawkish, calling the tearful reunion scene forced and overly sweet.

The greeting card's mawkish verse, dripping with forced rhymes about eternal love, made her roll her eyes and put it back on the shelf.

Mawkish sentimentality feels manufactured or excessive, like emotion poured on too thick to feel genuine.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cloying: Cloying means so sweet or rich that it becomes sickening -- a dessert, a perfume, or a compliment can all cloy through sheer excess. Mawkish narrows to sentiment and adds insincerity: the emotion feels feeble and fake, not just overdone.

    treacly: Treacly compares a tone to treacle -- thick, sticky syrup -- so it points at the sugary surface of the words or music themselves. Mawkish goes past texture to a verdict: the sentiment is feeble and insincere, emotion that fails to convince.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    restrained: controlled, not showing strong emotion

    understated: presented in a subtle or low-key way

    Where it sits · how thickly the sentiment is piled on

    tender → mawkish → nauseating

    tender vs. mawkish: A tender moment shows real, unforced warmth with nothing piled on -- gentler than mawkish, where the sweetness is troweled on so thick it stops feeling genuine.

    nauseating vs. mawkish: A truly nauseating tribute piles on so much forced emotion it turns the stomach, beyond ordinary mawkishness.
