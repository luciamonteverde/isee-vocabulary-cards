stagnant

STAG-nunt -- two syllables, stress on the first syllable, STAG.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Stagnant is stuck in an unhealthy standstill, not advancing or developing.

See the word in context

By August the pond had turned stagnant, its unmoving surface filmed with algae.

The novelist's first book sold well, so she wrote it again with new names. Then again. Five books later her career is stagnant: the reviews all say the same thing, the sales chart is a flat line, and her editor gently asks whether she has considered trying literally anything new.

Stagnant means not advancing or developing -- standing still like water with no current. Stagnant does not mean collapsing; it means stuck. The numbers are not falling, but nothing moves, nothing grows, and the stillness itself becomes the problem.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    stalled: Stalled means stopped partway through progress, usually by some obstacle, and waiting for a restart -- a stalled negotiation, a stalled engine. Stagnant is a longer, unhealthier standstill: nothing is blocking the pond, yet nothing in it moves or grows. Stalled expects to move again; stagnant has settled into staleness.

    motionless: Motionless means not moving at all, and it stays perfectly neutral -- a motionless heron is hunting, and a motionless lake at dawn is lovely. Stagnant is stillness that has gone bad: water, wages, or a career stuck long enough to turn stale. Motionless reports the stillness; stagnant condemns it.

    static: Static means unchanging -- a static price list stays the same from year to year, and the word passes no judgment on whether that is good. Stagnant adds the judgment: the stillness has turned stale and unhealthy, like the pond the word comes from. Numbers can be static and perfectly fine; stagnant means stuck and suffering.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    booming: growing rapidly and successfully

    dynamic: full of energy and constant change

    Where it sits · how completely the movement has stopped

    sluggish → stagnant → paralyzed

    sluggish vs. stagnant: Sluggish still crawls forward -- a sluggish economy grows, barely -- while a stagnant one has stopped moving entirely, like the pond the word comes from.

    paralyzed vs. stagnant: Paralyzed goes further than stagnant: a stagnant office still runs its routines while nothing advances, but a paralyzed one cannot act at all -- every decision and every routine freezes where it stands.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Stagnant starts at the pond -- stagnant water is still, airless, and stale -- and extends to anything that should be moving but is not: a stagnant economy, stagnant wages, a stagnant career. Note the difference from decline: stagnant things are not falling, they are frozen in place, which on the ISEE is usually presented as its own kind of problem.
