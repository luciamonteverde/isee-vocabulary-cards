contentious

kun-TEN-shus -- shares a root with 'contend,' meaning to compete or argue.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Contentious means likely to cause heated, ongoing disagreement or argument.

See the word in context

Whether to raise property taxes remained the most contentious issue at the town meeting.

Every time the contentious topic of realigning district lines comes up at a school board meeting, the room erupts into heated arguments that last well past midnight.

That tendency to spark heated disagreement is exactly what contentious describes. Unlike 'popular,' which nearly everyone agrees on, a contentious issue divides people sharply.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    controversial: Controversial means causing public disagreement -- the arguing happens out in the open, in newspapers, meetings, and comment sections. Contentious is close but points at the fight itself: a contentious issue is one likely to set people arguing heatedly whenever it comes up, public stage or not.

    thorny: Thorny means tangled with difficulties -- a thorny problem is hard to handle without getting scratched, full of complications no matter which way you grab it. Contentious is about people clashing: an issue can be contentious yet simple, with everyone understanding it perfectly and still arguing.

    argumentative: Argumentative describes a person who enjoys picking fights with words and will dispute almost anything. Contentious usually describes the topic or issue instead -- the thing likely to cause the argument, not the person who loves having one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    uncontroversial: not likely to cause disagreement

    agreeable: pleasant and easy to get along with

    Where it sits · how much conflict it stirs up

    disputed → contentious → divisive

    disputed vs. contentious: Disputed is milder than contentious -- something disputed is simply questioned or argued over, while contentious implies the disagreement is heated and ongoing.

    divisive vs. contentious: Divisive can feel stronger than contentious: contentious describes an issue likely to spark argument, while divisive suggests it actually splits people into opposing sides.
