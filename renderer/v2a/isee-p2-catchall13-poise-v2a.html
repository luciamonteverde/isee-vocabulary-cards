poise

POYZ -- from Old French pois, 'weight,' from Latin pendere, 'to weigh, to hang': a mind perfectly balanced.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Poise is calm grace and dignity that hold up when something goes wrong.

See the word in context

Even at fourteen, the skater carried herself with the poise of a seasoned performer.

A speaker's microphone dies halfway through her talk; with unshaken poise, she waits a beat, smiles at the audience, raises her voice, and finishes as though nothing had happened.

That calm dignity holding up when something goes wrong is her poise. Unlike 'confidence,' which is a feeling inside a person, poise is how that steadiness looks from the outside.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dignity: Dignity is a bearing that commands respect -- serious, self-respecting, a little formal. Poise is lighter on its feet: it is calm grace under pressure, and it can smile. A judge on the bench has dignity; a dancer whose music skips shows poise.

    confidence: Confidence is the feeling inside -- being sure you can do something. Poise is how that steadiness looks from the outside: calm, graceful, dignified, holding up when something goes wrong. A performer can feel confidence backstage; the audience only ever sees her poise.

    self-possession: Self-possession is full command of your own feelings and reactions under stress. Poise includes that command but adds the outward grace and dignity that others can see. Someone self-possessed keeps their head; someone with poise keeps their head and still looks graceful doing it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    panic: sudden uncontrollable fear

    awkwardness: clumsy discomfort in front of others

    Where it sits · how much outward polish joins the calm

    composure → poise → elegance

    composure vs. poise: Composure is the plain core of poise -- staying calm inside -- while poise adds an outward grace and dignity to that calm.

    elegance vs. poise: Elegance is showier than poise: poise is calm dignity that holds up when things go wrong, while elegance is polished beauty of style, whether or not anything is at stake.

    Word Choice
