Hear It

What It Means

Gumption is initiative, resourcefulness, and spirited courage; the willingness to take bold action.

See the word in context

It took real gumption for the new intern to speak up and correct the CEO's mistake in front of the whole board.

When the town's only bridge washed out, it was the teenagers' gumption -- not the officials' -- that got a temporary crossing built within a week.

Gumption is that mix of boldness and practical resourcefulness that gets things done when nobody else will act.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    initiative: Initiative is the readiness to act on your own, before anyone tells you to. Gumption starts with initiative and adds more -- practical resourcefulness and a dash of spirited courage -- so it praises not just moving first but solving the problem boldly and cleverly.

    spunk: Spunk is spirited courage -- the pluck to stand your ground when something is frightening or discouraging. Gumption needs less fear to push against and more cleverness: it shines when someone not only dares to act but finds an inventive, practical way to get the thing done.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    timidity: a lack of courage or confidence

    passivity: a tendency to accept things without acting

    Where it sits · how much nerve is behind it

    willingness → gumption → audacity

    willingness vs. gumption: Willingness is much tamer than gumption -- a basic readiness to do something when asked, without much boldness, while gumption supplies the nerve and inventiveness to act unprompted.

    audacity vs. gumption: Audacity is bold, sometimes reckless daring -- even bolder than gumption.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Gumption is a warm, informal, almost old-fashioned word -- it praises someone's bold resourcefulness, and it works better in a story or conversation than in a formal report.
