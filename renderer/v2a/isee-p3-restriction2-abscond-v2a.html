What It Means

To abscond is to leave secretly, especially to avoid detection or arrest.

See the word in context

The suspect absconded across the border before the warrant was even signed.

Investigators discovered the accountant had absconded with nearly a decade of company funds.

This wasn't an ordinary resignation -- it was a secret, planned escape meant to avoid getting caught, which is exactly abscond.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flee: To flee is to run away from immediate danger or pursuit — a fire, a flood, an advancing army — and it says nothing about hiding. To abscond is quieter and more calculating: a secret, planned departure meant to dodge detection, arrest, or an obligation. People flee in the open; they abscond under cover.

    bolt: To bolt is to take off suddenly and impulsively, like a startled horse — no plan, just an instant dash. To abscond is the opposite in tempo: a departure prepared quietly and in secret, timed so no one notices until the person is long gone. Bolting is instant and visible; absconding is patient and hidden.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    surrender: give oneself up to authorities

    Where it sits · how secretly the departure happens

    leave → abscond → vanish

    leave vs. abscond: Leaving is a plain, ordinary departure; absconding is secretive and specifically meant to dodge detection or punishment.

    vanish vs. abscond: Vanishing is even more extreme than absconding: a person who absconds has secretly fled to somewhere, and investigators can still hunt for the trail, but a person who vanishes leaves no trace at all of where they went.
