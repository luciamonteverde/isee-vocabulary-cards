What It Means

Gaudy is extravagantly bright, showy, or overdecorated, in poor taste.

See the word in context

The amusement park's gaudy entrance arch was covered in flashing lightbulbs and mismatched plastic banners.

Every inch of the diner's gaudy jukebox was covered in glittering rhinestones and mismatched neon tubing.

The jukebox wasn't tastefully decorated -- it was covered in clashing, over-the-top decoration, exactly what gaudy describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    garish: Garish zeroes in on color and light — so harshly bright that it almost hurts to look at, like neon stacked on neon. Gaudy is the wider verdict of taste: overdecorated, showy, and tacky whether or not anything glows. A garish sign glares; a gaudy one piles it on.

    flashy: Flashy is showiness meant to catch eyes and impress — a flashy car, a flashy watch, glittering and new. Gaudy passes a harsher judgment: the decoration is overdone and in poor taste. Flashy things are angling for admiration; gaudy things earn a wince instead.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tasteful: having or showing good aesthetic judgment

    subdued: quiet and restrained in appearance

    Where it sits · how over-the-top the look is

    bright → gaudy → loud

    bright vs. gaudy: Something merely bright is vivid or colorful, without the tacky, over-the-top quality of gaudy.

    loud vs. gaudy: Calling an outfit 'loud' pushes past gaudy into something even more overwhelming and impossible to ignore.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Gaudy is a judgment about taste, not necessarily about cost -- something gaudy can be cheap or expensive, as long as it's tacky and overdone. That sets it apart from ostentatious, which specifically points to a showy display of wealth.
