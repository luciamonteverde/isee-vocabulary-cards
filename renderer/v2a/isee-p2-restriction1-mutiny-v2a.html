Meaning

What It Means

Mutiny means an open rebellion by people bound to obey, such as soldiers or sailors, against their own commanders or leaders.

See the word in context

Facing spoiled rations and brutal discipline, the sailors plotted a mutiny against the captain.

Halfway across the ocean, the crew rises in mutiny: they seize the armory, lock the officers in their quarters, and vote to sail the ship home.

This is no complaint or protest but an organized uprising by a crew against its own commanders. A mutiny is open rebellion -- the group rises up to defy and displace the authority set over it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rebellion: A rebellion is open resistance to any authority -- colonists against a crown, citizens against a law -- and it can come from anyone, anywhere. A mutiny is a rebellion from inside the ranks: sailors, soldiers, or staff who are bound to obey rise against their own commanders.

    insubordination: Insubordination is one person's refusal to obey a direct order -- talking back, ignoring an instruction -- and it stops short of any takeover. A mutiny is collective and goes much further: the group rises together to defy its own leaders and take command out of their hands.

    coup: A coup is a swift, plotted seizure of ruling power, carried out so the plotters can govern in the old leaders' place. A mutiny is defiance from below: those bound to obey rise against their own commanders, usually with no plan to rule anything themselves.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    obedience: willing compliance with orders

    allegiance: loyalty and devotion to a leader or cause

    Where it sits · how serious the rising becomes

    revolt → mutiny → insurrection

    revolt vs. mutiny: Revolt is broader and can be smaller in scale than mutiny -- any group can revolt against any authority, while a mutiny is a pointed uprising by people bound to obey, such as a crew turning on its own captain.

    insurrection vs. mutiny: Insurrection goes further than mutiny: a mutiny overthrows the leaders of one ship, unit, or organization, while an insurrection is a violent rising aimed at a government itself.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Mutiny classically belongs to ships and armies -- crews mutiny against captains, soldiers against commanders -- but it extends to any organized group openly defying its own leadership: 'the staff was on the verge of mutiny.' It is far stronger than grumbling or dissent; mutiny means open rebellion.
