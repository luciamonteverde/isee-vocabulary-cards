Hear It

What It Means

Vehement is showing strong, forceful, and intense feeling, almost to the point of violence in expression.

See the word in context

His vehement denial -- red-faced, fist pounding the table -- convinced no one that he was telling the truth.

The senator's vehement speech against the bill included three shouted objections and one thrown water bottle.

Vehement describes the sheer force and intensity of an emotion or argument -- it doesn't have to be angry, but it must be strongly, unmistakably felt.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fervent: Fervent means having or showing passionate intensity, and it usually glows warm and positive -- fervent hope, fervent support. Vehement runs harder and hotter: a vehement denial or protest is forceful and insistent, almost violent in how it is expressed. Fervent glows; vehement erupts.

    heated: Heated describes an exchange where tempers have risen on all sides -- a heated argument. Vehement belongs to one speaker at a time: a vehement objection carries overwhelming force whether or not anyone argues back, and a person can be vehement even in a whisper.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    apathetic: showing no interest or concern

    indifferent: having no particular interest or concern

    Where it sits · how much emotional heat it carries

    adamant → vehement → vitriolic

    adamant vs. vehement: Adamant means firmly insistent and unwilling to change one's mind, without necessarily showing vehement's emotional heat.

    vitriolic vs. vehement: Vitriolic goes beyond vehement's passionate force into bitter, scathing hostility -- a vitriolic attack does not just press hard, it means to wound.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Vehement describes the sheer intensity of a feeling or argument -- it's about how strongly something is felt or expressed, not about volume or tone by itself. This unit's strident describes a harsh, grating quality specifically in a sound or voice, and boisterous describes noisy, high-energy cheerfulness -- a person could be vehement in a whisper, but strident and boisterous both describe how something actually sounds.
