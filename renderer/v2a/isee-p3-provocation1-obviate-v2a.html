obviate

OB-vee-ayt

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To obviate is to remove the need for something by acting ahead of time, rather than blocking or defeating it.

See the word in context

The new online portal obviated the need for parents to drive to the school office just to update an address.

Because the two companies had already agreed on every major term in private meetings, the formal negotiation session was largely obviated -- there was almost nothing left to discuss.

The negotiation wasn't cancelled by force; it simply became unnecessary because the real work was already done. That's the special flavor of obviate -- it removes the need for something, rather than blocking or defeating it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    eliminate: To eliminate something is to remove it completely after it already exists — you eliminate errors, waste, or a rival team. Obviate works earlier and more quietly: to obviate something is to arrange matters ahead of time so it is never needed at all.

    preempt: To preempt is to beat something to the punch — you act first so that a coming event loses its force or its place. To obviate is gentler: you don't race the thing; you simply remove the need for it in advance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    necessitate: to make something necessary or required

    require: to make something needed

    Where it sits · how completely the need is removed

    reduce → obviate → preclude

    reduce vs. obviate: To reduce a need is a weaker move than to obviate it: reducing only lessens the need somewhat, while obviating removes it entirely.

    preclude vs. obviate: To preclude goes even further than to obviate: obviating removes the need for something, while precluding shuts off the possibility itself, so the thing cannot happen at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Obviate is a formal, almost technical word -- you'll often see it in legal, business, or policy writing ('this policy obviates the need for...') rather than in casual conversation. Unlike thwart or stymie, which describe blocking something already in motion, obviate works earlier -- it removes the need for something before it ever becomes an issue.
