avert

uh-VURT -- Latin ab- ('away') plus vertere ('to turn'): to turn danger away before it lands.

Meaning

What It Means

To avert is to turn aside a looming harm so it never happens at all, or to turn away one's eyes.

See the word in context

The signal operator's quick thinking helped avert a head-on train collision.

Noticing a hairline crack in the dam wall, an engineer orders the reservoir drained overnight -- and by morning, the flood that would have drowned the valley has been averted.

Stopping the disaster before it ever strikes is exactly why avert is the right word. Unlike 'survive,' which means enduring a disaster that happens, avert means making sure it never happens at all.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prevent: To prevent is the broad, everyday word for stopping something before it happens, by any means and at any pace. To avert is narrower and more dramatic: a specific harm is already looming -- a crash, a crisis -- and someone turns it aside at the critical moment.

    stave off: To stave off trouble is to hold it back for a while -- sandbags stave off the rising river for one more night -- without ending the threat. To avert is final: the danger is turned aside completely, so it never arrives at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cause: to make something happen

    invite: to bring something on, often unintentionally

    Where it sits · how early and actively danger is stopped

    avoid → avert → forestall

    avoid vs. avert: Avoid is looser than avert -- you avoid something by staying clear of it yourself, while to avert is to actively turn the danger aside so it strikes no one.

    forestall vs. avert: Forestall is a more strategic sibling of avert: to avert is to turn away a looming harm, while to forestall is to act so early that the threat never even takes shape.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Avert pairs most often with dangers -- 'avert a crisis,' 'avert disaster' -- and with eyes: to 'avert your gaze' is to turn it away. Don't confuse the verb avert with this unit's adjective averse ('averse to risk').
