paltry

PAWL-tree -- of uncertain, likely Scandinavian or Low German origin, related to dialect words for rags or rubbish.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Paltry means insultingly small, especially of money, expressing disappointment or contempt at how little was given.

See the word in context

After a year of record profits, shareholders were outraged by the company's paltry dividend of two cents a share.

A restaurant advertises a huge 'thank-you bonus' for its exhausted staff after the holiday rush, and the bonus turns out to be a paltry single free meal.

Paltry describes exactly that -- a reward or amount so small it barely counts, especially when it disappoints compared to what was expected.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    trifling: A trifling amount is one too small to matter or to deserve a second thought. Paltry is harsher: a paltry sum is not just minor but insultingly small, an amount that stings because far more was expected or deserved.

    pitiful: A pitiful amount is so small it stirs pity -- you feel sorry for whoever has to get by on it. Paltry points the feeling the other way: scorn or contempt aimed at the giver for offering so little.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    substantial: of considerable size or value

    generous: giving or given freely and abundantly

    Where it sits · how serious the shortage really is

    measly → paltry → meager

    measly vs. paltry: Measly is an informal, everyday word for something disappointingly small, much like paltry, though paltry is slightly more formal and often used when talking about money specifically.

    meager vs. paltry: Meager is a bit more serious than paltry -- it can describe genuinely scarce resources, like a meager harvest that barely feeds a family, while paltry more often expresses disappointment or contempt at how little was given.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Paltry almost always signals disappointment or contempt at how little was given, especially of money or effort -- it should not be confused with "inconsequential," taught earlier in this unit, which describes a lack of importance rather than a small quantity.
