What It Means

To condescend is to show feelings of superiority; to act as if one is better than someone else.

See the word in context

The acclaimed novelist would condescend to sign a young fan's paperback, sighing loudly as if the request were beneath him.

Rather than explaining the software patiently, the it director condescended to the new hires, sighing loudly every time someone asked a basic question.

Condescend isn't just being an expert -- it's making sure everyone knows you think you're better than they are.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    patronize: To patronize is to talk down to someone while wearing a mask of kindness -- the patronizer sounds helpful, even generous, while making sure you feel the difference in rank. To condescend is the broader act of treating people as beneath you, disguised or not. Condescension can skip the fake warmth entirely.

    belittle: To belittle is to actively make a person or their work seem small -- mocking a report, waving off an achievement. To condescend is about posture rather than attack: a condescending person signals superiority in tone and manner without necessarily criticizing anything. Belittling strikes at its target; condescending merely looks down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    defer: to respectfully yield to someone else's judgment

    respect: to hold someone in high regard

    Where it sits · how openly the superiority shows

    instruct → condescend → sneer

    instruct vs. condescend: Instruct simply means to teach or direct someone, without necessarily implying any superiority the way condescend does.

    sneer vs. condescend: Sneer goes further than condescend, adding an openly contemptuous facial expression or tone to the same sense of superiority.

    Word Choice

    Condescend almost always carries a negative, critical tone -- it describes someone who makes their sense of superiority obvious, even while pretending to be generous or helpful.
