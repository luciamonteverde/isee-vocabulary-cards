What It Means

Prodigious is remarkably great in size or degree.

See the word in context

The young pianist showed prodigious talent, mastering pieces that challenge performers twice her age.

In a single season, the rookie breaks the league's fifty-year-old scoring record, and sportswriters run out of superlatives for his prodigious point totals.

A total that shatters a fifty-year-old record is great beyond any ordinary scale. Prodigious describes sizes, amounts, or abilities so remarkably large that they astonish.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tremendous: Tremendous means very great in amount or intensity -- tremendous effort, tremendous noise -- and it fits almost any big thing. Prodigious is more specific: so remarkably great in size or degree that people marvel at it. Tremendous says huge; prodigious says huge enough to astonish.

    exceptional: Exceptional means rare and far above the usual standard -- one of the best of its kind. It praises quality without measuring amount: an exceptional essay may be short. Prodigious always points at scale -- sizes, quantities, and abilities so great they astonish. Exceptional means unusually good; prodigious means astonishingly big.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tiny: very small

    insignificant: too small or unimportant to matter

    Where it sits · how much it amazes people

    enormous → prodigious → staggering

    enormous vs. prodigious: Enormous measures only size, and more calmly than prodigious -- an enormous stadium is simply very big, while a prodigious achievement is so great it makes people marvel.

    staggering vs. prodigious: Staggering pushes even past prodigious: a prodigious memory amazes people, while a staggering figure practically knocks them off their feet with disbelief.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Prodigious pairs with nouns of size, quantity, and ability: a prodigious appetite, a prodigious memory, prodigious amounts of data, prodigious strength. It always signals astonishment at scale -- if something is merely big without being remarkable, plain 'large' will do.
