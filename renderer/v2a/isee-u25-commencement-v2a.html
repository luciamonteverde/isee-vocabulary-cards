commencement

kuh-MENS-ment

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A commencement is the official beginning of something that was arranged in advance — the point in time at which a scheduled activity or period, such as a trial, a school term, or a stretch of building work, actually gets under way. It names a moment, not an action anyone performs, and it is the word a legal notice or a printed program uses, never the word for a start that simply happens on its own.

See the word in context

The judge ruled that the commencement of the trial would be pushed back to Monday, because two of the jurors had fallen ill over the weekend.

Residents had waited two years for the new bridge, so the sign that listed the commencement of construction as the third of April drew a small crowd of people who just wanted to read the date.

Construction is a stretch of work with a set starting date, and the sign names the moment that work gets under way — that moment is the commencement. Nothing is being celebrated and nobody is doing anything at the moment named; the word points only at the scheduled start. If the town had held speeches and cut a ribbon on that morning, the event itself would be an inauguration; the date the work began is its commencement.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    onset: An onset is the start of something that arrives on its own and then runs its course — a storm, a fever, winter, a wave of panic. Nobody schedules an onset. A commencement is the start of something people arranged and announced: a trial, a term, a period of work. Ask whether anyone set the date.

    inception: An inception is the moment a thing came into existence — a club, a company, a plan — and it usually looks back to date that thing: the school has grown since its inception. A commencement is the start of an activity or a period, not of a thing, and it usually looks ahead to a set time: the commencement of the hearing at nine. Ask whether the sentence dates when something was created or names when an activity gets under way.

    launch: A launch is something people do, and do with a splash — a company launches an app or a campaign to grab the public's attention. A commencement is not an action anyone performs; it is the point in time when a scheduled activity begins, with or without any noise around it. Ask whether the sentence describes people doing something to get a thing going or simply names the time an activity begins.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    conclusion: the end or final part of something

    termination: the formal ending of an activity or an agreement

    Where it sits · how much formality the word for a beginning carries

    kickoff → commencement → inauguration

    kickoff vs. commencement: Kickoff is the relaxed, sporty word for a start — a game, a party, or a fundraising drive kicks off, and the word is cheerful and loose. Commencement is the formal word: a notice or a program announces the commencement of proceedings, and it would sound stiff at a picnic. Ask whether the sentence belongs in a locker room or in a legal document.

    inauguration vs. commencement: An inauguration is a ceremony held to mark a beginning — a new president, a new building, or a new bridge gets speeches, ribbons, and a crowd. A commencement needs no ceremony at all; it is simply the point when the scheduled activity begins, even if the only witness is a clerk watching the clock. Ask whether the sentence describes an event people attend or just the moment things start.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Commencement is a formal, official word — you meet it in legal notices, contracts, and printed programs rather than in conversation, and nobody says 'the commencement of lunch.' Its verb is commence: work commences on Monday. In American schools the word also names the graduation ceremony, and the two senses are really one idea — graduation is called commencement because it marks the beginning of the graduates' adult lives, not the end of school. On the ISEE the word is tested in its plain sense, beginning, so the answer to look for is a word meaning start. Keep initial, this unit's adjective, separate: initial describes the earliest stretch of a process, while a commencement is the moment a process begins.
