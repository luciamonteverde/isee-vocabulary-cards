Meaning

What It Means

Unkempt is untidy and not cared for, especially in personal appearance.

See the word in context

After two weeks camping without a mirror, the whole hiking group looked thoroughly unkempt by the time they reached the trailhead.

The landlord finally complained about the tenant's unkempt yard, where the grass hadn't been mowed in months and old furniture sat rusting by the fence.

The yard clearly hadn't been cared for at all -- overgrown grass and rusting furniture are signs of neglect, exactly what unkempt describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slovenly: Slovenly describes a settled habit of carelessness — someone routinely sloppy in dress, grooming, or work, week after week. Unkempt describes the state itself: hair or clothes that currently look uncared-for, whether the neglect is a lifelong habit or just one bad month.

    neglected: Neglected means denied the care something needs — a neglected garden or building — and it points at the carer who failed. Unkempt zooms in on how that neglect looks, especially untidy hair, clothing, or grounds, rather than on who stopped caring.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tidy: neat and orderly

    well-groomed: neatly and carefully maintained in appearance

    Where it sits · how neglected the appearance is

    messy → unkempt → filthy

    messy vs. unkempt: Something messy is simply disordered, without the deeper sense of long-term neglect in unkempt.

    filthy vs. unkempt: Something filthy is not just untidy but visibly dirty, a step beyond merely unkempt.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Unkempt suggests ongoing neglect -- of hair, clothing, or a space -- rather than the brief, casual messiness of tousled hair or the plain unfashionability of dowdy clothing.
