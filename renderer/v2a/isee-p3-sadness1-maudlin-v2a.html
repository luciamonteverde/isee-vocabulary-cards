Hear It

What It Means

Maudlin is tearfully sentimental to an excessive, self-pitying degree.

See the word in context

By his third toast, the best man had grown maudlin, tearfully listing every childhood memory he'd shared with the groom.

After a couple of glasses of wine, Uncle Ray always got maudlin, calling everyone at the table to tell them how much he loved them while wiping away tears.

Maudlin describes a person becoming weepy and overly sentimental, often once their guard is down -- classically after drinking.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    weepy: Weepy just means inclined to cry easily, from any cause -- a sad movie, an onion, a proud moment. Maudlin adds a judgment: the tears are excessive, self-pitying, and oversentimental. A weepy person may be genuinely and briefly moved; a maudlin one is wallowing.

    gushing: Gushing describes feelings poured out with exaggerated enthusiasm -- loud praise, delight, thanks. Maudlin overflow runs sad instead: tearful, self-pitying sentimentality. A gushing fan raves happily; a maudlin uncle weeps over old snapshots.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    composed: calm and in control of one's emotions

    unsentimental: not influenced by emotion or nostalgia

    Where it sits · how far the emotion overflows

    sappy → maudlin → inconsolable

    sappy vs. maudlin: A sappy movie moment only leans too hard on sweetness and stays dry-eyed -- far milder than maudlin, which spills over into actual tears and self-pity.

    inconsolable vs. maudlin: Someone inconsolable can't be calmed down at all, well beyond a maudlin toast.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Maudlin is often confused with the milder word sentimental, which you may have met earlier in this course -- maudlin specifically adds an excessive, tearful, self-pitying quality that plain sentimentality doesn't require.
