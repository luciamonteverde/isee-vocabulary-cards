parody

PAIR-uh-dee — rhymes with 'melody'; both end in the same Greek root meaning song.

What It Means

Parody is a whole piece of writing, music, or performance, structured to closely imitate a specific work, person, or style for comic or mocking effect.

See the word in context

The comedy troupe's parody of the superhero movie exaggerated every dramatic pause for laughs.

A famous pop song has an unmistakable beat and dramatic chorus. A comedian writes a parody about doing laundry, keeping the exact same melody and over-the-top delivery, and performs it for a laughing audience.

It copies the original's style closely but changes it for comic effect. Unlike 'tribute,' which honors the original sincerely, a parody pokes fun at it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spoof: A spoof is a lighthearted, affectionate send-up: it copies a movie or genre purely for laughs, with no real wish to wound. A parody also imitates something's style closely, but parody is the wider word — it can be gentle fun or genuinely mocking.

    satire: Satire uses humor to attack something bigger than one work — greed, politics, human folly — and it needs no imitation at all to do it. A parody works the opposite way: it copies one specific song, film, or writer and bends the copy for comic effect.

    caricature: A caricature exaggerates one target's most recognizable features until they look ridiculous, and it is usually aimed at a person, often in a drawing. A parody imitates a whole work or style, keeping its shape recognizable while twisting it for laughs.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tribute: something done to honor someone sincerely

    homage: a sincere show of respect or honor

    Where it sits · how biting the imitation is

    impression → parody → mockery

    impression vs. parody: An impression is weaker than a parody — an impression is a brief imitation of someone's voice or manner, while a parody is a whole, structured comic imitation of an entire work.

    mockery vs. parody: Mockery can be stronger than parody: a parody usually has a clear comic structure and craft, while mockery can be a harsher, less structured form of ridicule.
