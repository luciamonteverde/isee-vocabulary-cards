What It Means

Vague is lacking definite detail; too fuzzy and general to give anyone much to go on.

See the word in context

His directions were so vague that we ended up on the wrong side of town.

You ask a friend where the party is. She gives you a vague answer: 'Somewhere downtown, near some restaurant, I think, around eight-ish?' You hang up knowing no more than when you called.

Her answer is too fuzzy and detail-free to act on. Unlike 'wrong,' vague information isn't necessarily false — it's just too unclear to be useful.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unclear: Unclear is the broadest label for anything hard to make out — a garbled announcement, smudged handwriting, a confusing rule. Vague is one particular way of being unclear: the words come through perfectly, but they carry too little detail to act on. A muffled voice is unclear without being vague.

    hazy: Hazy pictures something blurred, as if seen through fog, and it fits faded memories and distant shapes. Vague is about information that lacks detail — plans, answers, promises. A hazy memory has softened with time; a vague answer never contained the specifics in the first place.

    imprecise: Imprecise means not exact — a clock that runs a few minutes off is imprecise, though you can still plan your day by it. Vague is a deeper failing: so short on detail that you cannot act on it at all. Imprecise information is roughly right; vague information gives you almost nothing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    precise: exact and accurate

    specific: clearly and definitely stated

    Where it sits · how little can be pinned down

    general → vague → incomprehensible

    general vs. vague: General is weaker than vague — a general answer is broad but still understandable, while a vague one is too unclear to pin down at all.

    incomprehensible vs. vague: Incomprehensible is stronger than vague: a vague statement gives you a fuzzy idea, but an incomprehensible one gives you nothing to understand whatsoever.
