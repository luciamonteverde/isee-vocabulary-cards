What It Means

Predisposition is a tendency, laid down ahead of time by nature or prior conditioning, to think, feel, or act in a particular way.

See the word in context

Researchers believe a predisposition toward anxiety can run in families, though environment plays a role too.

Having grown up sailing with her grandfather every summer, Maya seemed to have a natural predisposition toward a career on the water.

A predisposition is a leaning or tendency someone already has -- it doesn't guarantee an outcome, but it makes that outcome more likely.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tendency: A tendency is any regular pull toward acting or turning out a certain way, and you can pick one up in a week. A predisposition is a tendency that was in place ahead of time -- laid down by nature or long conditioning -- so it is already at work before the situation ever arrives.

    penchant: A penchant is a strong natural liking for something -- a penchant for spicy food or for crossword puzzles -- so a penchant always points toward what someone enjoys. A predisposition is broader and completely neutral: it is a built-in pull that can just as easily lead somewhere unwelcome.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    aversion: a strong dislike or disinclination

    resistance: opposition to something

    Where it sits · how strong the built-in tendency is

    leaning → predisposition → propensity

    leaning vs. predisposition: A leaning is a mild, casual tendency, without predisposition's stronger sense of something built in ahead of time.

    propensity vs. predisposition: Propensity describes a strong, almost inevitable-feeling tendency, more forceful than a simple predisposition.
