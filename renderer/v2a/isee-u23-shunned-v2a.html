Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To be shunned is to be deliberately kept at a distance as a form of rejection: the person or people around you refuse to talk to you, sit with you, or have anything to do with you, they keep it up over time, and they mean it as a message — that you are not welcome among them.

See the word in context

In the small town where she grew up, anyone who married an outsider was shunned — neighbors stopped saying hello, and the store owner would not serve them.

Once the fraud came out, the senator was shunned by the same donors who had lined up for photos with her a year earlier.

The donors do not stop calling by accident, and they do not simply forget her. They pull away on purpose, and they keep pulling away — and everyone understands what it means: she is no longer wanted. Deliberate, lasting, pointed keeping-away from a person is being shunned.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    excluded: Excluded means left out of a particular thing — a game, a club, a meeting — and it can happen by rule, once, and even politely. Being shunned is about people refusing you their company, on purpose, to show you are unwanted. Ask whether the person was kept out of one activity or cut off from the people themselves.

    snubbed: Snubbed means given the cold shoulder in one moment — a greeting not returned, a hand not shaken, a look straight past you. It is a single sting. Being shunned is that same cold treatment kept up day after day by the people around you. Ask whether the sentence describes one cold moment or a lasting pattern of being kept away from.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    welcomed: gladly received into a group

    embraced: accepted warmly as one of the group

    Where it sits · how deliberate and complete the keeping-away is

    ignored → avoided → shunned → ostracized

    ignored vs. shunned: Ignored means given no attention, and it is often not even on purpose — someone lost in a phone can ignore you without meaning anything by it. Being shunned is never an accident: people go out of their way to keep away from you. Ask whether the lack of attention could have been careless (ignored) or was clearly meant (shunned).

    avoided vs. shunned: Avoided means kept away from for any reason at all — you avoid a bully out of fear, a shy person avoids crowds, you avoid a talkative neighbor to save time. It is about the comfort of the person doing the avoiding. Being shunned is done to you as a judgment, to show you are not wanted. Ask whether the keeping-away is about the other person's own reasons or is aimed at you as a rejection.

    ostracized vs. shunned: Ostracized goes further: a whole group — a class, a workplace, a town — acts together to shut one person out completely and for a long time, as if by decision. One person can shun another alone and quietly; it takes a whole community for someone to be ostracized. Ask whether the sentence shows one or a few people keeping away or a whole group shutting someone out as one.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Shunned is the past form of shun, and it is the form you will usually meet, because the word is most often used about the person on the receiving end: he was shunned by the village, she was shunned at school. It is a cold word — the punishment is silence and turned backs rather than shouting. You can also shun a thing on purpose and on principle (a writer might shun interviews, a family might shun sugar), but on the test the person sense is the one to know.
