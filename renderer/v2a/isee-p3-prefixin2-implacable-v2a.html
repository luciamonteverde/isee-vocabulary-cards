What It Means

Implacable is impossible to appease -- no apology, gift, or peace offering will ever soften the anger.

See the word in context

The old general remained implacable toward his former rival, refusing every peace offering for the rest of his life.

No apology or gift could soften the implacable landlord, who evicted the family the moment their rent was a single day late.

Implacable describes a hostility or refusal to forgive so total that nothing -- no apology, no gesture -- can change it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    relentless: Relentless describes force or effort that never eases -- relentless rain, a relentless pursuer, relentless questioning. It is about pressure that keeps coming. Implacable is about a closed door: an implacable person cannot be soothed or won back, no matter what is offered.

    unforgiving: Unforgiving means not willing to pardon an offense once it happens. Implacable is unforgiving carried to its extreme: not only is the offense not pardoned, but no apology, gift, or peace offering will ever soften the anger at all.

    merciless: Merciless describes how someone treats a person in their power -- punishing without a drop of pity. Implacable is about what someone refuses to accept: an implacable enemy simply cannot be brought to peace. A judge can be merciless yet satisfied; an implacable one never is.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forgiving: willing to pardon an offense

    conciliatory: willing to make peace

    Where it sits · how impossible the anger is to calm

    irritated → implacable → vindictive

    irritated vs. implacable: Irritated is the shallow end of the scale next to implacable: irritation at a friend usually fades by dinner, while an implacable person shrugs off every apology, year after year.

    vindictive vs. implacable: Vindictive goes a step beyond implacable: an implacable person cannot be won back but at least stands still, while a vindictive one turns the grudge into action, looking for ways to strike back.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Implacable describes a deep, personal refusal to forgive or be satisfied -- often toward a specific rival or enemy. Intransigence describes a refusal to compromise in a negotiation, a more procedural stance.
