scion

SY-un

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Scion is a descendant of a notable, wealthy, or influential family; also (in gardening) a plant shoot used for grafting.

See the word in context

As the scion of one of the city's oldest banking families, she felt enormous pressure to take over the business herself.

The scion of the shipping dynasty surprised everyone by walking away from the family business to become a schoolteacher instead.

A scion often carries an expectation -- of continuing a family's wealth, name, or business -- whether or not they want that role.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    heir: An heir is the person legally entitled to inherit property, a title, or a position when someone dies. A scion may stand to inherit nothing at all -- the word marks birth into a distinguished family. An heir waits for something concrete; a scion simply carries the famous name.

    descendant: A descendant is anyone who comes down from a particular ancestor, however ordinary the family. A scion is specifically a descendant of a famous, powerful, or moneyed line -- the word carries the weight of a great name and its expectations, which a plain descendant never promises.

    successor: A successor is the person who takes over a particular role or office from whoever held it before. A scion needs no handover: ancestry alone makes one, born of a distinguished family, whether or not any job, title, or duty ever passes down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ancestor: a person from whom one is descended

    predecessor: someone who came before, in a role or lineage

    Where it sits · how close to inherited rank

    relative → scion → heir apparent

    relative vs. scion: A relative is any member of a family at all, a much weaker tie to rank than a scion, who descends from a distinguished house and carries its expectations.

    heir apparent vs. scion: An heir apparent is the person certain and next in line to inherit a title or position -- even more specific than a general scion.

    Word Choice

    Scion sounds like it should decompose with the -ion suffix, but it doesn't -- see the note on the lead card. Use it for a notable family's descendant, often with a hint of privilege or expectation attached to the name.
