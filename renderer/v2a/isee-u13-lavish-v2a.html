lavish

LAV-ish

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Lavish describes something given, spent, or provided in a rich, overflowing amount by someone holding nothing back — a giver's open hand made visible — and the word praises that open hand rather than finding fault with it.

See the word in context

Grandpa's lavish tips left every waiter at the diner grinning for the rest of the shift.

The school's new science room was lavish — a microscope at every seat, a fish tank the length of the wall, and a rock collection donated by a retired geologist.

The room is lavish because of how much was poured into it: not one microscope but one at every seat, a wall-length tank, a whole collection handed over by a donor. The word points at the open hand behind the room — the people who gave held nothing back — and it says this with approval. A big room would only be large; a room this generously supplied is lavish.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    luxurious: Luxurious describes the thing itself — soft, rich, comfortable, costly: a luxurious hotel room, a luxurious coat. It says nothing about anyone giving. Lavish is about how much was poured out and by whom: a lavish room is one someone filled to overflowing. Ask whether the sentence describes the rich feel of the thing or the generous hand that supplied it.

    abundant: Abundant means a large amount, well beyond what is needed — abundant rain, abundant grass — and it needs no giver at all; it only measures how much there is. Lavish always has a hand behind it: someone gave, spent, or provided. Rain can be abundant, but only a host can be lavish. Ask whether anyone is doing the giving.

    excessive: Excessive means too much — an amount or degree that has gone past what is reasonable — and the word always finds fault. Lavish describes the same overflow with the opposite feeling: it praises the open hand. Ask whether the sentence is pleased by the plenty or bothered by it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    meager: thin and barely enough

    stingy: giving as little as possible

    Where it sits · how far the giving goes past what was expected

    generous → lavish → extravagant

    generous vs. lavish: Generous is the plain, wider word — a generous person gives more than expected, and the gift can be small and quiet: an extra cookie, a kind hour of time. Lavish is generosity grown so large that it spills over for everyone to see — not a slice of cake but the whole bakery counter. (On a test, generous is the closest single match for lavish.) Ask whether the giving is simply more than expected, or so much that it makes a show.

    extravagant vs. lavish: Extravagant is the stronger word, and it finds fault — spending or giving beyond what good sense allows, so that the word carries a hint of waste. Lavish praises the overflow; extravagant adds up the bill. Ask whether the sentence approves of the plenty or shakes its head at it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Lavish is a warm, admiring word for plenty that someone provides: a lavish welcome, lavish praise, a lavish gift. It can also be a verb — to lavish attention on a puppy is to pour attention over it. On the ISEE, expect generous as the closest single match for lavish; the difference this card teaches is that generous can be quiet and small, while lavish is generosity so great that you can see it overflowing.
