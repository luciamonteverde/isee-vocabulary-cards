What It Means

Strident is loud and harsh; grating to the ear, or (of a claim or tone) unpleasantly forceful.

See the word in context

The strident whistle of the referee cut through the noisy stadium every time a foul was called.

Her strident objections to the schedule change -- delivered in a screeching tone that made three coworkers wince -- did not win her any allies.

Strident specifically describes a harsh, grating quality -- whether in an actual sound or in a tone of complaint that comes across as unpleasantly forceful.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shrill: Shrill means high-pitched and piercing, like a whistle or a scream -- the word is all about pitch. Strident is broader and harsher: a strident sound is loud and grates on the ear at any pitch, and a strident tone of complaint is unpleasantly forceful.

    grating: Grating means harsh and irritating, wearing on the nerves the way a squeaky hinge does -- and a sound can be grating even when it is quiet. Strident adds loudness: a strident sound or voice is both harsh and hard to ignore from across a room.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soothing: having a calming effect

    melodious: pleasant and tuneful

    Where it sits · how harsh the sound becomes

    sharp → strident → ear-splitting

    sharp vs. strident: Sharp suggests an edge or bite to a sound or tone, without strident's full harshness.

    ear-splitting vs. strident: Ear-splitting takes strident's harshness to a nearly unbearable extreme.
