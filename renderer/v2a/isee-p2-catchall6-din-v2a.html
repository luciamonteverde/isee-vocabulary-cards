What It Means

Din is a loud, prolonged, and irksome noise.

See the word in context

The din of the crowded marketplace made it nearly impossible to hold a conversation.

Hundreds of fans pack into a stadium before a championship game, chanting, cheering, and stomping their feet until the din is so loud and continuous that the players can't hear their own coach shouting instructions from just a few feet away.

That constant, overwhelming wall of noise is what makes it a din. Unlike 'sound,' which is neutral, din specifically means a noise loud and irritating enough to be unpleasant.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    racket: A racket is a loud, disturbing noise — the neighbor's drum practice, a dropped tray of dishes. Din adds duration and inescapability: a din is a prolonged wall of noise that grinds on and on, while a racket can be one short burst.

    clamor: Clamor is a loud, confused noise made by voices — a crowd shouting demands or complaints. Din is broader and less human: traffic, machinery, or a stadium can make a din, and the word stresses how long and irksome the noise is, not who makes it.

    hubbub: A hubbub is the busy, confused sound of many people talking at once — a crowded lobby before a show. It is livelier than it is painful, while a din is louder, longer, and genuinely irksome — noise you want to escape.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    silence: the complete absence of sound

    hush: a quiet stillness

    Where it sits · how loud and chaotic the sound gets

    noise → din → uproar

    noise vs. din: Noise is weaker and more neutral than din — noise can be pleasant or unpleasant, while din specifically means a noise loud enough to be irritating and hard to escape.

    uproar vs. din: Uproar can feel bigger than din: din describes constant loud noise, but uproar often adds a sense of chaotic shouting or protest on top of the volume.
