What It Means

Omniscient is having unlimited knowledge; knowing everything.

See the word in context

The novel is narrated by an omniscient voice that can reveal every character's private thoughts at once.

Students joked that their strict hall monitor seemed almost omniscient, somehow catching every whispered rule-break from three floors away.

Seeming to know absolutely everything, as if nothing could be hidden, is exactly what omniscient describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    all-knowing: All-knowing is the plain, everyday English way to say it: aware of absolutely everything. Omniscient means the same thing in a more formal, literary register -- it is the word critics use for a narrator who can reveal every character's thoughts. Casual speech says all-knowing; literary analysis says omniscient.

    encyclopedic: Encyclopedic describes knowledge that is huge and wide-ranging -- facts on nearly every subject, like an encyclopedia holds. It is still human-sized: learned, and limited to facts. Omniscient goes beyond any amount of learning; it means knowing absolutely everything, even things no book records, like people's hidden thoughts.

    knowledgeable: Knowledgeable means knowing a great deal about a subject or many subjects -- an expert level of human learning. A knowledgeable person still looks things up and can still be surprised. Omniscient leaves human limits behind entirely: knowing everything there is to know, with nothing hidden anywhere.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ignorant: lacking knowledge or awareness

    clueless: having no understanding of a situation

    Where it sits · how far knowledge goes beyond watching

    all-seeing → omniscient

    all-seeing vs. omniscient: All-seeing is weaker than omniscient: an all-seeing camera network merely watches every hallway, while an omniscient mind actually knows everything, understanding far more than it can watch.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Omniscient is most often used for a special kind of narrator in fiction (one who can see every character's thoughts) or, more loosely and often jokingly, for a person or system that seems to know an impossible amount. Compare it with omnipresent (everywhere) and omnipotent (all-powerful) -- all three share the Latin prefix omni-, 'all,' but each pairs it with a different root: know, be present, or hold power.
