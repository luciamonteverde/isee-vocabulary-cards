What It Means

To expound is to explain an idea fully and at length, layering point on point.

See the word in context

Once the guest lecturer got going, he continued to expound on the fall of the empire for nearly an hour, layering example on top of example.

The professor was happy to expound on any question, sometimes for twenty minutes at a stretch.

Rather than a quick answer, he kept adding detail after detail -- that expansive, elaborating quality is exactly what expound describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    elaborate: To elaborate is to add more detail to something already said — a few extra sentences can do it, and then you stop. To expound is bigger: it unpacks a whole idea at length, layering point on point. Elaboration extends an answer; expounding fills the hour.

    hold forth: To hold forth is to speak at length about your own views, holding the floor whether or not anyone asked. To expound shares the length but not the self-display: a teacher can expound purely to explain a topic fully.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    summarize: to state the main points briefly

    Where it sits · how long and showy the explaining

    mention → expound → pontificate

    mention vs. expound: To mention a topic is a passing reference; to expound on it is to explain it fully and at length.

    pontificate vs. expound: To pontificate goes further than to expound: both run long, but pontificating adds a pompous, self-important edge, as if the speaker's view were beyond question.
