What It Means

Luminous is full of light, or glowing with light of its own.

See the word in context

Deep in the cave, tiny luminous organisms lit the ceiling like a scattering of stars.

On a moonless night at sea, the water itself turns luminous every time a wave breaks, lit from within by countless microscopic creatures.

That glow coming from within the water itself is what makes it luminous. Unlike 'dim,' which suggests weak light, luminous means bright enough to notice immediately.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    incandescent: Incandescent means glowing white-hot from intense heat, the way a lamp filament burns. Luminous is broader and cooler: anything filled with light or shining with its own light counts as luminous, and no heat is required at all.

    gleaming: Gleaming describes light bouncing off a polished surface -- a gleaming trophy shines only while light is hitting it. Luminous is different: a luminous thing is filled with light or gives off light of its own, so it can glow even in the dark.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    murky: dark and gloomy

    dim: not bright; giving little light

    Where it sits · how bright and striking the light is

    glowing → luminous → radiant

    glowing vs. luminous: Glowing is a bit softer than luminous -- glowing often suggests a gentle, steady light, while luminous can describe something bright enough to be genuinely striking in the dark.

    radiant vs. luminous: Radiant can feel even brighter than luminous: luminous simply means giving off light, while radiant suggests that light seems to spread outward with real warmth or energy.
