Hear It

What It Means

Diffident is shy due to lack of self-confidence, especially about speaking up or claiming credit.

See the word in context

Despite writing most of the winning proposal, the diffident intern let her louder teammate present it to the board.

Diffident about his singing, the boy mouthed the words during choir practice instead of actually using his voice.

Holding back out of self-doubt, not fear of danger, is exactly what diffident describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bashful: Bashful means shy in an easily embarrassed way -- blushing and tongue-tied when attention lands on you. Diffident points at a different cause: a lack of confidence in your own abilities that keeps you from speaking up or claiming what you have earned.

    modest: Modest describes a choice: playing down your achievements even though you know their worth. Diffident describes a limitation -- you stay quiet because you genuinely doubt yourself, so credit goes unclaimed rather than merely unmentioned.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    assertive: confident and direct in stating one's views

    forthright: direct and outspoken in expressing one's views

    Where it sits · how shaky the confidence is

    unassuming → diffident → self-doubting

    unassuming vs. diffident: Unassuming is milder than diffident: an unassuming athlete just never brings up her medals, while a diffident person is held back by a deeper lack of confidence about speaking up at all.

    self-doubting vs. diffident: Self-doubting is more intense than diffident: a diffident person hesitates to claim credit, while someone truly self-doubting questions his own judgment so severely he rewrites every email five times.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Diffident is about self-doubt in social or professional situations -- hesitating to speak up, claim credit, or assert an opinion. Unlike timorous, it isn't about fear of danger, and unlike pusillanimous, it carries no moral blame -- it's simply modesty taken to an unhelpful extreme.
