What It Means

Trainable is capable of being taught or trained, especially through practice or instruction.

See the word in context

Talent scouts at the gymnastics academy considered the preteen prodigy remarkably trainable, since she could correct an entire floor routine after a single walkthrough with her coach.

Unlike the aging factory equipment it replaced, the new robotic arm was fully trainable, learning a new assembly task in under an hour.

Trainable simply means capable of learning through instruction or repetition -- it can describe animals, people, or even machines.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    teachable: Teachable emphasizes willingness: a teachable student stays humble and open to learning from anyone. Trainable points at results -- whether practice and instruction will actually build the skill -- and it stretches beyond people to animals and even machines.

    coachable: Coachable describes a person's attitude -- an athlete or student who welcomes instruction and correction instead of resisting it. Trainable is about capability: whether the skills will actually sink in with practice, whether the learner is a person, an animal, or a machine.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stubborn: refusing to change or follow instruction

    incorrigible: impossible to correct, improve, or reform

    Where it sits · how readily new skills sink in

    willing → trainable → quick-witted

    willing vs. trainable: Willing is a milder compliment than trainable: it just means someone is open to trying, without promising that the new skills will actually sink in.

    quick-witted vs. trainable: A quick-witted learner goes even further than trainable -- picking up new skills almost instantly, with barely any repetition needed.
