alienate

AY-lee-uh-nayt -- four syllables, stress on the first syllable, AY.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To alienate is to cut people off and push them away from a group they once felt part of.

See the word in context

His constant bragging began to alienate even his oldest friends.

The new club president redesigns the logo, cancels the annual picnic, and changes the meeting night -- all without asking anyone. Within two months she has managed to alienate most of the club: attendance drops from thirty members to nine, and the nine who remain sit in the back exchanging looks.

To alienate people is to cut them off and push them away from a group they once felt part of. You alienate people who used to be on your side -- the word describes turning belonging into distance, usually without meaning to.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    repel: To repel is to drive someone back or away on the spot -- rudeness or a bad smell repels instantly. To alienate works far more slowly: it turns people who were once on your side into outsiders, cooling their loyalty over weeks rather than pushing them back in a moment.

    offend: To offend is to sting someone's feelings in the moment -- a rude joke offends, and a sincere apology can patch it up by evening. To alienate cuts deeper and lasts longer: offended people are angry tonight, while people you alienate stop feeling they belong with you at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unite: to bring together as one

    befriend: to make a friend of someone

    Where it sits · how completely the bond breaks

    distance → alienate → estrange

    distance vs. alienate: To distance people is simply to cool the connection a little, while to alienate them is to push them out of the circle entirely -- they no longer feel they belong.

    estrange vs. alienate: Estrange is the strongest and most personal: alienated voters may still come back next election, but estranged relatives have let the bond itself break, often for years.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Alienate takes people as its object -- you alienate friends, voters, customers, teammates -- and it almost always describes an unintended result: the bragging, the price hike, or the sarcasm does the alienating. Watch for the pattern 'alienate the very people you need,' one of the word's most common shapes in test passages.
