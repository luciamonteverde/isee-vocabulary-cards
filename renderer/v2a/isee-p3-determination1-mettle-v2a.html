What It Means

Mettle is a person's inner strength and courage of character, revealed when real difficulty puts them to the test.

See the word in context

The championship game's final penalty shootout revealed more about the young goalkeeper's mettle than an entire season of practice ever had.

When the power failed halfway through surgery, the calm, quick-thinking response of the surgical team truly showed their mettle.

Revealing genuine inner strength and courage when things get difficult is exactly what showing one's mettle means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    courage: Courage is the broad willingness to face fear or danger, and a single brave act can show it. Mettle is what hard testing reveals: the reserve of strength and character a person turns out to have when things truly go wrong.

    resolve: Resolve is firm determination to carry out a decision you have already made. Mettle is discovered rather than decided -- the inner strength a person proves only when real difficulty puts them under pressure.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    weakness: lack of strength or resolve

    frailty: a weakness in character

    Where it sits · how deep the inner toughness goes

    backbone → mettle → grit

    backbone vs. mettle: Backbone is a milder toughness than mettle: it takes backbone to say one uncomfortable thing to your boss, while mettle is the deeper courage of character that a real crisis puts to the test.

    grit vs. mettle: Grit runs even deeper than mettle: mettle shows itself in one hard trial, while grit grinds on through months of hardship without ever letting go.

    Word Choice

    Mettle almost always appears in the phrase 'test/show one's mettle' -- it describes an inner quality that only becomes visible under real pressure or hardship, not a trait you simply announce about yourself.
