demoralize

dih-MOR-uh-lyz

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To demoralize is to drain someone of confidence, hope, and discipline together, until they stop believing effort matters.

See the word in context

A string of careless blunders in the final round left the chess player thoroughly demoralized, doubting every move he made afterward.

Weeks of grim news from home demoralized the soldiers long before the battle even began.

The soldiers weren't physically weaker -- their spirit and resolve had been worn down, which is exactly what demoralize describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dishearten: To dishearten someone is to make them lose hope or enthusiasm, usually about one effort -- a rejection letter disheartens. Demoralize cuts deeper and wider: it drains confidence, hope, and discipline together, often across a whole team or unit, until people stop believing effort matters.

    discourage: To discourage someone is to sap their confidence about trying -- often talking them out of attempting something at all. Demoralize describes a heavier collapse: spirit and discipline drain away in people already in the fight, worn down by setbacks rather than warned off beforehand.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    encourage: to give someone confidence or hope

    embolden: to give someone the courage to do something

    Where it sits · how much of someone's spirit it destroys

    unsettle → demoralize → crush

    unsettle vs. demoralize: To unsettle someone is only to make them briefly and mildly uneasy -- far gentler than demoralize, which drains their confidence and discipline away.

    crush vs. demoralize: To crush someone's spirit is to destroy their confidence completely, an even more total blow than demoralize.

    Word Choice

    Demoralize describes a drop in confidence or spirit, not a physical injury -- it's most often used about teams, soldiers, workers, or students facing repeated setbacks.
