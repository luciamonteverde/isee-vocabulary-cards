Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Idyllic is extremely happy or peaceful; charmingly perfect.

See the word in context

They spent an idyllic week at the lake cottage, swimming at dawn and reading on the porch until dark.

In the old painting, the harbor at sunset is idyllic: fishermen mend their nets on the calm shore while children chase gulls along the quiet wall.

The peace is perfect and untroubled from edge to edge of the canvas. Idyllic describes places, times, and scenes that feel almost too lovely to be real.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    picturesque: Picturesque means charmingly pretty -- striking enough to belong in a painting or photo. It judges only how a scene looks. Idyllic judges the whole experience: an idyllic afternoon is peaceful and happy as well as lovely, while a picturesque scene can be gorgeous and gloomy at once.

    unspoiled: Unspoiled describes a place left undamaged -- a beach with no litter, a forest no one has logged. It only says the place has not been ruined. Idyllic goes further: an idyllic spot is charmingly perfect, a scene of complete peace and happiness.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    chaotic: wildly disordered and confusing

    dreary: dull, gloomy, and cheerless

    Where it sits · how deeply joyful the quiet feels

    peaceful → idyllic → blissful

    peaceful vs. idyllic: Peaceful is calmer and plainer than idyllic -- a peaceful afternoon is simply quiet, while an idyllic one is quiet and so charming and happy that it feels like a scene from a poem.

    blissful vs. idyllic: Blissful matches idyllic for happiness and can even exceed it: idyllic paints the whole perfect scene, while blissful zooms in on the feeling of pure, overflowing contentment inside it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Idyllic attaches to places, times, and scenes: an idyllic village, an idyllic childhood, an idyllic honeymoon. Writers often use it with a wistful edge -- the idyllic summer that could not last -- because perfection that complete rarely does.
