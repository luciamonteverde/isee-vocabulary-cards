panacea

pan-uh-SEE-uh

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Panacea is an imagined solution or remedy that would cure every problem or disease.

See the word in context

Company executives hoped the new marketing campaign would be a panacea for years of declining sales, though the numbers barely moved.

City planners knew that a single new bus route would not be a panacea for the whole region's traffic problems.

Panacea is almost always used to point out that no single solution can fix every part of a complicated problem.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cure-all: A cure-all is the plain English name for a single remedy claimed to solve every problem -- the promise painted on a snake-oil bottle. Panacea is its formal, literary twin, and writers mostly use it to deny the idea: a new law is called no panacea for crime.

    miracle solution: A miracle solution is any fix that works amazingly, suspiciously well, even on just one problem -- a stain, a debt, a squeak. A panacea claims far more: one remedy for every problem at once. Something can be a miracle solution without pretending to fix everything.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    partial fix: a solution that addresses only part of a problem

    band-aid solution: a temporary fix that doesn't address the real, underlying problem

    Where it sits · how much one fix claims to solve

    remedy → panacea → silver bullet

    remedy vs. panacea: A remedy is something that relieves or improves one specific problem, a more modest claim than panacea.

    silver bullet vs. panacea: Silver bullet is an idiom for a single, perfectly effective solution, an even more emphatic claim than panacea.

    Word Choice
