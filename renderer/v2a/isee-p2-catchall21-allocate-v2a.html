allocate

AL-uh-kayt

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To allocate is to divide a limited supply -- money, time, space, staff -- into shares and decide how much of it each purpose or person gets.

See the word in context

The relief agency will allocate most of its supplies to the villages hit hardest by the flood.

Before the road trip, Dad allocated forty dollars of the vacation fund to snacks and told us not to ask for more.

Dad took one limited pool, the vacation fund, and decided how large a share of it would go to snacks -- that act of portioning out a supply and fixing each share's purpose is what it means to allocate.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assign: to hand a task, role, or duty to a particular person -- you assign a job or a seat, not a share of a supply; ask whether something is being portioned out of a limited pool.

    earmark: to reserve one specific sum for one specific future use, often officially -- ask whether a single amount was fenced off for a single named purpose rather than a whole supply divided up.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    withhold: to keep back; to refuse to give

    retain: to keep for oneself instead of giving out

    Where it sits

    give → allocate → earmark

    give vs. allocate: To give simply hands something over -- to allocate first divides a limited supply into shares and decides how much of it each purpose will get.

    earmark vs. allocate: To earmark is even more specific than to allocate -- it locks one particular sum to one particular use, often officially, while allocating deals out the whole pool.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Allocate is a practical, planning word -- you allocate money, time, staff, or supplies to a purpose or person. Its tone is matter-of-fact: it describes the careful dividing of a limited pool into shares, not generosity or stinginess.
