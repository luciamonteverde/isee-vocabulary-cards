tawdry

TAW-dree

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Tawdry describes something that is showy but cheaply made and poor in quality — glitter and bright trim laid over shoddy material — so that the attempt to look fancy only makes the cheapness more obvious.

See the word in context

The prize at the carnival booth was a tawdry plastic crown whose gold paint was already flaking off.

The old motel tried to look glamorous, with a blinking neon sign and gold-painted columns, but the stained carpets and cracked mirrors made the whole place feel tawdry.

Everything about the motel is reaching for glamour — the neon, the gold paint — and everything underneath is cheap and worn out: stained carpet, cracked mirrors. That gap is what makes it tawdry. Show alone would only be overdone; wear alone would only be shabby. Tawdry is the two together: poor quality showing through a fancy front.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vulgar: Vulgar means crude and lacking in good taste — coarse jokes, rude language, a loud show of money — and it fits behavior and words as easily as things. Tawdry is narrower: it needs something cheaply made that is dressed up to look fancy, so the poor quality shows through. (On a test, vulgar is the closest single match for tawdry.) Ask whether the fault is crude taste in general, or cheap glitter over poor quality.

    garish: Garish is about color and light that are too harsh and bright — neon on neon, colors that clash and glare. A garish thing can be expensive and well made; it simply hurts to look at. Tawdry is about quality: cheap material trying to pass as fancy, and a tawdry thing can be faded and dim rather than bright. Ask whether the problem is the glare or the cheapness.

    tacky: Tacky is the everyday word for poor taste in things and style — a plastic trophy, a novelty tie — and a tacky thing can be brand new and solidly made. Tawdry adds poor quality and wear: the thing is cheaply made and falling short of the glamour it reaches for. Ask whether the sentence shows poor quality, or only poor taste.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    elegant: graceful, refined, and simple in a way that shows real quality

    tasteful: showing good judgment about what looks right

    Where it sits · how badly the show fails

    flashy → gaudy → tawdry

    flashy vs. tawdry: Flashy is the mildest of the three — bright, glittering, and new, meant to catch eyes and win admiration, like a chrome-trimmed sports car. Tawdry has failed at that: the glitter is cheap, the material is poor, and the thing looks worn and shoddy instead of impressive. Ask whether the shine still impresses anyone, or the cheapness has shown through.

    gaudy vs. tawdry: Gaudy means piled with too much decoration, showy and in poor taste, and it says nothing about cost — a gold-plated bathtub can be gaudy and worth a fortune. Tawdry adds cheapness: the material is poor, the make is shoddy, and the glitter is trying to hide it. Ask whether the thing is merely overdone, or overdone AND cheaply made.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Tawdry is always an insult; there is no admiring way to use it. It began as 'tawdry lace', a cheap ribbon sold at country fairs, and it still carries that fairground feeling of glitter that turns out to be junk. By extension, writers call behavior tawdry when it is cheap and shameful in the same way — a tawdry scandal. On the ISEE, expect vulgar as the closest single match; the difference this card teaches is that vulgar is any crude lack of taste, while tawdry needs cheap glitter over poor quality.
