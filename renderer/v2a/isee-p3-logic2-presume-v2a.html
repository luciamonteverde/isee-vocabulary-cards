Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To presume something is to take it as true because it is the likely or usual case — treating it as settled, without checking, unless and until something shows otherwise.

See the word in context

When the email arrived signed 'Dr. Okafor,' the clinic receptionist presumed the writer was a physician and booked her a seat at the medical conference, never guessing that Dr. Okafor taught history.

In an American courtroom, a person charged with a crime is presumed innocent, and it is the prosecutor's job, not the defendant's, to prove otherwise.

Nobody in the courtroom has checked whether this particular person is innocent; the court simply starts from innocence because that is the position the law treats as the usual, settled one. The belief holds as the starting point and stays in force until proof knocks it down — which is exactly what the phrase 'until proven otherwise' means. Taking something as true from the outset and letting it stand until something shows it to be false is what presume names, and the courtroom is the word's most famous home.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assume: To assume is to take something as true without checking, and it asks for no reason at all — you can assume for the sake of argument ('assume the train never stops'), assume out of habit, or assume wrongly with nothing behind it. Presume leans on likelihood: you presume what is probably true, and the belief stands until something knocks it down. Ask whether the sentence backs the belief with the way things usually go (presume) or takes it for granted with no such backing, perhaps only to see where it leads (assume).

    suppose: To suppose is softer: you think something is probably so but hold it loosely — 'I suppose so' — or you try it out as a what-if ('suppose we left right now'). To presume is to treat the thing as settled and act on it until it is shown to be wrong. Ask whether the belief is held loosely or floated as a what-if (suppose) or treated as settled and acted on (presume).

    infer: To infer is to work your way to a conclusion from clues — the muddy boots by the door, the empty parking lot — and the working-out is the whole point. To presume leaves the working-out out: you take the likely case as given without weighing anything. Ask whether the sentence shows someone reasoning from evidence (infer) or simply taking the usual case for granted (presume).

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    verify: to check that something is true before treating it as true

    doubt: to hold back from believing something until it is shown

    Where it sits · how settled the belief is treated as being

    suspect → presume → conclude

    suspect vs. presume: Suspect is the lower step — you think something may be true, but doubt sits right beside the thought, and you would not act on it yet. To presume is to treat the belief as settled: you go ahead and act on it. Ask whether the sentence keeps doubt in view (suspect) or treats the belief as good enough to act on (presume).

    conclude vs. presume: Conclude is the far step — you decide something is true after weighing the evidence, and the thinking is finished. To presume is to treat something as true before any weighing, because it is the usual case. Ask whether the belief comes after the evidence has been weighed (conclude) or before, as the natural starting point (presume).

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Presume is a shade more formal than its everyday cousins, and it carries a quiet confidence: the presumer is not unsure, just has not checked. It turns up in polite questions — 'I presume you have met?' — where the speaker signals that the thing is so obvious it hardly needs asking, and in fixed legal phrases like 'presumed innocent' and 'presumed dead,' where a belief is given official standing until evidence knocks it down. Because the word admits that no checking was done, it often appears with a twist: 'we presumed the bridge was open' is usually the setup for discovering it was not.
