emancipate

ih-MAN-suh-payt

What It Means

To emancipate is to free a group or class of people from legal, social, or political restrictions through law or official proclamation.

See the word in context

The reform movement worked for decades to emancipate married women from outdated property laws.

The proclamation aimed to emancipate an entire class of laborers bound by generations of debt.

This wasn't a small policy change -- it was a sweeping legal liberation freeing an entire group from restriction, which is exactly emancipate.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    liberate: To liberate is to set someone or something free from control or confinement — armies liberate cities, a key liberates a prisoner — often by direct action. To emancipate is narrower and more formal: freeing a person or group from binding legal or social restrictions through law. Liberation can happen in an afternoon; emancipation is written into law.

    free: Free is the everyday, all-purpose word for releasing someone or something from whatever holds it back — a stuck drawer, a heavy debt, a cage. To emancipate is far more specific and formal: an act of law that releases people from legal, social, or political restrictions. Anything can be freed; only the legally bound are emancipated.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    oppress: keep someone under unjust or harsh control

    Where it sits · how big and formal the liberation

    manumit → emancipate → enfranchise

    manumit vs. emancipate: Manumit — this unit's own word — is the smaller act: a single owner formally releasing the individuals he holds, one document at a time. Emancipating goes further, freeing an entire class of people at once by law or proclamation.

    enfranchise vs. emancipate: Enfranchising outdoes even emancipating: emancipation lifts the legal restraints from a group, while enfranchisement goes on to grant its members full civic rights — above all the vote — the most sweeping and formal liberation of the three.
