What It Means

To atone is to make amends or take action to right a wrong one has done.

See the word in context

Desperate to atone for missing his daughter's recital, Dad took the whole family on a special weekend trip she'd been begging for.

To atone for the prank that had gotten his friend suspended, Malik went to the principal himself and admitted the whole plan had been his idea.

Atone describes taking real action to make up for a wrong -- not just apologizing, but doing something to repair the damage.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    redress: To redress a wrong is to set the situation itself right -- returning what was taken, correcting the unfair rule, compensating the injured person. Redress fixes the outside situation; to atone is more personal -- the wrongdoer makes amends for their own act, and you can atone even when the damage cannot be undone.

    repair: To repair is to mend the damaged thing itself -- a fence, a friendship, a reputation -- so it works again. Repair is about the object; to atone is about the person who caused the harm, who still owes amends even when the thing itself can never be mended.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    repeat: to do the same wrong thing again, rather than making up for it

    ignore: to pay no attention to, without addressing a wrong done

    Where it sits · how much real repair gets done

    apologize → atone → restitution

    apologize vs. atone: Apologizing is weaker than atoning: a quick apology only acknowledges a mistake in words, while to atone you must go further and actually act to repair the wrong.

    restitution vs. atone: Making full restitution -- actually paying back every dollar taken -- goes even further than an ordinary act of atoning.
