sobriety

Meaning

What It Means

Sobriety is grave seriousness of manner joined to clear-headed judgment, free of any silliness.

See the word in context

She answered the reporters' questions with a sobriety that ended the joking at once.

The rest of the crew is still trading jokes when the pilot's voice comes over the radio with total sobriety -- level and unhurried, giving the fuel figure and the nearest airfield in the same steady tone she uses for the weather.

Sobriety is seriousness joined to clear-headedness. It is not gloom -- it is the calm, unrattled gravity of someone who understands exactly how much a moment matters.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    levelheadedness: Levelheadedness is calm good judgment when things go wrong -- the knack for thinking clearly under pressure. Sobriety includes that clear head but adds a grave, no-nonsense manner: a levelheaded person may still crack jokes; sobriety has put the jokes away.

    gravity: Gravity is the weight a manner or moment carries -- the hush of a courtroom, the slow voice of bad news. Sobriety is gravity joined to a working, clear-headed judgment: gravity says how serious it feels; sobriety says the person is serious and still thinking straight.

    composure: Composure is keeping your self-control when events press hard on you -- not panicking, not raising your voice. Sobriety is less about staying unrattled and more about the manner itself: grave, free of silliness, weighing what the moment means.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    frivolity: lack of seriousness

    giddiness: light-headed silliness

    Where it sits · how heavy and clear-headed the manner is

    seriousness → sobriety → solemnity

    seriousness vs. sobriety: Seriousness is the plain word for the same manner; sobriety adds clear-headedness to it -- a serious person may still panic, while someone who meets a crisis with sobriety keeps their judgment intact.

    solemnity vs. sobriety: Solemnity is heavier and more ceremonial: a memorial is conducted with solemnity, but a surgeon assesses a difficult case with sobriety -- grave, yes, but still working.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Sobriety has two lives. In health and law it means abstaining from alcohol. In the sense taught here -- the one the ISEE tests -- it means seriousness and clear-headedness: 'she assessed the damage with sobriety,' 'a moment that called for sobriety.' Read the surrounding sentence to tell which sense is meant; if the passage is about manner and judgment rather than drinking, it is this one.
