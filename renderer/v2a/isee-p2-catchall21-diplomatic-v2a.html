diplomatic

dip-luh-MAT-ik

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Diplomatic describes the business that the government of one country carries on with the government of another — the talks, visits, agreements and representatives through which one nation deals with another.

See the word in context

The two countries broke off diplomatic contact after the border dispute, and each ordered the other's ambassador to leave within a week.

Marisol's aunt works at the embassy in Tokyo, where her diplomatic duties include setting up meetings between American officials and their Japanese counterparts.

The aunt's job is the word in action: she works for her own government inside another country, and her whole task is helping the two governments talk to each other. Business between one nation's government and another's — that is what diplomatic means. Her work is also political, since governments are involved, and international, since it crosses a border; but only diplomatic says that it is governments dealing with governments.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foreign: Foreign means belonging to another country — a foreign language, a foreign car, a foreign visitor. It tells you where something comes from, not what is being done. Diplomatic describes what the governments of different countries do with each other. Ask whether the word points at something from another country or at two governments working with each other.

    official: Official means approved by or coming from whoever is in charge — a school's official schedule, a team's official uniform, a company's official statement. It can belong to any group at all. Diplomatic is official business of one particular kind: between countries. Ask whether more than one country's government is involved.

    formal: Formal means following set rules and proper ceremony — a formal dinner, a formal letter, formal clothes. Diplomatic business is usually formal, but formal by itself says nothing about countries; a wedding is formal too. Ask whether the ceremony is happening between the governments of different nations or simply between people.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    domestic: happening inside one country's own borders, with no other nation involved

    private: belonging to an individual or a family rather than to any government

    Where it sits · how narrowly the word points at nations dealing with one another

    political → international → diplomatic

    political vs. diplomatic: Political is the most general word — anything to do with government, elections, parties and power, even inside a single town. Diplomatic is one slice of that world: the part where the government of one country deals with the government of another. Ask how many countries' governments are involved; if the answer is one, the matter is political but not diplomatic.

    international vs. diplomatic: International means crossing the borders between countries in any way — an international flight, an international soccer tournament, an international company. Diplomatic limits that to governments dealing with governments through the people they send to speak for them. Ask whether it is governments talking to each other or simply people and goods moving between countries.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Diplomatic has a second, everyday meaning that grew out of the first. Because the people who do this work must keep talks going without anyone walking out, a diplomatic person is one who handles a delicate situation so carefully that no one is offended — and several other cards in this course use the word that way. On the ISEE, though, the closest match offered for diplomatic is political — the countries sense. This card teaches that sense, so when you meet diplomatic, first ask which of the two is in play.
