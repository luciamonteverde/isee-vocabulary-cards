oppressive

uh-PRESS-iv

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Oppressive describes power, rules, or conditions that press down on the people under them so hard and so unfairly that they cannot live or act freely — a government that jails its critics, a boss who fines workers for talking. The weight comes from above and stays on; what makes it oppressive is what it does to the people beneath it, not the manner of whoever imposes it.

See the word in context

Under the oppressive new curfew, no one in the village could leave home after sunset, and soldiers checked every doorway.

The factory's oppressive rules — a ten-minute lunch, fines for talking, and shifts that ran fourteen hours — left the workers too worn down to complain.

The rules are oppressive because they press down on the workers from above, unfairly and without let-up, until the workers have no room left to live their own lives or push back. Notice that no single cruel person is named; the system itself is the weight. Unfair, heavy, and kept on the people beneath — that is oppressive.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    harsh: Harsh means rough, severe, or cutting — harsh words sting, a harsh winter bites, harsh light hurts the eyes — and it is over as soon as its cause is. Oppressive is a weight rather than a sting: it presses down on people from above and keeps pressing, so that their freedom shrinks. Ask whether the sentence describes something that stings or cuts, or something that bears down on people and stays; only the second is oppressive.

    repressive: Repressive describes power used to shut people up — banning newspapers, breaking up protests, punishing anyone who speaks against the leader. It is about silencing. Oppressive is wider: it covers any unfair weight on people's lives — crushing taxes, endless work, no right to move about — whether or not anyone is silenced. Ask whether the harm is specifically that people cannot speak or protest; if the weight falls on their whole way of life, the word is oppressive.

    onerous: Onerous describes a task or duty that is a heavier burden than it fairly should be — onerous paperwork, an onerous fee — and it stays with the job in front of you. Oppressive goes further: the burden is so harsh it feels crushing, and it lands on people's lives, not just on one task. Ask whether the sentence is about one heavy duty or about people held down under a weight they cannot escape; only the second is oppressive.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lenient: easy on people, letting the rules bend

    liberating: setting people free to act as they choose

    Where it sits · how harshly power bears down on the people under it

    strict → oppressive → tyrannical

    strict vs. oppressive: Strict means demanding that the rules be followed exactly, and it can be perfectly fair — a strict teacher grades hard and treats everyone the same. Oppressive rules are unfair as well as firm: they weigh people down and take their freedom away. Ask whether the rules are simply enforced to the letter or whether they crush the people under them; only the second is oppressive.

    tyrannical vs. oppressive: Tyrannical goes further and points at a person: a tyrannical ruler uses power in a cruel way, personally, to get their own way. Oppressive can describe a system, a law, or a workload — a weight with no single villain behind it. Ask whether the sentence names a cruel ruler wielding power or describes the weight on the people beneath; a whole system that crushes people is oppressive even when no one person is to blame.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Oppressive is the word for power felt from underneath — you meet it in history and in news about governments, landlords, bosses, and laws, and it always carries a judgment: the weight is unfair. The word reaches beyond people too: oppressive heat or an oppressive silence presses on you the same way, heavy and hard to escape, though nobody is to blame for the weather. This card teaches the power sense, the one your unit is about. Related forms: oppress (the verb), oppression (the condition), the oppressed (the people under it).
