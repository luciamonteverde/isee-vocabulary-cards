Meaning

What It Means

Reprobate is a person without principles or scruples; an unprincipled person.

See the word in context

The old sailor was known throughout the port as a lovable reprobate.

Everyone in town had a story about the old reprobate who'd gambled away three fortunes and never once apologized.

A person with a long history of unprincipled, disreputable behavior -- described almost fondly here -- is exactly what a reprobate is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scoundrel: A scoundrel is a dishonest schemer -- someone who cheats and lies to get what he wants. A reprobate need not deceive anyone: the word marks a person as living without moral principles at all, and it is old-fashioned enough to be said half-fondly.

    rascal: A rascal is a mischief-maker whose tricks stay mostly harmless -- the word is light and often affectionate. Calling someone a reprobate goes much further: it says the person has no real principles, even when it is said with a smile.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    saint: a person of great moral virtue

    paragon: a model of excellence or virtue

    Where it sits · how deeply immoral the person is

    troublemaker → reprobate → villain

    troublemaker vs. reprobate: A troublemaker causes minor mischief; a reprobate is someone genuinely without moral principles.

    villain vs. reprobate: A villain goes further than a reprobate: a reprobate merely lives without principles, while a villain sets out to do real harm to others.
