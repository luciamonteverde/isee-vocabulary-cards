What It Means

Ambivalent means holding two strong, opposite feelings about something at the same time.

See the word in context

Ambivalent about the move, Sam packed his boxes slowly, torn between excitement and dread.

The critic felt ambivalent about the sequel, admiring its visuals while feeling let down by its rushed ending.

Ambivalent describes genuinely mixed feelings held at the same time -- not simply not caring, but real internal conflict.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    conflicted: Conflicted stresses the struggle: opposing feelings wrestling inside you, often with guilt or unease about the clash itself. Ambivalent is the cooler, steadier state of holding two opposite feelings about one thing at once -- a person can be calmly ambivalent, but conflicted always implies an active fight.

    torn: Torn means pulled hard in two directions at once, usually at the moment of choosing -- the tug is urgent and even painful. Ambivalent is quieter and can last for years: you hold both feelings side by side without any tug-of-war forcing a choice. Torn is the yank; ambivalent is the standing mixture.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    certain: having no doubt at all about a feeling or decision

    unequivocal: leaving no doubt; completely clear

    Where it sits · how painful the inner conflict feels

    unsure → ambivalent → anguished

    unsure vs. ambivalent: Unsure is much milder than ambivalent: feeling unsure about which movie to watch is a small, passing wobble, while to be ambivalent is to hold two strong, opposite feelings at the same time.

    anguished vs. ambivalent: Anguished goes beyond ambivalent: an ambivalent person holds mixed feelings without necessarily suffering, but to feel anguished is to be in real emotional pain over the conflict.
