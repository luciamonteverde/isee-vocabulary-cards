What It Means

Novice is a person who is new to and inexperienced in a particular activity or role.

See the word in context

As a novice in the kitchen, he burned the toast twice before breakfast was even ready.

A freshly hired lifeguard sits nervously at her first shift, a novice still unsure how to use the rescue equipment that her veteran coworkers handle without a second thought.

That inexperienced newcomer, still learning the basics everyone else already knows, is what a novice is. Unlike 'expert,' a novice is only beginning.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    amateur: An amateur does something for enjoyment rather than as a paid professional, and can be very skilled -- a lifelong amateur photographer may be excellent. A novice is defined by inexperience: someone still learning the basics, paid or not.

    newcomer: A newcomer is someone who has just arrived in a place or group -- a new neighbor, a new hire. A novice is new to a skill or role specifically: the word points at inexperience with the activity itself, not just recent arrival.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    expert: a person with great skill or knowledge in a field

    veteran: a person with long experience in a particular field

    Where it sits · how formal or casual the label is

    beginner → novice → rookie

    beginner vs. novice: Beginner is a bit more neutral than novice -- beginner simply states someone is starting out, while novice can carry a slightly more formal, and sometimes mildly critical, tone about someone's inexperience.

    rookie vs. novice: Rookie can feel more casual than novice: novice is a general, formal term for inexperience, while rookie specifically evokes a first-year athlete or employee still proving themselves.
