What It Means

A contaminant is a substance that does not belong where it is found and makes something impure, polluted, or unsafe.

See the word in context

The recall notice warned that a single batch of the product may have contained a chemical contaminant introduced during packaging.

Before the astronauts could return to Earth, their equipment was thoroughly checked for any contaminant that might have hitched a ride from the lunar surface.

A contaminant is a substance that does not belong where it is found and makes something impure or dangerous -- here, a possible foreign substance that could pollute Earth's environment.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    pollutant: A pollutant is a harmful substance loose in the shared environment -- exhaust in the air, chemicals seeping into a river. A contaminant can turn up anywhere purity matters: a water supply, a food batch, a hospital sample. Every pollutant is a contaminant of the environment; most contaminants never reach that scale.

    impurity: An impurity makes a substance less pure without necessarily making it harmful -- a fleck of copper in gold, minerals clouding tap water. Contaminant is the more alarming word: it says the substance does not belong at all and may make the thing polluted or unsafe, not merely imperfect.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    purifier: something that removes impurities and makes a substance clean

    disinfectant: a substance used to destroy harmful germs and keep something clean

    Where it sits · how dangerous the substance is

    residue → contaminant → toxin

    residue vs. contaminant: A residue is often just a small, harmless trace left behind, without necessarily being dangerous like a true contaminant.

    toxin vs. contaminant: A toxin is a substance poisonous enough to cause serious harm or death, a far more dangerous contaminant than average.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Contaminant is mostly a technical or scientific word, used for substances found in water, food, air, or samples -- you're far more likely to see it in a lab report or news article than in casual conversation.
