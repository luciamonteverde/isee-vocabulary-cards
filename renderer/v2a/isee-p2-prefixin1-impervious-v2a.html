impervious

im-PUR-vee-us -- stress on the second syllable, PUR.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impervious is unable to be passed through or affected by whatever reaches it, no matter how long the pressure lasts.

See the word in context

The volcano's slopes are paved in rock impervious to water, so every rainstorm runs straight off into the sea.

A referee keeps a steady hand through ninety minutes of booing, players shouting in her face, and a coach hurling his clipboard -- impervious to all of it, she calls the final play exactly as she saw it.

None of it gets through to her. Impervious began as a word for materials that water cannot penetrate and grew into a word for people that insults, criticism, or panic cannot penetrate either. Notice that it almost always brings the little word 'to' with it: impervious to water, impervious to pressure.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impermeable: Impermeable is the laboratory word for one specific test: liquid cannot pass through the material. Impervious covers far more ground -- water, heat, insults, panic; nothing gets through, whether the barrier is a raincoat or a person's calm. Every impermeable membrane is impervious to liquid, but impervious does not stop at liquids.

    resistant: Resistant means you fight an influence off, and resistance can be worn down -- a resistant strain, a resistant listener, each holding out for now. Impervious is stronger: nothing gets through at all, no matter how long the pressure lasts. Resistance is a battle; being impervious means there was never a battle to win.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permeable: allowing liquids or gases to pass through

    susceptible: easily affected or influenced

    Where it sits · how completely outside forces are kept out

    unaffected → impervious → impenetrable

    unaffected vs. impervious: Unaffected is the milder, more everyday word -- an unaffected listener simply is not moved this time, while an impervious one could not be moved however long you kept trying.

    impenetrable vs. impervious: Impenetrable presses impervious to its physical extreme: an impervious jacket keeps the rain out, while an impenetrable wall keeps out everything -- water, light, sound, and people alike.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Impervious nearly always takes the preposition 'to': impervious to water, impervious to heat, impervious to criticism, impervious to reason. If you write it without 'to,' the sentence will usually sound unfinished. The physical sense (nothing gets through the material) and the human sense (nothing gets through to the person) are equally common.
