rebuke

rih-BYOOK -- from Old French, literally 'to beat back': a rebuke beats back bad behavior with sharp words.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To rebuke is to scold or criticize someone sharply.

See the word in context

The judge paused the trial to rebuke the lawyer for interrupting the witness again.

When a cadet shows up late with a sloppy uniform, the drill sergeant plants himself an inch away and rebukes him with thirty seconds of ice-cold, cutting words that leave the whole line silent.

That sharp, stern scolding is a rebuke in full. Unlike 'nag,' which drips criticism over time, a rebuke lands all at once, hard and direct.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    admonish: To admonish is to correct someone earnestly, with a warning attached -- firm but caring, eyes on the future. A rebuke looks backward at what was just done wrong and strikes sharply, with little softening in it.

    chide: To chide is to scold mildly, often half-smiling -- over muddy boots or a lost umbrella. A rebuke sits at the other end of the same act: sharp, stern, and delivered with real weight, meant to sting.

    berate: To berate is to scold angrily and at length, hammering away on and on. A rebuke lands all at once -- one hard, direct hit -- and it need not even be loud; a judge's single icy sentence can be a rebuke.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: to express warm approval

    commend: to praise formally

    Where it sits · how official the criticism is

    scold → rebuke → reprimand

    scold vs. rebuke: Scold is the everyday, home-and-classroom version of a rebuke -- an annoyed parent scolds a child over chores, while a rebuke comes down more sharply and more formally, often from a judge or a commanding officer.

    reprimand vs. rebuke: Reprimand is more official than rebuke: a rebuke is any sharp scolding, while a reprimand is typically formal discipline, sometimes entered into an employee's record.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Rebuke usually takes a person as its object -- you rebuke someone for something. Some word lists tag rebuke as a noun (as in 'a sharp rebuke'), but it is taught here as the verb, 'to scold sharply.'
