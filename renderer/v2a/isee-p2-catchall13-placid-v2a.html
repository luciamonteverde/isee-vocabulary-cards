placid

PLASS-id -- Latin placidus, 'gentle, quiet,' from placere, 'to please': the same root as please and pleasant.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Placid is not easily upset or excited.

See the word in context

Our placid old dog sleeps through thunderstorms and fireworks alike.

A toddler knocks a full glass of juice across the table, and the placid babysitter simply reaches for a towel, still smiling, without a single word of complaint.

Staying untroubled when most people would be rattled is what makes the babysitter placid. Unlike 'patient,' which is about waiting, placid is about not getting stirred up in the first place.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    untroubled: Untroubled means showing no sign of worry at this moment -- an untroubled sleep, an untroubled expression. Placid goes deeper than the moment: a placid person or animal has a settled temperament that stays even as a rule, not just for one easy hour.

    serene: Serene means peacefully calm with a touch of quiet beauty -- a serene lake at dawn, a serene smile. Placid is plainer and more practical: it points at a temperament that is not easily upset, while serene paints the peaceful mood itself.

    easygoing: Easygoing means relaxed about plans, rules, and other people's choices. Placid is about what happens when something goes wrong: a placid person is not easily upset or excited, while an easygoing one simply doesn't mind changes of plan -- and may still panic in a crisis.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    agitated: stirred up and restless

    excitable: quick to get worked up

    Where it sits · how hard it is to rattle

    calm → placid → unflappable

    calm vs. placid: Calm is the plain, general word -- anyone can be calm for a moment, while placid describes a settled temperament that stays calm as a rule.

    unflappable vs. placid: Unflappable is placid tested and proved: placid means not easily upset, while unflappable means nothing whatsoever can rattle you, even in an emergency.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Placid describes people and animals with even temperaments, and also still water -- 'a placid lake.' Applied to a person it is usually a compliment, though it can hint that someone is a little too unbothered to react when they should.
