What It Means

To sanction is to formally approve or authorize something, or to formally punish or penalize something -- a word with two nearly opposite meanings, depending on context.

See the word in context

The international committee voted to sanction the country's Olympic team, banning it from the next three competitions.

The board voted to formally sanction the merger, giving their official blessing for the two companies to combine.

Sanction is one of the rare English words with two nearly opposite meanings: it can mean to formally approve or permit something (as here), or to formally punish or penalize something (as in economic sanctions against a country). Always check the context to know which sense is meant.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    penalize: To penalize is to punish someone for breaking a rule, and it only ever points that one way. To sanction can mean the same formal punishing, but sanction cuts both ways: an authority can also sanction a plan by approving it. Penalize never carries that approving sense.

    authorize: To authorize is to give official permission before something happens -- a single, unambiguous meaning. To sanction, in its approving sense, is the same formal green light, but sanction can also mean the opposite, to punish. When you only mean permission, authorize is the safer word.

    fine: To fine is to punish with a required money payment -- one specific kind of penalty. To sanction is broader: an authority that decides to sanction wrongdoing may choose any formal penalty at all, from docked points to suspended permits, with or without money changing hands.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    pardon: to forgive or excuse someone for an offense -- opposite of formally punishing

    forbid: to formally refuse to allow something -- opposite of formally approving

    Where it sits · how harsh the official response is

    warn → sanction → blacklist

    warn vs. sanction: To warn is only to caution someone that a penalty may follow -- much milder than to sanction, which actually imposes the punishment.

    blacklist vs. sanction: To blacklist is to formally exclude or ban someone from a group or activity as punishment, an even harsher step than a single sanction.
