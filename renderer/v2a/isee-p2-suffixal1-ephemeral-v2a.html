ephemeral

ih-FEM-er-ul -- from ephemera ('things used or enjoyed only briefly') + -al.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To be ephemeral is to last only a very short time -- the thing's whole life is strikingly short, blooming and fading rather than standing in for something permanent.

See the word in context

Cherry blossoms are prized precisely because they're ephemeral -- the pink flowers last barely a week before falling.

Social media trends feel ephemeral: a joke or dance that seems inescapable in April is often completely forgotten by June.

Ephemeral emphasizes the brief, temporary nature of something -- it draws attention to how quickly a thing will fade or disappear, often making it feel more precious.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    passing: A passing thing is moving through a longer scene -- a passing shower, a passing phase -- an interruption that fades and lets things return to normal. Ephemeral looks at the thing itself: its whole life is built to be brief, and that vanishing is part of its beauty.

    temporary: Temporary means lasting a limited time on purpose, usually with a planned end -- a temporary bridge, a temporary job -- often standing in until something permanent arrives. Ephemeral things are not placeholders; they simply bloom and fade, and their shortness makes them precious.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permanent: lasting forever or for a very long time

    everlasting: never ending

    Where it sits · how quickly it comes and goes

    brief → ephemeral → momentary

    brief vs. ephemeral: Brief is the mild, everyday word for anything short -- a brief pause, a brief meeting -- and it claims nothing beyond length. Ephemeral goes further: the thing's whole life is strikingly short -- days, sometimes hours -- and that vanishing is the point.

    momentary vs. ephemeral: Momentary suggests something lasting barely an instant, even shorter than merely ephemeral.
