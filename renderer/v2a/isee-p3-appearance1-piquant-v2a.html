What It Means

Piquant is having a pleasantly sharp or spicy taste; also, pleasantly stimulating or intriguing.

See the word in context

The novel's piquant plot twist, revealed in the very last paragraph, sent readers straight back to page one.

A single piquant slice of pickled ginger cut through the richness of the fatty fish perfectly.

The ginger wasn't overwhelming -- it added a sharp, zesty kick that made the whole dish more interesting, which is what piquant describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    zesty: Zesty describes a bright, lively kick — the fresh burst that lemon peel or a good spice blend gives a dish. Piquant covers that same pleasant sharpness, but it reaches further: a plot twist or a clever remark can be piquant, while zesty stays in the kitchen.

    tangy: Tangy means sharp in a distinctly sour way — yogurt, citrus, and vinegar are tangy. Piquant is broader: any pleasant sharpness counts, spicy or sour, in food or even in a story, so a tangy sauce is piquant, but a spicy one is piquant without being tangy.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bland: lacking flavor or interest

    insipid: disappointingly dull and lifeless

    Where it sits · how much kick the flavor has

    mild → piquant → fiery

    mild vs. piquant: A mild flavor barely stands out at all, much less noticeable than a piquant one.

    fiery vs. piquant: A fiery flavor burns and overwhelms the tongue, far more intense than a merely piquant kick.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Piquant most often describes a pleasant sharpness -- in food, in conversation, or in a detail of a story -- unlike acrid or pungent, which describe something far more overwhelming to the senses.
