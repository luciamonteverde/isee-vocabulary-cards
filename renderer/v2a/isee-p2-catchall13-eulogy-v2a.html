eulogy

YOO-luh-jee -- Greek eu- ('good, well') plus logos ('word, speech'): good words about someone.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A eulogy is a whole speech or piece of writing devoted to high praise of one person.

See the word in context

Her essay was less a review than a eulogy for a writer she clearly adored.

At the end of the season the team captain stands up at the banquet and delivers a five-minute eulogy for the retiring equipment manager, describing everything he has quietly done for twenty years.

A speech given over entirely to praise is what makes those five minutes a eulogy. Unlike an 'introduction,' which simply presents someone, a eulogy exists to honor them.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    commendation: A commendation is official recognition of someone's worth, issued by an authority -- a general commends a soldier, a school board commends a teacher. A eulogy needs no authority behind it: it is a whole speech or piece of writing given over to praising someone, personal and warm rather than official.

    compliment: A compliment is a brief, friendly remark of praise -- one nice sentence about someone's work or shoes, over in a moment. A eulogy is praise built to last: a whole speech or written piece honoring one person, the kind you draft and polish for a real occasion.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    criticism: words pointing out faults

    insult: words meant to offend

    Where it sits · how large and ceremonial the honoring grows

    praise → eulogy → tribute

    praise vs. eulogy: Praise is the everyday word and can be a single sentence, while a eulogy is a whole speech or piece of writing devoted to praising one person.

    tribute vs. eulogy: Tribute is the more ceremonial word: a eulogy is praise in words, while a tribute can be a statue, a concert, or a moment of silence as easily as a speech.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    A eulogy is most familiar as the speech given at a funeral, but the word covers any whole speech or written piece of high praise, for the living as well as the dead. Do not confuse it with elegy, a sorrowful poem of mourning.
