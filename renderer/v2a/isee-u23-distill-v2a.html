distill

dih-STIL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Distill means to boil something down to its purest, most concentrated form: literally, by heating a liquid and collecting only the vapor that rises off it; figuratively, by working through a whole mass of facts, notes, or experience and keeping only its concentrated heart — often a single idea, rule, or lesson — while everything else is left behind.

See the word in context

Over the winter the coach distilled three seasons of game film into a single page of plays that actually worked.

In the lab, Priya distilled a cup of salt water by boiling it in a flask and catching the steam in a cooled tube, ending up with a few spoonfuls of pure water and a crust of salt left behind.

The water Priya ends up with is far less than she started with, but it is the pure part — the salt never made it into the tube. That is the literal picture behind every use of distill: heat drives off the essence and the rest stays in the flask. When someone distills a long book into one sentence, the same thing has happened to ideas.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    refine: To refine something is to improve it by removing its flaws or impurities, step by step — refined sugar, a refined plan — and the whole thing is still there at the end, just cleaner and better. To distill is to keep only a small, concentrated core and leave the bulk behind. Ask whether the result is the same thing made better or a much smaller part drawn out of it.

    summarize: To summarize is to give the main points of something in less space, walking through the whole in order. To distill is to go further and keep only the concentrated heart of it — often one idea or one line. Ask whether the result still covers all the main points or has been boiled down to a single strong core.

    extract: To extract is to pull out something that was already sitting there whole — a tooth, a quote, the juice from an orange. To distill is to make the pure part by boiling the rest away; the result did not exist as a piece until the work was done. Ask whether the thing was lifted out or made by cooking down the whole.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dilute: to weaken something by adding water or filler

    expand: to make something bigger or longer by adding to it

    Where it sits · how much of the original is boiled away to reach its core

    shorten → condense → distill

    shorten vs. distill: Shorten is the mildest word — you cut some length off and the rest stays as it was, no stronger than before. Distill keeps only the concentrated core. Ask whether anything about the result is purer or more powerful than the original, or whether it is simply cut down.

    condense vs. distill: Condense squeezes everything into a smaller space — a condensed report still has all its parts, packed tight. Distill drops most of the material on purpose and keeps only the essence. Ask whether anything was left out on purpose.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Distill is a word for careful reduction, and it always implies that what remains is better and stronger than the mess it came from — a distilled version of a speech is the part worth keeping. Use it when someone has worked through a lot and come out with a little that matters. It is not the word for simply cutting something short, and it is never used for making something bigger.
