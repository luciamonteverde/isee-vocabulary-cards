illiterate

ih-LIT-er-it -- built from 'literate' (able to read/write) plus the negative prefix il-.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Illiterate is unable to read or write; lacking basic knowledge in a particular subject.

See the word in context

Because the remote village had no school for generations, many older residents remained illiterate their entire lives.

A grandmother, illiterate because no one ever taught her the alphabet as a child, signs important documents with an X instead of her name.

That inability to read or write, often through no fault of one's own, is exactly what illiterate describes. Unlike 'uneducated,' which is broader, illiterate specifically refers to reading and writing.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    uneducated: Uneducated means lacking formal schooling or broad knowledge of any kind. Illiterate is far narrower: it names the specific inability to read or write. An uneducated person may still read perfectly well, and a well-schooled person can be illiterate in one field, like finance.

    ignorant: Ignorant means not knowing something -- anyone can be ignorant of a fact, however clever they are. Illiterate points at one particular missing skill, reading and writing, rather than knowledge in general, and it usually reflects missing teaching, not missing intelligence.

    unread: Unread describes someone who can read but has read very little -- few books, little of anything printed. Illiterate is the sharper condition: the person cannot read or write at all. An unread farmer skips the poetry; an illiterate one cannot read the seed catalog.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    literate: able to read and write

    educated: having received formal schooling and knowledge

    Where it sits · how specific the missing learning is

    unschooled → illiterate → unlettered

    unschooled vs. illiterate: Unschooled is broader than illiterate -- unschooled can mean lacking any formal education at all, while illiterate specifically refers to the inability to read or write.

    unlettered vs. illiterate: Unlettered can feel like a more old-fashioned, literary way of saying illiterate, emphasizing a complete lack of exposure to written language.
