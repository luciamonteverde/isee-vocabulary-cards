jarring

JAR-ing -- from the verb jar, 'to strike with a harsh, grating effect'; imitative origin.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Jarring is clashing or contrasting in a way that is both sudden and unpleasant, breaking an otherwise smooth flow.

See the word in context

Wearing bright yellow sneakers with her formal black interview suit gave off a slightly jarring first impression.

The peaceful nature documentary's soothing narration was suddenly interrupted by a jarring, blaring commercial for a mattress sale, with no warning at all.

That sudden, uncomfortable clash between two things that should flow smoothly together is exactly what jarring describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    discordant: Discordant describes a clash of sounds that refuse to blend -- discordant notes, a discordant choir -- and it stays close to music and voices. Jarring reaches anywhere: a color, a scene, or a shift in mood can be jarring when the clash is sudden and unpleasant.

    startling: Startling means making you jump with sudden surprise, and a startling thing can even be delightful -- a startling gift, a startling recovery. Jarring is never welcome: it is an unpleasant clash, two things grinding against each other where a smooth fit was expected.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmonious: forming a pleasing and consistent whole

    seamless: smooth and continuous, with no noticeable breaks

    Where it sits · how sudden and unpleasant the clash feels

    mismatched → jarring → grating

    mismatched vs. jarring: Mismatched is the mild end of this scale: mismatched socks or curtains simply do not go together, and nobody is shocked. Jarring is harsher -- the clash is sudden and unpleasant enough to break the moment and make people flinch.

    grating vs. jarring: Grating is stronger and more personal than jarring -- it describes something that keeps irritating, wearing on you over time like a grating voice, while jarring is usually a single sudden, shocking clash.
