What It Means

Serendipitous means discovered by happy chance, a valuable find stumbled on without being planned or looked for.

See the word in context

Their book club's serendipitous choice of venue -- a bookstore that turned out to be hosting the very author they were discussing -- became the story they told for years.

Alexander Fleming's discovery of penicillin was famously serendipitous -- he simply noticed that mold had accidentally killed the bacteria in a forgotten petri dish.

Nothing about the discovery was planned; a lucky accident led to one of medicine's biggest breakthroughs, which is exactly what serendipitous describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fortuitous: Fortuitous describes something that happens by pure chance and, in modern use, usually turns out well -- a fortuitous delay that makes you miss a canceled flight. Serendipitous adds discovery: you stumble on something valuable you were never looking for. Chance timing is fortuitous; a chance find is serendipitous.

    accidental: Accidental means unplanned, nothing more -- and an accident can as easily ruin your day as make it. Serendipitous is reserved for the happy kind of chance: an unplanned event or discovery that turns out to be a delight.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    planned: deliberately arranged in advance

    predictable: able to be foreseen or expected

    Where it sits · how fated the happy accident seems

    lucky → serendipitous → providential

    lucky vs. serendipitous: Lucky is a weaker, everyday word than serendipitous: a lucky guess is mere good fortune, while a serendipitous find carries the deeper thrill of a happy, unplanned discovery.

    providential vs. serendipitous: Providential goes further than serendipitous: a serendipitous meeting is a happy accident, but a providential one feels so perfectly timed it seems arranged by fate itself.
