Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Lackadaisical is carelessly lazy and lacking enthusiasm or effort.

See the word in context

The lackadaisical crossing guard barely glanced up from her phone as students crossed the street.

Their lackadaisical defense allowed three easy goals in the first half alone.

The players weren't trying hard or paying attention -- their careless, half-hearted effort is exactly lackadaisical.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    halfhearted: Halfhearted means showing little enthusiasm or effort in one attempt -- a limp apology, a token try. Lackadaisical stretches that into a whole attitude: a careless, can't-be-bothered laziness toward the task itself.

    unenthusiastic: Unenthusiastic simply means showing no interest or eagerness, and an unenthusiastic worker might still do the job correctly. Lackadaisical adds carelessness on top: so little effort and attention that the work itself gets sloppy.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hardworking: showing steady effort and care

    Where it sits · how risky the sloppiness becomes

    casual → lackadaisical → negligent

    casual vs. lackadaisical: A casual approach is simply relaxed, not necessarily careless; a lackadaisical one shows real carelessness and a lack of effort.

    negligent vs. lackadaisical: Lackadaisical effort is careless and low-energy; negligent carelessness is serious enough to cause real harm.

    Word Choice

    Lackadaisical describes an attitude toward a task -- carelessness, low effort, lack of interest -- distinct from this unit's "torpid," "lassitude," "somnolence," and "languor," which describe a physical or mental state rather than an approach to work.
