ubiquitous

yoo-BIK-wih-tus -- built on the Latin word for 'everywhere.'

What It Means

Ubiquitous is present, appearing, or found everywhere.

See the word in context

Free public Wi-Fi has become so ubiquitous in the city that almost every cafe offers it.

A single soft-drink brand's logo is ubiquitous, appearing on billboards, vending machines, restaurant menus, and even stadium scoreboards in nearly every country on Earth.

That overwhelming, everywhere-at-once presence is what ubiquitous means. Unlike 'popular,' which just means widely liked, ubiquitous specifically means found almost everywhere.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    universal: Universal means applying to all cases or all people -- a universal rule holds for everyone. Ubiquitous is about being physically or visibly everywhere; a coffee chain can be ubiquitous without its appeal being universal.

    commonplace: Commonplace means ordinary and unremarkable -- so usual that nobody looks twice. Ubiquitous is a claim about place, not ordinariness: found everywhere you go. Something can be ubiquitous for a week and still feel strange every time.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rare: not found in large numbers; uncommon

    scarce: insufficient for the demand; hard to find

    Where it sits · how total its reach is

    pervasive → ubiquitous → omnipresent

    pervasive vs. ubiquitous: Pervasive is slightly milder than ubiquitous -- pervasive means spreading into every part of an area, group, or culture, while ubiquitous implies an even more total, inescapable presence everywhere at once.

    omnipresent vs. ubiquitous: Omnipresent can feel even more absolute than ubiquitous: ubiquitous describes something found almost everywhere, while omnipresent suggests being present absolutely everywhere, without exception.
