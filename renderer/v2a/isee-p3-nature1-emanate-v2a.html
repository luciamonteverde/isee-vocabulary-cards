EM-uh-nayt

What It Means

To emanate is to spread out or flow continuously from a source, especially of light, heat, sound, or a feeling.

See the word in context

A low hum seemed to emanate from beneath the floorboards, growing louder the closer she walked toward the old furnace room.

Genuine confidence emanated from the veteran pilot as she calmly walked the nervous passengers through the safety procedures.

Emanate describes something spreading out or flowing continuously from a source -- here, a feeling of confidence radiating from a person's calm manner.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    radiate: To radiate is to give off light, heat, or energy outward in all directions -- the sun radiates warmth, and the verb can take the thing given off as its object. To emanate is quieter and always points back at its source with 'from': a smell emanates from the kitchen.

    originate: To originate is to begin or come from a starting point -- the rumor originated in the mailroom, the recipe originated in Sicily. To emanate keeps the flow going: something continuously spreads out from its source rather than simply starting there once.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    absorb: to take something in rather than give it off

    contain: to keep something held inside rather than letting it spread out

    Where it sits · how strongly it pours from its source

    hint → emanate → blaze

    hint vs. emanate: To hint at something is to suggest it very faintly, a much subtler trace than something clearly emanating from its source.

    blaze vs. emanate: To blaze is to shine or spread outward with intense, overpowering force, a far stronger image than the more neutral emanate.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Emanate is often used for things you can sense but can't quite pin down a single cause for -- light, warmth, a mood, a smell -- and it always needs a source named with 'from.'
