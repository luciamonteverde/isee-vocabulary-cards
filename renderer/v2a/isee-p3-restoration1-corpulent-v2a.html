corpulent

KOR-pyuh-lunt

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Corpulent is fat; having a body made large and heavy by excess flesh rather than muscle.

See the word in context

Centuries-old tavern records describe the innkeeper as a corpulent man famous for eating three full meals before noon.

The retired boxer had grown so corpulent since his championship days that old fans barely recognized him.

Corpulent specifically describes a large, heavy body -- it's a formal, almost clinical way to say 'fat.'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    portly: Portly means somewhat fat, and it is a polite, almost affectionate word, most often used of a dignified older man. Corpulent is blunter and heavier: it describes a body made genuinely large by excess flesh, with none of the courtesy that softens portly.

    stout: Stout describes a solidly built, thickset body and often suggests sturdy strength as much as fat -- a stout hiker may simply be broad and powerful. Corpulent leaves no such doubt: the bulk it names is soft excess flesh, not a solid build.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gaunt: so thin that bones are visible, often from illness

    lean: thin in a healthy, fit way

    Where it sits · how heavily overweight someone is

    plump → corpulent → obese

    plump vs. corpulent: Plump means pleasantly rounded, only mildly overweight -- milder than corpulent.

    obese vs. corpulent: Obese is a medical term for being dangerously overweight, an even stronger term than corpulent.
