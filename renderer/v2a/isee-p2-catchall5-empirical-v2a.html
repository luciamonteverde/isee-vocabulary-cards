empirical

em-PEER-ih-kul — contains 'peer,' as in looking closely; empirical knowledge comes from actually peering at the evidence.

What It Means

Empirical is based on evidence gathered through observation or experiment.

See the word in context

The professor dismissed the claim outright because it had no empirical support behind it.

A student believes plants grow faster if you talk to them. Instead of just guessing, she gathers empirical evidence: she plants twenty identical seedlings, talks to half of them daily, and carefully measures their height every week for a month.

Her conclusion will rest on measured results, not on guesses. Unlike 'theoretical,' which is based on ideas alone, empirical means grounded in actual observed evidence.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    observed: Observed means directly seen, watched, or measured as it happened, one noted fact at a time. Empirical is the wider term: empirical evidence is any evidence gathered through observation or experiment, so each observed fact is a single building block of an empirical case.

    experimental: Experimental means based on deliberate tests in which researchers control the conditions and change one thing at a time to see what happens. Empirical is broader: evidence counts as empirical whether it comes from controlled experiments or simply from careful, patient observation of the world.

    factual: Factual means based on facts rather than opinion; a factual statement is simply true. Empirical says something more specific about where the facts came from: they were gathered through direct observation or experiment rather than taken from authority, theory, or belief.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    theoretical: based on ideas rather than tested evidence

    speculative: based on guessing rather than evidence

    Where it sits · how well proven it is

    reasonable → empirical → irrefutable

    reasonable vs. empirical: Reasonable is weaker than empirical — a reasonable guess sounds sensible, but empirical evidence has actually been tested and observed.

    irrefutable vs. empirical: Irrefutable is stronger than empirical: empirical evidence has been observed and tested, but irrefutable evidence is so solid it cannot possibly be denied.
