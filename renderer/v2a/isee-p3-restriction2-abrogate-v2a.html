abrogate

AB-ruh-gayt

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

To abrogate is to repeal or officially do away with a law, formal rule, or agreement.

See the word in context

The new parliament voted to abrogate the decades-old trade agreement in a single session.

Rather than revise the outdated rule, the board simply chose to abrogate it entirely.

The board didn't tweak the rule -- they officially canceled it altogether, which is exactly abrogate.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    revoke: To revoke is to officially take back something that was granted to someone — a license, a privilege, a right. To abrogate aims at the rule itself: a government or ruling body formally does away with a whole law or agreement. You revoke one citizen's passport; a parliament abrogates the passport law.

    void: To void something is to strip it of legal force, treating a contract or document as if it never counted. To abrogate is the official act of repealing a law or agreement that did count, ending its authority from that point on. A judge voids a flawed contract; a legislature abrogates a standing treaty.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enact: make a law or plan official

    Where it sits · how completely the rule is undone

    revise → abrogate → overturn

    revise vs. abrogate: Revising a law changes part of it; abrogating a law cancels it entirely.

    overturn vs. abrogate: Overturning a law goes further than abrogating it: a legislature that abrogates a law repeals it from that day forward, while a court that overturns it declares it never had valid force at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Abrogate shares its exact Latin root with this unit's own "arrogate," but points the opposite direction: abrogate cancels an existing law or agreement, while arrogate means seizing power or authority for oneself.
