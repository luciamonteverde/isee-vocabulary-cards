What It Means

To muddle is to carelessly throw an arrangement, whether of objects, a schedule, or ideas, into a confused, disordered mess that nobody intended.

See the word in context

Rushing to leave, he muddled the seating chart and sat three couples with their exes.

The new intern muddled the filing system so badly that it took the office two full days to find a single missing contract.

Muddle describes mixing something up carelessly, not an intentional act of wrecking it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    jumble: To jumble is to toss physical things together until no order is left -- a jumbled drawer, a jumbled bin of puzzle pieces. To muddle reaches further: schedules, instructions, and ideas can all be muddled, and a muddled thing is not a heap but an arrangement gone wrong.

    scramble: To scramble is to mix something up with vigor, often on purpose -- cooks scramble eggs, and engineers scramble signals so outsiders cannot read them. To muddle is quieter and accidental: a careless slip that leaves things in a confused mess nobody intended.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    organize: to arrange something in an orderly, sensible way

    sort: to arrange items into a clear, logical order

    Where it sits · how badly the order breaks down

    mix up → muddle → wreck

    mix up vs. muddle: Mixing up is lighter than muddling: you might mix up two similar keys for a moment, while muddling something turns the whole thing into a confused, disordered mess.

    wreck vs. muddle: To wreck something goes further than muddling it: a muddled bookshelf is disordered but fixable, while a wrecked one has lost its usefulness entirely.
