plebeian

pluh-BEE-un

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Plebeian describes taste or manner that is unrefined and mass-market -- the sort a snob dismisses as belonging to the crowd rather than to cultivated people.

See the word in context

Though she had married into old money, the countess's plebeian love of loud television dramas embarrassed her in-laws at every dinner party.

The art critic sneered that the hotel lobby's neon signs and plastic furniture gave the whole space a plebeian, downmarket feel.

Plebeian doesn't just mean 'ordinary' -- it usually carries a hint of looking down on something as common or unrefined.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    common: Common means widespread and often met -- a common name, a common cold. In American use it states how frequently something occurs and passes no judgment. Plebeian is never neutral: it looks down on a taste or manner as unrefined and mass-market.

    lowbrow: Lowbrow describes entertainment and ideas -- reality shows, tabloid gossip, slapstick -- that aim at mass tastes rather than intellectual ones. It judges cultural content only. Plebeian reaches wider, sneering at manners, food, dress, or whole social origins, not just at what someone watches or reads.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    aristocratic: belonging to or typical of the nobility or upper class

    refined: elegant and cultured in taste or manner

    Where it sits · how unpolished it comes across

    ordinary → plebeian → crude

    ordinary vs. plebeian: Ordinary is far milder than plebeian: it just means typical or usual, recording that something is unremarkable without passing any judgment on its refinement.

    crude vs. plebeian: Crude is a stronger word, suggesting something is rough, unpolished, or even offensive -- a harsher put-down than plebeian.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Plebeian almost always carries a note of looking down on something -- it's rarely a neutral word. You'll usually hear it from someone who thinks their own tastes are more refined (like this unit's aristocrat).
