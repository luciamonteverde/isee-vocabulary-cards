Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To shirk is to get out of a duty that is yours — a chore, a shift, your share of a group project — on purpose, slipping off and leaving the work undone or to someone else. Two things make it shirking: the task was a responsibility you owed, and you got out of it deliberately, not through carelessness or forgetting.

See the word in context

Whenever the dishes piled up after dinner, Ravi would shirk his turn by suddenly remembering homework he had already finished.

The night guard shirked his rounds, watching movies in the booth while the back gate stood unlocked until dawn.

Walking the rounds was the guard's job — a duty he was paid to do — and he chose movies over doing it. Both halves of shirk are here: the task was his responsibility, and he got out of it on purpose, leaving it undone. If he had walked the rounds and simply missed the gate, that would be a slip, not shirking.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    avoid: Avoid is the broadest word — to keep away from anything at all: a puddle, a crowded street, a hard conversation, a person you dislike. Nothing about it has to be your job. Shirk is narrower: the thing you get out of is a duty you owed, and you leave it undone or to someone else. Ask whether the thing kept away from was a responsibility of the person's own; if it was not, avoid is the word.

    dodge: Dodge is a quick sidestep — of a ball, a question, a chore for one evening — done in the moment and often with a bit of nimble skill. Shirk is slower and lazier: a pattern of getting out of a duty and letting the work fall on others. Ask whether the sentence shows a quick sidestep of one thing, or a duty steadily left undone.

    evade: Evade means to slip out of reach of something by cleverness or trickery — evading capture, evading a direct question, evading taxes with hidden accounts. It stresses the cunning of the escape. Shirk needs no cleverness at all; it is simply not doing the work you owe. Ask whether the sentence shows a trick or clever move used to get clear, or a duty plainly left undone.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fulfill: to do everything a duty requires

    shoulder: to take on a burden or duty willingly

    Where it sits · how completely a duty gets dropped

    neglect → shirk → abandon

    neglect vs. shirk: Neglect is the milder word — a duty slips because the person did not pay attention to it: the plants died because he forgot to water them. It is a failure of care, not a decision. Shirk is on purpose: the person knows the duty is theirs and slips out of it. Ask whether the duty went undone through carelessness, or by choice.

    abandon vs. shirk: Abandon is the stronger word — the duty is dropped openly and for good: the captain abandons the ship, the builder abandons the half-finished house. Shirk is quieter and smaller: the person still officially holds the duty and just keeps slipping out of doing it. Ask whether the person has walked away from the duty entirely, or is still there and simply not doing their share.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Shirk is a word of blame; nobody is praised for shirking. It almost always takes an object — you shirk your duties, your chores, your share, a responsibility — and that object is nearly always something you were supposed to do, not something you merely wanted to escape. A shirker is the person who makes a habit of it. Grammar note for pairing: shirk needs a thing after it (you shirk something), so it cannot drop into a sentence built for a verb that stands alone, the way persist does in this same unit.
