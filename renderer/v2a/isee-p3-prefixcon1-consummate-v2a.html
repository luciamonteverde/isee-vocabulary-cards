What It Means

Consummate is of the highest degree; complete and masterful.

See the word in context

The retiring surgeon was remembered by every colleague as a consummate professional who never once cut a corner.

The violinist's consummate technique made the notoriously difficult concerto sound almost effortless.

Consummate describes total mastery -- skill so complete that nothing about it feels lacking.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    masterful: Masterful praises great skill and command — a masterful pianist plays with authority. Consummate reaches further on one point: completeness. Masterful work can still have a weak spot or an off night, while a consummate performance is of the highest degree, with nothing lacking anywhere.

    accomplished: Accomplished describes a person made highly skilled by years of practice — it praises a background and a track record. Consummate judges the mastery itself, often in a single performance: complete, polished on every front. A newcomer with no record at all can still give a consummate performance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    amateurish: lacking professional skill or finish

    incompetent: lacking the skill to do something successfully

    Where it sits · how close to perfection the mastery comes

    skilled → consummate → peerless

    skilled vs. consummate: Skilled simply means capable and trained, without the flawless, top-of-the-field mastery that consummate implies.

    peerless vs. consummate: Peerless goes even further than consummate, claiming that literally no one else can match the skill at all.
