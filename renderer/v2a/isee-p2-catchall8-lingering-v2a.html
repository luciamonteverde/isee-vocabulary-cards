What It Means

Lingering means lasting past its expected end, hanging on after the original cause has passed rather than fading on schedule.

See the word in context

A lingering smell of smoke remained in the house long after the candles were put out.

Months after recovering from a bad cold, a runner notices a lingering shortness of breath during workouts that used to feel easy.

That stubborn effect that refuses to fully go away, long after the original cause has passed, is what lingering describes. Unlike 'brief,' which ends quickly, lingering implies something that overstays.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prolonged: Prolonged means stretched out longer than expected, usually because something or someone kept it going -- a prolonged meeting, a prolonged dispute. Lingering is quieter: nothing is stretching it out; the thing simply hasn't faded yet, hanging on past its natural end.

    residual: Residual describes the small remainder left behind after most of something is gone -- residual moisture after a spill is wiped up. A lingering effect is not necessarily small: it is whatever refuses to fade on schedule, overstaying its expected end.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fleeting: lasting for a very short time

    momentary: lasting only a moment

    Where it sits · how actively it resists going away

    enduring → lingering → persistent

    enduring vs. lingering: Enduring can carry a more positive tone than lingering -- an enduring friendship sounds admirable, while a lingering symptom or smell usually sounds unwanted.

    persistent vs. lingering: Persistent can feel slightly more active than lingering: lingering often describes something that simply hasn't faded yet, while persistent can suggest something that keeps recurring or resisting efforts to remove it.
