articulate

ar-TIK-yuh-layt (verb) — the root means 'joint'; to articulate is to join your words together clearly, piece by piece.

What It Means

To articulate is to express an idea clearly and fluently.

See the word in context

The young student stood confidently and managed to articulate her complex argument without a single pause.

Asked to explain a complicated scientific theory to a room of young children, a researcher articulates the idea in simple, clear steps with everyday examples, so that every child in the room leaves fully understanding it.

Turning a complicated idea into words that are clear and easy to follow is what it takes to articulate something. Unlike 'mumble,' which makes speech unclear, articulate means expressing something with real clarity.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    convey: To convey is to get an idea or feeling across by any channel at all -- a shrug, a chart, or a tone of voice can convey meaning without a single word. To articulate is narrower: it means putting an idea into clear, fluent words.

    voice: To voice something is to share your own opinion or feeling out loud -- voicing doubts, voicing support. To articulate is about the skill of the expression itself: any idea, yours or not, put into clear and fluent words.

    enunciate: To enunciate is to pronounce the sounds of words crisply and distinctly -- a matter of diction, of how the syllables leave your mouth. To articulate is to make the idea itself clear and fluent; you can enunciate perfectly and still leave your idea a muddle.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mumble: to speak unclearly or indistinctly

    stammer: to speak with hesitation or repeated pauses

    Where it sits · how clearly the idea comes across

    express → articulate → spell out

    express vs. articulate: Express is a more general word than articulate -- express simply means to communicate a thought at all, while articulate specifically emphasizes doing so with real clarity and fluency.

    spell out vs. articulate: Spelling something out goes a step beyond articulate: to articulate a plan is to put it into clear, fluent words, while to spell it out is to break it into explicit, step-by-step detail so the listener is left to infer nothing.
