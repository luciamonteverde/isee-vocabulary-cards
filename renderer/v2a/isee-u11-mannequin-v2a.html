mannequin

MAN-ih-kin

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A mannequin is a life-size figure of a human body, usually made of plastic or fiberglass, whose whole job is to have clothes put on it so shoppers or a tailor can see how they hang — it stands in a shop window or a sewing room and represents no one in particular.

See the word in context

Backstage, the finished costumes hung on a row of headless mannequins, waiting for the actors.

Every Monday night, the boutique's owner undressed the three mannequins in the front window and dressed them in the week's new arrivals.

The figures are there to be dressed and undressed, so the clothes can be seen on a body shape before anyone buys them. Nobody knows or cares whose face they have — they are not copies of any real person. Full human size, made for showing clothes, standing for no one: that is exactly what mannequin names.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    model: A model is a living person hired to wear clothes and show them off, walking and posing so the clothes move; the word also names a small copy of something built to show a design, like a model airplane. A mannequin is neither alive nor small: a full-size figure that stands still while clothes are put on it. (On a test, model is the closest single match for mannequin.) Ask whether the thing breathes and moves, or stands where it is put.

    dummy: A dummy is a full-bodied figure that takes a person's place when the real thing would be hurt or is not there — a crash-test dummy strapped into a car, a stuffed dummy propped up in a bed to fool a guard. Nobody dresses a dummy to sell clothes. A mannequin exists to be dressed and looked at. Ask whether the figure is standing in for a body, or showing off an outfit.

    effigy: An effigy is a rough copy of one particular person, usually built so a crowd can shout at it, hang it, or burn it in protest — crude, but meant to be recognized. A mannequin copies no one: it has a blank, general face and exists only to wear clothes. Ask whether the figure is meant to be a certain person.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    person: a real, living human being

    wearer: the living person who will actually put the clothes on

    Where it sits · how large and lasting the human figure is

    doll → mannequin → statue

    doll vs. mannequin: A doll is the small end — a toy figure of a person, small enough to carry, made for a child to play with and dress for fun. A mannequin is full human size and belongs to a shop or a sewing room, where it is dressed so that real clothes can be seen and sold. Ask whether the figure is a toy or a fixture, and whether it could wear a real coat.

    statue vs. mannequin: A statue is the grand end — carved or cast to last, usually of a particular person or a hero, and made to be looked at for itself. A mannequin is a working fixture: dressed, undressed, moved about, and thrown away when an arm snaps off, and no one looks at it for its own sake. Ask whether people come to see the figure, or the clothes on it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Mannequin is the shop word: use it for the figure in a store window or a tailor's sewing room. It comes from a Dutch word meaning 'little man', and long ago it also meant a living person who showed off clothes. On the ISEE, expect model as the closest single match — and remember that today the mannequin is the figure and the model is the person.
