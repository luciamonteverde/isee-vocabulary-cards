scathing

SKAY-thing -- from the old verb scathe, 'to harm': scathing words leave a mark.

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Scathing is severely critical, meant to burn and to destroy what it attacks.

See the word in context

The senator's scathing reply left the reporter with nothing at all to say.

An editor hands back a first draft covered in red ink, and the scathing note at the bottom reads only: 'Nothing here is worth keeping. Start again.'

Criticism harsh enough to sting long after you read it is what makes that note scathing. Unlike 'honest,' which can be delivered gently, scathing criticism is meant to burn.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cutting: A cutting remark is sharp enough to wound feelings -- one line, aimed at one person, often in conversation. Scathing is broader and heavier: a scathing review or report attacks its subject at length and in detail. A rival can be cutting in a hallway; a critic is scathing in print.

    withering: A withering look or comment makes its target feel small, shriveling their confidence on the spot. Scathing points at the criticism itself rather than the target's reaction: a scathing review burns the work it reviews, whether or not its author is even in the room.

    scornful: Scornful means full of contempt -- it sneers, treating its target as beneath serious attention. Scathing takes the target seriously enough to attack in detail, fault by fault. Scorn waves a thing away; a scathing review works through it and leaves it in pieces.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flattering: full of praise, perhaps more than deserved

    gentle: mild and considerate

    Where it sits · how fierce the criticism is

    harsh → scathing → blistering

    harsh vs. scathing: Harsh is the plain, general word that scathing sharpens -- a harsh review is unkind, while a scathing review sets out to destroy what it is reviewing.

    blistering vs. scathing: Blistering is scathing at full heat: scathing criticism is severe and cutting, while blistering criticism suggests fury as well as severity, delivered at top volume.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Scathing almost always attaches to words: a scathing review, a scathing remark, a scathing report. People are rarely called scathing themselves -- their criticism is.
