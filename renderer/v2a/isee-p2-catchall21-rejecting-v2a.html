rejecting

rih-JEK-ting

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Rejecting something means turning it away after looking it over and judging it unacceptable — not good enough, not right, or not wanted — so that it goes back where it came from or is pushed aside for good; the verdict is on the thing put forward, not a statement about what the person will do.

See the word in context

The quality inspector spent the whole morning rejecting any jar whose lid did not click shut, and by noon the bin beside her was full.

After rejecting three batches of dough that had not risen, the baker finally slid the fourth into the oven.

Each batch was put in front of the baker, looked over, and judged not good enough, and each one went into the bin instead of the oven — that is rejecting. Notice that the baker was not saying he would not bake bread today, and he was not tossing the batches aside unseen; he looked each one over and gave a verdict on it. The fourth passed the same test, which is why the word stops applying to it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dismissing: Dismissing an idea or a request means waving it aside as not worth a serious look — brushed off before anyone thinks it through, often with a shrug. Rejecting comes after the thing has been looked over and judged. Ask whether the thing was considered and found unfit, or waved away without a real look.

    denying: Denying does two jobs: saying that a statement is untrue ('he denied breaking the window'), or officially not granting a request ('the bank denied the loan'). Rejecting is a verdict on a thing put forward — an offer, a plan, a piece of work — judged unacceptable. Ask whether the sentence is about calling a claim false or withholding permission, or about a thing being turned back as not good enough.

    ignoring: Ignoring means giving no answer at all — the offer or the message just sits there with no reply. Rejecting is an answer: a clear no, delivered. Ask whether the person answered with a no or gave no response at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    accepting: taking what is offered and keeping it.

    approving: judging something good enough and giving it the go-ahead.

    Where it sits · how firmly and finally the no is given

    declining → refusing → rejecting

    declining vs. rejecting: Declining is the gentle end — a polite no to an invitation or an offer, often with thanks, and saying nothing bad about the thing itself; the door stays open for next time. Rejecting says the thing itself falls short. Ask whether the person simply chose not to take part, or judged the thing not good enough.

    refusing vs. rejecting: Refusing is the middle — a firm no about what a person will or will not do: refused to sign, refused to leave, refused the medal. It is about the person's will. Rejecting is a verdict on the thing put forward, which is judged unacceptable and sent back. Ask whether the sentence is about someone not doing or taking something, or about a thing being judged and turned back; if a 'to + verb' follows the blank, only refusing fits.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    This card teaches the word in the form the exam uses, rejecting, but the base verb is reject and it behaves the same in every form: you reject an offer, an idea, a plan, an application, a gift, or a person's plea, and the thing rejected has been judged and sent back. It is the plain, everyday word for a firm no to something offered; its formal cousin, repudiate, is another word in this course and adds a public, official statement that the thing is wrong. A person can also be rejected — by a team, a college, a friend — and that use carries real sting, so the word is rarely gentle.
