Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Plaintive describes a sound — a cry, a voice, a tune — that is sad in a thin, small way and seems to be asking for something: comfort, company, help. The word tells you how the sound reaches your ear, not what its maker is feeling. A plaintive note can come from an animal, from an instrument, or from a child too young to explain what is wrong.

See the word in context

A single plaintive note from the harmonica drifted across the campfire, and the chatter died down as everyone turned to listen.

Alone in the barn after its mother was sold, the calf let out a plaintive bleat every few minutes, so thin and hopeful that the farmer's daughter finally went out with a blanket to sit beside it.

The calf's cry is not loud or angry, and it is not a shriek of pain. It is small and sad, and it seems to be asking for something — for the mother who is gone, for someone to come — and it works: the girl cannot hear it without going to it. A sound that is sad in that small, asking way is plaintive. The word describes the bleat, not the calf's inner life. If the sound were a drawn-out, irritating complaint that made you want to shut the barn door, the word would be whiny; plaintive is the sound that pulls you toward it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sorrowful: Sorrowful means full of or showing sorrow, and it can describe almost anything — eyes, news, a face, a whole chapter of a book. Plaintive is narrower: it lives in sound, and it adds a note of asking. Ask two things: can you hear it, and does it seem to want something?

    whiny: Whiny describes a complaining sound — a high, drawn-out, grumbling tone — and it irritates the listener rather than moving them. Plaintive is sad rather than grumbling, and it draws you toward the maker instead of making you want to leave the room. Ask whether the sound makes you want to help or want it to stop.

    melancholy: Melancholy, used of a sound or a piece of music, means quietly sad and slow to fade, with no particular cause and no request in it — a melancholy tune can be beautiful to sit inside. Plaintive is thinner and more pointed: it sounds as if it is asking for something. Ask whether the sound is simply sad or seems to be pleading.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cheerful: bright and happy in tone

    joyful: ringing with gladness

    Where it sits · how much sorrow the sound carries

    wistful → plaintive → mournful → anguished

    wistful vs. plaintive: Wistful is the gentlest: a soft, dreamy sound or look tinged with longing for something far away — a sigh over an old photograph, a slow tune that wishes summer back. It wishes; it does not ask. Plaintive is sadder and reaches out for help or comfort right now. Ask whether the sound is quietly wishing or actually appealing for something.

    mournful vs. plaintive: Mournful is heavier and points at grief over a real loss — a funeral bell, a slow march, the voice of someone who has lost something for good. Plaintive asks less of the situation: a plaintive cry can come from a hungry puppy with nothing lost at all. Ask whether something has actually been lost.

    anguished vs. plaintive: Anguished is the strongest: a sound torn out by deep pain — a scream, a sob that will not stop — with no thinness or gentleness left in it. Plaintive is small and sad, a sound that asks rather than tears. Ask whether the sound is a raw cry of pain or a thin, sad appeal.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Plaintive is a describing word for sounds — a plaintive cry, wail, voice, melody, whistle — and only by a stretch for a look ("a plaintive glance"). It is a gentle, slightly literary word, and it always carries a little appeal in it: the sound seems to want something. That is what separates it from dejection, its neighbor in this unit: dejection is a low mood a person is in, while plaintive is a quality a sound has, and a plaintive cry can come from a hungry animal that is in no mood at all.
