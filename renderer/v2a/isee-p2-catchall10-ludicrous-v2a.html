What It Means

Ludicrous is so foolish, unreasonable, or out of place as to be laughable.

See the word in context

The idea of building a ski resort in the middle of the desert struck everyone as ludicrous.

Someone proposes hosting an ice-skating competition on a beach in the middle of summer, and the idea sounds so ludicrous that everyone in the room bursts out laughing.

That absurd, laughably unreasonable quality is exactly what ludicrous describes. Unlike 'unlikely,' which just means improbable, ludicrous means so foolish it invites laughter.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    absurd: Absurd means wildly unreasonable or illogical -- it points at the broken logic itself. Ludicrous adds the laughter: a ludicrous idea is not only unreasonable but so foolish that people cannot help laughing at it.

    farcical: Farcical describes events that actually play out like a staged comedy of errors -- mishap piling on mishap. Ludicrous fits ideas, claims, and plans instead: a ludicrous proposal is laughably foolish on paper before anything ever happens.

    outlandish: Outlandish means strikingly strange or unconventional in style -- a wild costume, an exotic scheme -- and an outlandish plan might even work. Ludicrous is about failed sense: a ludicrous plan is laughably foolish, however plain and ordinary it looks.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sensible: having or showing good judgment

    reasonable: fair and sensible

    Where it sits · how hard it is to take seriously

    ridiculous → ludicrous → preposterous

    ridiculous vs. ludicrous: Ridiculous is a more everyday word than ludicrous -- ridiculous can describe anything mildly silly, while ludicrous often implies the idea is so unreasonable it's genuinely laughable.

    preposterous vs. ludicrous: Preposterous can feel even stronger than ludicrous: ludicrous emphasizes something laughably foolish, while preposterous adds a sense of shock that anyone could seriously propose it.
