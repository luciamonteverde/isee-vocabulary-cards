What It Means

Jingoism is national pride hardened into aggressive hostility toward other countries.

See the word in context

The rally's speeches slid quickly from national pride into outright jingoism.

Editorials whipped up jingoism in the weeks before the vote, framing any compromise as a betrayal of the nation.

This wasn't ordinary love of country -- it was an aggressive, us-against-them nationalism designed to shut down disagreement, which is exactly jingoism.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    chauvinism: Chauvinism is blind, unreasoning devotion to one's own group -- a nation, a team, even a profession -- plus the belief that it outranks all others. Jingoism is narrower and more aggressive: it fixes on one's country and welcomes conflict with rival nations.

    flag-waving: Flag-waving is the loud, showy display of patriotism -- parades, banners, anthems belted at full volume. Jingoism is the attitude underneath the show: national pride hardened into hostility, ready to treat other countries as enemies.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    internationalism: cooperation and goodwill among different nations

    Where it sits · how aggressive the national pride gets

    patriotism → jingoism → warmongering

    patriotism vs. jingoism: Patriotism is an ordinary, healthy love of one's country; jingoism pushes that pride into aggressive hostility toward outsiders.

    warmongering vs. jingoism: Warmongering pushes even further than jingoism: jingoism is hostile bragging about one's own nation, while warmongering actively agitates for real armed conflict.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Jingoism almost always carries a negative, critical tone -- it's a word used to describe someone else's excessive nationalism, rarely one's own.
