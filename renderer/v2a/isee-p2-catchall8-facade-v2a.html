facade

fuh-SAHD -- from a root meaning 'face,' the outward appearance something presents.

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Facade is a false outward appearance that is maintained over time to conceal a less pleasant reality.

See the word in context

Behind its cheerful facade, the company was actually losing money every month.

A family keeps up a picture-perfect facade online, posting only smiling vacation photos and celebratory announcements, never once mentioning the financial stress and arguments happening at home.

That carefully maintained, misleading outward appearance is what a facade is. Unlike 'appearance' alone, which can be perfectly honest, facade specifically implies the truth is being hidden.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    front: A front is the false appearance you put up in the moment -- a brave front after bad news. It is casual and often short-lived, while a facade is a fuller false exterior, built and maintained over time by a person, a family, or a company.

    pretense: A pretense is a false show of one particular quality -- a pretense of interest, of surprise, of calm. A facade is the whole misleading exterior at once: not one faked feeling but an entire false surface of prosperity or happiness concealing the truth.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reality: the true state of things, as they actually exist

    transparency: the quality of being open and easy to see through

    Where it sits · how thin and fragile the disguise is

    cover → facade → veneer

    cover vs. facade: A cover is less fragile than a facade -- a spy's cover is a whole constructed life meant to survive close inspection, while a facade is only the outward face, convincing at a distance but thinner up close.

    veneer vs. facade: Veneer can feel thinner and more fragile than facade: a facade can be maintained for years, while a veneer often suggests just a very thin, easily cracked layer of politeness or civility.
