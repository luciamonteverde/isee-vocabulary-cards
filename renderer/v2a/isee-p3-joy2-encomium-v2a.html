What It Means

An encomium is a full, formal speech or piece of writing praising someone at length.

See the word in context

The retiring professor's colleagues wrote a lengthy encomium praising her four decades of research.

At the retirement dinner, his former students delivered a heartfelt encomium celebrating his decades of teaching.

An encomium is a formal tribute, usually spoken or written for a specific occasion honoring someone's achievements.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tribute: A tribute is any act that shows respect or admiration -- flowers at a memorial, a moment of silence, a song dedicated to a hero. An encomium is one specific kind of tribute: a formal speech or piece of writing devoted to praising someone. Every encomium is a tribute, but a tribute needs no words at all.

    commendation: A commendation is an official expression of praise, usually short and issued by an authority -- a medal citation, a letter from the principal's office. An encomium is longer, warmer, and more personal: a full speech or composition celebrating what someone has achieved. A commendation certifies; an encomium celebrates at length.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    criticism: the expression of disapproval

    condemnation: a strong expression of disapproval

    Where it sits · how formal the praise grows

    compliment → encomium → commemoration

    compliment vs. encomium: A compliment can be a single sentence; an encomium is a full, formal piece of praise.

    commemoration vs. encomium: A commemoration is grander still than an encomium: a whole ceremony or observance honoring a person or occasion, where an encomium is a single speech or written text of praise.
