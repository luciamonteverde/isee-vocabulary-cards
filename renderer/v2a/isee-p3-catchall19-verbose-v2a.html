What It Means

Verbose means using far more words than needed, padding a simple point into something much longer.

See the word in context

His verbose text messages regularly ran three screens long, even for questions that only needed a yes or no.

Asked for a one-word answer, the witness instead delivered a verbose five-minute account of his entire morning, including what he'd eaten for breakfast.

Using far more words than the situation actually calls for -- padding a simple answer into a rambling account -- is exactly what verbose describes.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rambling: Rambling means wandering from point to point with no clear direction -- a rambling story keeps changing the subject halfway through. Verbose is a different complaint: a verbose report can be perfectly organized and still use three times the words it needs. Rambling is lost direction; verbose is sheer excess.

    flowery: Flowery describes language dressed up with fancy, ornamental words -- perfumed adjectives and poetic flourishes on every line. Verbose is a plainer criticism: too many words, fancy or not. A flowery sentence can be short but overdecorated; a verbose one can be perfectly plain and still twice the needed length.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    concise: giving information clearly in few words

    succinct: briefly and clearly expressed

    Where it sits · how badly it runs on

    wordy → verbose → long-winded

    wordy vs. verbose: Wordy is milder than verbose: a wordy email just needs a trim here and there, while a verbose one buries its point under so much padding that the reader has to dig for it.

    long-winded vs. verbose: Long-winded is stronger than verbose: a verbose writer merely uses far more words than needed, while a long-winded speaker drags on so relentlessly that half the audience checks out entirely.
