What It Means

To conciliate is to end anger and restore peace between disputing sides by finding common ground both can accept.

See the word in context

The camp counselor stepped between the two cabins, hoping a shared pizza night might conciliate both sides before the prank war escalated further.

The union representative worked for weeks to conciliate the factory workers and management, proposing a compromise schedule that both sides could accept.

Conciliate isn't about picking a winner -- it's about finding common ground that calms both angry sides.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    appease: To appease is to calm someone by giving them what they demand -- the concession does the calming, and it can shade into giving in. To conciliate is to end anger by finding common ground both sides can accept, without simply surrendering to either one. Appeasing buys quiet; conciliating builds peace.

    placate: To placate is to make an angry person less angry, often quickly and often only for the moment -- a manager placates one unhappy customer. To conciliate runs deeper and more formal: it works to restore peace between disputing sides, not just to quiet one person's temper.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    provoke: to deliberately anger someone

    antagonize: to cause someone to become hostile

    Where it sits · how much of the conflict gets mended

    soothe → conciliate → reconcile

    soothe vs. conciliate: Soothe is a gentler word for calming mild upset, without necessarily resolving a real conflict the way conciliate does.

    reconcile vs. conciliate: Reconcile goes further than conciliate, restoring a full, lasting friendship or agreement rather than just calming anger in the moment.
