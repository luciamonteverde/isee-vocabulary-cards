interactions

in-ter-AK-shunz

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Interactions are the moments when two or more people or things act on each other — each side does something and the other side responds, so that what happens depends on both. One such moment is an interaction.

See the word in context

The keeper filmed the interactions between the new baby elephant and the grown females, noting every push, every call and every answering reply.

Mrs. Okafor grades her students partly on their interactions in group work: who asks a question, who answers it, and who builds on someone else's idea.

Each of those three things is one side acting and another side responding — a question that draws out an answer, an idea that gets built upon. That back-and-forth is what makes them interactions. Simply sitting at the same table would not count, because nobody is acting on anybody; and meeting the group for the first time would not count either, because meeting is only the start. Interactions are what the sides do to each other once they are together.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    exchanges: An exchange is a swap: something goes one way and something comes back — an exchange of gifts, of letters, of angry words. Interactions cover any way two sides act on each other, including a smile answered with a wave, or two chemicals mixing in a test tube. Ask whether something was actually traded back and forth, or whether the two sides simply acted on each other.

    encounters: An encounter is the meeting itself — coming across someone, often briefly or by chance, on a street corner or on a trail. Interactions are what the two sides do to each other once they have met, and one encounter can hold many interactions or none at all. Ask whether the sentence is about running into someone, or about what the two did to each other.

    communication: Communication is the passing of information — a message sent, a sign held up, a speech given — and it can run one way, to people who never answer. Interactions need both sides to act and respond, and they need not pass information at all. Ask whether a message is being sent, or whether two sides are acting on each other.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    isolation: being kept apart from everyone, with no one to act on and no one acting back

    separation: the two sides held apart, so that neither can do anything to the other

    Where it sits · how much the two sides actually do with each other

    contact → interactions → collaboration

    contact vs. interactions: Contact is the lowest step: two sides merely touch or are in touch — a hand on a shoulder, a phone number you still have, a radio signal that gets through. Interactions need more: each side does something and the other answers. Ask whether the two are only in touch, or actually acting on each other.

    collaboration vs. interactions: Collaboration goes further than interactions: the two sides not only act on each other but work steadily toward one shared goal — a poster, a report, a cure. Interactions can be brief, light or even hostile, with no goal in common. Ask whether the sentence names a shared project the two are building together.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Interaction is a neutral, slightly technical word: scientists speak of interactions between drugs, between forces and between species, and teachers speak of interactions between students. It almost always takes 'between' or 'with'. The word is used far more often for many moments than for one, because interactions are counted moments — a single day at school is full of them. On the ISEE the word appears in fill-in-the-blank questions about the everyday back-and-forth between people, and it says nothing about whether that back-and-forth is friendly or hostile.
