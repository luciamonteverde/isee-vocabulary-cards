What It Means

Caustic is sarcastic in a bitter, biting way.

See the word in context

The comedian's caustic jokes about the town's losing streak drew laughter from some neighbors and winces from others.

After the nervous singer finishes her first recital, her older cousin smirks and offers a caustic remark: 'Well, at least the piano was in tune.'

It wears the disguise of a compliment but is really a bitter, mocking put-down designed to sting. Caustic humor does not just tease -- it burns.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sardonic: Sardonic means mocking in a dark, scornfully amused way, and it often lives in a look, a smirk, or a tone without a single word spoken. Caustic is bitter sarcasm delivered in words, built to sting its target. A sardonic observer may stay silent; caustic wit always bites out loud.

    sarcastic: Sarcastic means saying the opposite of what you mean in order to mock -- 'nice going' after a spill. Caustic goes further: the sarcasm is soaked in real bitterness and aims to wound. Most sarcastic remarks just tease; a caustic one leaves a burn.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gentle: mild and kind in manner

    soothing: calming and comforting

    Where it sits · how deeply the words wound

    biting → caustic → scathing

    biting vs. caustic: Biting is sharp but a shade lighter than caustic -- a biting comment nips and stings, while a caustic one is soaked in bitterness and leaves a mark that lasts.

    scathing vs. caustic: Scathing turns the burn up even past caustic: a caustic remark wounds with bitter sarcasm, while a scathing review or rebuke sets out to scorch its target completely.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Caustic almost always describes words and wit: a caustic remark, caustic humor, a caustic review. It is stronger than 'sarcastic' alone -- the sarcasm has real bitterness in it, meant to sting the person it targets.
