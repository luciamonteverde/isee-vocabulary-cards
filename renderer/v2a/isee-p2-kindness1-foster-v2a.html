Meaning

What It Means

To foster is to encourage the development of something by creating the right conditions and tending them over time until it flourishes.

See the word in context

The mentorship program pairs new hires with veterans to foster confidence during the first year.

The librarian sets out puzzles by the window, hosts a build-your-own-comic club, and lets kids vote on new books to foster a reading habit -- and within a year, twice as many students spend their lunch period reading.

To foster something is to nourish and encourage its development: you create the right conditions and tend them until the thing flourishes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cultivate: To cultivate something is to develop it through deliberate attention and effort, the way a farmer works a field -- you choose it, plan for it, and work at it directly. To foster is gentler and less direct: you supply the right conditions and let the thing grow on its own.

    kindle: To kindle interest or hope is to spark it into being with a single stroke -- one great lesson, one thrilling match. To foster is the slower work that follows: patient encouragement over time, tending the conditions so what was sparked keeps growing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hinder: hold back the development of something

    suppress: prevent something from growing or being expressed

    Where it sits · how publicly the support is given

    nurture → foster → promote

    nurture vs. foster: Nurture is hands-on and close, while foster works at arm's length -- a parent nurtures a child directly, but a city fosters the arts by building the theaters and funding the grants that let them grow.

    promote vs. foster: Promote is louder than foster: to promote a cause is to push it publicly, while to foster it is quieter -- creating the conditions in which it can take root and grow on its own.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Foster takes growing things as its object: foster creativity, foster trust, foster a love of music, foster economic growth. It implies patient encouragement over time rather than a single push. It also keeps its literal family sense -- a foster family takes in and cares for a child.
