iniquitous

in-IK-wi-tus

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Iniquitous is grossly unfair and morally wrong.

See the word in context

Historians now describe the colony's labor system as iniquitous, a deeply unjust arrangement that enriched a handful of officials while workers starved.

Critics called the new toll iniquitous, since it charged the same flat fee to a delivery truck and to a family driving to a funeral.

Iniquitous is reserved for arrangements or actions that are not just unfair in a minor way, but wicked and unjust at their core.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unjust: Unjust says a rule, decision, or outcome is not fair or right, and it can describe fairly small wrongs — an unjust penalty in a game. Iniquitous is far heavier: the unfairness is gross, deliberate, and morally wicked at its core.

    wicked: Wicked means morally wrong or evil in any form — a wicked lie, a wicked ruler, a wicked plan. Iniquitous is narrower: it condemns arrangements and systems, like laws and schemes, whose deep unfairness is exactly what makes them evil.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    equitable: fair and unbiased

    righteous: morally good and just

    Where it sits · how deep the injustice cuts

    unfair → iniquitous → monstrous

    unfair vs. iniquitous: Unfair is far milder than iniquitous: a referee's unfair call stings for an afternoon, while something iniquitous is so grossly and morally wrong that it damages whole lives.

    monstrous vs. iniquitous: Monstrous goes past even iniquitous: an iniquitous scheme is wicked and deeply unjust, but a monstrous act is so shockingly cruel that it seems scarcely human.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Iniquitous describes something systemically wrong -- a law, a scheme, or an arrangement -- not just an individual person's rudeness.
