prerogative

prih-ROG-uh-tiv -- stress on ROG; note the first syllable sounds like 'prih,' not 'pree'.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Prerogative is a right or privilege belonging exclusively to a particular role, office, or class.

See the word in context

Granting pardons is the governor's prerogative, a power the state constitution gives to no one else.

Under the orchestra's bylaws, choosing the season's final program is the conductor's prerogative; the musicians may suggest pieces, but the last word belongs to her office alone.

The right is exclusive and attached to a particular office -- a privilege that belongs to her role and to no one else's. That exclusivity is exactly what makes it a prerogative rather than a mere habit or tradition.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    authority: Authority is the broad power to direct people and make decisions across a whole area of responsibility. A prerogative is far narrower: one specific right carved out and reserved to a role, so an official can hold a single prerogative without any wider authority at all.

    birthright: A birthright is a right that belongs to someone simply by being born into a particular family, nation, or position. A prerogative attaches to an office rather than to birth: whoever currently holds the position holds its prerogatives, and gives them up on leaving it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    obligation: something one is required to do

    duty: a task or responsibility one is bound to perform

    Where it sits · how firmly the right is guaranteed

    privilege → prerogative → entitlement

    privilege vs. prerogative: Privilege is broader and often weaker than prerogative -- privileges can be handed out widely and taken away, while a prerogative is an exclusive right built into a particular office or position.

    entitlement vs. prerogative: Entitlement can sound even more absolute than prerogative: a prerogative is an exclusive right that comes with a role, while an entitlement is a guaranteed claim its holder can demand outright, no questions asked.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Prerogative names a right that belongs exclusively to a particular role, office, or class: the crown's prerogative, a parent's prerogative, the editor's prerogative. In casual speech it gets watered down to mean any personal choice -- in formal writing and on the ISEE, keep it tied to a right that comes with a position.
