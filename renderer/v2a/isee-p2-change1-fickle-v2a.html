Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Fickle is changing one's mind, loyalties, or affections frequently and without much reason.

See the word in context

Fashion is famously fickle: the boots everyone mocked last year sold out by March.

In September, Jade declares Priya her best friend and they sit together every day. By October it is Amara, and Priya is 'so last month.' By Thanksgiving, fickle Jade has moved on to someone from her soccer team, and both Priya and Amara have stopped saving her a seat.

To be fickle is to change your mind -- and your loyalties -- frequently and without much reason. Fickle describes people whose affections, opinions, or allegiances flip easily; the cost, as Jade is discovering, is that nobody trusts a fickle friend for long.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inconstant: Inconstant means failing to stay loyal or steady -- an inconstant ally drifts off when the wind changes. Fickle carries the same disloyalty but adds speed and shallowness: the fickle switch sides frequently and for barely any reason, which is why nobody counts on a fickle friend.

    flighty: Flighty describes a person too easily distracted to settle on anything -- a flighty student starts four projects and finishes none. Fickle is about loyalty rather than attention: fickle fans can concentrate perfectly well; what keeps changing is whom they cheer for.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    loyal: faithful and constant

    steadfast: firmly unchanging in purpose or loyalty

    Where it sits · how unpredictable the shifts become

    changeable → fickle → capricious

    changeable vs. fickle: Changeable is the neutral fact that something shifts often, while fickle adds a reproach -- a fickle friend's shifting is a character flaw, not just a pattern.

    capricious vs. fickle: Capricious is the strongest and most unpredictable: a fickle fan drifts to whichever team is winning, but a capricious ruler changes the rules mid-game on pure whim, and no one can plan around it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Fickle belongs first to people and their shifting loyalties and tastes -- fickle fans, a fickle friend, fickle voters, the fickle public. One licensed extension is weather ('fickle spring weather'), which changes its mind the way people do. Either way the register is the same: unreliable, quick to switch allegiance, not to be counted on.
