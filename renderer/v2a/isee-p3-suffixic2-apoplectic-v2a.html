apoplectic

ap-uh-PLEK-tik

What It Means

Apoplectic is overcome with extreme anger; furious to the point of losing composure.

See the word in context

The landlord went apoplectic when he discovered the tenants had painted every wall neon green without asking.

When the referee's call cost them the championship, the coach became apoplectic, screaming and slamming his clipboard onto the court.

Apoplectic describes fury so extreme it looks almost like a medical emergency -- red-faced, shaking, sputtering with rage.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    livid: Livid is fury you can read on someone's face -- white-lipped, glaring, barely contained. A livid person is still holding themselves together, though; apoplectic means the fury has taken over completely, leaving its victim shaking and sputtering, past the point of composure.

    incensed: Incensed means burning with indignation at a specific offense -- an insult, an injustice -- while still able to argue the point in sentences. Apoplectic anger has no argument left in it: composure is gone, and the anger is all there is.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unruffled: not upset or disturbed

    composed: calm and in control of one's emotions

    Where it sits · how completely anger takes over

    annoyed → apoplectic → berserk

    annoyed vs. apoplectic: Annoyed describes mild irritation, far short of the explosive fury of apoplectic.

    berserk vs. apoplectic: Berserk goes even further than apoplectic: an apoplectic person sputters and shakes with fury, but someone gone berserk has lost all restraint, lashing out wildly at anything in reach.
