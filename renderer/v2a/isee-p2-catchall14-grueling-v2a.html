grueling

GROO-uh-ling -- possibly from old slang 'to get one's gruel,' meaning to be punished or defeated; the exact origin is debated among etymologists.

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Grueling means so prolonged and demanding that it wears a person down toward exhaustion.

See the word in context

Basic training was so grueling that recruits later said the first two weeks felt longer than the rest of the program combined.

By hour six of the grueling job of moving furniture up four flights of stairs with no elevator, even the strongest volunteers were stopping every few steps just to catch their breath.

Grueling describes physical or mental effort pushed to the point of near-exhaustion -- volunteers who started strong but need to stop every few steps by hour six are experiencing exactly that kind of demanding strain.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    arduous: Arduous is the formal word for a task that demands great, sustained effort -- an arduous climb, an arduous restoration. Grueling looks at the toll instead: it says the effort wears a person down toward exhaustion. A job is arduous by its nature; it becomes grueling in the enduring.

    backbreaking: Backbreaking is strictly about hard physical labor -- hauling, digging, lifting until the body aches. Grueling stretches over mental strain just as easily: a grueling exam schedule or interview week. A day of hauling gravel is backbreaking; a day of medical-board testing is grueling with your back just fine.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    easy: requiring little effort

    effortless: done without any noticeable effort

    Where it sits · how harshly the effort wears you down

    exhausting → grueling → punishing

    exhausting vs. grueling: Exhausting is a common, everyday word for anything that drains your energy, while grueling adds a sharper sense of prolonged, punishing difficulty.

    punishing vs. grueling: Punishing goes further than grueling by suggesting the effort feels almost like a deliberate punishment, not just tiring but genuinely harsh.
