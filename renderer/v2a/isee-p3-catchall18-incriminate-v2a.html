incriminate

in-KRIM-uh-nayt -- from Latin in- ('into') + criminare ('to accuse'), from crimen ('crime, accusation').

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

To incriminate is to point to or strongly imply someone's guilt in a crime or wrongdoing.

See the word in context

The security footage clearly seemed to incriminate the night-shift cashier, though he insisted he was innocent.

Detectives found a receipt in the suspect's jacket that placed him at the exact store, at the exact time, that would go on to incriminate him more than any witness testimony.

That piece of evidence pointing directly toward someone's guilt -- even without a confession -- is exactly what it means to incriminate someone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    expose: To expose wrongdoing is to drag it into public view -- a reporter can expose a bribery scheme without pointing at any one person. To incriminate is narrower and more personal: it points to a particular person's guilt. Exposure names the wrong; incriminating evidence names the wrongdoer.

    frame: To frame someone is to plant or fake evidence so an innocent person looks guilty. To incriminate carries no trickery: genuine records can incriminate a suspect honestly. Framing manufactures the appearance of guilt, while incriminating evidence simply reveals where guilt already points.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    exculpate: to show that someone is not guilty of a crime

    absolve: to free someone from blame or guilt

    Where it sits · how directly guilt is stated

    implicate → incriminate → accuse

    implicate vs. incriminate: To implicate someone is milder than to incriminate them: phone records might implicate a driver merely by tying him to the scene, while evidence that incriminates points squarely at his guilt.

    accuse vs. incriminate: Accuse is stronger than incriminate: testimony can incriminate someone by pointing toward guilt, but to accuse them is to go further and declare that guilt openly and directly.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Incriminate describes evidence, statements, or actions that point toward guilt -- it's the opposite direction from acquit and exonerate, both taught elsewhere in this unit, which point away from guilt.
