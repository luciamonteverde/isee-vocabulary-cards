What It Means

Urbane means showing easy, courteous charm and seasoned social grace, staying smoothly at ease even in awkward moments.

See the word in context

Even at the chaotic press conference, the ambassador remained urbane, deflecting hostile questions with an easy smile.

Even when the waiter spilled wine on his sleeve, the urbane host simply laughed it off, refilled the glass himself, and kept the conversation flowing.

Handling an awkward moment with easy charm and unshaken social polish, rather than embarrassment, is exactly what urbane describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sophisticated: Sophisticated means having refined taste, culture, and experience -- knowing art, food, and ideas well. Urbane narrows that refinement to social manner: the easy, courteous charm a person shows in conversation. A sophisticated person knows the right wine to order; an urbane one keeps the whole dinner party at ease.

    debonair: Debonair describes charming, stylish, lighthearted confidence -- the well-dressed guest who breezes in with a grin. Urbane is quieter and more seasoned: courteous, practiced polish that smooths over awkward moments. Debonair is about dash and style; urbane is about graceful social skill.

    genteel: Genteel means polite, proper, and a little prim -- careful never to say anything out of place. Urbane is warmer and more relaxed: an urbane speaker can risk a playful aside and carry it off, smoothing tension instead of tiptoeing around it.

    worldly: Worldly means experienced in practical life and the ways of the world -- well-traveled, hard to shock, wise about how things really work. By itself it says nothing about charm. An urbane person turns that experience outward as smooth, gracious social ease; a worldly one may still be blunt or gruff.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    awkward: lacking social grace or ease

    unpolished: lacking refinement or grace

    Where it sits · how smoothly the charm works

    polished → urbane → suave

    polished vs. urbane: Polished is milder than urbane: a polished interviewee has practiced away the rough edges -- firm handshake, tidy answers -- while an urbane host goes beyond rehearsal, gliding through surprises with effortless, seasoned charm.

    suave vs. urbane: Suave goes further than urbane: an urbane guest is gracefully at ease in any room, while a suave charmer turns the smoothness up so high -- every word silk -- that you begin to wonder what he is selling.
