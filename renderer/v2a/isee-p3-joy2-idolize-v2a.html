What It Means

To idolize is to worship a mere human greatly or excessively, treating them as a flawless hero.

See the word in context

Millions of young skaters openly idolize the reigning Olympic gold medalist.

Countless fans idolize the retired quarterback, quoting his interviews from decades ago.

Idolize describes treating a person from afar as a hero worthy of near-worship.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    worship: Worship, in its strict sense, is devotion offered to something sacred — a god, a temple, a holy day. To idolize is to aim that same intensity at a mere human: pop stars, athletes, and heroes get idolized, treated as flawless even though they are only people.

    adore: To adore someone is to love and delight in them warmly, usually up close — a grandmother adores her grandchildren, and they adore her back. To idolize is one-way and distant: you set a person you barely know on a pedestal as a flawless hero.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    despise: feel contempt for

    scorn: regard with disdain

    Where it sits · how far respect turns into worship

    admire → idolize → lionize

    admire vs. idolize: Admiring someone is respectful and measured; idolizing them crosses into starstruck hero-worship.

    lionize vs. idolize: To lionize someone is to treat them as a celebrity in public, a more social step beyond privately idolizing them.
