menagerie

muh-NAJ-uh-ree -- from French menagerie, 'housing for animals,' from menage, 'household,' ultimately from Latin mansio, 'a dwelling' (related to manere, 'to remain, stay').

What It Means

A menagerie is a varied collection of live animals kept together, often private and smaller than a zoo, and figuratively any odd, lively mixture of things or people.

See the word in context

The retired circus owner kept a small menagerie of aging lions and one very opinionated camel in his backyard.

A wealthy collector's mansion turns out to house not just paintings but an entire menagerie: a pair of peacocks, several tortoises, and a llama that wanders the halls.

A menagerie is exactly that odd, varied collection of animals kept together in one place -- whether in a private home or a formal zoo.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assortment: An assortment is any varied collection at all -- candies in a tin, tools in a drawer, opinions around a table -- with nothing alive implied. A menagerie is literally a varied collection of animals kept together, and even its figurative uses keep that liveliness: a menagerie of houseguests practically squawks.

    hodgepodge: A hodgepodge is a jumbled mix thrown together with no order at all -- furniture styles, leftover paint colors, half-finished ideas. A menagerie, even used figuratively, keeps a hint of the exhibit about it: a varied collection of living, colorful creatures or characters gathered in one place, odd but on display.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    solitude: the state of being entirely alone, with nothing else around

    uniformity: the quality of being all the same, without variety

    Where it sits · how grand the animal collection is

    flock → menagerie → zoo

    flock vs. menagerie: A flock is the humblest kind of animal-keeping -- sheep or geese, all of one kind, grazing a single field -- while a menagerie is a grander, more curious sight: many different animals gathered into one varied collection.

    zoo vs. menagerie: Zoo is a more formal and larger-scale word than menagerie -- a zoo is typically a public institution built for a huge collection of animals, while a menagerie is often smaller and can even be a private, personal collection.
