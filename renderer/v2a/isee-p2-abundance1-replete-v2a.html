What It Means

Replete means abundantly filled or well supplied -- not merely containing a thing, but supplied with it in generous measure.

See the word in context

The new aquarium is replete with touch pools, glowing jellyfish tanks, and a walk-through shark tunnel.

Every shelf of the pantry is stacked two cans deep, the freezer is packed to the lid, and jars of preserves line the counter from end to end -- a kitchen replete with supplies.

A pantry filled to capacity like that leaves no corner bare. Replete describes something abundantly filled or well stocked -- not merely containing a thing, but supplied with it in generous measure.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    abounding: Abounding means existing in great plenty -- opportunities abounding in a boomtown, fish abounding on the reef. Abounding spotlights the plentiful things themselves, while replete describes their holder as richly supplied: when fish are abounding, the reef is replete with them.

    teeming: Teeming means swarming with busy, moving life -- a pond teeming with tadpoles, a market teeming with shoppers. Replete is calmer and broader: a shelf can be replete with trophies that never move, but only something alive and stirring can teem.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    empty: containing nothing

    devoid: completely lacking something

    Where it sits · how close to overflowing it is

    stocked → replete → brimming

    stocked vs. replete: Stocked is plainer and weaker than replete -- a stocked cupboard simply has what it needs, while a cupboard replete with food is generously, impressively full.

    brimming vs. replete: Brimming is even more vivid than replete: something replete is thoroughly well supplied, while something brimming is filled right up to the very edge, about to spill over.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Replete is nearly always followed by 'with': a museum replete with treasures, an essay replete with vivid details, a menu replete with choices. It is more formal and more emphatic than 'full of,' and it suggests generous, satisfying abundance rather than overcrowding.
