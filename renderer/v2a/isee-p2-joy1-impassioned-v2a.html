What It Means

Impassioned means filled with deep feeling that pours out at one charged moment of expression.

See the word in context

The coach's impassioned halftime talk turned the whole game around.

At the town meeting, a neighbor rises to make an impassioned defense of the ancient oak on Elm Street. Halfway through, she abandons her notecards entirely -- her voice cracks, her hands fly, and when she finally sits down, the room is silent.

Impassioned means filled with deep feeling on full display. It describes the delivery -- the plea, the appeal, the speech -- at one charged moment. It is about how the feeling pours out, not about a person's lasting temperament.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fiery: Fiery describes a speaker's whole style -- a fiery senator blazes in every debate, on every topic, every week. Impassioned belongs to one charged moment: a single plea, speech, or letter filled with deep feeling, even from someone otherwise quiet.

    stirring: Stirring describes the effect on the audience -- a stirring anthem rouses strong emotion in the people listening. Impassioned describes the speaker's side: the deep feeling pouring out of the person delivering the words, whether or not anyone listening is moved.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    halfhearted: done with little energy or enthusiasm

    unemotional: showing no feeling at all

    Where it sits · how deep the feeling behind it runs

    heated → impassioned → overwrought

    heated vs. impassioned: Heated only says the temperature rose -- a heated exchange can be nothing but temper -- while an impassioned speech burns with deep feeling for a cause, not just irritation.

    overwrought vs. impassioned: Overwrought goes further than impassioned: an impassioned speaker is filled with deep feeling but still in command of it, while an overwrought one has been overwhelmed -- the emotion runs the speaker instead of the speech.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Impassioned attaches to moments of expression: an impassioned plea, speech, defense, letter. It describes how feeling comes out at one charged moment. Its cluster neighbor fervent describes the feeling itself, which may stay quietly inside -- a fervent believer might never give an impassioned speech in her life.
