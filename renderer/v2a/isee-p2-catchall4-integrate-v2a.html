integrate

IN-tih-grayt — starts like 'integer,' a whole number; to integrate is to make things whole together.

What It Means

To integrate is to combine separate parts so that they work together as one whole.

See the word in context

The two departments worked all year to integrate their separate computer systems into one.

A new student moves to a school partway through the year, knowing no one. By spring she has integrated into school life: she has joined the debate team, eats lunch with a steady group of friends, and volunteers with the yearbook club.

She has become a working, connected part of the school community. Unlike 'attend,' which just means showing up, to integrate means becoming a joined, functioning part of the whole.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    combine: To combine is the everyday, general word for putting things together in any way at all — mixing, stacking, or joining. To integrate goes further: the combined pieces are fitted so they work together as one functioning whole. You can combine ingredients in a bowl without integrating anything.

    incorporate: To incorporate is to fold something into a larger whole that already exists — a chef incorporates a new spice into an old recipe. To integrate treats the parts more evenly: they are joined so they cooperate as one working system, rather than one piece being swallowed by another.

    unify: To unify is to bring things under one banner or identity — one flag, one name, one policy. To integrate is more practical: it makes the parts actually function together day to day. A company can be unified under one name while its two offices never integrate their work.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    separate: to keep or set apart

    segregate: to set apart from the main group

    Where it sits · how completely the parts are combined

    add → integrate → merge

    add vs. integrate: To add something is weaker than to integrate it — adding just places it alongside the rest, while integrating actually makes it a working part of the whole.

    merge vs. integrate: To merge is stronger than to integrate: integrating joins parts so they function together, but merging can blend them so completely that the original separate parts disappear.
