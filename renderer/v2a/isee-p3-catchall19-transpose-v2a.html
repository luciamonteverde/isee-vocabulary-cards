What It Means

To transpose is to switch two items, such as letters, digits, or notes, so that each takes the other's place within a single arrangement.

See the word in context

Copying the phone number in a hurry, Dana transposed two digits and spent the evening calling a confused stranger.

Proofreading his essay, Malik realized he had transposed the 'i' and 'e' in 'receive,' writing 'recieve' in every paragraph.

Switching two items -- letters, digits, notes -- so that each takes the other's place is exactly what transpose describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    swap: To swap is the casual, everyday word for two things trading places -- swapping seats on the bus, swapping sandwiches at lunch. To transpose is the same trade made precise and deliberate: the careful, almost technical switching of two letters, digits, or notes inside a larger arrangement.

    exchange: To exchange is to trade between parties -- you hand something over and receive something back, like exchanging gifts or phone numbers. To transpose involves no giving or receiving at all: within a single arrangement, two items simply switch positions, and nothing changes hands.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    preserve: to keep something in its original state

    maintain: to keep something unchanged

    Where it sits · how much of the order changes

    shift → transpose → invert

    shift vs. transpose: Shift is milder than transpose: shifting slides a single item to a new spot -- moving one appointment later in the day -- while transposing makes two items trade places outright, a bigger change to the order.

    invert vs. transpose: Invert goes further than transpose: transposing switches two neighboring items, while inverting flips an entire order upside down, opening with what had originally been the very last scene.
