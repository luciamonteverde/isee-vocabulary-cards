What It Means

Bravado is a bold, swaggering manner intended to impress or unsettle others, often masking fear or doubt.

See the word in context

Despite the bravado in his pre-fight interview, the challenger looked visibly pale as he walked to the ring.

The freshman's loud bravado about not being scared of the haunted house evaporated the moment the door creaked open.

The freshman wasn't actually fearless -- he was putting on a bold front to seem tough, which is exactly what bravado describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    swagger: Swagger is bold confidence carried in the body — a rolling, show-off way of walking and holding yourself. Bravado is the whole false-front act: a bold manner put on to impress or unsettle, often with fear underneath. A swagger can be entirely genuine; bravado is a performance.

    bluster: Bluster is loud, aggressive talk — threats and boasts with little real substance behind them. Bravado is broader and can be completely silent: a swaggering manner meant to impress, all posture and no promises. Bluster needs a voice; bravado only needs an audience.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    humility: a modest, unassuming manner

    timidity: a lack of courage or confidence

    Where it sits · how much of a performance it is

    confidence → bravado → bombast

    confidence vs. bravado: Confidence is simply a sense of self-assurance, without the exaggerated, performance-like quality of bravado.

    bombast vs. bravado: Bombast pushes bold display even further into pompous, over-the-top speech, beyond ordinary bravado.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Bravado describes a person's swaggering behavior or manner, not an object's appearance -- it's the human, psychological cousin of showiness, distinct from an ostentatious mansion or a gaudy outfit.
