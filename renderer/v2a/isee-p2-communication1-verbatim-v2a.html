What It Means

Verbatim is in exactly the same words as were used originally.

See the word in context

She memorized the poem and recited it verbatim, without dropping a syllable.

For his report on volcanoes, Diego copies a paragraph from the encyclopedia and pastes it in unchanged. His teacher lays the two texts side by side: every word identical. 'If you use a source verbatim,' she says, 'it goes in quotation marks with credit -- otherwise you must put the ideas in your own words.'

Diego copied the paragraph verbatim: in exactly the same words the encyclopedia used, down to the last comma. Verbatim is neither good nor bad by itself -- court reporters are paid to capture testimony verbatim -- but copying verbatim without credit is plagiarism, which is why the word appears in every writing class.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    to the letter: To the letter means carrying out instructions or rules with every detail exactly right -- you follow a recipe or a contract to the letter. Verbatim is about repeating words: a quotation is verbatim when not one word differs from the original.

    unabridged: Unabridged promises completeness: nothing has been cut, every chapter intact. Verbatim promises exactness instead -- whatever is repeated keeps the original's exact words. A one-line quotation can be perfectly verbatim without being anywhere near complete.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    loosely: in a free, inexact way

    roughly: only approximately

    Where it sits · how faithful to the original phrasing

    precisely → verbatim → word-for-word

    precisely vs. verbatim: Precisely makes a weaker promise than verbatim: it guarantees accuracy of meaning -- the facts arrive intact -- while the wording itself may be entirely recast in the reporter's own words.

    word-for-word vs. verbatim: Word-for-word sits at the very top of this ladder because nothing can be more faithful to the original phrasing: not one word replaced, reordered, or dropped. It is the plain-English twin of verbatim -- the two make the same absolute promise.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Verbatim is an adverb that rides on verbs of quoting: quote, repeat, transcribe, recite, record something verbatim. The test is absolute -- change even one word and it is no longer verbatim; it is a paraphrase. On reading passages, 'quoted verbatim' signals that the exact original wording matters, often because someone is checking it against what was really said.
