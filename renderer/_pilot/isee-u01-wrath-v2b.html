What It Means

Wrath is intense anger, often with a desire to get back at whoever caused it.

See the word in context

The king's wrath was so great that his advisors feared even mentioning the failed harvest.

A myth tells of a sea god who discovers that sailors broke their promise and, in his wrath, gathers a storm to punish their whole fleet.

The anger here is enormous and aimed at payback — that vengeful, punishing anger is exactly what wrath names. Because revenge is the whole point of the sentence, wrath is more precise than the plain word "anger."

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    indignation: Indignation is anger sparked by unfairness or an insult — the heat of feeling that something is simply wrong. It usually stops at protest. Wrath goes much further: intense anger that wants to strike back at whoever caused the injury. A line-cutter stirs indignation; a betrayal calls down wrath.

    rage: Rage is violent, uncontrolled anger — a person in a rage shouts, slams doors, and cannot think straight. Wrath burns just as hot but stays aimed: grand, serious anger that plans consequences and payback. Rage is anger with the steering wheel gone; wrath knows exactly where it is driving.

    spite: Spite is a small, petty urge to hurt or annoy someone — keying a car, hiding the remote, replying with a dig. Wrath is anger on a grand scale, the anger of kings and gods, answering a real injury with real consequences. Spite is mean and minor; wrath is mighty and momentous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    serenity: calm, untroubled peace of mind

    forbearance: patiently holding back anger or revenge

    Where it sits · how fierce and vengeful the feeling

    annoyance → anger → fury → wrath

    annoyance vs. wrath: Annoyance is far weaker than wrath: it is the mild irritation of a buzzing fly, nowhere near wrath's fierce, vengeful rage.

    anger vs. wrath: Anger is weaker than wrath; anger is the ordinary heat of being upset, while wrath burns hotter and often carries a wish to punish.

    fury vs. wrath: Fury is intense, but wrath is stronger still — fury is anger boiling over in the moment, while wrath adds a lasting determination to strike back.

        Word Choice

        formal / literary

        Wrath is grand, serious anger — the anger of kings, gods, and storms. Reach for it when the anger is powerful and threatens consequences. For everyday irritation, the plain word "anger" is the simpler, better choice.
