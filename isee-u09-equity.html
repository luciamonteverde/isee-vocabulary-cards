Equity

/EK-wuh-tee/ — rhymes with purity · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Equity is fairness and impartiality in the way people are treated or decisions are made.

Show it in context

The new grading policy was praised for its equity — every student faced the same standard.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when equity is the exact word.

Worked Example

The scholarship program required the same essay from all applicants and graded them on identical criteria, ensuring that every candidate was measured by the same fair standard.

Equity is more precise than 'fairness' because it emphasizes a systematic, impartial standard applied equally to everyone. This is not just being fair in general—it's the specific practice of measuring all people by a single consistent measure.

Your Turn

The teacher gave different students different amounts of homework based on how quickly they worked.

Is this an example of equity?

Yes

Not quite — By this card's root sense, equity means applying one equal standard to everyone. Giving students different amounts of work is differentiation by individual need, not the same measure for all.

No

Correct — Equity here means treating everyone by the same standard and criteria. Adjusting homework to each student's pace is individualized, so it is not equity in this equal-treatment sense.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
fairness

Being just and treating people well, but doesn't necessarily require the same systematic standard that equity demands.

impartiality

Not showing bias or favoring one side, but doesn't necessarily mean applying the same standard to everyone the way equity does.

justice

Dealing with right and wrong in a moral sense; broader than equity's focus on equal treatment by the same measure.

Antonyms · opposites

discrimination — Unfair treatment based on bias; the opposite of equity's impartial and equal standards.

Build the Word

aequus Latin: 'equal, level, fair'

Equal treatment is the heart of equity — it means giving everyone the same measure of fairness and the same standard of judgment.

The two teams had equal numbers of skilled players on the field.

Same root, new word — what does equal mean here?

the same in number or amount

Correct — Equity and equal both grow from the Latin aequus, 'equal, level.' Equity gives everyone the same standard of fairness, and equal numbers means each team has the same count.

greater in number than the other

Not quite — Competition makes an advantage tempting, but the root aequus means 'level, same.' Equal numbers means the two teams match, not that one has more.

skilled or talented

Not quite — The sentence mentions skilled players, so this word feels close, but 'equal' describes the matching count, and aequus means 'level, same,' not 'talented.'

The root connection →

The root aequus ('level, same') runs through both: equity levels the standard so all are judged the same way, and two teams are equal when their numbers sit level with each other.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Equity is a formal word often used in policy, education, and law contexts. Use it when you're talking about treating everyone by the same fair standard. For everyday situations, the simpler word 'fairness' is usually better.
