Repose

/ri-POHZ/ — stress on second syllable, rhymes with 'suppose' · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Repose is a state of rest and peaceful calm, free from activity or stress.

Show it in context

After the harvest, the farmhands enjoyed a well-earned day of repose.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when repose is the exact word.

Worked Example

After weeks of intense competition, the athlete finally found repose in a quiet resort, free from pressure and expectation.

Repose is more precise than 'rest' or 'sleep' because it specifically emphasizes PEACEFUL CALM and freedom from stress—not just physical recovery. You can rest anxiously; you cannot have repose without peace.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
rest

Stopping activity or recovering from fatigue; rest doesn't necessarily imply the peaceful calm that repose requires.

relaxation

Unwinding and releasing tension; similar to repose but less about deep peace or true restoration.

respite

A temporary break from something stressful; respite is briefer and more reactive than repose's deeper, lasting peace.

Antonyms · opposites

turmoil — Chaos and disturbance; while turmoil is inner conflict and stress, repose is complete peace and calm.

Build the Word

re- + pausare Latin: again + to stop/rest

Re- means again, pausare means to stop or rest; together they suggest returning to a state of peace and rest.

As the harsh winter set in, the beetle entered diapause, remaining completely still and taking no food until the warmth of spring returned.

Same root, new word — what does diapause mean here?

a sudden burst of rapid growth

Not quite — That is the opposite of stopping, but the sentence shows the beetle staying still and taking no food. The root 'pausare' means 'to stop or rest,' so the answer should describe activity halting, not speeding up.

a shedding of the skin or outer shell

Not quite — Molting is a different process entirely, while the sentence describes stillness and stopped activity. The root 'pausare' means 'to stop or rest,' pointing toward paused activity rather than shedding a shell.

a resting state in which growth and activity stop

Correct — The root 'pausare' means 'to stop or rest,' the same root that gives repose its sense of settling back into rest. Diapause carries that 'stop': the beetle stays completely still and takes no food, the resting state where growth and activity halt.

The root connection →

Repose comes from 're-' (again) plus 'pausare' (to stop or rest), a return to a state of rest. Diapause shares that 'pausare': a resting state in which an animal's growth and activity stop, just as the beetle's do through winter.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Repose is the word for profound, peaceful rest—not just physical recovery but true peace of mind. Reach for it when describing deep restoration and calm, especially in reflective or literary moments. For temporary breaks, 'respite' is lighter; for simple recovery from tiredness, 'rest' is plainer.
