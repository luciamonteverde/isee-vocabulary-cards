Agenda

/uh-JEN-duh/ — stress on JEN · noun

Hear It
What It Means

An agenda is a list of items or topics to be discussed or accomplished, such as during a meeting or over the course of a day.

Show it in context

The first item on the meeting's agenda was the leaking roof.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when agenda is the exact word.

Worked Example

The principal walks into the staff meeting with a printed list: discuss the science lab renovation, review the new attendance policy, approve the field trip budget. She presents each item in order and keeps the group focused on completing every topic before dismissal.

Agenda is more precise than "list" or "plan" because it names a *structured list of topics or items to be addressed in a meeting or organized event*. A plain "list" is too vague; agenda suggests purpose, order, and formal discussion in a specific context.

Your Turn

Before grocery shopping, Mom writes down: milk, eggs, bread, cheese, cereal.

Is agenda the precise word for what Mom has written?

shopping list

Correct — Milk, eggs, bread, cheese, and cereal are items to pick up at the store, exactly what a shopping list holds.

agenda

Not quite — Mom is jotting groceries to buy, not topics to discuss or tasks to work through at an organized meeting or event, which is what an agenda lays out.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
list

A simple enumeration of items. Agenda is a list with formal purpose, usually for discussion in a meeting.

plan

A broader strategy or schedule. Agenda is a specific list for one meeting or day, not a long-term strategy.

itinerary

A detailed schedule for travel or an event. Agenda is a list of topics to discuss, not a timed schedule of activities.

Antonyms · opposites

improvisation — Making things up without a plan. Agenda requires advance planning and structure.

Build the Word

agendum Latin: "a thing to be done" (from agere, "to do")

Agenda is the plural of the Latin agendum—it literally means "things to be done," suggesting a list of actions or topics that need to be addressed or accomplished.

The real estate agent helped her find a house that met all her requirements.

someone who does or acts on behalf of others

Correct — Correct. Agent comes from the Latin agere (to do or act), the same source as agenda, so an agent is a person who acts on someone's behalf.

a document that must be signed at a sale

Not quite — Agent comes from the root agere (to do or act): it names the person who acts for someone, not a paper that gets signed.

The root connection →

Agenda and agent both trace to Latin agere, meaning to do or act. An agenda is a set of things to be done, and an agent is someone who does or acts for another.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Use agenda when referring to a structured list of topics for a meeting or organized activity. For a personal to-do list, shopping list, or casual checklist, list or plan fits better. For a detailed timetable of a journey, itinerary is more precise.
