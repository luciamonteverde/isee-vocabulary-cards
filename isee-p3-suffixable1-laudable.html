Laudable

LAW-duh-bul adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Laudable is deserving praise or admiration.

Show it in context

Though the fundraiser only collected a fraction of its ambitious goal, organizers called the community's laudable turnout on a rainy Tuesday night a real success in itself.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Critics agreed that the freshman senator's laudable attempt to reform the outdated voting system, even though it ultimately failed in committee, showed real political courage.

Laudable praises the effort or intention behind an action -- something can be laudable even if it doesn't fully succeed.

rootlaud-to praise (Latin laudare)
suffix-ableworthy of (Latin -abilis)

Laudable comes from Latin laudare ('to praise') -- the same root behind applaud, literally 'to praise toward' someone, which began as clapping the hands at a performance. A laudable act is, at root, one worth praising.

Use the root in a new word: applaud

The entire stadium rose to applaud the retiring pitcher's final game.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Laudable is a formal way to praise an effort or intention -- it works even when the outcome fell short, since it's really commenting on how worthy the attempt itself was.
