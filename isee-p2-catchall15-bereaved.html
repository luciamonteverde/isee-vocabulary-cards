Bereaved

buh-REEVD -- from Old English be- (an intensifying prefix) + reafian ('to rob, plunder'), a rare surviving relative of the same ancient root that also gave English the word 'rob.' adjective

What It Means

Bereaved is suffering the loss of a loved one.

Worked Example

Neighbors quietly take turns bringing dinner to a widow's house every evening for weeks after her husband's funeral, without ever being asked.

That state of grieving the recent loss of someone deeply loved is exactly what bereaved describes, and it's exactly why a community rallies around a family going through it.

rootreave-to rob, plunder (a rare surviving root, also behind the English word 'rob') (Old English (reafian))

Bereaved comes from Old English be- (an intensifying prefix) plus reafian ('to rob, plunder') -- the same ancient root that also gave English the everyday word 'rob' -- so to be bereaved is, at its etymological root, to have something precious violently 'robbed' away, which is exactly how a fresh loss can feel.

Use the root in a new word: rob

Thieves broke in and robbed the shop of nearly everything valuable overnight.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Bereaved is a gentle, respectful word used specifically for someone who has recently lost a loved one to death -- it's commonly paired with 'family' or 'spouse,' as in 'grief counselors work with bereaved families.'
