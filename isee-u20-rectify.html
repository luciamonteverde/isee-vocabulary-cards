Rectify

/REK-tuh-fy/ — rhymes with fly, stress on first syllable · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To rectify something is to correct a mistake or fix a problem so that it is right again.

Show it in context

The bank rectified the billing error within a day.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when rectify is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the lab discovered that their measurements were incorrect, they immediately rectified their calculations and resubmitted the accurate results.

Rectify captures both the correction of the mistake and the restoration of things to a right or correct state. Fix is too vague; correct alone doesn't emphasize the sense of making things right again.

Your Turn

The teacher rectified her seating chart by moving a few students to different seats.

Is rectify the most precise word here, or would another word fit better?

rectify

Not quite — The teacher's original seating chart was not wrong or broken; she simply wanted a different layout. Rectify would only fit if she were correcting an actual mistake in the chart.

rearrange

Correct — Moving a few students into different seats is a change of arrangement, which is precisely what this scenario shows rather than the fixing of an error.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
correct

Fix a mistake directly, but more neutral and plain; doesn't emphasize the sense of making things just or right.

fix

Repair or solve a problem, but very general and vague about what kind of problem or what makes things right.

remedy

Fix or cure a bad situation, but emphasizes the solution more than the correction of an actual error or mistake.

Antonyms · opposites

corrupt — Make something dishonest or wrong—the opposite of correcting something and restoring it to a right state.

Build the Word

rectus Latin: 'right, straight, just'

Rectify means to make something straight or right again, to guide it back to the correct course just as one who is just or right would.

She was admired for her rectitude in all business dealings.

Same root, new word — what does rectitude mean here?

moral uprightness, honesty, and integrity

Correct — Correct. Rectitude shares the Latin root rectus (right, straight, just) with rectify: rectify makes something right again, and rectitude is the quality of being morally upright.

great skill or talent at a craft

Not quite — That describes expertise, not moral character. Rectitude comes from the Latin rectus (right, straight), which points to being morally upright rather than being talented.

The root connection →

Rectify and rectitude both come from the Latin rectus, meaning right or straight. Rectify makes a wrong thing right, and rectitude is the quality of being morally straight and upright.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Rectify is for correcting errors and restoring things to a right state—it's more formal and precise than plain fix. Use it when you're emphasizing that a mistake was made and must be corrected, especially in professional or formal contexts. For casual situations, fix or correct works fine.
