Wane

/wayn/ — rhymes with 'rain' · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To wane is to gradually grow weaker, smaller, or less important over time.

Show it in context

Interest in the trivia club waned until only three members remained.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when wane is the exact word.

Worked Example

The king's patience waned with each interruption from his advisors, his tolerance shrinking from hours of listening to mere minutes.

Wane is more precise than 'decreased' or 'disappeared' because it captures the gradual, ongoing loss of something valuable—in this case, his willingness to listen. It's not that patience vanished all at once; it weakened step by step.

Your Turn

Sarah wanted to study for her history test, but her motivation completely disappeared by mid-afternoon.

Should this sentence say her motivation 'waned'?

Yes

Not quite — Tempting, since her motivation dropped. But wane means to fade gradually, step by step, while Sarah's motivation vanished all at once.

No

Correct — Correct. To wane is to weaken slowly over time, like a shrinking moon. Sarah's motivation disappeared suddenly and completely, so it did not wane.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
diminish

To become less. Diminish doesn't emphasize gradual decline the way wane does.

decline

To slope downward formally. Decline is more neutral about the rate or importance of the decrease.

ebb

To flow back like the tide. Ebb is similar to wane but sounds more poetic and literary.

Antonyms · opposites

wax — To grow larger and stronger—the opposite of waning.

How Strong Is It?
diminish→wane→dwindle→evaporate

Wane — to gradually weaken and lose power or importance over time. More inevitable and gradual than a simple 'diminish.' (our word)

Build the Word

wanian Old English: 'to diminish, become less'

To wane is to become less through a natural, inevitable process, like the moon dimming as it disappears.

The waning daylight made it hard to read the map without a lantern.

Same root, new word — what does waning mean here?

suddenly switching off all at once

Not quite — Tempting if you imagine the light simply disappearing. But waning means fading little by little, not shutting off in an instant.

growing gradually less or dimmer

Correct — Wane and waning share the Old English root wanian, 'to diminish, become less.' Waning daylight is light in the act of diminishing, the same slow decline that wane names.

shining brighter and growing stronger

Not quite — Tempting, because we associate daylight with brightness. But waning daylight is fading, the opposite of growing stronger.

The root connection →

The old root wanian, 'to grow less,' runs through both: to wane is to shrink gradually, and waning simply describes that fading in progress, like light or the moon slipping away.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Wane is precise when something gradually loses strength or importance over an inevitable cycle—interest in hobbies, power, reputation, health. For a simple shrinking or decrease, 'diminish' works fine. For something slowly wasting away, 'languish' might be better.
