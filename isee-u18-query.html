Query

/KWIR-ee/ — rhymes with worry · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A query is a question someone asks in order to get information or to clear up confusion about something.

Show it in context

The librarian answered my query about interlibrary loans within the hour.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when query is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the software developer encountered an unusual error message, she submitted a technical query to the support forum and received a solution within hours.

Query emphasizes seeking clarification or information to resolve confusion, which is more precise than simply saying 'question' — because it implies the person is actively trying to clear up something they don't understand.

Your Turn

During lunch, Jake asked his friend whether she wanted a sandwich.

Is 'query' the right word here?

Yes

Not quite — It might seem like any question counts, but query specifically means seeking clarification or information to resolve confusion, and Jake is only asking whether his friend wants a sandwich.

No

Correct — Jake is simply asking about a preference, an ordinary everyday question. Query means seeking clarification or information to resolve confusion, which is not what is happening here.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
question

Any request for information. Query is more formal and specifically implies seeking clarification on something confusing.

inquiry

A formal request for information. Both are formal, but inquiry often has a more official or institutional flavor.

request

An ask for something. Query is specifically about seeking information or clarification, not just any kind of ask.

Antonyms · opposites

statement — A declaration or assertion. Opposite of query — you're providing information rather than seeking it.

How Strong Is It?
question→inquiry→query→investigation

Query — more formal than a simple question, less exhaustive than a full investigation. (our word)

Build the Word

quaerere Latin: to seek, to ask

The same root as 'quest' — both are about seeking or searching for something you need.

The detective embarked on a quest to find the missing evidence from the case.

Same root, new word — what does quest mean here?

a long, purposeful search for something important

Correct — Query and quest both grow from Latin quaerere, 'to seek or ask.' A query seeks information with a question, and a quest is a drawn-out search that seeks a goal.

a quick question asked out of curiosity

Not quite — It is tempting because query and quest share a root and 'quest' looks like a short 'question,' but in this sentence the detective is carrying out a search, not asking something.

a difficult test or challenge to pass

Not quite — A quest can feel like a challenge, but the sentence is about actively searching for missing evidence, not about passing a test.

The root connection →

The shared root quaerere means 'to seek.' Watch how it points inward to a single question in query and outward to a whole journey of searching in quest.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / slightly formal

Query is more formal than 'question' — use it when you're asking for clarification or information in academic or professional settings. For everyday conversations, 'question' works fine.
