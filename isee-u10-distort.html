Distort

/dis-TORT/ — rhymes with court · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To distort something is to twist or change its natural shape or appearance so it looks different from reality.

Show it in context

The funhouse mirrors distort your reflection into wavy shapes.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when distort is the exact word.

Worked Example

The photographer used filters to distort the landscape, stretching the mountains into impossible shapes and colors.

Distort captures the idea of deliberately twisting something into a false or unreal appearance. A plain word like 'changed' doesn't convey the idea of bending it into something unrecognizable.

Your Turn

A reporter left out a few details when writing about the event.

Is distort the precise word here, or something else?

omitted

Correct — Leaving details out is omitting them. Nothing that remained was twisted or changed, so 'omitted' names exactly what happened.

distorted

Not quite — To distort is to twist what is there into a false shape. The reporter did not twist the facts that stayed, only dropped some, so 'distort' overstates it.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
twist

Bends or turns something; distort goes further, twisting appearance into something false or unrecognizable.

warp

Bends and distorts shape; very close to distort, but emphasizes the bending action rather than the unreality of the result.

alter

Changes something in a general way; distort is more specific, emphasizing the twisting into something unreal.

Antonyms · opposites

clarify — Reveals truth clearly; the opposite of distort, which twists something into confusion or falsehood.

How Strong Is It?
change→twist→warp→distort

Distort is the strongest: twisting appearance into something that looks unreal or false. (our word)

Build the Word

distortus Latin: 'twisted apart'

From dis- (apart) + torquere (to twist): something twisted away from its true form.

The acrobat could contort her body into impossible positions.

Same root, new word — what does contort mean here?

to stretch the body to become taller

Not quite — Tempting because acrobats are so flexible, but contort carries torquere ('to twist'), meaning bending and twisting into odd shapes, not lengthening.

to twist or bend into an unusual shape (from con- 'together' + torquere 'to twist')

Correct — Both share Latin torquere, 'to twist.' Distort twists a fact away from its true form, and contort twists a body away from its natural shape.

to hold the body perfectly still

Not quite — Tempting as a feat of control, but the root torquere means 'to twist,' the opposite of staying rigid and motionless.

The root connection →

Distort and contort are built on the same Latin root torquere, 'to twist,' with different prefixes: dis- ('apart') twists something away from the truth, while con- ('together') twists limbs into a knot. The twisting motion is the shared thread.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Use distort when something is deliberately or noticeably twisted into a false or unreal appearance. For simple changes or modifications, 'change' or 'modify' is simpler and often better.
