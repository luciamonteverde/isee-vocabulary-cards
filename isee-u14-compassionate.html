Compassionate

/kum-PAH-shun-it/ — rhymes with 'passionate' · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is compassionate feels genuine concern for people who are suffering and wants to help ease their pain.

Show it in context

The compassionate nurse stayed late to comfort the frightened patient.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when compassionate is the exact word.

Worked Example

The nurse stayed late to hold the hand of a patient facing surgery the next morning, listening to their fears without judgment.

Compassionate is precise here because it captures not just kindness, but genuine concern for the patient's suffering combined with a desire to help. The nurse didn't just perform her job; she felt the patient's anxiety and actively eased it. That combination—understanding pain and acting to help—is exactly what compassionate means.

Your Turn

A manager shook hands with an employee before an important presentation.

Is compassionate the precise word here, or should it say something else?

courteous

Correct — A handshake before a presentation is a polite, professional gesture, and 'courteous' names exactly that kind of good manners.

compassionate

Not quite — Compassionate means sharing in someone's suffering or distress, and a routine handshake shows no sign that the manager felt or responded to the employee's stress.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
kind

General goodness or politeness. Compassionate goes deeper: it's concern sparked by another's suffering.

empathetic

Understanding another's feelings from their perspective. Compassionate adds action: you don't just see their pain; you move to ease it.

benevolent

Wishing well toward others in general. Compassionate is more specific: an active response to present suffering.

Antonyms · opposites

callous — Indifferent to others' pain; the emotional opposite of compassion.

How Strong Is It?
indifferent→kind→empathetic→compassionate

Compassionate — the strongest response: genuine concern for suffering that moves you to help. (our word)

Build the Word

com- + pathos Latin: 'com-passio' — literally 'suffering with'

The root shows the core image: to feel another's pain not from a distance, but alongside them—entering into their experience.

Even after the fourth delay, she stayed patient, quietly enduring the long wait without a single complaint.

Same root, new word — what does patient mean here?

able to endure delay or hardship calmly

Correct — Patient traces to the Latin verb 'pati,' meaning to suffer or endure, the very same root inside compassionate (com- + pati, 'to suffer with'); a patient person endures hardship, and a compassionate person endures it right alongside someone else.

receiving care in a hospital or clinic

Not quite — 'Patient' can name a person under medical care, and that meaning also grows from this root, but here 'patient' describes how she acts, not that she is a hospital case.

eager to hurry things along

Not quite — The delays might make you expect impatience, but the sentence says she endured the wait calmly, which is the opposite of rushing.

The root connection →

Heads up: this card's chip labels the root 'pathos,' but that is Greek and belongs to sympathetic, not compassionate. Compassionate is built from Latin 'pati' (suffer, endure), which also gives us patient (one who endures) and passion (originally 'suffering'). The honest bridge here is compassionate to patient through Latin pati, not a shared Greek 'pathos.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Compassionate describes deep, genuine concern for those suffering—the kind that moves you to action. When someone shows basic kindness, they're kind; when they feel another's pain and want to help ease it, they're compassionate.
