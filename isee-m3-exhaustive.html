Here's how to decide when exhaustive is the exact word.

Worked Example

The detective's exhaustive review of the surveillance footage revealed every second of the suspect's movements that night.

Exhaust + -ive: Exhaust means to use up completely or deplete. The -ive suffix means 'having the quality of, inclined to.' So exhaustive = having the quality of using something up completely. In analysis and search contexts, this means a review that uses up every possibility or detail, leaving nothing out. This decomposition reveals why exhaustive doesn't mean tiring — that's exhausting, a different adjective from the same verb.

Your Turn

After the movie, we felt exhaustive and couldn't stop talking about it.

Is exhaustive the precise word to describe how the viewers felt?

exhausting

Correct — The viewers felt tired, and 'exhausting' describes something that drains a person's energy.

exhaustive

Not quite — 'Exhaustive' comes from the same verb 'exhaust' but means thorough and complete, covering every detail, so it fits a search or review, not how people feel.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
thorough

Complete and careful, but doesn't emphasize the sense of 'leaving nothing out' to the same degree that exhaustive does.

comprehensive

Covers a wide range; similar to exhaustive but slightly less intense in its absolute 'nothing left out' quality.

meticulous

Extremely careful and precise; exhaustive emphasizes complete coverage of all details, while meticulous emphasizes care in handling each one.

Antonyms · opposites

cursory — Quick, superficial, and incomplete; the opposite of thoroughly examining every detail.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

exhaust + -ive

exhaust + -ive: having the quality of using something up completely — a search so thorough it uses up every single possibility. Do not confuse it with exhausting (tiring), a different adjective from the same verb.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Reach for exhaustive when describing a review, search, or analysis that covers every possible detail or option with nothing left out. For everyday contexts, thorough is simpler. Exhaustive is stronger and implies absolute completeness. Key distinction: exhausting uses the same root but is a different word entirely.
