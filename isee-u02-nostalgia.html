Nostalgia

/no-STAL-jə/ — rhymes with regalia · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Nostalgia is a wistful, fond longing for a time or place from the past.

Show it in context

Hearing the old school bell filled her with nostalgia for her childhood summers.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when nostalgia is the exact word.

Worked Example

She discovered a box of letters from her grandmother, written in fading ink decades ago, and suddenly found herself weeping — not from sadness, but from a tender, bittersweet longing for those long-ago summers on her grandmother's porch.

Nostalgia is far more precise than 'longing' or 'sadness.' It's not just wanting something, and it's not just feeling low — it's that specific wistful, fond ache you feel when remembering a cherished time that's gone forever and can never return.

Your Turn

A student discovers that the school is demolishing her elementary building and feels upset.

Is the student feeling nostalgia, or something else?

Sadness

Correct — She's simply sad that a cherished place is being destroyed, which is plain sadness or loss rather than the fond remembering nostalgia demands.

Nostalgia

Not quite — Nostalgia requires wistfully, fondly remembering happy times in the past, but we only know she's upset — not that she's fondly recalling anything.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
longing

Wanting something, but it doesn't necessarily mean for the past, and it lacks nostalgia's bittersweet, wistful, and fondly remembering quality.

homesickness

Longing for a specific place (home), sharper and more acute than nostalgia, and less about reflection on cherished memories.

yearning

Deep, intense longing with an ache, but not necessarily for the past, and without nostalgia's flavor of fond, wistful remembering.

Antonyms · opposites

apathy — Indifference to the past — the opposite of the wistful, fond longing that defines nostalgia.

How Strong Is It?
memory→reminiscence→nostalgia→melancholy

Nostalgia — stronger than mere memory or reminiscence: a wistful, bittersweet longing for a cherished time from the past. (our word)

Build the Word

nostos + algos Greek: 'return home' + 'pain'

Nostalgia was coined in the 17th century to name the pain Swiss mercenaries felt when far from home — the ache of longing to return to a cherished place or time you can never truly go back to.

The neuralgia sent sharp, stabbing pains shooting along the nerves of her face whenever a cold wind touched her cheek.

Same root, new word — what does neuralgia mean here?

a sudden numbness or loss of feeling in a limb

Not quite — Wrong-direction guess: describes lost sensation, opposite of pain.

sharp, shooting pain that travels along the path of a nerve

Correct — neuralgia = neuron 'nerve' + algos 'pain'; shares nostalgia's algos (pain) root but is rarer and more vocabulary-expanding than 'analgesic'.

a swelling or inflammation of the joints

Not quite — Plausible medical term but points to joints/swelling, unrelated to nerve-pain.

The root connection →

The root nostos + algos pairs 'return home' with algos, meaning 'pain.' Neuralgia keeps that identical algos and attaches it instead to neuron ('nerve'), so where nostalgia is the ache of longing for a lost home, neuralgia is pain running literally along a nerve.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / somewhat literary

Nostalgia names something specific: the tender, bittersweet longing for a cherished time in the past. Don't use it just for sadness or missing something. Reserve it for that particular wistful, fond ache — the feeling that comes from remembering good times that can't return.
