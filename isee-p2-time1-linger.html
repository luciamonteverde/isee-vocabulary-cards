Linger

LING-gur -- from Middle English lengen ('to prolong, delay'), related to 'long.' verb

Hear It
What It Means

To linger to remain somewhere or in some state longer than necessary or expected.

Show it in context

Even after the guests went home, the smell of burnt toast continued to linger in the kitchen for hours.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Long after the last customer left, the smell of fresh bread would still linger in the empty bakery until well after closing.

Notice this is about a lingering PRESENCE -- a smell hanging around, not a whole process dragging on. Linger always describes something, like a scent, a feeling, or a guest, that simply stays behind longer than you'd expect.

root-leng-long (Middle English (lengen))

Linger comes from Middle English lengen, meaning 'to prolong or delay' -- related to the plain word 'long' -- so to linger is simply to stay a little too long, whether that's a scent in a room or a guest at the door.

Use the root in a new word: lengthy

The lengthy delay at the airport left dozens of passengers pacing near the gate.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Linger is about a lingering PRESENCE -- a smell, a feeling, a person who doesn't quite leave -- not a whole extended process. Compare Protracted, which describes an entire process or event, like a negotiation or an illness, dragging on longer than expected.
