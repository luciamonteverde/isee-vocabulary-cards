Meticulous

/muh-TIK-yuh-lus/ — stress on TIK · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is meticulous is extremely careful and pays close attention to every small detail in their work.

Show it in context

The meticulous jeweler checked every link of the chain twice.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when meticulous is the exact word.

Worked Example

The meticulous surgeon reviewed the X-rays three times, checking every bone before making any cut.

Meticulous captures not just carefulness but obsessive attention to every tiny detail. 'Careful' alone misses the sense of systematic checking and fear of error that defines meticulous work.

Your Turn

The teacher graded papers in 20 minutes.

Is meticulous the right word here?

meticulous

Not quite — Grading a full set of papers in only 20 minutes leaves no time for the slow, exhaustive checking that meticulous work demands, so it actually contradicts the scenario rather than describing it.

hasty

Correct — Rushing through grading in 20 minutes fits hasty perfectly, since hasty captures the speed and lack of careful attention that this scenario shows.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
careful

Any cautious attention; meticulous is far more extreme and obsessively systematic.

precise

About accuracy alone; meticulous adds the time, fussiness, and checking required to achieve that precision.

thorough

Complete coverage of a task; meticulous adds the obsessive attention to the smallest possible details.

Antonyms · opposites

careless — Indifferent to details — the opposite of meticulous fear of error and systematic checking.

Build the Word

metus Latin: fear, dread

Meticulous people are so fearful of making mistakes that they check every detail obsessively — their dread of error drives their extreme precision.

Same root, new word — what does vigilant mean here?

carefully watchful and alert, on the lookout for possible danger

Correct — Correct. Vigilant means carefully watchful and alert, the same close, careful attentiveness that meticulous applies to detailed work.

sleepy and slow to notice what is happening nearby

Not quite — This is the opposite of the guard's behavior; the sentence shows him actively watching all night for any breach, which is alert attention, not drowsiness.

The root connection →

Honest note: vigilant and meticulous are NOT actually from the same root. Vigilant comes from Latin vigilare, meaning to watch or stay awake, while meticulous comes from Latin metus, meaning fear or dread (a meticulous person is careful as if afraid of making a mistake). They only share a theme of careful attentiveness, not an etymology.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / professional

Meticulous is the word for work that is almost obsessive — painstaking, detail-obsessed, and driven by a fear of making even tiny mistakes. Reach for it when the precision is extreme and slow. For everyday carefulness, 'careful' is simpler and more natural.
