Imprudent

/im-PROO-dent/ — emphasis on PROO, rhymes with prudent · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

An action that is imprudent is unwise or careless, taken without properly thinking through the consequences.

Show it in context

Spending the whole allowance on candy was an imprudent choice.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when imprudent is the exact word.

Worked Example

His imprudent decision to drop out of school left him struggling to find work.

Imprudent is more precise than 'foolish' here because it captures not just lack of wisdom but specifically the failure to think through the consequences of his choice — he didn't foresee how much he would struggle.

Your Turn

A worker accidentally left an email unsent in the drafts folder.

Is 'imprudent' the right word, or would 'careless' be better?

imprudent

Not quite — Leaving an email unsent in the drafts folder is an accidental slip, but imprudent describes a deliberate choice made with poor judgment, so it does not fit a simple oversight.

careless

Correct — Forgetting to send the email is a momentary lapse in attention, which is exactly what careless means, without implying a bad decision was deliberately made.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
hasty

rushed and not fully thought through — hasty stresses the speed of the decision, imprudent stresses the poor judgment behind it

reckless

doesn't care about danger or consequences — reckless suggests defiance, imprudent suggests naive lack of foresight

careless

not paying enough attention — careless is narrower (a single lapse), imprudent is about judgment in major decisions

Antonyms · opposites

prudent — Wisely cautious and thinking ahead — the direct opposite of imprudent

Build the Word

im- + prudent Latin prudentia: 'foresight, wisdom'

The prefix 'im-' means 'not,' so imprudent literally means 'not prudent' — lacking the foresight to think ahead.

The law student spent years studying jurisprudence to understand the principles behind every ruling.

Same root, new word — what does jurisprudence mean here?

the practice of arguing cases loudly in front of a jury

Not quite — This confuses courtroom drama with the word's real meaning; jurisprudence is the reflective study of legal principles, not persuasive speaking, as the sentence about understanding principles shows.

the theory, study, or philosophy of law

Correct — Correct. Jurisprudence joins juris (law) with prudentia (wisdom, foresight), so it literally means wisdom applied to law, the same prudentia that sits inside imprudent.

The root connection →

Root connection: imprudent and jurisprudence both come from Latin prudentia, meaning foresight or wisdom. Imprudent means lacking that wisdom (im- = not), while jurisprudence is that wisdom applied to law.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Imprudent describes a failure of judgment that should have been foreseen. Use it when someone makes a major choice that was unwise in an obvious way. For a single careless moment, 'careless' is clearer; for an action done with reckless defiance, 'reckless' fits better.
