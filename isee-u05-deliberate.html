Deliberate

/dih-LIB-uh-rut/ — accent on LIB · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Deliberate describes something done consciously and on purpose, often carried out slowly and carefully.

Show it in context

Her deliberate insult was planned days in advance — nothing about it was accidental.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when deliberate is the exact word.

Worked Example

The city council debates a zoning change for three months, reviews every legal document, and discusses each potential impact before voting. The council's deliberate decision protects both residents and future development.

'Deliberate' is perfect here because it captures both the intention and the careful, slow process. You could say 'intentional decision,' but that misses the methodical planning. You could say 'careful decision,' but that misses the purposefulness. 'Deliberate' combines both.

Your Turn

A student accidentally hits 'send' on a sarcastic text meant as a draft.

Is 'deliberate' the right word to describe the student's action?

No

Correct — The send was an accident, while deliberate requires intent and careful weighing of the choice, so deliberate does not fit.

Yes

Not quite — It is tempting because the text was sarcastic and sounds intentional, but 'deliberate' means done on purpose after careful thought, and hitting send by accident is neither.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
Intentional

Done on purpose, but doesn't capture the careful slowness that deliberate emphasizes.

Measured

Slow and careful, but less focused on the purposefulness that deliberate stresses.

Conscious

Done with full awareness and intent, but doesn't emphasize the measured, careful execution.

Antonyms · opposites

Impulsive — Done without thinking — the opposite of deliberate's careful intent.

Build the Word

librare Latin: "to weigh, balance"

Deliberate comes from 'to weigh' — the root image is carefully weighing evidence on a balance scale. When you deliberate, you're balancing different options.

Maintaining equilibrium on the tightrope required constant balance.

Same root, new word — what does equilibrium mean here?

a state of balance and stability

Correct — Deliberate and equilibrium share the Latin libra and librare ('to weigh, to balance'). Equilibrium is literally a state of balance, the same weighing image behind deliberating, where you balance your options carefully before deciding.

a sudden loss of control

Not quite — Falling off would be losing control, the opposite of what the sentence needs; the root libra means 'balance,' so equilibrium is a steady, balanced state.

a great height above the ground

Not quite — The tightrope is high up, but equilibrium refers to the balance the walker keeps, not the height, matching the libra 'balance' root.

The root connection →

The root libra ('balance, scale') links them: equilibrium is a balanced, stable state, and to deliberate is to weigh choices on a mental scale before acting.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Deliberate is the word for something done with full intention and careful thought. If you just mean 'on purpose,' use intentional; if you mean 'slow and careful,' use measured. Use deliberate when both the purpose and the care matter.
