Depiction

/dih-PIK-shun/ — stress on PIK · noun

REVIEW (Communication, Writing & Storytelling)

What It Means

A depiction is a picture, description, or other representation of someone or something.

Show it in context

The mural's depiction of the old harbor includes ships that sank a century ago.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when depiction is the exact word.

Worked Example

The film's depiction of the 1950s era was so accurate that viewers felt transported by the costumes, architecture, music, and social customs shown on screen.

de- + pict (Latin pingere, 'to paint,' also in picture and pigment) + -ion (act of) = the act of 'painting' or representing the 1950s era through vivid visual and narrative details. Depiction is the portrayal itself—how the filmmaker chose to show and characterize that historical period for the audience. The root 'paint' reminds us that depiction creates a picture, whether literal or in the reader's mind.

Your Turn

The history textbook's depiction of ancient Rome included maps, architectural drawings, and written explanations of daily life and government structure.

Is 'depiction' equally precise for all three elements—maps, drawings, and written explanations—or would another word fit the text better?

Precise for the visual parts only; the written parts are better called descriptions

Correct — Depiction traces back to Latin pingere ('to paint'), so it fits the maps and architectural drawings that actually picture Rome. The written explanations of daily life and government convey information in words rather than images, so 'description' captures those more accurately than 'depiction'.

Equally precise for all three

Not quite — This treats 'depiction' as a catch-all for any presentation of Rome, but the word's painting root ties it to visual portrayal. Stretching it to cover the textbook's written explanations blurs the line between showing a picture and describing something in words.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
portrayal

The act of showing or presenting someone or something. Portrayal is close to depiction but slightly more focused on how people or characters are shown.

representation

A showing or image of something. Representation is broader and more neutral; depiction carries more sense of vivid or intentional portrayal.

illustration

A picture or example that clarifies something. Illustration often means a visual image; depiction can be visual or narrative—any vivid portrayal.

Antonyms · opposites

omission — The act of leaving something out. Depiction is the act of showing or portraying; omission is the act of not showing or excluding something.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

depict + ion

Latin pingere = 'to paint' — the same root hiding in picture and pigment. The verb depict (to show in images or words) ends in -ct, so it takes -ion directly: depiction, the act or result of 'painting' something out for an audience.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Reach for depiction when describing a vivid representation, portrayal, or showing of something in images, description, or narrative. It carries the sense of 'painting' a picture for an audience. Use it when the way something is represented or shown matters—in film, art, literature, or any medium where imagery or vividness is key.
