Rouse

/rouz/ — rhymes with 'house' and 'mouse' · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To rouse someone is to wake them up or stir them into action or alertness.

Show it in context

The smell of pancakes finally roused the campers from their sleeping bags.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when rouse is the exact word.

Worked Example

The coach's passionate halftime speech roused the team from their discouraged silence, and they returned to the court with renewed determination.

Rouse is more precise than just 'wake' because it captures not only waking but stirring someone into alertness and action. The word carries the sense of energy and purpose—something actively moving a person from inactivity into engagement. That's what rouse names exactly.

Your Turn

Dan's coffee maker began brewing while he was still asleep.

Did the coffee maker rouse Dan?

No

Correct — Rouse requires something actively stirring a person awake or into action. The coffee maker merely brews and is not reaching Dan, so it cannot rouse him by itself.

Yes

Not quite — 'Yes' is tempting because the coffee maker is what starts up while Dan sleeps, but rouse needs an active agent that actually stirs someone into alertness. The machine brewing on its own does not reach him; only the smell drifting over could rouse him.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
wake

Simply to stop sleeping. Rouse includes waking but adds the sense of stirring someone into active alertness or deliberate action.

stir

To provoke into action or emotion; shares rouse's active, energetic quality, but doesn't necessarily involve waking from sleep.

awaken

To wake up; more formal and literary than 'wake,' but lacks rouse's implication of vigorous stirring or rousing to purposeful action.

Antonyms · opposites

lull — To soothe and calm into passivity; the opposite of stirring someone into action and alertness.

Build the Word

reysa Old Norse/Germanic: 'to raise, lift up'

The word originally meant to lift someone up, which extended to lifting them out of sleep or inactivity into alertness and action.

Parents raise their children with love and care.

Same root, new word — what does raise mean here?

to lower slowly toward the ground

Not quite — This is an opposite trap, which is why it tempts, but the root reysa means 'to raise, lift up.' Raise is about lifting or bringing up, not lowering.

to lift up, bring up

Correct — Rouse and raise are usually traced back to the Norse root reysa (reisa), 'to raise, lift up.' Raise keeps the plain sense of lifting or bringing up, while rouse extends it to lifting someone out of sleep into alertness.

to praise or speak well of

Not quite — This sounds a bit like 'raise,' and parents do praise children, so it feels close, but reysa points to lifting or bringing up, and here it means rearing children.

The root connection →

The root reysa ('to raise, lift up') is the bridge: raise means to lift or bring up directly, and rouse applies that same lifting to a person, drawing them up out of sleep or stillness. Note that the link is historically plausible but rouse's own origin is treated as uncertain.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Use rouse when someone or something actively wakes or stirs another person into alertness or action. A smell rouses you from sleep. A speech rouses a crowd to action. A sound rouses you from deep thought. 'Wake' is simpler and works for plain falling asleep; 'rouse' is more precise when energy, purpose, or stirring is involved.
