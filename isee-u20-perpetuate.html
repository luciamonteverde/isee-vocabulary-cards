Perpetuate

/per-PEH-choo-ate/ — stress on second syllable · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To perpetuate something is to cause it to continue or persist, often for a long time.

Show it in context

Statues and street names perpetuate the memory of the city's founders.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when perpetuate is the exact word.

Worked Example

By teaching these old recipes to her grandchildren, Carmen perpetuated her family's culinary traditions for another generation.

Perpetuate is more precise than 'continued' because it emphasizes the deliberate *cause* to continue. Carmen is actively passing on traditions, making them persist into the future—that's perpetuating, not just letting something happen on its own.

Your Turn

The website's automatic backup system continued to update every night.

Is perpetuate the right word here?

perpetuate

Not quite — The backup system runs on its own each night with no purpose behind it, and this scene lacks the deliberate effort to keep something alive that perpetuate would require.

continued

Correct — An automatic process simply keeps going night after night, so a plain word for ongoing action fits better than one implying someone purposely sustains a tradition or memory.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
continue

Keep going without stopping. Perpetuate adds the sense of deliberately making something go on, often across generations or time.

preserve

Keep something safe and intact. Preserve emphasizes maintaining the original condition; perpetuate emphasizes active transmission or making it last.

sustain

Support and keep going. Sustain focuses on ongoing support; perpetuate emphasizes making something last across time or through deliberate transmission.

Antonyms · opposites

discontinue — Stop or end something—the opposite of making it continue or persist.

How Strong Is It?
discontinue→pause→perpetuate→immortalize

Perpetuate sits between pausing (temporarily stopping) and immortalizing (making eternal): deliberately making something continue, often for a long time. (our word)

Build the Word

perpetuus Latin: constant, continuous, endless

Perpetuate comes from 'perpetual'—both share the root meaning of something that goes on and on. When you perpetuate something, you're making it perpetual.

The monument serves as a perpetual reminder of the victims of the war, standing unchanged for decades.

Same root, new word — what does perpetual mean here?

happening only once and then finished

Not quite — That describes a one-time event, the opposite of perpetual. The Latin perpetuus means constant and endless, so perpetual keeps something going on and on rather than ending after a single moment.

continuing forever or without interruption

Correct — Correct. Perpetual shares the Latin root perpetuus (constant, endless) with perpetuate: perpetuate makes something continue, and perpetual means continuing without end.

The root connection →

Perpetuate and perpetual both come straight from the Latin perpetuus, meaning constant or endless. Perpetuate keeps something going, and perpetual describes what goes on forever.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Perpetuate is the word for deliberately making something continue, especially across time or generations. Use it when someone or something actively keeps a tradition, memory, practice, or belief alive. For simple continuation, 'continue' is more neutral and direct.
