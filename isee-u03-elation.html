Here's how to decide when elation is the exact word.

Worked Example

She felt unmistakable elation when the acceptance letter arrived from her dream college, dancing around the room and shouting with joy.

Elation is the precise word here because it captures not just happiness but the explosive, exuberant joy that has to burst out physically. Happiness would miss the sense of overwhelming excitement that can't be contained.

Your Turn

Marcus got a B on his math quiz, which was better than he expected.

Is elation the right word here, or should it say 'satisfaction'?

satisfaction

Correct — A better-than-expected B is a modest, pleasant result, and 'satisfaction' fits that quiet, contented reaction.

elation

Not quite — Elation is tempting because the grade beat his expectations, but elation is an overwhelming, soaring high, and a decent quiz score is a mild win, not a mountaintop moment.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
joy

A deep, genuine happiness. Elation is joy that also includes excitement and physical exuberance.

delight

Pleasure at something good. Elation is more intense and much harder to contain.

euphoria

An intensely elevated mood. Elation is usually tied to a real reason (like an acceptance); euphoria can float free.

Antonyms · opposites

despair — The complete loss of hope or happiness — the lowest pit to elation's peak.

Build the Word

elatus Latin: lifted up, elevated

Elation comes from a root meaning 'to lift up'—your joy is so strong it lifts you physically off the ground.

The scholar worked to translate the ancient poem so English readers could enjoy it.

Same root, new word — what does translate mean here?

to carry a text across from one language into another

Correct — Correct. Elation is ex- plus latus, joy that 'lifts you up,' and translate is trans- plus that same latus, 'carrying' words across from one language into another.

to write a brand-new poem from scratch

Not quite — This is tempting because both involve producing text, but the latus ('carried') root means moving an existing work across languages, not inventing a fresh one.

to read something silently to oneself

Not quite — This is tempting because it involves a text, but the latus root points to carrying words across into another language, not simply reading in place.

The root connection →

Elation and translate both contain the Latin latus, 'carried' or 'lifted': in elation your spirits are carried upward, and in translation words are carried across a language boundary. The shared latus root is the bridge, even though one lifts and the other transports.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / literary

Reach for elation when someone experiences a peak moment of exuberant triumph—an acceptance letter, a championship win, a life-changing yes. It captures the sense that joy has become so intense it bursts out physically. For milder happiness, the simpler word 'happiness' or 'pleasure' usually fits better.
