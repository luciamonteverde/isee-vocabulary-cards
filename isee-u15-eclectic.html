Eclectic

/ih-KLEK-tik/ — rhymes with hectic · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something eclectic is made up of a wide variety of different styles, sources, or influences mixed together.

Show it in context

Her eclectic playlist jumps from opera to hip-hop to folk.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when eclectic is the exact word.

Worked Example

A museum curator designed the exhibition to blend Renaissance paintings with contemporary sculptures, vintage photographs, and modern installations—a deliberate eclectic space.

The curator is intentionally selecting and mixing pieces from many different time periods and traditions. 'Diverse' would work, but eclectic more precisely captures the sense that the curator is celebrating and deliberately combining these distinct styles from different sources.

Your Turn

A closet contained a blue dress, a white sweater, three pairs of black pants, and gray socks—items collected over many years.

Is eclectic a good way to describe this closet?

mixed

Correct — These are ordinary clothes accumulated over time, not deliberately chosen from different styles and traditions, so a plain word like 'mixed' fits.

eclectic

Not quite — 'Eclectic' implies an intentional selection of diverse pieces from many styles and traditions. A closet of everyday clothes gathered by chance does not qualify.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
diverse

Emphasizes breadth and variety, but lacks eclectic's flavor of intentional curation from distinct traditions and sources.

varied

Simply means 'many different kinds,' but doesn't capture eclectic's idea of purposefully mixing styles from multiple distinct sources.

miscellaneous

Implies a random assortment, whereas eclectic suggests the mixing is deliberate, thoughtful, and celebrates the diversity.

Antonyms · opposites

uniform — All of one style or type—the opposite of eclectic's intentional blending of different sources.

How Strong Is It?

Build the Word

eklektikos Greek: 'selecting, choosing'

Eclectic comes from the Greek word for 'selecting' or 'choosing.' An eclectic person is one who selects and appreciates diverse things from many traditions, picking the best from each.

The committee was selective about which films to screen, choosing only the most innovative options.

Same root, new word — what does selective mean here?

carefully choosing only certain things; showing careful and deliberate selection

Correct — Eclectic comes from Greek legein and selective from Latin legere, two 'to choose, gather' roots that most etymologists trace back to the same ancient Proto-Indo-European source, leg-. Both words are about deliberately picking things out, so selective means choosing carefully.

accepting everything without judging

Not quite — Tempting because a film committee sees a great many entries, but selective means the opposite: they accept only some, after careful judgment.

choosing at random with no reason

Not quite — Tempting because the word is about choosing, but the sentence stresses picking 'only the most innovative options,' which is deliberate, not random.

The root connection →

Eclectic and selective are best thought of as distant cousins rather than close relatives: their immediate roots (Greek legein, Latin legere) are separate, but scholars link both back to the far older root leg-, 'to gather or pick out.' The shared idea of deliberate choosing survives in each word.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Eclectic is the word when you're describing an intentional, thoughtful mix of different styles or sources. Use it for curated collections that celebrate diversity. For a simple mixture without that sense of deliberate curation, 'varied' or 'diverse' often work fine.
