Ponder

/PON-der/ — rhymes with wonder · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To ponder something is to think about it slowly and carefully before reaching a conclusion.

Show it in context

He pondered the chess board for five full minutes before moving.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when ponder is the exact word.

Worked Example

The architect pondered the blueprints for an hour, sketching alternatives before deciding on the final design.

Ponder emphasizes slow, careful thought before reaching a conclusion, which is more precise than 'think' — it suggests the person is taking time to weigh options thoughtfully, not just having a quick thought.

Your Turn

Emma quickly glanced at the test question and wrote down an answer.

Is 'ponder' the right word here?

Yes

Not quite — It might look like any thinking counts, but ponder means weighing an idea slowly and carefully, and Emma barely paused before writing her answer.

No

Correct — Emma glanced and responded quickly, without much deliberation. Ponder means to think something over slowly and carefully, which is the opposite of a fast glance and answer.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
think

Any mental activity. Ponder implies slow, deliberate thought, whereas think can be quick or automatic.

contemplate

To think deeply about something. Both involve careful thought, but contemplate can be more meditative, while ponder is about reaching a decision.

reflect

To think back on something that already happened. Ponder looks forward or is in the present moment, while reflect looks backward to the past.

Antonyms · opposites

ignore — To pay no attention to something. Opposite of ponder — you're refusing to think about it rather than thinking carefully about it.

How Strong Is It?
glance→consider→ponder→obsess

Ponder — more thought than a quick glance, less fixated than obsessing. (our word)

Build the Word

ponderare Latin: to weigh

The same root as 'weight' — ponderare originally meant to weigh things on a scale, as if you're weighing options in your mind.

The recipe called for a pound of flour, so she measured it carefully on the kitchen scale.

Same root, new word — what does pound mean here?

to hit or strike something over and over

Not quite — 'Pound' really can mean to hit something repeatedly, like pounding on a door, but the sentence is about measuring flour on a scale, so here it names an amount of weight.

a unit used to measure weight, equal to about sixteen ounces

Correct — The pound of weight comes from Latin pondo, from pondus, 'weight,' the same pond- root behind ponderare, 'to weigh,' which gives us ponder.

a fenced area where stray animals are kept

Not quite — A dog pound is a real place, yet nothing in the sentence is about animals or an enclosure. The flour is being measured, so 'pound' is a unit of weight.

The root connection →

To ponder is to 'weigh' an idea in your mind, while a pound weighs an object on a scale. Careful: the everyday word 'weight' means the same thing but is native Germanic (Old English gewiht) and does not share this Latin root, so it only translates the idea rather than descending from it.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / reflective

Ponder suggests slow, careful deliberation — use it when someone is taking time to think through a difficult decision. For quick thinking, 'consider' or 'think' is simpler and more direct.
