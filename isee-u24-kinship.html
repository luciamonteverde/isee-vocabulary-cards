Kinship

/KIN-ship/ — rhymes with friendship · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Kinship is a feeling of closeness and connection with someone, as though they were family, or the actual bond between relatives.

Show it in context

Growing up on farms, the two students felt an instant kinship.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when kinship is the exact word.

Worked Example

Though the two writers had never met before, they discovered an instant kinship over their shared passion for mystery novels.

Kinship is more precise than 'connection' or 'friendship' here because it captures both the depth (like family) and the immediacy of feeling bonded despite being strangers.

Your Turn

Maria felt kinship with her new classmate because they both liked the same color.

Is kinship the precise word here, or should it say 'similarity'?

kinship

Not quite — Kinship is a deep, family-like bond. Sharing a favorite color is one small thing in common, not the strong emotional connection kinship implies, so it overstates what Maria and her classmate have.

similarity

Correct — Liking the same color simply means the two classmates have something in common, which is precisely a similarity rather than a close, family-like tie.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
affinity

A natural liking or attraction. Kinship is deeper and more like family; affinity is more about natural compatibility.

rapport

A harmonious connection between people. Kinship emphasizes family-like closeness; rapport is more about getting along smoothly.

camaraderie

Mutual friendship and loyalty. Kinship suggests blood or family bonds; camaraderie is based on shared circumstances.

Antonyms · opposites

alienation — Feeling of estrangement and disconnection — the opposite of family-like bonding.

Build the Word

kin Old English: 'family member'

Kinship comes from 'kin' — it literally names the bond you feel with family, or a feeling so close it seems familial.

She felt a kindred spirit in her new friend, someone who understood her heart.

Same root, new word — what does kindred mean here?

unfamiliar and very different from oneself

Not quite — That is the opposite of the kin root ('family, same nature'). A kindred spirit is someone who feels like family or shares your nature, not a stranger unlike you.

having the same family or nature

Correct — Correct. 'Kindred' shares the kin root ('family') with kinship; a kindred spirit is someone of the same nature, almost like family.

The root connection →

Kinship comes from kin ('family member') plus -ship. Kindred keeps that same kin root, describing people who share a family tie or a like nature.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / literary

Kinship describes a deep emotional bond like family, not just friendship or agreement. Use it when two people feel genuinely connected at a profound level, often instantly despite being new to each other. For casual friendship, 'connection' is simpler; for shared circumstances, 'camaraderie' is more precise.
