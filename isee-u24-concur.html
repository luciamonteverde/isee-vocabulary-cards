Concur

/kən-KUR/ — rhymes with her · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To concur with someone is to agree with their opinion, decision, or judgment.

Show it in context

All three doctors concurred that surgery was unnecessary.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when concur is the exact word.

Worked Example

After studying the research data, even the skeptical board member had to concur with the majority's conclusion about the trend.

Concur is more precise than 'agree' here because it emphasizes that the skeptical member reached a shared judgment after deliberation, not just casual assent.

Your Turn

My friend asked if I wanted pizza for lunch, and I concurred.

Is concur the precise word here, or should it say 'agreed'?

concur

Not quite — Concur suggests reaching the same conclusion after weighing reasons, the way judges concur on a ruling. Casually saying yes to a pizza suggestion involves no such deliberation, so concur is too heavy for this moment.

agreed

Correct — You simply accepted a casual lunch idea, and 'agreed' captures that everyday, low-stakes acceptance without implying careful judgment.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
agree

Any alignment of opinion or view. Concur is weightier, emphasizing reasoned judgment after thought.

assent

Formal acceptance or approval. Concur emphasizes shared judgment; assent is more about approval.

align

To come into agreement. Concur is more definitive; align suggests a process of moving toward agreement.

Antonyms · opposites

dissent — To disagree or object — the opposite of sharing a judgment.

Build the Word

com- + currere Latin: 'to run together'

Two streams running together — concur is when minds run together toward the same conclusion.

The strong current in the river pulled the swimmers steadily downstream.

Same root, new word — what does current mean here?

a still pool where the water has stopped moving

Not quite — The curr- root means 'to run,' so a current is water that runs or flows. Still, stopped water is the opposite of running, so this meaning contradicts the root.

a body of water or air that runs, or flows, steadily in one direction

Correct — Correct. 'Current' shares the currere root ('to run') with concur; a river current is water running steadily in one direction.

The root connection →

Concur comes from com- ('together') plus currere ('to run'), literally 'to run together' toward the same conclusion. Current keeps that currere root for water that runs, or flows, along.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Concur is more formal and precise than 'agree.' Use it when describing an important judgment or decision reached after deliberation, especially in professional or academic settings. For casual agreement, 'agree' is simpler and more natural.
