Undermine

/un-der-MYN/ — stress on the last syllable · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To undermine something is to gradually weaken it or damage its support, often little by little.

Show it in context

Rumors undermined the manager's authority long before the vote.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when undermine is the exact word.

Worked Example

For weeks, the rival candidate released stories suggesting the mayor had mismanaged funds, and with each story her credibility with voters slipped away.

The weakening here is deliberate and gradual — each rumor chipped away at her standing, not all at once but little by little from beneath. Undermine is more precise than 'damage' because it captures that slow, hidden weakening of support from underneath, like digging away at a foundation.

Your Turn

When the earthquake struck, the ground shook so violently that the bridge collapsed immediately.

Is undermine the best word here, or would 'destroyed' be better?

destroyed

Correct — Correct. A violent, instant collapse is best named by destroyed. Undermine would wrongly imply a slow, hidden weakening rather than sudden ruin.

undermine

Not quite — Tempting, because the bridge did give way. But undermine means to weaken something gradually from below, while the earthquake caused sudden, total destruction in an instant.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
weaken

To make less strong. Undermine has a sneaky quality — weakening from beneath, often without being noticed. Weaken is broader and more neutral about how the strength is lost.

sabotage

To deliberately destroy or damage something. Undermine can be intentional or unintentional, and it works by slow erosion of support rather than direct, deliberate attack.

subvert

To corrupt or work against something from within, usually with political or moral implications. Undermine is less political and doesn't require intentional corruption; it simply means weakening or damaging support.

Antonyms · opposites

bolster — To strengthen and solidify support, the opposite of gradually weakening from beneath.

How Strong Is It?
doubt→weaken→undermine→demolish

Undermine — between simple weakening and complete destruction; specifically the gradual weakening of authority or support from beneath. (our word)

Build the Word

under + mine (to dig) Old English: 'to dig beneath'

Originally it meant literally digging tunnels beneath a fortress to make it collapse. Later it came to mean weakening anything by attacking its foundation, whether literal or figurative.

Workers will mine the hillside for precious metals and gems.

Same root, new word — what does mine mean here?

to guard the hillside from trespassers

Not quite — Tempting if you imagine workers stationed on the hill. But mine means to dig into it for its resources, not to protect it.

to dig into the earth to extract minerals or resources

Correct — Undermine and mine share the idea of digging beneath (under + mine, 'to dig below'). To mine is to dig into the earth for resources, and to undermine is to dig away support from underneath something.

to blast the hillside apart with explosives

Not quite — Tempting, since blasting can be part of mining and 'mine' can also mean a buried explosive. But here mine means to dig systematically for metals and gems, not to blow the hill up.

The root connection →

Both rest on mine, 'to dig beneath': miners dig into a hillside for its treasures, while to undermine is to dig out the ground beneath something until it can no longer stand.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Undermine is the word when something is being weakened from the inside or from its foundation, often secretly or gradually. Use it for authority, credibility, trust, or arguments being slowly eroded. For straightforward damage or direct attack, simpler words like 'weaken' or 'damage' are better choices.
