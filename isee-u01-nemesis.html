Nemesis

/NEM-uh-sis/ — stress on the first syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A nemesis is a longtime rival or enemy who seems especially difficult to defeat or overcome.

Show it in context

For years, that debate team was our team's nemesis.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when nemesis is the exact word.

Worked Example

Every season, when the elite volleyball team from across town arrived at the regional tournament, our school's team felt the weight of facing them — year after year, that team seemed to find a way to beat us, no matter our preparation.

This team is more than just an enemy or a rival; nemesis captures both the history of battles between the teams and the sense that they're especially hard to defeat. Just saying 'rival' would miss that special quality of being your 'signature struggle.'

Your Turn

A student was paired with an unfamiliar classmate for a group project and immediately didn't like them.

Is nemesis the right word for this new classmate?

Clash

Correct — A first-day dislike is a simple clash of personalities, not a long-standing rivalry earned through repeated conflict.

Nemesis

Not quite — A nemesis requires time and repeated encounters, so it doesn't fit a classmate you just met with no history of struggle yet.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
rival

A competitor. Nemesis is a rival you've fought many times and keep losing to.

opponent

Someone facing you in competition or battle. Nemesis is an opponent with a long track record of besting you.

scourge

A source of great trouble or ruin. Nemesis is the enemy who seems unstoppable and causes you repeated defeats.

Antonyms · opposites

ally — A friend and supporter. The opposite of nemesis — someone who has your back instead of standing against you.

Build the Word

nemesis (from Greek goddess) Greek: 'divine retribution, goddess of justice'

The goddess Nemesis punished human arrogance — she represented fate catching up with the proud. Your nemesis is like her: the opponent who seems destined to oppose you.

After years of protest, the island region was finally granted autonomy, allowing its people to govern themselves and pass their own laws.

Same root, new word — what does autonomy mean here?

a formal ceremony that marks a change in leadership

Not quite — A ceremony fits a political-transition sentence, but it names a ritual, not self-rule. Autonomy's '-nomy' comes from the Greek nomos ('law'), a root that linguists trace back, alongside nemesis, to nemein ('to deal out what is due') - a law-and-order idea, not a ceremony.

the right or condition of self-government; independence

Correct — Autonomy's '-nomy' comes from the Greek nomos, 'law,' and joined to autos ('self') it means governing yourself, which fits the region winning the right to pass its own laws. Linguists trace both nemesis and nomos back to the older Greek verb nemein ('to deal out what is due'), so the two are distant cousins rather than an obvious shared piece.

a strong feeling of loyalty toward one's own country

Not quite — Patriotic feeling matches the independence tone, but it names an emotion, not self-government. The '-nomy' in autonomy comes from Greek nomos ('law'), a distant relative of the root behind nemesis, and a root about law points to governing yourself, not to loyalty.

The root connection →

Autonomy joins autos ('self') with nomos ('law') to mean ruling yourself. Nemesis began as the name of the Greek goddess of due punishment, and linguists trace both her name and nomos back to the older verb nemein, 'to deal out what is due,' so the two words share a distant root ancestor rather than an obvious shared piece.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Nemesis is a grand, mythic word — your nemesis isn't just any rival, but the one you've struggled against repeatedly and who seems almost destined to defeat you. For everyday competition, 'rival' or 'opponent' is simpler. But when you want to capture that sense of an almost-fated struggle, nemesis is the word that lands.
