Motley

/MOT-lee/ — emphasis on first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is motley is made up of many different and mismatched kinds of people or things gathered together.

Show it in context

A motley collection of mismatched chairs filled the community hall.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when motley is the exact word.

Worked Example

The community theater's cast was motley: a retired banker played the villain, two high school students were the lovers, a nurse delivered comic relief, and a schoolteacher gave the soliloquies.

The cast members come from completely different professions and backgrounds—they don't match or coordinate in any obvious way. 'Diverse' would work, but motley more precisely captures the sense of mismatched people gathered together, emphasizing their incongruous origins.

Your Turn

The company hired a design team carefully: an industrial designer known for innovation, a web developer with twelve years' experience, and a marketer specializing in product launches.

Would motley be a good word to describe the design team?

diverse

Correct — The members have different backgrounds but were carefully chosen for their complementary expertise, so 'diverse' fits.

motley

Not quite — 'Motley' suggests a random, mismatched jumble thrown together. This team was deliberately and skillfully assembled, not haphazard.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
assorted

Means 'various types selected together,' but lacks motley's implication that the collection is visibly mismatched and odd-looking.

miscellaneous

Indicates a random collection of different things, but doesn't capture motley's emphasis on visual or tonal mismatch among what's gathered.

hodgepodge

Also means a confused jumble of different things, but hodgepodge emphasizes confusion; motley emphasizes the visible mismatch and diversity itself.

Antonyms · opposites

uniform — All of one type or style—the opposite of motley's diverse and visibly mismatched collection.

How Strong Is It?
uniform→matching→varied→motley

Motley — diverse elements that don't match or coordinate, notably mismatched when gathered together. (our word)

Build the Word

mot- Origin uncertain, possibly Old French or Germanic: 'spotted, patchwork'

Motley likely comes from an old word meaning 'spotted' or 'patterned,' as in a patchwork garment with patches of many different colors. Something motley looks like a patchwork—many different colored or styled pieces brought together.

The old stone wall was mottled with moss and lichen, creating a patterned surface of green and gray.

Same root, new word — what does mottled mean here?

polished to a smooth, even shine

Not quite — Tempting because walls can be finished and smoothed, but mottled means blotchy and uneven, the opposite of a clean, glossy surface.

painted a single solid color

Not quite — Tempting because many walls are one color, but the sentence describes green and gray patches, so the surface is spotted, not solid.

marked with spots or blotches of different colors; having an irregular, patterned appearance

Correct — Mottled is generally taken as a back-formation from motley, so the two share the same 'patchy, many-colored' idea. A mottled wall shows blotches of different colors, just as motley describes a patchwork of mixed pieces.

The root connection →

Motley and mottled both picture a surface broken into patches of different colors. Most dictionaries treat mottled as having grown out of motley, which is why the mossy, two-toned wall reads as mottled.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / informal

Motley is the word when you want to emphasize that a collection is made up of different and visibly mismatched elements. Use it for groups or objects that don't coordinate well but are gathered or assembled together. If things are intentionally mixed from different sources (like an artistic collection), eclectic is more precise.
