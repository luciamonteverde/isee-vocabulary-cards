Mosaic

moh-ZAY-ik — stress on second syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A mosaic is a picture or design created by fitting together many small pieces of colored stone, glass, or tile.

Show it in context

The courtyard floor was a mosaic of tiny blue and gold tiles forming a peacock.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when mosaic is the exact word.

Worked Example

An artist collects fragments of broken pottery and colored glass from a demolished building. She carefully arranges them in a frame, fitting each piece snugly against the next until the fragments transform into a striking image of a flying bird.

Mosaic is precise here because it emphasizes not just that there is a picture or design, but specifically that it is made by fitting together many small, distinct pieces. Simply saying "picture" or "design" would miss the crucial element of assembly from fragments.

Your Turn

A newspaper article reported on the social networks of students, showing how different friend groups connect and overlap across the school.

Would calling this pattern a 'mosaic of friendships' be the best word choice?

Yes

Not quite — Mosaic names a physical visual artwork made from fitted pieces, so applying it to abstract social ties stretches the word past its real meaning.

No

Correct — These are abstract social connections, not an assembled artwork of physical tiles, so 'network' or 'pattern' fits far better than mosaic.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
picture

Any visual representation or image. Picture lacks the specific sense of small pieces fitted and assembled.

collage

Also made from assembled pieces, but typically pasted rather than fitted, and often uses varied modern materials.

composition

An intentional arrangement of visual elements. Composition is more general and does not emphasize the discrete-piece aspect.

Antonyms · opposites

monolith — A single solid, unified block with no separate parts. The exact opposite of mosaic's many fitted pieces.

Mosaic — carefully fitted pieces forming a unified visual design, from many small fragments. (our word)

Build the Word

musivum Medieval Latin: 'work made for the Muses' or 'mosaic-work'

The Romans called intricate assembled artwork 'musivum' after the Muses, goddesses of artistic inspiration — a fitting name for work requiring artistic vision.

The museum houses masterpieces of art belonging among humanity's greatest creations.

Same root, new word — what does museum mean here?

a private artist's workshop

Not quite — Art is clearly involved, which makes a workshop tempting, but a museum is a public place for housing and displaying art, tied through its name to the Muses as patrons of the arts.

a place where artworks are housed and preserved

Correct — Mosaic and museum both look back to the Muses, the goddesses of the arts: mosaic from Medieval Latin musivum, 'work of the Muses,' and museum from mouseion, 'seat of the Muses.'

a large outdoor sculpture

Not quite — A sculpture is an art object, so this feels close, but museum names the building that holds art, echoing the Muses' role over the arts rather than any single piece.

The root connection →

The bridge is the Muses themselves, the ancient patrons of art and inspiration. A mosaic is artful Muse-work assembled from pieces, and a museum is a house of the Muses where such art is kept, both honoring those goddesses in their very names.
