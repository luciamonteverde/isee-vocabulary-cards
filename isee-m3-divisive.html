Divisive

/dih-VY-siv/ — stress on VY · adjective

PREVIEW — taught in depth later in Conflict, Disaster & Difficulty

Hear It
What It Means

Divisive describes something that causes strong disagreement and splits people into opposing sides.

Show it in context

Pineapple on pizza is the most divisive question in our entire cafeteria.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when divisive is the exact word.

Worked Example

The new school dress code became divisive when half the students supported it and half protested loudly.

Divide (split into parts) + -ive (inclined to): the dress code has the quality of splitting the student body. More precise than 'unpopular' because divisive captures the sense of creating opposing sides rather than just being disliked.

Your Turn

The decision to extend lunch by five minutes was divisive.

Is divisive the right word for this decision?

Yes

Not quite — 'Divisive' would fit only if the decision split people into opposing camps, but a small, welcome change like this doesn't.

No

Correct — Most people would happily agree to more lunch time, so the change unites rather than divides; 'popular' or 'welcomed' fits better than divisive.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
contentious

Causing heated disagreement or argument. Contentious emphasizes the passionate, angry quality of the conflict, while divisive emphasizes the splitting into opposing sides.

controversial

Arousing public disagreement or debate. Controversial indicates disagreement exists, while divisive implies a deeper split into clearly opposing camps.

polarizing

Causing people to move to extreme opposite positions. Polarizing is stronger than divisive — it implies people take up positions at the extremes.

Antonyms · opposites

uniting — Bringing people together toward shared goals. The opposite quality — gathering people into agreement rather than splitting them into opposing sides.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

divis (divide) + -ive

divide → divis- + -ive. The -de ending swaps to -s-: inclined to divide people. Divisible ('able to be split into equal parts,' as in math) shares the base but not the meaning.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Reach for divisive when something creates a clear split between people into opposing camps. It's stronger than 'unpopular' or 'controversial'—divisive implies fundamental disagreement that breaks communities apart.
