Opaque

/oh-PAYK/ — rhymes with wake, stake · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is opaque cannot be seen through because light does not pass through it clearly.

Show it in context

The bathroom's opaque glass lets in light but no prying eyes.

Here's how to decide when opaque is the exact word.

Worked Example

The company's opaque hiring process left applicants with no visibility into how decisions were made.

Opaque emphasizes that something cannot be seen through—literally, light does not pass. It's more precise than 'secretive' because opaque describes a physical property of blocking light, not a deliberate choice to hide information.

Your Turn

A spy refused to answer questions about her assignment.

Is 'opaque' a good word to describe her response?

Yes

Not quite — Opaque literally means blocking light, so it fits frosted glass or a thick liquid, not a person who chooses to withhold answers. For hiding information you would say secretive or evasive.

No

Correct — Opaque describes light-blocking materials, not behavior. A spy refusing to answer is being secretive or evasive, not 'opaque.'

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
unclear

Difficult to understand or perceive. Opaque specifically refers to blocking light physically; unclear can describe any confusing information.

cloudy

Murky or reducing visibility. Cloudy is similar but more informal and usually describes weather or liquids, while opaque applies to any non-transparent material.

secretive

Intentionally hiding information. While opaque material blocks light, secretive behavior deliberately conceals—a different kind of blocking.

Antonyms · opposites

transparent — Allowing light and images to pass through clearly. Transparent is the complete opposite: where opaque blocks all light, transparent lets it through.

How Strong Is It?
translucent→opaque→impenetrable→sealed

Opaque — blocks all light and vision, stronger than translucent (partial light) but not as completely sealed off. (our word)

Build the Word

opacus Latin 'opacus' = 'shady, dark, obscure'

The Latin root opacus literally means 'dark' or 'shady'—and opaque describes anything that blocks light to create darkness or obscurity, the way a tree's shadow is cast because its leaves are opaque.

The scientist measured the opacity of the solution to determine how much light it blocked.

Same root, new word — what does opacity mean here?

the temperature of the solution

Not quite — Tempting because scientists measure many properties, but opacity comes from opaque (light-blocking), not heat.

the degree to which something blocks light

Correct — Opaque and opacity both come from the Latin opacus, 'shady, dark, obscure.' Opaque describes something that blocks light; opacity names the degree to which it does so.

how fast the solution flowed

Not quite — Tempting as another measurable property, but opacity is specifically about blocking light, not motion.

The root connection →

Opacity is simply opaque turned into a measurable quantity: the same opacus root, now naming how much light a material stops rather than just stating that it stops light.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Opaque is precise when describing materials or substances that block light and vision. Unlike 'unclear' (information that's confusing), opaque specifically refers to physical properties. Use it for glass, fabric, paint, and other tangible things, not for behavior or communication.
