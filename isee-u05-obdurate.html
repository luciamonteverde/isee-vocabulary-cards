Obdurate

/AHB-dur-it/ — emphasis on first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is obdurate stubbornly refuses to change their mind or behavior, even when others try to persuade them.

Show it in context

The obdurate landlord refused every offer, no matter how generous.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when obdurate is the exact word.

Worked Example

After his friends explained the dangers in detail, the obdurate driver ignored every warning and drove recklessly across town.

Obdurate is more precise than 'stubborn' because it emphasizes not just resistance to change, but the active refusal to be persuaded despite others' efforts. The stubbornness here is specifically about ignoring appeals — that's what obdurate names.

Your Turn

The teacher held to her carefully researched and tested grading rubric throughout the semester.

Is 'obdurate' the right word here, or something else?

steadfast

Correct — She is holding to a rubric she carefully researched and tested, so she is being firmly loyal to a well-reasoned position, which is exactly what 'steadfast' names.

obdurate

Not quite — It is tempting because she will not change the rubric, but 'obdurate' means stubbornly hardened against all persuasion, and she has sound reasons rather than blind stubbornness.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
stubborn

Describes general resistance to change, but doesn't emphasize the deliberate refusal to listen to others' persuasion that obdurate requires.

inflexible

Won't bend or adapt, but doesn't necessarily involve actively refusing to hear others' arguments the way obdurate does.

intransigent

Refuses to compromise, but usually describes an ideological or political stance, not merely stubborn personal refusal like obdurate.

Antonyms · opposites

amenable — Willing to listen and be persuaded — the opposite of obdurate's hardened refusal to change.

How Strong Is It?
resistant→stubborn→obdurate→immovable

Obdurate sits between stubborn (mere resistance) and immovable (absolutely fixed) — it's hardened refusal despite others' pressure. (our word)

Build the Word

obdurare Latin: 'ob-' (against) + 'durare' (to harden) = to harden against

Obdurate describes someone whose mind is hardened against persuasion — their thoughts and feelings have become as hard as stone, resistant to all appeals.

This hiking boot is made from durable material that can withstand years of heavy use.

Same root, new word — what does durable mean here?

lasting only a short time

Not quite — Tempting as a quick guess, but the root durare means 'to harden or last,' the opposite of short-lived, and a durable boot endures years of wear.

made to last and tough enough to resist wear

Correct — Obdurate and durable both come from the Latin durare ('to harden, to last'). The boot is hard and strong enough to last for years, the same hardness that, turned inward, makes an obdurate person's mind hardened against persuasion.

easy to bend or reshape

Not quite — A flexible material might sound useful, but durare points to hardness and endurance, so durable means tough enough to resist wear, not easy to bend.

The root connection →

The shared root is durare ('to harden'): a durable object is hardened to withstand wear, while an obdurate person's feelings have hardened against every appeal, adding ob- ('against') to that same hardness.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Obdurate is stronger and more formal than 'stubborn.' Use it when you want to emphasize that someone has hardened themselves against others' attempts to persuade them — there's an almost willful resistance. For simple stubbornness without that layer of defiance, 'stubborn' is the plainer word.
