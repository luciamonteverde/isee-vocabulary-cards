Here's how to decide when visible is the exact word.

Worked Example

The theater's new stage lighting made the actor's facial expressions clearly visible even from the back row.

Vis (see, from Latin) + -ible (can be done) = can be seen. The design ensured that subtle facial expressions were fully seen from distant seats — visible captures precisely what the lighting achieved: the ability to perceive the actor's face clearly throughout the theater.

Your Turn

The detective studied the grainy security footage, squinting at the dark figure on the screen.

Is visible the right word to describe the figure on the screen?

visible

Not quite — The detective is squinting at a dark shape on grainy footage and still cannot make it out. If the figure were truly visible, there would be nothing to squint at or study so hard. The poor image quality means the figure cannot actually be seen clearly, so this word overstates what the detective can perceive.

obscured

Correct — The graininess and darkness hide the figure's details, which is exactly what obscured means: partly blocked from being seen. It matches the detective's struggle to discern anyone in the footage, capturing why clearer images are needed.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
perceptible

Able to be noticed or detected through any means. Visible is specifically about sight; perceptible works for any sense or mode of detection.

apparent

Clear and obvious to perceive. Visible focuses specifically on sight; apparent can mean obviously clear in any way.

discernible

Able to be distinguished or recognized with difficulty. Discernible requires clarity and recognition; visible is more directly about being able to see something.

Antonyms · opposites

invisible — Cannot be seen: the in- prefix negates visible, creating the direct opposite.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

vis + ible

Latin vis (see) — the same 'see' hiding in vision, visual, and television ('seeing at a distance') — + -ible (can be done) = can be seen. 'Vis' is a bare Latin stem, not an English word, so it takes -ible.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Visible is the precise word when something can be perceived by sight, often emphasizing that it was previously hidden or hard to detect. Use it when visibility itself — the ability to see something — is the key point.
