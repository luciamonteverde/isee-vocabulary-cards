Agile

/AJ-ul/ — rhymes with fragile · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone or something that is agile can move quickly, easily, and with good balance.

Show it in context

The agile gymnast flipped across the beam without a single wobble.

Here's how to decide when agile is the exact word.

Worked Example

The ninja scaled the wall, jumping and leaping with agile movements, never losing balance even on the narrowest ledges.

Agile describes quick movement with excellent balance and control — the combination of speed AND balance is what makes the word precise. Plain 'fast' misses the balance; 'graceful' misses the speed. Agile captures both together.

Your Turn

A forklift slowly lifted heavy boxes in a warehouse.

Is agile the right word for how the forklift moves?

Yes

Not quite — It is tempting because a forklift does move and lift, but 'agile' means quick, nimble, easily balanced movement, which a slow, bulky forklift lacks.

No

Correct — A forklift is slow and cumbersome, while agile describes light, quick, well-balanced movement, so agile does not fit.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
nimble

Quick and light-footed — similar to agile, but nimble emphasizes lightness and speed; agile includes balance and overall coordination.

lithe

Gracefully slender and flexible — close to agile in movement quality, but lithe emphasizes flexibility and grace more than speed.

spry

Active and lively, especially despite age — similar to agile in quickness, but spry is often used for older people; agile has no age implication.

Antonyms · opposites

sluggish — Slow and lacking energy — the opposite of agile's quick, easy, balanced movement.

Build the Word

agere Latin: 'to drive, move, act'

Agile comes from the Latin root meaning 'to move' — when you're agile, you move with quick, easy control.

The manager will act quickly to resolve the problem.

Same root, new word — what does act mean here?

to sit still and wait

Not quite — This is the opposite of the sentence, where the manager moves quickly; the root agere means 'to move or drive,' so act means to take action, not to wait.

to pretend to be someone else

Not quite — An actor 'acts' on a stage, but even that sense comes from doing and moving; here to act quickly means to take real action to fix the problem, matching agere's sense of moving and doing.

to take action or do something

Correct — Agile and act both descend from the Latin agere ('to move, drive, do'). The manager acts by moving into action, the same driving movement that makes an agile body quick and nimble.

The root connection →

Agere ('to move or drive') is the shared root: to act is to set yourself in motion and do something, and to be agile is to move with quick, easy control.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Agile describes quick, easy movement with balance — use it for people and animals moving skillfully. For just speed without balance, 'fast' or 'swift' works; for just flexibility, 'flexible' is better.
