Timid

/TIM-id/ — rhymes with vim · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Timid describes someone who is shy and lacks confidence, especially in new or uncertain situations.

Show it in context

The timid new student ate lunch alone rather than ask to join a table.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when timid is the exact word.

Worked Example

The timid intern barely spoke during the team meeting, despite having prepared thoughtful comments, because she feared being judged or saying something wrong.

Timid captures the combination of shyness and lack of confidence, which is more precise than just calling someone 'quiet' or 'anxious.'

Your Turn

A student is timid about raising his hand in math class even though he knows the answer.

Is timid the best word here, or would 'reluctant' work better?

reluctant

Correct — The scenario only tells us he holds back from raising his hand, and 'reluctant' names that unwillingness to act without claiming to know what is driving it.

timid

Not quite — 'Timid' is tempting since shyness could explain the hesitation, but timid insists on a lack of confidence and fear of being judged. The scenario shows him holding back, not that he is afraid, so timid reads more into his feelings than we are told.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
shy

Shy means avoiding social attention. Timid includes shyness but emphasizes the deeper lack of confidence.

diffident

Diffident means lacking confidence in yourself. Timid is more emotional—it includes fear alongside the self-doubt.

meek

Meek means quiet and submissive. Timid can look meek, but it stems from fear or self-doubt, not accepting a lower position.

Antonyms · opposites

bold — Bold people act decisively without fear; timid people hold back due to lack of confidence and fear.

Build the Word

timidus Latin: fearful, afraid

Timid comes from the Latin word for fearful — timidity is the fear and self-doubt that keeps people from taking action.

His timorous behavior meant he refused to try anything new or risky.

Same root, new word — what does timorous mean here?

quick to anger or easily irritated

Not quite — This is tempting because timorous looks a little like 'temper,' but the Latin root timidus means 'fearful, afraid.' Timorous is about fear, not anger.

showing fear or lack of courage

Correct — Timid and timorous both descend from the Latin timidus, 'fearful, afraid.' The timid student fears judgment, and the timorous person lets that same fear stop him from trying anything new.

bold and daring, eager to take risks

Not quite — This is the opposite of timorous, which makes it a trap. The root timidus ('fearful, afraid') means a timorous person shrinks from risk rather than chasing it.

The root connection →

Both words trace to Latin timidus ('fearful, afraid'): timid names the fear and self-doubt itself, while timorous describes someone whose behavior is steered by that fear.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Timid is your word for someone held back by lack of confidence and shyness, especially in social or performance situations. Use it when the hesitation comes from fear of judgment or self-doubt. 'Shy' alone is simpler; 'timid' captures the deeper paralysis.
