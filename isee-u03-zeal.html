Zeal

/zeel/ — rhymes with feel · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Zeal is intense enthusiasm or eagerness in pursuing something.

Show it in context

She tackled the recycling project with such zeal that the whole school joined in.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when zeal is the exact word.

Worked Example

The young activist displayed such zeal for environmental reform that she organized three campus cleanups in a single month.

The intensity here—the relentless drive to act—is exactly what zeal names. Because the enthusiasm is so focused and purposeful, zeal is more precise than the milder word 'interest.'

Your Turn

Marcus enjoyed the school play and applauded at the curtain call.

Is zeal the precise word here, or would 'enjoyment' work better?

enjoyment

Correct — Marcus claps politely at a play he liked, which is exactly the mild, casual pleasure that 'enjoyment' names.

zeal

Not quite — Zeal is tempting because he clearly liked the show, but zeal means fierce, driven enthusiasm, and polite applause at a curtain call shows nothing that intense or focused.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
passion

Strong feeling and commitment. Zeal adds the sense of energetic, purposeful pursuit.

fervor

Intense feeling, like zeal, but fervor emphasizes heat and emotion; zeal emphasizes focused action.

devotion

Loyal commitment over time. Zeal is more about energetic pursuit right now.

Antonyms · opposites

apathy — Lack of interest or enthusiasm — the opposite of zeal's driven energy.

Build the Word

zelos Greek: 'eager rivalry'

In ancient Greece, zelos captured the fire of competing hard against rivals — that burning drive to win and excel. The word shifted to mean any intense, driven enthusiasm.

The students' emulation of their teacher's dedication inspired the whole class.

Same root, new word — what does emulation mean here?

the effort to rival or match an admired person's achievement

Correct — Correct. The root zelos ('eager rivalry') fuels zeal's burning drive, and it powers emulation too, the eager effort to rival and match the teacher's dedication.

bitter resentment of someone who has more

Not quite — This is tempting because rivalry can curdle into jealousy, but the zelos root here means eager, driven striving to match excellence, not sour envy of what others have.

copying someone exactly with no effort to improve

Not quite — This is tempting because emulation involves following an example, but zelos ('eager rivalry') means actively striving to equal or surpass a model, not passively imitating it.

The root connection →

Zeal and emulation both rest on the idea in zelos, 'eager rivalry': zeal is the inner fire of that striving, and emulation is the outward push to equal an admired model. Because zelos is Greek and emulation reached English through Latin, treat them as sharing a root sense of rivalry rather than one clean word ancestor.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Zeal is the energy of purpose — reach for it when someone's enthusiasm drives them to action. For simple liking or interest, plain words like 'interest' or 'enjoyment' are simpler.
