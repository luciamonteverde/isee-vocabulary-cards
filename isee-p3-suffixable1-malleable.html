Malleable

MAL-ee-uh-bul adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Malleable is capable of being shaped, molded, or hammered into a new form; easily influenced or changed.

Show it in context

The metalworking apprentice learned to heat the copper sheet until it grew malleable enough to hammer into the curved shape of the lamp's base.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Marketing researchers often describe teenage brand loyalty as highly malleable, since a single viral trend can completely reshape what an entire generation considers cool.

Malleable can describe a physical material shaped by pressure or heat, or -- just as often -- a person, mind, or situation with real potential to be changed or developed.

Malleable comes from Latin malleus ('hammer') -- the same root behind the everyday tool mallet. Something malleable is, at root, able to be hammered into a new shape without breaking.

Use the root in a new word: mallet

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Malleable is usually neutral or even positive -- it suggests real potential for growth or change, especially when describing young minds, new organizations, or raw materials, rather than criticizing weakness.
