Candid

/KAN-did/ — rhymes with sandal · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is candid speaks honestly and directly, even about things that might be uncomfortable to admit.

Show it in context

Her candid review admitted the play's first act was dull.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when candid is the exact word.

Worked Example

During the parent-teacher meeting, the teacher gave a candid assessment of the student's work, pointing out exactly where he struggled without softening the critique.

Candid is more precise than 'honest' because it emphasizes speaking directly about uncomfortable truths. The teacher wasn't just being truthful; she was directly addressing difficult shortcomings in a way that cannot be avoided, which is exactly what candid means.

Your Turn

My friend asked me what I thought of her new haircut, and I said, 'It looks nice.'

Is this an example of being candid?

Yes

Not quite — Being candid means openly sharing your honest thoughts, even uncomfortable ones. Saying only 'It looks nice' while hiding your real opinion is polite, not candid.

No

Correct — Candid requires actually speaking your true opinion. A safe 'It looks nice' that conceals what you really think is the opposite of candid honesty.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
honest

Truthful, but not necessarily direct or about uncomfortable things. Candid adds the sense of willingly admitting what's hard to say.

frank

Direct and truthful, very similar to candid, but less emphasis on the emotional difficulty of the admission.

straightforward

Clear and direct, but doesn't require the same willingness to speak uncomfortable truths that candid does.

Antonyms · opposites

evasive — Avoiding direct answers; the opposite of candid's openness and directness.

Build the Word

candidus Latin: 'white, shining, pure'

White light reveals everything — nothing is hidden — so candid came to mean 'revealing the truth openly and directly.'

The candle illuminated the dark room, casting warm light on every corner.

Same root, new word — what does candle mean here?

a source of light that reveals what was hidden in the dark

Correct — Candid and candle both come from the Latin candidus, 'white, shining.' Candid light reveals the truth openly, and a candle sheds shining light that reveals a dark room.

a container that stores heat

Not quite — Candles feel warm, so this seems plausible, but the root candidus means 'white, shining.' The candle in the sentence reveals the room by shining, not by holding heat.

a decoration used only for its scent

Not quite — Scented candles are common, which makes this tempting, but candidus points to shining whiteness and light, and here the candle lights up the room rather than just smelling nice.

The root connection →

The Latin candidus ('white, shining') lights up both words: to be candid is to let the truth shine plainly, and a candle is the shining flame that makes hidden things visible.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Candid is a more formal, serious word than 'honest.' Use candid when someone is speaking difficult truths directly, even though it's uncomfortable. For everyday truthfulness, 'honest' is simpler and more natural.
