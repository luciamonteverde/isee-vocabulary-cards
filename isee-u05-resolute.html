Resolute

/RES-uh-loot/ — rhymes with absolute · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is resolute is firmly determined and unwilling to give up, even when facing doubt or difficulty.

Show it in context

Despite three rejections, the resolute inventor kept refining her design.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when resolute is the exact word.

Worked Example

The climber pushed through exhaustion and mounting doubt on the mountain, refusing to turn back despite the gathering storm.

Resolute is more precise than 'determined' because it emphasizes not just the intention to reach a goal, but the unwillingness to abandon it despite facing obstacles and internal doubt. Resolute captures that firmness in the face of adversity.

Your Turn

After thinking it over for a week, Maria decided to switch colleges.

Is 'resolute' the right word here, or should it be something else?

decided

Correct — The sentence only tells us Maria made a choice after thinking it over, and 'decided' names that thoughtful choice exactly without adding anything extra.

resolute

Not quite — It is tempting because she thought hard first, but 'resolute' means firmly set and unwilling to turn back, and nothing here shows she refuses to reconsider.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
determined

Shows clear intent to achieve something, but lacks the sense of standing firm against doubt and repeated setbacks that resolute requires.

steadfast

Emphasizes loyalty and unwavering consistency, but doesn't capture the specific struggle against doubt and difficulty that resolute implies.

unyielding

Means rigid and stubborn; lacks the noble, purposeful connotation that resolute carries, and doesn't necessarily involve facing doubt.

Antonyms · opposites

wavering — Hesitating and uncertain — the opposite of the firm, unwavering determination that resolute names.

How Strong Is It?
willing→determined→resolute→unflinching

Resolute stands between determined (mere intention) and unflinching (absolute certainty) — it's firm commitment despite doubt and difficulty. (our word)

Build the Word

resolvere Latin: 're-' (again) + 'solvere' (to loosen, settle) = to settle or decide firmly

When you resolve a question, you settle it in your mind and become firm about it — resolute describes someone whose mind is already settled and won't shift.

The detective worked to resolve the mystery, gathering evidence until the truth became clear.

Same root, new word — what does resolve mean here?

to settle a problem firmly by finding its answer

Correct — Both resolute and resolve grow from the Latin resolvere ('re-' plus 'solvere', to settle firmly). The detective settles the mystery by fixing on its answer, the same firm settling that makes a resolute person's mind already made up.

to break something apart into smaller pieces

Not quite — Tempting because the root 'solvere' means to loosen, and dissolving does break things apart, but here the detective is settling the mystery and finding its answer, not physically breaking anything.

to make a formal promise for the future

Not quite — This echoes a New Year's resolution, but in this sentence resolve means the detective settles the question by uncovering the truth, not vowing to do something later.

The root connection →

Resolute and resolve share the root solvere ('to loosen or settle'): to resolve a problem is to settle it firmly, and a resolute person is someone whose mind is already settled and will not shift.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Resolute is more formal and elevated than the everyday word 'determined.' Use it when you want to emphasize that someone has firmly made up their mind and won't waver despite real obstacles and self-doubt. For simple determination without those added layers, 'determined' is the plainer choice.
