Tamper

/TAM-per/ — rhymes with hamper · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To tamper with something is to meddle with it in a way that changes, damages, or interferes with how it normally works, usually without permission.

Show it in context

Someone had tampered with the scoreboard, adding fifty points overnight.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when tamper is the exact word.

Worked Example

Investigators discovered that someone had tampered with the evidence, planting false documents to frame the suspect.

Tamper captures the idea of unauthorized meddling that deliberately alters or damages something's normal function. It's not casual touching or examining—it's stealthy interference with intent to harm. The word conveys both the violation and the harmful effect.

Your Turn

The mechanic adjusted the engine settings to improve fuel efficiency.

Is tamper the right word here, or is there a better choice?

Tampered

Not quite — Tamper implies unauthorized meddling that harms or interferes with normal operation. The mechanic had permission and was actually improving the engine, so tamper misrepresents helpful, sanctioned work as damage.

Adjusted

Correct — This neutrally describes a mechanic making authorized improvements to the engine settings, without the sense of harmful, unauthorized meddling that tamper carries.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
meddle

To interfere in someone else's affairs. Tamper is more specific—meddling with an object to change its function.

interfere

To obstruct or hinder. Tamper is more specific about meddling with a particular thing in a way that damages or changes it.

manipulate

To control or influence skillfully. Tamper implies clumsy or reckless unauthorized meddling, not skillful or intentional control.

Antonyms · opposites

preserve — To keep something in its original, undamaged state. Preserve and tamper are opposites.

Build the Word

tampen Middle English: 'to pack down; to stop up'

The word originally meant to pack or compress something (tamp it down firmly), then evolved to mean tampering with or meddling—interfering in a way that disrupts normal function.

The worker tamped down the soil to create a solid, level foundation.

Same root, new word — what does tamped mean here?

loosened and spread apart

Not quite — Not quite. 'Tamp' shares the tampen 'to pack down, stop up' root with 'tamper,' so it means to press down firmly, not to loosen or scatter.

packed down firmly, compressed

Correct — Correct. 'Tamped' comes from the same tampen 'to pack down, stop up' family as 'tamper': to tamp soil is to press it down firmly.

The root connection →

Both 'tamper' and 'tamp' come from the Middle English tampen, 'to pack down or stop up.' Over time 'tamper' drifted toward the idea of meddling with something, while 'tamp' kept the original sense of pressing down firmly.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Tamper suggests deliberate, unauthorized meddling that interferes with something's normal function or purpose. Use it when someone secretly alters or damages something they shouldn't touch. For authorized changes or minor adjustments, 'modify' or 'adjust' is more appropriate.
