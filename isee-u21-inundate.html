Inundate

/in-UN-date/ — rhymes with mandate · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To inundate something is to flood or overwhelm it completely, as with water or an excessive amount of something else.

Show it in context

Fan letters inundated the studio after the finale aired.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when inundate is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the concert tickets went on sale, the box office received thousands of calls per minute, and their phone system completely broke under the load.

The caller volume here is truly overwhelming and literally floods the system — inundate captures both the sense of being swamped and the image of water overwhelming everything, making it more vivid and precise than the plain 'overwhelm.'

Your Turn

A teacher receives five extra emails this morning on top of her usual inbox.

Would inundate be the right word for this situation?

inundate

Not quite — Five extra emails is a small addition to her inbox, but inundate means to flood or overwhelm, which is far too strong for a slightly fuller morning.

busy

Correct — A few extra messages just make her a bit busier; inundate would mean a flood of email swamping her completely.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
overwhelm

To overpower someone's ability to cope. Inundate emphasizes the sheer volume and the sense of being completely submerged.

flood

A sudden rush of something. Inundate is more formal and suggests complete submersion; flood is more everyday.

deluge

A heavy, continuous downpour — literal or figurative. Inundate and deluge are close, but deluge emphasizes the rain-like quality more.

Antonyms · opposites

trickle — A slow, tiny flow — the opposite of an inundating flood.

How Strong Is It?

Inundate — the strongest in the group: completely overwhelmed by a massive, irresistible volume. (our word)

Build the Word

inundare Latin: in- (in) + undare (to surge, wave)

The Latin root unda means 'wave' — inundate pictures waves surging in and completely submerging you.

The dancers' movements were fluid and undulating, like water flowing.

Same root, new word — what does undulating mean here?

moving in smooth, wave-like motions

Correct — Correct. Undulating shares the Latin unda (wave) root with inundate, both picturing water and waves.

moving in sharp, jerky bursts

Not quite — The Latin unda means wave, so undulating movement is smooth and wave-like, not sharp or jerky.

The root connection →

Inundate and undulating both come from the Latin unda, wave. To inundate is to flood with waves of water, and to undulate is to move in waves.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Inundate is a stronger, more vivid choice than 'overwhelm' or 'flood' when you want to emphasize that someone or something is completely submerged in a tidal wave of requests, information, or problems. For everyday situations with mild excess, 'overwhelm' is simpler and more honest.
