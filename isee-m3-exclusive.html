Exclusive

/ik-SKLOO-siv/ — stress on SKLOO · adjective

PREVIEW — taught in depth later in Conflict, Disaster & Difficulty

Hear It
What It Means

Something exclusive is limited to a select few people, often because it is expensive, high-status, or closed to the general public.

Show it in context

The chef opened an exclusive dining room with just six tables, booked a year ahead.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when exclusive is the exact word.

Worked Example

The country club kept its membership exclusive, turning away applicants who weren't referred by a current member.

Exclus (from exclude) + -ive: The root exclud- comes from Latin exclusus, 'shut out.' The -ive suffix means 'inclined to, having the quality of.' So exclusive literally means 'inclined to shut others out.' In social and commercial contexts, this evolved into 'reserved for a select few with special status or access.'

Your Turn

The store advertised a 'Super Exclusive Sale — 50% off everything, open to all shoppers.'

Is exclusive the precise word for a sale open to everyone?

Yes

Not quite — It is tempting because the ad itself uses the word, but 'exclusive' means limited to a select few, which a sale open to all shoppers is not.

No

Correct — A sale open to all shoppers is the opposite of exclusive; the word would only fit with something like 'exclusive to members' or 'by invitation only.'

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
selective

Choosy about choices, but doesn't carry the high-status or scarcity implication that exclusive does.

restricted

Limited or off-limits, but emphasizes barriers rather than the prestige and luxury associated with exclusive.

elite

The upper tier or best; exclusive is broader and can apply to any deliberate limitation, not just the highest-ranking.

Antonyms · opposites

inclusive — Open to everyone or all possibilities; the direct semantic opposite of exclusive.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

exclus (exclude) + -ive

exclude → exclus- + -ive. The -de ending swaps to -s- (Latin exclusus): inclined to shut others out — which drifted into 'high-status, reserved for a select few.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / aspirational

Reach for exclusive when you want to convey both restriction and prestige or high value — something not only limited but desirable. For mere limitation without status, use restricted or off-limits. For a group with superior status, elite is more direct.
