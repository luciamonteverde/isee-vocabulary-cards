Pathetic

/puh-THET-ik/ — rhymes with 'athletic' adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Pathetic is so weak or inadequate that it deserves pity or scorn.

Show it in context

The umbrella offered pathetic protection against the sideways hail.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

The school's 'security system' was a rusty gate that no longer latched and a camera that had been broken for years. When bikes went missing from the rack, no one was surprised.

The security was pathetic — miserably inadequate for its job. 'Imperfect' security merely has some gaps; pathetic security fails so completely it invites pity or scorn.

Greek pathos means 'feeling' or 'suffering.' Pathetic originally meant 'stirring the feelings'; today it names things so inadequate that the feeling they stir is pity.

Use the root in a new word: sympathy

She sent a card expressing sympathy after her neighbor's loss.

Root Check

Pathetic (stirring pity) and sympathy (feeling along with someone) share the Greek root path-. What does path- mean?

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

A harsh, scornful word in everyday use — save it for genuine failures, not small flaws.
