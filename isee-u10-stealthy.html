Stealthy

/STEL-thee/ — rhymes with healthy · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Stealthy describes an action carried out quietly and carefully so that no one else notices it.

Show it in context

With stealthy steps, the cat crossed the room without waking the dog.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when stealthy is the exact word.

Worked Example

The spy moved through the building in stealthy silence, pressing against shadows and listening at each door before passing.

Stealthy captures not just quiet movement but careful, intentional action designed to avoid detection. It's not merely quiet; it's the deliberate sneakiness of trying to go unnoticed.

Your Turn

The library was quiet at midnight, with only the faint hum of the refrigerator.

Is stealthy the right word for the library's quietness?

quiet

Correct — The library is simply silent on its own, and no one is deliberately hiding. 'Quiet' describes that natural stillness, which is exactly the situation.

stealthy

Not quite — Stealthy means moving carefully on purpose to avoid being noticed. Nothing in the library is taking secret action, so 'stealthy' does not fit.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
quiet

Lacking noise; stealthy is quiet AND deliberately hidden to avoid detection.

sneaky

Acting in a secretive, underhand way; sneaky is deceptive, while stealthy is about avoiding notice through careful movement.

silent

Making no sound; silent describes the lack of noise, while stealthy includes both silence and deliberate careful action.

Antonyms · opposites

clumsy — Awkward and loud; the opposite of stealthy movement, which is careful and undetectable.

Build the Word

stealth From Old English: 'to steal' (to move secretly)

The word comes from 'steal,' capturing the idea of moving secretly or taking something without being detected.

The thief managed to steal away before the police arrived, slipping out the back door.

Same root, new word — what does steal mean here?

to move away quietly and secretly so as not to be noticed; also, to take something without permission

Correct — Stealthy comes straight from steal. To 'steal away' is to slip off secretly, and 'stealthy' describes that same quiet, unnoticed movement.

to rush away as fast as possible

Not quite — Tempting because the thief is fleeing, but 'steal away' stresses moving secretly and unnoticed, not sheer speed.

to grab an object on the way out

Not quite — Tempting because thieves take things, but in 'steal away' the word describes the secret manner of leaving, not the taking of an object.

The root connection →

Stealthy is built directly on the word steal, whose older sense was to move in secret as much as to take. Whether you steal away out a back door or move in a stealthy way, the shared idea is acting without being seen.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / informal

Use stealthy when an action is carried out quietly and carefully to avoid detection or notice. It implies deliberate, intentional concealment. For something that is naturally quiet or silent, 'quiet' or 'silent' is simpler.
