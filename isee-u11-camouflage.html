Camouflage

/KAM-uh-flahj/ — stress on the first syllable or sometimes the last · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Camouflage is a coloring or pattern that lets a person or animal blend into its surroundings so it is hard to spot.

Show it in context

The moth's camouflage made it nearly invisible against the bark.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when camouflage is the exact word.

Worked Example

The caterpillar's green camouflage let it disappear completely against the leaf it clung to.

Camouflage is precise because it names not just any color or pattern, but specifically one designed to blend into surroundings so the animal becomes nearly invisible. The plain word "color" or "pattern" wouldn't capture that functional purpose of concealment.

Your Turn

The artist used camouflage to create an eye-catching mural on the wall.

Is camouflage the precise word here?

pattern

Correct — A mural is meant to be seen, so the eye-catching design on the wall is simply a 'pattern,' something created to stand out.

camouflage

Not quite — Camouflage is designed to hide something from sight, which is the exact opposite of an eye-catching mural made to be noticed.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
disguise

Something that hides identity or appearance; camouflage is specifically color or pattern that blends into an environment, not a change of costume.

cover

Something that conceals or hides; camouflage is more specific—it works by blending seamlessly rather than just covering.

concealment

The act or method of hiding; camouflage is a specific type that uses coloring or pattern to blend into natural surroundings.

Antonyms · opposites

exposure — The state of being visible or uncovered; camouflage works by reducing exposure through blending.

Camouflage — the most complete concealment: color and pattern that blend so seamlessly the object vanishes into its surroundings. (our word)

Build the Word

camouflet French: originally 'a puff of smoke blown in someone's face'; later 'a hidden attack or deception'

A camouflet was originally a joking puff of smoke used to trick or deceive, so the word camoufler (to deceive) eventually gave rise to "camouflage" for any color and pattern that hides an organism in its natural surroundings.

The leopard seemed to vanish into the spotted shadows of the tall grass.

Same root, new word — what does vanish mean here?

to leap forward suddenly

Not quite — A leopard might well pounce, so this is tempting, but vanish means to drop out of view, which is the effect good camouflage produces rather than a burst of movement.

to growl or make a warning noise

Not quite — Big cats do growl, but vanish is about disappearing visually, not making sound; it names the disappearing effect camouflage is meant to create.

to disappear from sight

Correct — Camouflage and vanish are linked by idea more than by any documented shared root. Camouflage, from French camouflet (a trick or deception), works by making something seem to vanish into its surroundings, the way the leopard disappears into the grass.

The root connection →

Here the connection is thematic rather than a proven common root. The French camouflet gave us camouflage, a disguise that hides, and its whole purpose is to make a creature appear to vanish, even though vanish itself traces to a separate Latin source.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Camouflage is the word to use when color or pattern helps someone or something blend into and nearly disappear within its surroundings. It's more specific than "hiding" or "cover" because it emphasizes the seamless blending. Use it for animals, soldiers, and anything designed to blend naturally; for intentional disguises or costumes, "disguise" is more precise.
