Precocious

prih-KOH-shus — rhymes with ferocious · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Precocious describes a child who shows abilities, skills, or maturity well beyond what is typical for their age.

Show it in context

The precocious six-year-old corrected the museum guide's dinosaur facts.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when precocious is the exact word.

Worked Example

The precocious eight-year-old had already read calculus textbooks and was inventing her own mathematical proofs.

Precocious specifically emphasizes that the child's abilities are far beyond what's normal or expected for her age — she's developmentally advanced, not just smart. It's more precise than 'gifted' or 'smart' because it highlights the unusual earliness of her maturity and skills.

Your Turn

The precocious five-year-old enjoyed playing with dolls and asking questions during storytime.

Is precocious the right word here?

typical

Correct — Her activities are exactly what you would expect from a child her age, and typical captures that ordinary, age-appropriate behavior.

precocious

Not quite — Playing with dolls and asking questions at storytime is ordinary five-year-old behavior; precocious would require abilities well beyond her age, which the scene never shows.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
gifted

Describes exceptional ability or talent. Doesn't necessarily emphasize how unusually early the development is.

mature

Advanced in behavior or thinking for one's age. Doesn't always mean exceptional ability in a specific domain like precocious does.

advanced

Ahead of typical development. Like precocious, it suggests moving past age-level, but without the same sense of remarkable earliness.

Antonyms · opposites

delayed — Behind in development for one's age. The opposite of precocious advancement.

Build the Word

praecox Latin: cooked or ripened beforehand

Precocious originally described fruit that ripened early in the season — now it means a child who matures and gains skills unusually early.

The opening ceremony will precede the main event by exactly one hour.

Same root, new word — what does precede mean here?

to come before in time or order

Correct — Correct. Precocious and precede both open with the Latin prefix prae- (before), so precede means to go before. Note, though, that they build on different root verbs, so the link is in the prefix, not the whole word.

to follow closely after something

Not quite — Precede is built on the prefix prae- (before) plus cedere (to go), so it means to go before, not to come after.

The root connection →

Precocious and precede both start with the Latin prefix prae- (before): precocious literally means 'cooked before its time,' and precede means 'to go before.' They build on different root verbs, though, so the connection is in the prefix, not a shared root.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Precocious is the precise word when you want to emphasize that a child's abilities or maturity are far beyond their age. In casual conversation, 'smart' or 'mature for their age' works fine.
