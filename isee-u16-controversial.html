Controversial

kun-TROV-ur-shal — stress on TROV · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something controversial sparks strong disagreement because people hold very different, often opposing, opinions about it.

Show it in context

The controversial mural drew both daily protests and daily crowds of admirers.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when controversial is the exact word.

Worked Example

The statue in the town square became controversial—some residents saw it as a tribute to their heritage, while others viewed it as celebrating a shameful past.

Controversial captures that something sparks genuine disagreement because people hold sincere but opposite opinions about it. Unlike 'unpopular' (which just means most people dislike it), controversial shows that strong feeling exists on both sides.

Your Turn

The new pizza shop was a huge success, with lines out the door every day.

Would you call this controversial?

No

Correct — Controversial requires real disagreement with strong feelings on both sides, and a well-liked pizza shop with lines out the door has people agreeing, not clashing.

Yes

Not quite — A busy, popular shop might feel like a big deal, but success alone is not controversy; nobody is strongly arguing against it.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
questionable

Open to doubt or suspicion. Questionable suggests uncertainty or mildly negative judgment, while controversial suggests passionate disagreement between two convinced sides.

disputed

Subject to disagreement. Disputed can be more factual (two parties disagree about a fact), while controversial is rooted in strongly held, opposed values or beliefs.

debatable

Open to argument or different opinions. Debatable is milder—controversial implies not just that people disagree, but that they feel strongly and passionately about it.

Antonyms · opposites

uncontroversial — Something everyone agrees on or feels neutral about—the opposite of controversial's passionate disagreement.

How Strong Is It?
unpopular→questionable→disputed→controversial

Controversial — the strongest: sparks strong, sincere disagreement across opposing sides. (our word)

Build the Word

controversia Latin: dispute, disagreement

When people are turned against each other (contra- 'against' + vertere 'to turn'), controversy follows—opinions turned in opposite directions.

She had to revert to her earlier position after reconsidering the evidence.

Same root, new word — what does revert mean here?

to argue loudly against someone

Not quite — Controversy involves arguing, so 'argue against' feels connected, but revert is about turning back, not fighting.

to review a set of facts carefully

Not quite — 'Reconsidering the evidence' mentions review, but that is what she did before reverting; revert itself means going back to her old position.

to return to a previous state or condition

Correct — Controversial and revert both come from the Latin vertere, 'to turn.' Controversy is opinions turned against each other (contra- plus vertere), while to revert is to turn back (re- plus vertere).

The root connection →

Keep the root vertere, 'to turn.' Turn opinions against one another and you get controversy; turn yourself back to where you started and you revert.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Controversial is perfect for something that genuinely divides people based on strongly held beliefs. Use it when you want to emphasize that disagreement runs deep and both sides feel passionately. For milder disagreement, debatable or questionable is often clearer.
