Lackluster

/LAK-lus-ter/ — rhymes with cluster · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Lackluster describes something that is unimpressive and lacking in energy or brilliance.

Show it in context

The sequel earned lackluster reviews and quietly left theaters.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when lackluster is the exact word.

Worked Example

The long-awaited fireworks display proved lackluster—no bright colors exploding across the sky, no thunderous booms, just a handful of faint sparks that flickered briefly.

Lackluster is perfect here because it emphasizes that these fireworks failed to deliver the expected brilliance and spectacle. It's not just 'bad'—it's specifically disappointing because fireworks are supposed to be impressive and brilliant, and these weren't. The plain word 'dull' would miss this sense of failed expectations.

Your Turn

The movie had a plot hole, confusing dialogue, and actors who forgot their lines.

Would we call this movie lackluster or just bad?

Lackluster

Not quite — Lackluster means falling short of an expected brilliance, but nothing tells us this movie ever aimed high; the plot holes and blown lines just make it plainly bad.

Bad

Correct — Plot holes, confusing dialogue, and forgotten lines are simply poor filmmaking, so bad fits, since there is no sign of a bright goal the movie failed to reach.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
dull

Boring and uninteresting. Dull describes something tedious; lackluster implies it failed to deliver expected brilliance or energy.

mediocre

Average and unremarkable. Mediocre is neutral disappointment; lackluster suggests a specific failure to shine.

uninspired

Lacking creativity or originality. Uninspired describes the creative failure; lackluster is about the lack of energy or brilliance.

Antonyms · opposites

dazzling — Strikingly brilliant and impressive. The opposite of lackluster's disappointing lack of energy or shine.

How Strong Is It?
plain→mediocre→dull→lackluster

Lackluster — the strongest: unimpressive in its failure to deliver expected brilliance or energy, disappointingly lacking shine. (our word)

Build the Word

luster Latin: 'lux' meaning 'light'; luster is a noun meaning 'shine or brightness'

Lackluster combines 'lack' + 'luster'—literally 'lacking shine.' The word paints the image of something that should gleam but doesn't.

The lustrous pearls gleamed with a soft brilliance in the museum light.

Same root, new word — what does lustrous mean here?

dull, faded, and colorless

Not quite — The root luster, from Latin lux meaning light, is about shine, so lustrous means full of gleam, not dull and faded.

shining brightly; brilliant

Correct — Correct. Lustrous shares the luster in lackluster, both from Latin lux, light, so lustrous means shining with brilliance.

The root connection →

Lackluster and lustrous both come from Latin lux, light, by way of luster, a shine. Something lustrous is full of shine, while lackluster is missing that shine.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Lackluster is the word for something that disappoints by failing to shine or impress. Use it when you want to suggest that something failed to deliver the brilliance or energy you expected. For something simply boring or average, plainer words like 'dull' or 'mediocre' work better.
