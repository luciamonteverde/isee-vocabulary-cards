Embargo

/em-BAR-go/ — rhymes with cargo · noun

Hear It
What It Means

An embargo is an official ban or restriction, often preventing trade with a country or blocking the release of certain information.

Show it in context

The embargo stopped all oil shipments between the two countries.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when embargo is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the diplomatic crisis, the nation imposed a full embargo on all trade with its neighbor, effectively shutting down the supply of imported goods.

Embargo is precise here because it captures an official governmental ban—especially on trade—with serious international consequences. 'Ban' is too generic; embargo specifically names the geopolitical trade restriction that carries legal weight.

Your Turn

The publisher placed an embargo on the celebrity's tell-all memoir, refusing to release it until after the election.

Is embargo the precise word here, or should it just say something else?

restriction

Correct — Embargo most often names an official ban on trade between nations. For a publisher holding back a book, the plainer 'restriction' or 'delay' is clearer and more precise.

embargo

Not quite — Though embargo can cover holding back information, it usually points to trade sanctions between countries. Here it is heavier and less clear than simply saying 'restriction.'

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
ban

A prohibition or rule against something. Embargo is the formal, governmental, especially trade-related version.

blockade

A barrier preventing movement or trade, often with military enforcement. Embargo is broader and more diplomatic.

sanction

A penalty imposed for breaking rules. Refers to punishment; embargo refers to the blocking action itself.

Antonyms · opposites

trade — The exchange of goods between nations—the opposite of an embargo.

How Strong Is It?
restriction→ban→blockade→embargo

Embargo — the strongest: an official governmental trade or information ban with serious geopolitical weight. (our word)

Build the Word

embargar Spanish: 'to impede, seize, detain'

To embargo is to put a 'bar' against trade or goods—to block them from passage.

The fence had iron bars that barred entry to the property.

Same root, new word — what does bar mean here?

to decorate or line something with metal

Not quite — Iron bars can sound like a decorative touch, but the root embargar means to impede or seize, and 'barred entry' means the fence blocked the way in, not that it was ornamental.

to open something wide

Not quite — Gates and fences swing open, which makes this tempting, but bar and embargar are about impeding passage, the very opposite of opening the way.

to block or prevent passage

Correct — Embargo comes from the Spanish embargar, built on the idea of setting a bar against something. To bar a gate, like an embargo on trade, is to block passage.

The root connection →

The 'bar' inside embargar is the bridge: an embargo puts a bar across trade so nothing can pass, and to bar a door is literally to block the way through.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Embargo is the formal, official term for governmental trade restrictions or information bans—use it when discussing nations or official policy. For a simple prohibition, 'ban' works fine.
