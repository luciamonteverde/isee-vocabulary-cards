Jocular

/JAH-kyuh-lur/ — accent on first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Jocular describes someone who is playful and fond of joking around, always ready with a laugh.

Show it in context

His jocular remarks kept the long car ride from feeling boring.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when jocular is the exact word.

Worked Example

Even during the stressful debate tournament, the team captain's jocular asides kept everyone laughing and loose.

The blend here—the playfulness combined with ready humor—is exactly what jocular names. Because the remarks are meant to entertain and amuse, jocular is more precise than 'light' or 'funny.'

Your Turn

The comedian's performance was very crude and often offensive.

Is jocular the precise word here, or would 'crude' work better?

crude

Correct — The act is described as crude and often offensive, so 'crude' names exactly what the audience is getting.

jocular

Not quite — Jocular is tempting because a comedian is telling jokes, but jocular means playful and warmly good-humored, and humor that is crude and offensive lacks that affectionate, well-meant spirit.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
witty

Clever and amusing in speech. Witty is about intellectual humor; jocular is about playful, informal joking.

droll

Amusing in a quiet, wry way. Droll humor is dry and understated; jocular is warm and openly playful.

facetious

Joking when seriousness is expected, sometimes inappropriately. Facetious is joking out of place; jocular is naturally playful.

Antonyms · opposites

solemn — Serious and formal — the opposite of jocular's warm, playful tone.

Build the Word

jocus Latin: 'joke' or 'jest'

The Romans had jocus for any playful joke or jest, and from it grew joculus (a little joke) — both roots live in jocular, the spirit of lighthearted, playful joking.

His jocose manner during the project made the work feel less tedious and more fun.

Same root, new word — what does jocose mean here?

serious and strictly business-like

Not quite — This is tempting because 'manner during the project' sounds workplace-serious, but the jocus root means joke, so jocose points to playfulness, the opposite of grim seriousness.

clumsy and awkward

Not quite — This is tempting because an unfamiliar word can sound like it means bumbling, but jocus ('joke') ties jocose to lighthearted joking, not physical clumsiness.

playfully joking and full of good humor

Correct — Correct. The root jocus ('joke') makes jocular lighthearted and playful, and it gives jocose the very same joking, good-humored spirit.

The root connection →

Jocular and jocose are near-twins from the Latin jocus, 'joke': both describe someone whose default mode is playful joking. Jocular is the more common form today, but jocose carries the identical jocus root and the same fun-loving meaning.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Jocular is the warmth of joking — reach for it when someone's humor is playful, affectionate, and meant to entertain. For simple humor or jokes, plain words like 'funny' or 'amusing' are simpler.
