Hierarchy

/HY-rar-kee/ — stress on first syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A hierarchy is a system that ranks people or things by level of importance or authority, from the highest down to the lowest.

Show it in context

In the office hierarchy, interns report to analysts, and analysts to managers.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when hierarchy is the exact word.

Worked Example

The marine captain read the command hierarchy before the mission: sailors reported to petty officers, petty officers to lieutenants, and lieutenants to the captain.

Hierarchy is more precise than 'chain of command' or 'pecking order' because it emphasizes the *entire structure* of authority ranked from top to bottom. The word captures the complete *system* of authority, not just that some people rank higher than others.

Your Turn

A family has Mom, Dad, two teenagers, and a toddler, and parents make the big decisions while kids have input on smaller things.

If a family member just wants to say parents make bigger choices than kids do, would 'hierarchy' be the right word?

Simple everyday wording

Correct — Just saying the parents make the bigger choices while the kids weigh in on smaller ones describes the roles plainly, without the formal, organizational feel that calling it a hierarchy would add.

Hierarchy

Not quite — A family sharing bigger and smaller decisions has informal, shifting roles. Hierarchy implies a fixed, structured chain of authority like an organization chart, which makes this everyday household sound far more rigid than it is.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
ladder

A ranking system that suggests upward climbing and opportunity; lacks hierarchy's sense of a complete, formal structure.

chain of command

Focuses on *who reports to whom*, while hierarchy shows the *whole system* of ranks and authority.

pecking order

Suggests competition or informal ranking; hierarchy is more formal and neutral about how the system is organized.

Antonyms · opposites

equality — Everyone has the same rank or authority—the opposite of a hierarchical system.

Build the Word

hierarchia Greek: 'rule of the sacred' (from hieros 'sacred' + archia 'rule')

Hierarchy originally described how angels were ranked in heaven—the sacred and the ruling, ordered in strict layers from highest to lowest.

The country descended into anarchy when the government collapsed and no one held authority over anyone else.

Same root, new word — what does anarchy mean here?

complete absence of rule or authority

Correct — Correct. Anarchy joins an- (without) to the archia root (rule), which is why it means a total absence of authority, the same archia found in hierarchy.

a strict system where one ruler holds total power

Not quite — That describes rule concentrated in one person, the reverse of anarchy. The prefix an- means without and archia means rule, so anarchy is the absence of authority, not its concentration.

The root connection →

Hierarchy and anarchy share the Greek root archia, meaning rule. Hierarchy is a sacred or ranked order of rule, while anarchy, with an- meaning without, is rule's complete absence.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Hierarchy is the word when you need to name a *system* of authority or ranking—think organizational charts, military chains, or social structures. For a simple statement that one person is 'above' another, plain words like 'the parents decide more' work better. Reach for hierarchy when the *structure* itself is important.
