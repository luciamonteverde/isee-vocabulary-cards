Parasite

/PARE-uh-syt/ — stress on first syllable, 'site' rhymes with 'knight' · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A parasite is an organism that lives on or inside another organism, taking what it needs to survive while harming or giving nothing back to its host.

Show it in context

The tick is a parasite that feeds on the blood of deer.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when parasite is the exact word.

Worked Example

A lamprey attaches to a salmon, draining its blood meal after meal, leaving the fish weakened and scarred but still alive.

The lamprey lives on and in the salmon without killing it, feeding over time by draining its blood. Parasite is more precise than 'predator' because it names the relationship of living in or on a host and depending on it, not hunting and killing.

Your Turn

A lion hunts and kills a zebra for food, then moves on to find another prey animal.

Is parasite the precise word here, or would 'predator' be better?

predator

Correct — Because the lion hunts and kills its prey for food, predator is the precise word for this relationship, unlike parasite.

parasite

Not quite — The lion kills the zebra outright and moves on, but a parasite feeds on a living host without killing it, so parasite does not describe this hunting.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
pest

Any annoying creature or problem. Parasite is specifically an organism that lives in or on the host and depends on it for survival.

predator

A hunter that kills its prey. Parasite lives inside or on the host, feeding without immediately killing it.

invader

Something that enters and takes over a space. Parasite enters and becomes dependent on the host, forming a lasting one-sided bond.

Antonyms · opposites

mutualist — An organism that benefits both parties in a relationship. Parasite benefits only itself at the host's expense.

How Strong Is It?
guest→dependent→parasite→infestation

Parasite — the strongest: an organism living on or in the host, taking resources without returning anything. (our word)

Build the Word

parasitus Latin: 'beside + food' (para- 'beside' + situs 'grain/food')

In ancient Greece, a parasite was a dinner guest who showed up uninvited, sitting beside others at feasts and eating at their expense, contributing nothing.

The two hiking trails ran parallel to each other, never intersecting as they descended the mountain.

Same root, new word — what does parallel mean here?

running alongside

Correct — Correct. Parallel shares the Greek prefix para-, 'beside,' with parasite, so it means running alongside.

crossing at a single point

Not quite — That is the opposite of parallel. The para- prefix means 'beside,' so parallel trails run alongside each other and never cross, just as the sentence says they never intersect.

The root connection →

Parasite and parallel share only the prefix para-, 'beside,' not a whole root. In parasite it joins sitos, 'food,' to picture something feeding beside its host; in parallel it joins a different base meaning 'one another.' The link is the prefix alone.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Parasite is the precise term for an organism living in or on another organism, depending on it while causing harm. Use it when describing this specific biological relationship. For casual reference to an annoying person or creature, 'pest' or 'freeloader' might work, but parasite is far more exact and scientific.
