Depiction

/duh-PIK-shun/ — stress on PIK · noun

What It Means

A depiction is a picture, description, or other representation of someone or something.

Show it in context

The mural's depiction of the harbor includes every boat by name.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when depiction is the exact word.

Worked Example

The historical novel's rich depiction of life during the Civil War included authentic details about clothing, food, rationing, and the daily struggles of ordinary people.

Depiction is precise here because it means a full representation — the novel shows us both the big picture and small details, both visual and descriptive. Words like 'story' or 'account' are narrower; depiction captures the full scope of representation.

Your Turn

The recipe's depiction of ingredients was hard to follow.

Is depiction the right word here, or should it be something different?

depiction

Not quite — A recipe explains its ingredients in words and lists, but 'depiction' suggests a visual or artistic representation, like a painting or drawing. That is the wrong fit for text-based instructions.

description

Correct — The recipe names and explains its ingredients in language, which is exactly what a description or listing does. No picture is involved, so 'description' fits.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
description

A detailed account in words. Description is linguistic only; depiction can be visual, textual, or both.

illustration

A picture or visual representation. Illustration is visual only; depiction can include written explanation alongside images.

portrayal

A showing of someone or something in a particular way or light. Portrayal emphasizes the angle or interpretation; depiction is more neutral and factual.

Antonyms · opposites

omission — Something left out or not shown — the opposite of a full depiction.

Build the Word

pingere Latin: 'to paint'

The root 'ping' means 'to paint' — a depiction literally 'paints' a picture of something, whether with words, images, or both.

The artist mixed different pigments to create the perfect shade of deep blue.

Same root, new word — what does pigment mean here?

a rough sketch or outline drawn first

Not quite — This misses the root. 'Pigment' comes from pingere, 'to paint,' so it names the coloring matter in paint, not a preliminary sketch.

colored powder used in paint

Correct — Correct. 'Pigment' comes from the Latin pingere, 'to paint,' so it names the colored powder that gives paint its color.

The root connection →

Depiction and pigment both come from the Latin pingere, 'to paint.' A depiction paints a picture of something, and pigment is what paint is colored with.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Use depiction when you want to emphasize the full act of showing or representing something — especially when both visual and written elements matter. For a purely written account, 'description' is simpler. For just pictures, say 'illustration.' Depiction is the broader, more artistic choice.
