Obsolete

/AHB-suh-leet/ — stress on first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is obsolete is no longer used because it has been replaced by something newer or better.

Show it in context

Fax machines are nearly obsolete, kept alive only by a few offices.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when obsolete is the exact word.

Worked Example

Eight-track tapes were made obsolete by cassettes, then CDs, then digital downloads—each technology rendering the previous one unnecessary and unusable.

Obsolete is vastly more precise than "old." A classic car is old but not obsolete; it still works and people choose to drive it. A fax machine is obsolete—it has been replaced by something better, and it's no longer useful for its original purpose. Obsolete means not just aged, but replaced and abandoned.

Your Turn

My grandmother's coat is forty years old but still in perfect condition and she wears it regularly.

Is the coat obsolete?

old

Correct — The coat has simply existed for forty years while staying useful, and old describes that long existence without implying it has been replaced.

obsolete

Not quite — The coat still works perfectly and is worn regularly; obsolete means something has been replaced by something better and is no longer useful, which has not happened to this coat.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
outdated

Out of fashion or no longer current. Outdated means no longer trendy; obsolete means actively replaced and no longer functional.

abandoned

Left behind or given up. Abandoned doesn't imply replacement by something better; obsolete does.

archaic

From an earlier time period, often used for language or customs. Archaic emphasizes historical distance; obsolete emphasizes functional replacement.

Antonyms · opposites

current — In use now, up to date—the opposite of something that has been replaced and is disused.

How Strong Is It?

Build the Word

obsoletus Latin: "worn out, wasted away"

From the image of something gradually worn away and worn out of usefulness—obsolete means time and replacement have rendered something useless.

The root connection →

Obsolete and insolent both trace to Latin solere/solitus (to be accustomed). Obsolete (ob- plus solere) is what has gone out of customary use, and insolent (in- plus solere) is someone not behaving with customary respect.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / technical

Obsolete is precise for technologies and products that have been replaced by something better. It's stronger than 'old' or 'outdated' because it implies the thing is no longer useful or functional. Use obsolete for technologies, products, or practices that have been superseded; use 'old-fashioned' for things merely out of style.
