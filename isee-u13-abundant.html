Abundant

/uh-BUN-dent/ — rhymes with redundant · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is abundant exists in a large quantity, well beyond what is needed.

Show it in context

Berries were so abundant that summer that every family made jam.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when abundant is the exact word.

Worked Example

The harvest that year was so abundant that farmers filled every barn and still had crops piling up outside.

Abundant captures exactly what happened: not just a large quantity, but a quantity that exceeds what is needed, which is the core meaning. It is more precise than "large" or "plentiful" because it emphasizes the excess beyond necessity.

Your Turn

The store had five types of cereal on the shelf.

Is abundant the right word for the cereal selection?

Yes

Not quite — Five kinds of cereal is only a modest range of choices. Abundant points to a large quantity well beyond what is needed, not simply a handful of options.

No

Correct — Having a variety of five types is modest, not abundant. Abundant means a large quantity well beyond what is needed, not merely a range of options.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
plentiful

Means plenty of something; similar to abundant but less emphasis on exceeding need.

ample

Means enough and more to spare; less focused on raw quantity and more on sufficiency than abundant.

profuse

Means produced or growing in great quantities; overlaps with abundant but emphasizes unstoppable production or overflow.

Antonyms · opposites

scarce — Rare or in short supply; the direct opposite of abundant.

Build the Word

undare Latin: 'to flow, wave'

Undare is about water flowing and waving; when something is abundant, it flows toward you in waves of plenty.

The ocean waves undulated gently against the shore.

Same root, new word — what does undulate mean here?

to move up and down or back and forth in smooth, wavelike motions

Correct — Both abundant and undulate grow from the Latin undare, 'to flow or wave.' Abundant plenty flows toward you in waves, and to undulate is to move in that same wavelike, rolling way.

to crash violently and break apart

Not quite — Ocean waves can crash, which makes this tempting, but undulate comes from undare ('to flow, wave') and names the gentle rolling motion of a wave, not a violent break.

to dry up and disappear

Not quite — This is the opposite of flowing water. The root undare means 'to flow, wave,' so undulate is about wavelike movement, not drying up.

The root connection →

Undare pictures water rising and falling in waves. Something abundant overflows like those waves of plenty, while to undulate is literally to ripple and roll like the water itself.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Abundant is the word when you emphasize not just large quantity but quantity exceeding expectation or need. For everyday 'plenty,' simpler words like plentiful work just as well.
