What It Means

To cater to someone is to shape what you offer or do around their particular needs or preferences.

Show it in context

The menu caters to every diner, with vegan, spicy, and kids' options.

Here's how to decide when cater is the exact word.

Worked Example

The hotel concierge catered to each guest's preferences by remembering their favorite room temperature, preferred pillow types, and breakfast orders from their previous stays.

Cater is precise here because the concierge isn't just providing service�she's actively shaping what she offers around each guest's specific needs and preferences. The whole service is adjusted to fit individual tastes. That's exactly what cater means.

Your Turn

A restaurant served pasta to all customers, regardless of dietary preferences.

Is cater the precise word here, or should it say something else?

serve

Correct — The restaurant simply gave the same pasta to everyone, and 'serve' names providing food without tailoring it to anyone.

cater

Not quite — Cater means adjusting what you offer to fit particular tastes or needs, but this kitchen ignored dietary preferences entirely, so nothing was tailored.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
provide

To give or supply something. Cater is more specific: you don't just provide something; you shape it around what someone needs.

accommodate

To adjust for someone's specific needs. Very close to cater, but often means just fitting someone in rather than redesigning your whole offering.

indulge

To gratify a desire or whim. Cater is broader: you cater to needs and preferences, not just whims or luxuries.

Antonyms · opposites

standardize — To make the same for everyone; the opposite of adjusting offerings to individual needs.

Build the Word

cater Middle English / Old French: 'cateour' (one who buys provisions)

Catering originally meant providing and preparing food for events; over time it came to mean adjusting any offering to fit someone's specific tastes and needs.

Same root, new word — what does capture mean here?

to invent or create something from scratch

Not quite — A photographer does make an image, but 'capture' means seizing a moment that is already happening, not inventing one out of nothing.

to guard or protect from harm

Not quite — Waiting patiently might suggest careful protecting, yet the sentence is about seizing the instant the wave crashes, not shielding it.

to catch or take hold of something, here to record a fleeting moment

Correct — Capture comes from Latin 'capere/captare,' to seize or take hold of, the same root behind cater; a caterer was first someone who went out to seize and buy provisions, and to capture a moment is to seize it.

The root connection →

Note: this card's transfer word was 'curate,' but that is a false friend. Curate comes from Latin 'cura' (care), a different root that only resembles cater in theme. Cater actually traces to 'captare/capere' (to seize, catch), which genuinely gives us capture, so capture is the honest cognate to bridge to here.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Cater means to actively shape what you offer or do around someone's particular needs or preferences. Use it when you're adjusting your entire approach or menu, not just saying yes to a request. 'Serve' means providing; 'cater' means adjusting.
