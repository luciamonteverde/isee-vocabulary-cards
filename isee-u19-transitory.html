Transitory

/TRAN-suh-tor-ee/ — rhymes with 'memory' but slower · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Transitory describes something that lasts only a short time before passing away.

Show it in context

Fame from a viral video is usually transitory, gone within a month.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when transitory is the exact word.

Worked Example

The snow from the winter storm fell thick and white across the fields, but the March sun melted it all away within a week—a transitory blanket of cold.

Transitory captures more than just 'temporary'—it emphasizes the inevitability of fading. The snow didn't just disappear; it naturally and gradually passed away. Saying 'temporary snow' would miss that sense of natural, irreversible decline.

Your Turn

The worker felt embarrassed after making a mistake at the meeting, but forgot about it completely by lunchtime.

Is transitory the right word for the embarrassment?

Yes

Correct — Correct. His embarrassment arrived and then faded on its own by lunchtime, so it passed through him without lasting. That brief, passing quality is exactly what transitory names.

No

Not quite — This misses it. The feeling did not stay, and transitory is the precise word for an emotion that lasts only a short while before fading.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
temporary

Can mean any duration (a temporary job lasts years). Transitory always means fading away naturally.

fleeting

Fast-passing, but doesn't capture the sense of something naturally losing power or importance.

ephemeral

Delicate and short-lived, but sounds poetic. Transitory is more matter-of-fact about natural decline.

Antonyms · opposites

permanent — Meant to last forever—the opposite of transitory's natural fade.

How Strong Is It?
momentary→temporary→transitory→ephemeral

Transitory — something that naturally fades away over time, more inevitable than 'temporary' but less poetic than 'ephemeral.' (our word)

Build the Word

transire Latin: 'to cross over, pass through'

Transitory things are crossings—temporary passages that move through and then are gone.

The caravan crossed the desert in three days, transitioning from one oasis to the next.

Same root, new word — what does transition mean here?

the act of moving or passing from one state or place to another

Correct — Transitory and transition both come from Latin transire, 'to cross over, pass through.' A transition is a passing across, and something transitory is here only in passing.

stopping and settling permanently in one spot

Not quite — Tempting if you picture the caravan resting at an oasis. But transition is about moving from one point to the next, not staying put.

carrying heavy supplies across long distances

Not quite — Tempting, since a caravan hauls goods across the desert. But transition names the passing from one oasis to another, not the cargo being carried.

The root connection →

The root transire, 'to pass through,' ties them together: a transition is the crossing from one state to the next, while a transitory feeling merely passes through and is soon gone.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Transitory fits when something is clearly fading or will naturally disappear—interest, moods, trends, effects. For something brief but deliberately kept short (like a temporary visit or job), 'temporary' is clearer. For something impossibly delicate and poetic, reach for 'ephemeral' instead.
