Detrimental

/det-ruh-MEN-tul/ — stress on MEN · adjective

REVIEW (Harm, Illness & Destruction)

Hear It
What It Means

Detrimental describes something that causes harm or damage, especially over time.

Show it in context

Leaving the bike out in the rain all winter was detrimental to its gears.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when detrimental is the exact word.

Worked Example

The coach warned that skipping practice would be detrimental to their performance in the tournament.

Detriment (harm, damage) + -al = characteristic of harm. Skipping practice causes harm to their athletic development and readiness. The base word 'detriment' and adjective 'detrimental' travel together: something works to a team's detriment (noun) / something is detrimental to a team (adjective).

Your Turn

Adding preservatives to the food was found to be detrimental during the study.

Is detrimental precise here, or could 'unnecessary' or 'artificial' work better?

detrimental

Not quite — 'Detrimental' specifically means something causes harm, and the study result described here never establishes that the preservatives damage health or quality.

unnecessary

Correct — The sentence shows only that preservatives were added, not that they caused harm, so a word like 'unnecessary' or 'artificial' is more precise than one requiring actual damage.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
harmful

Causes damage or injury. Harmful is simpler and more direct; detrimental adds emphasis on lasting or gradual damage over time.

injurious

Causing harm or injury, especially in a medical or legal sense. More formal and technical than detrimental.

adverse

Opposed to one's interests or unfavorable. Broader term; adverse can mean simply opposed, while detrimental specifically means causally harmful.

Antonyms · opposites

beneficial — Produces good effects or advantages. The opposite of causing detriment is producing benefit.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

detriment + -al

detriment (harm, damage, loss) + -al (characteristic of) = characteristic of harm. If a plan works 'to your detriment,' it is detrimental — base and adjective travel together.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Use detrimental to describe something that causes lasting harm or damage, especially gradually or cumulatively. The key is that it's not just bad — it actively works against you. 'This habit is detrimental to your health' means the habit actively damages your health, not just that it's unhelpful.
