Vertebrate

/VER-tuh-brate/ — rhymes with PERT-uh-brate · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A vertebrate is an animal that has a backbone and an internal skeleton, such as a fish, bird, reptile, or mammal.

Show it in context

A frog is a vertebrate; its tiny backbone shows clearly on the X-ray.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when vertebrate is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the paleontologist carefully brushed away the rock, a delicate backbone of a vertebrate creature from millions of years ago came into view.

Vertebrate is more precise than just 'animal' because it specifies a key biological feature—the internal backbone and skeletal structure—that distinguishes vertebrates from invertebrates.

Your Turn

A butterfly landed on the flower.

Is 'vertebrate' the right word for a butterfly?

invertebrate

Correct — The butterfly on the flower has no backbone, and invertebrate is exactly the word for an animal that lacks one, so it matches this scenario.

vertebrate

Not quite — A butterfly is an insect, and insects have no backbone or internal skeleton, so calling the creature that landed on the flower a vertebrate does not fit.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
animal

Any living creature. Vertebrate narrows it: only animals with backbones.

organism

Any living thing, including plants and fungi. Vertebrate specifies a biological classification of backbone-bearing creatures.

creature

A being or living thing; too general to capture the skeletal distinction vertebrate describes.

Antonyms · opposites

invertebrate — An animal lacking a backbone—the opposite classification.

Build the Word

vertere Latin: 'to turn'

Vertebra comes from 'vertere' (to turn) because each vertebra is a joint allowing your spine to turn and bend.

Same root, new word — what does invert mean here?

to turn upside down or reverse

Correct — Correct. Invert shares the Latin root vertere, 'to turn,' with vertebrate, so it means to turn something upside down or reverse it.

to fill something to the brim

Not quite — That describes filling, not turning. Invert comes from the Latin root vertere, 'to turn,' so it means to turn the cup upside down, which is why the water spills out.

The root connection →

Vertebrate and invert both come from the Latin vertere, 'to turn.' A vertebrate's spine is built from vertebrae, the small bones it turns and bends on.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

scientific / neutral

Use vertebrate in science, biology, and zoology when classifying animals by their internal skeleton. In casual speech you'd just say 'animal,' but for homework or tests, vertebrate is the precise, academic term.
