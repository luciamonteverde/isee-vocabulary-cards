Vertebrate

/VER-tuh-brate/ — rhymes with PERT-uh-brate noun

Hear It
What It Means

A vertebrate is an animal that has a backbone and an internal skeleton, such as a fish, bird, reptile, or mammal.

Show it in context

A frog is a vertebrate; its tiny backbone shows clearly on the X-ray.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

When the paleontologist carefully brushed away the rock, a delicate backbone of a vertebrate creature from millions of years ago came into view.

Vertebrate is more precise than just 'animal' because it specifies a key biological feature—the internal backbone and skeletal structure—that distinguishes vertebrates from invertebrates.

Rootvertebr-backbone; joint of the spine (from Latin 'vertere', to turn) (Latin)
Suffix-atehaving or characterized by (Latin)

The root 'vertebr-' means backbone and the suffix '-ate' means 'having,' so a vertebrate is literally an animal 'having a backbone' — an internal spine built of linked, turning joints.

Use the root in a new word: invertebrate

A jellyfish is an invertebrate: the prefix 'in-' (not) plus the same 'vertebr-' root shows it has no backbone, the exact reverse of a vertebrate.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

scientific / neutral

Use vertebrate in science, biology, and zoology when classifying animals by their internal skeleton. In casual speech you'd just say 'animal,' but for homework or tests, vertebrate is the precise, academic term.
