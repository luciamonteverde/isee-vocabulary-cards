Lofty

/LOF-tee/ — rhymes with softy · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Lofty describes something that rises to an impressively great height.

Show it in context

From the lofty balcony, the whole valley lay below us.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when lofty is the exact word.

Worked Example

The cathedral's lofty spire pierced the clouds, visible from ten miles away.

Lofty captures both the physical height AND the sense of impressiveness—it's more than just 'tall.' It suggests something grand and majestic. 'Tall' would be flatter, missing the sense of majesty that makes lofty the precise word.

Your Turn

The office building is 40 stories high, made of glass and steel.

Is lofty the right word for this modern building?

Tall

Correct — The building is 40 stories of glass and steel, so tall (or high-rise) captures its height plainly. It reports the fact of the height without adding the nobility or majesty that lofty would imply.

Lofty

Not quite — The glass tower is impressive in scale, but lofty carries a sense of dignity and grandeur. A modern office block is structurally high without that majestic, awe-inspiring quality, so lofty overstates what this building actually feels like.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
high

Raised or topmost. Lofty adds the sense of majesty and inspiring impressiveness.

elevated

Refined, formal, exalted. Lofty combines height with the sense of worthy grandeur and awe.

grand

Impressive in scale and dignity. Lofty specifically conveys rising majestically and inspiring awe.

Antonyms · opposites

humble — Modest and understated—the opposite of lofty's sense of grandeur and majesty.

Build the Word

lopt Old Norse: 'air, sky'

From the Norse word for air and sky—originally meant something high enough to be in the air. Later used for rooms high up in buildings (attics), then any impressively tall or majestic thing.

The loft above the barn was a perfect place to store grain.

Same root, new word — what does loft mean here?

an upper room or upper floor of a building, especially an attic

Correct — Correct. Loft comes from the Old Norse root lopt, meaning air or sky, which is why it names the high, upper part of a building like a barn's grain store.

an underground room used for storing food and drink

Not quite — That describes a cellar, which sits below ground. The lopt root points upward toward air and sky, so a loft is the high, upper space of a building, not a buried one.

The root connection →

Both lofty and loft trace back to lopt (Old Norse for air or sky). That shared root is why both words point upward, one toward grand height and the other toward an upper floor.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Lofty is the word for impressive, majestic height—think cathedrals, mountains, noble ideas, and inspiring ambitions. For ordinary tallness (a skyscraper, a fence), 'tall' is better and simpler.
