Mutation

/myoo-TAY-shun/ — stress on TAY · noun

REVIEW (Change, Instability & Decline)

Hear It
What It Means

A mutation is a change in an organism's genetic material that can produce a new or different trait.

Show it in context

A single mutation gave the moths their smoky-gray wings.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when mutation is the exact word.

Worked Example

The virus picked up a new mutation that made it spread faster through the population.

Mutat(e) + ion: the verb mutate (Latin mutare = 'to change') drops its -e and takes -ion, the suffix for 'the act or result of.' So mutation is the act or result of changing—specifically, a change in genetic material. The same mutat- root appears in mutual (two-way change) and commute (change one's location).

Your Turn

The baker adjusted the recipe slightly, using honey instead of sugar.

Is 'mutation' the right word for this change, or should it be 'modification'?

Modification

Correct — Swapping honey for sugar is a deliberate tweak to a recipe, exactly what 'modification' names. The -ion suffix turns 'modify' into a noun for an intentional adjustment, which fits the baker's small change perfectly.

Mutation

Not quite — Even though 'mutation' also builds on a root meaning 'to change' (Latin mutare), the -ion noun it forms is reserved for changes to an organism's genetic material at the DNA level. A baker adjusting ingredients isn't altering any genes, so this word overshoots the situation.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
variation

A broader term for any difference. Mutation is narrower—it's a change in DNA that produces a new trait and can be inherited.

alteration

A change to something. Mutation is more precise—it refers to spontaneous genetic changes, not external edits or deliberate modifications.

transformation

A dramatic or complete change. Mutation is more technical—it's a specific genetic change that creates a new trait, not a general overhaul.

Antonyms · opposites

constancy — The state of remaining unchanged. Mutation is change; constancy is the absence of it.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

mutat(e) + ion

Latin mutare = 'to change.' The verb mutate ends in -ate, so it drops its e and takes -ion: mutation, the act or result of changing. Same root family: mutual, commute (change one thing for another).

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

scientific/neutral

Use mutation when discussing biological or genetic changes that produce new traits. Outside science, 'change' or 'alteration' is clearer. Mutation carries specificity about heredity and DNA—not just any modification.
