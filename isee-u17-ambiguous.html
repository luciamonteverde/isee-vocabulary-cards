Ambiguous

/am-BIG-yoo-us/ — stress on second syllable, like big-YOU-us · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is ambiguous can be understood in more than one way, so its exact meaning stays unclear.

Show it in context

The note said only 'meet at the bank' — ambiguous, since the river and the ATM were both nearby.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when ambiguous is the exact word.

Worked Example

The coach said 'You should work on your game' — ambiguous, since it could mean 'You're strong but need to push harder' or 'You're underperforming.'

Ambiguous is more precise than 'unclear' because it captures not just difficulty understanding, but the existence of multiple valid interpretations. The coach's words could legitimately mean two opposite things, and that's what ambiguous names.

Your Turn

The teacher's feedback said only 'Good work, but room to improve.'

Is 'ambiguous' the best word to describe this feedback?

vague

Correct — The feedback is simply imprecise and unspecific, which is exactly what 'vague' names. It does not split into two clashing readings.

ambiguous

Not quite — Ambiguous would fit only if 'room to improve' could reasonably mean both 'nearly perfect' and 'seriously flawed,' leaving you stuck between two conflicting meanings. Here it is just unspecific, not double-meaning.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
unclear

Difficult to understand. Unlike ambiguous, unclear doesn't necessarily mean multiple interpretations exist—just that something is hard to grasp.

vague

Lacking detail or precision. Vague means incomplete information; ambiguous means two or more readings are equally valid.

equivocal

Having two possible meanings; intentionally evasive. Like ambiguous but more formal and often implying deliberate avoidance.

Antonyms · opposites

explicit — Clearly and directly expressed, leaving no room for multiple meanings. Where ambiguous allows many readings, explicit rules them out entirely.

How Strong Is It?
vague→ambiguous→equivocal→contradictory

Ambiguous — more specific than vague (mere imprecision), yet lighter than contradictory (conflicting meanings). (our word)

Build the Word

ambiguus Latin 'ambiguus' = from ambi-, 'both,' + agere, 'to drive'—meaning 'driven both ways'

The ambi- in ambiguous means 'both,' so the word literally describes something that pulls you toward multiple meanings at once, leaving you driven both ways.

Though she was ambidextrous, she preferred to write with her right hand.

Same root, new word — what does ambidextrous mean here?

able to use both hands equally well

Correct — Ambiguous and ambidextrous both open with the Latin prefix ambi-, 'both.' Ambiguous means driven toward both meanings at once; ambidextrous means skilled with both hands (ambi- plus dexter, 'right hand').

unable to decide which hand to use

Not quite — Tempting because ambiguous is about being pulled two ways, but ambidextrous describes real skill in both hands, not indecision.

left-handed

Not quite — Tempting because the word is about hands, but ambidextrous means skilled with both hands, not favoring one side.

The root connection →

The link here is the prefix, not the whole word: ambiguous joins ambi- to agere ('to drive'), while ambidextrous joins that same ambi- to dexter ('right hand'). Spotting ambi- tells you 'both' is in play, even though the two words go their separate ways after that.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Ambiguous is precise when something can reasonably be understood in two or more ways; use it when multiple interpretations are actually possible. If something is just unclear or vague, the simpler words are better. Save ambiguous for genuine cases—as in 'The sign was ambiguous, since it could mean both the store's name and its location.'
