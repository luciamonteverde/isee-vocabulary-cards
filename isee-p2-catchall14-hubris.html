Hubris

HYOO-bris -- from ancient Greek hybris, 'excessive pride, insolence toward the gods'; the word's ultimate origin is still debated by scholars. noun

Hear It
What It Means

Hubris is exaggerated pride or self-confidence.

Show it in context

The captain's hubris convinced him the ship could outrun the storm, and he refused every warning to turn back.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Certain that his restaurant's reputation alone would keep customers coming no matter what, the owner stopped checking reviews, cut back on quality ingredients, and was shocked when the dining room emptied out within a year.

Hubris is the kind of excessive self-confidence that blinds someone to real risk -- the owner's certainty that nothing could touch his reputation is exactly what let the restaurant's quality quietly collapse.

Hubris comes from ancient Greek hybris, describing a level of pride so extreme it was considered an insult to the gods themselves; its ultimate origin before that is still debated, and it survives in English today as a single, unbroken borrowed word with no common English relatives.

Use the root in a new word: nemesis

In Greek tragedy, a hero's hubris often summons a nemesis -- the very downfall he never saw coming.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Hubris carries a strong warning tone, especially in stories, where it often comes right before a dramatic downfall.
