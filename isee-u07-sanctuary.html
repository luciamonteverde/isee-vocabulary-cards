Sanctuary

/SANG-chu-wair-ee/ — rhymes with January · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A sanctuary is a place that offers safety, peace, and protection from danger or from the outside world.

Show it in context

The attic room became her sanctuary, a place to draw and think in peace.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when sanctuary is the exact word.

Worked Example

After weeks of fighting with her siblings, Maya found sanctuary in the library after school, a quiet corner where she could lose herself in books and sketches.

Sanctuary is more precise here than escape or hideaway because it captures both the safety from conflict AND the peaceful, restorative quality of the space—not just a place to run to, but a place of genuine peace.

Your Turn

Tom went to the gym to get away from his noisy roommate.

Is sanctuary the right word for where Tom went?

Yes

Not quite — Sanctuary is too strong here. A gym is somewhere to get away from his roommate, but it isn't necessarily peaceful or quiet in itself.

No

Correct — Escape or refuge fits better. The gym lets Tom get away from his roommate, but sanctuary implies a peaceful, protected place, which a gym isn't.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
refuge

A safe place from danger or harm; emphasizes safety but not necessarily the peaceful quality that sanctuary carries.

haven

A peaceful, sheltered place—similar to sanctuary but without the formal, almost sacred tone.

retreat

A quiet place to withdraw from stress or the world; focuses on the withdrawal rather than sanctuary's protective separation from external demands.

Antonyms · opposites

chaos — Disorder and turmoil—the exact opposite of the peace and safety that sanctuary provides.

Build the Word

sanctus Latin: sacred, holy

Originally, sanctuary meant a sacred, protected space in a church or temple—a holy place set apart from the secular world. This sense of a special, protected place grew to mean any safe refuge that offers peace.

The team treated the club's founding charter as sacrosanct, refusing to change even a single word of it no matter who complained.

Same root, new word — what does sacrosanct mean here?

too sacred or important to be changed or criticized

Correct — The root 'sanctus' means 'sacred' or 'holy,' the same root that makes a sanctuary a set-apart, protected place. Sacrosanct carries that 'sacred' directly: the charter is treated as too holy to change, which is why not a single word may be altered.

kept hidden away from the general public

Not quite — Hidden away is about secrecy, not about being treated as too sacred to alter. The root 'sanctus' means 'sacred or holy,' so the answer should be about something held sacred, not something concealed.

extremely old and no longer useful

Not quite — Being outdated is different from being protected and untouchable; the charter is guarded, not obsolete. The root 'sanctus' means 'sacred or holy,' pointing toward something treated as sacred rather than something worn out.

The root connection →

Sanctuary comes from Latin 'sanctus,' meaning 'sacred or holy,' the sense of a set-apart, protected place. Sacrosanct joins that same 'sanctus' with 'sacro-': something so sacred it cannot be changed or criticized.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Sanctuary names a place that feels both safe and peaceful—a refuge specifically from the stress of the outside world, not just from physical danger. Reach for it when the space offers both protection and serenity. For a simple safe location, 'refuge' or 'hideaway' is simpler and just as clear.
