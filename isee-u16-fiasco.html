Fiasco

/fee-AH-sko/ — rhymes with tobacco · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A fiasco is a complete and often embarrassing failure.

Show it in context

The outdoor wedding became a fiasco when the tent collapsed into the cake.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when fiasco is the exact word.

Worked Example

The school's first robotics competition turned into a fiasco when half the teams' robots malfunctioned, the scoreboard broke, and the award ceremony was cancelled.

Fiasco suggests not just any failure, but a total, spectacular, and often humiliating collapse. Words like 'failure' or 'mistake' don't capture the full scale and embarrassment baked into fiasco.

Your Turn

During a family dinner, I overcooked the pasta by two minutes.

Is fiasco the right word here?

mishap

Correct — Overcooking pasta by two minutes is a small, fixable slip, which is exactly what a 'mishap' is.

fiasco

Not quite — A fiasco is a complete, humiliating, catastrophic failure, and a slightly overcooked dinner does not rise to that level.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
failure

Inability to succeed or accomplish something, but fiasco emphasizes the complete breakdown and public embarrassment.

disaster

A sudden, catastrophic event, but can result from accidents or nature, whereas fiasco is specifically about human missteps.

debacle

A sudden, humiliating collapse of an event or plan, very similar to fiasco but with slightly less emphasis on the comic or embarrassing aspect.

Antonyms · opposites

success — Achieving goals, going as planned—the opposite of fiasco's complete, spectacular collapse.

Build the Word

fiasco Italian: literally a flask or bottle, figuratively a failure

In Italian theater tradition, fiasco (literally 'flask') came to mean a catastrophic, humiliating performance failure—the exact origin of the metaphor is debated.

He dropped the glass flask on the stone floor, and it shattered into a thousand pieces.

Same root, new word — what does flask mean here?

a heavy stone tool

Not quite — The 'stone floor' might pull your eye toward stone, but the flask is the glass object that broke, not a tool made of stone.

a loud crashing sound

Not quite — The shattering makes a crash, but the flask is the thing that shattered, not the noise it made.

a small bottle or container for liquids

Correct — Fiasco and flask both trace to the Late Latin flasco, 'bottle.' In Italian, fiasco literally means 'flask,' and it came to mean a total failure, though exactly how that leap happened is debated.

The root connection →

Behind both words sits the Late Latin flasco, a bottle. English kept the plain object in flask, while Italian turned the same bottle into a metaphor for a spectacular flop. The figurative jump is disputed, but the shared bottle root is solid.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Fiasco names a complete, spectacular failure, often with an embarrassing or public element. For a smaller setback or simple unsuccessful attempt, choose 'failure' or 'mishap' instead.
