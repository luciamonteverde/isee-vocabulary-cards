Quota

/KWO-tuh/ — rhymes with iota · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A quota is a fixed share or amount of something that a person or group is allowed to have or required to meet.

Show it in context

Each volunteer had a quota of fifty calls to make before lunch.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when quota is the exact word.

Worked Example

The factory manager announced that each production line had a quota of one thousand units per day, and any line falling short would face penalties.

The emphasis is on a specific, fixed amount that must be achieved — that exact, mandatory quality is what quota names. Because the scenario hinges on the exact number and what happens if you miss it, quota is more precise than the plain word 'target.'

Your Turn

Sarah set a personal goal to read five books this summer.

Is quota the precise word here, or something else?

Goal

Correct — Reading five books this summer is a target Sarah picked for herself, which is exactly what a goal is. Nothing was assigned to her, as a quota would be.

Quota

Not quite — Sarah set the five-book target freely for herself. A quota is a required amount handed down by someone else, so it misdescribes a goal she chose on her own with no one imposing it.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
requirement

Any obligation a person must meet. Quota is a specific, fixed amount you must meet or achieve.

goal

Something to aim for. A goal is optional; a quota is typically required.

allotment

A share of something divided. An allotment might be freely chosen; a quota is often assigned or required.

Antonyms · opposites

freedom — Complete liberty without fixed limits. Quota imposes a specific, required amount.

How Strong Is It?
goal→target→requirement→quota

Quota — the strongest and most formal: a specific, fixed amount you must meet or achieve. (our word)

Build the Word

quota Latin: 'how many'

Quota comes from Latin meaning 'how many,' the same root as quotient — a fixed amount calculated by dividing something into parts.

The quotient of twelve divided by three is four.

Same root, new word — what does quotient mean here?

the result of dividing one number by another

Correct — Correct. Quotient shares the quot root (Latin for how many), which is why it names the answer to how many times one number divides another, just as a quota is a set how many.

the total you get from adding two numbers together

Not quite — Adding produces a sum, not a quotient. The quot root asks how many, and a quotient answers how many times one number divides into another.

The root connection →

Quota and quotient both come from quot, meaning how many. A quota is an assigned how many, and a quotient is the how many that results from dividing.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Use quota when you need to name a specific, fixed amount that someone is required to achieve or that limits what they can have. In casual conversation about dreams or wishes, the simpler word 'goal' works better. Quota is the word for managed, measured amounts — in business, law, admissions, and systems where fairness requires equal shares.
