Extol

/ɪkˈstoʊl/ — ik-STOLE, rhymes with stole · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To extol something is to praise it highly and enthusiastically.

Show it in context

The coach extolled the team's discipline after their flawless performance.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when extol is the exact word.

Worked Example

At the awards ceremony, the principal took the microphone and spoke glowingly about the debate team's rigorous preparation, their eloquent arguments, and their unwavering commitment to excellence.

Extol captures the enthusiastic, sustained PRAISE and CELEBRATION of the team's qualities. 'Praise' alone is too generic; extol specifically means to celebrate something highly and publicly in a way that highlights its virtues.

Your Turn

The reviewer mentioned that the new restaurant had decent service.

Is extol the right word here?

Yes

Not quite — Picking 'yes' upgrades a passing comment into high praise, but the reviewer only 'mentioned' that service was 'decent,' which is mild approval at best.

No

Correct — 'No' is right: extol means to praise loudly and enthusiastically, and casually noting that service was decent is lukewarm, nowhere near that lifted-up celebration.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
praise

To express approval, but it's more general; extol specifically means to praise HIGHLY and ENTHUSIASTICALLY.

commend

To speak favorably of, but it's more formal and measured; extol is more passionate and celebratory.

acclaim

To receive enthusiastic approval, but it focuses on the reception rather than the act of praising like extol does.

Antonyms · opposites

criticize — To express disapproval — the opposite of the high praise and celebration that extol represents.

Build the Word

extollere Latin: 'to lift up, to raise high' (ex- out/up + tollere to lift, raise)

The image is lifting something up — to extol is to lift something up for everyone to admire and celebrate.

We must tolerate uncertainty in life, even though it makes us uncomfortable.

Same root, new word — what does tolerate mean here?

to completely get rid of a problem

Not quite — This is tempting because we want discomfort gone, but the root sense of bearing or lifting a burden means tolerate is about enduring the difficulty, not eliminating it.

to enjoy something greatly

Not quite — This is tempting because it sounds positive, but tolerate's 'bear, endure' root means putting up with something unpleasant, not delighting in it.

to bear or endure something difficult

Correct — Correct. Extol lifts something up in praise from tollere ('to lift, raise'), and tolerate comes from the related tolerare, 'to bear,' the effort of holding up under a weight.

The root connection →

Extol and tolerate reach back to the same Latin root family for lifting and bearing (tollere and tolerare): extol lifts praise high, while to tolerate is to bear a burden up under strain. They share a root ancestor for carrying, even though praise and endurance feel far apart.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Extol is the word for enthusiastic, effusive praise — when you want to celebrate someone's or something's excellence publicly. For mild approval, use 'praise' or 'approve.' For an official commendation, 'commend' fits better.
