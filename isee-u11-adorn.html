Adorn

/uh-DORN/ — rhymes with corn · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To adorn something is to decorate it in order to make it more attractive.

Show it in context

Villagers adorned the square with lanterns for the festival.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when adorn is the exact word.

Worked Example

Villagers string lanterns from house to house, hang garlands around the gazebo, and place flower arrangements on every street corner to beautify the square for the festival.

Adorn captures not just 'to decorate' but 'to decorate specifically in order to make something more beautiful'—that purposeful enhancement of beauty is what makes adorn more precise than the plain word 'decorate.'

Your Turn

A bag of trash accidentally blows over in the wind and scatters throughout a park.

Would you say the trash adorns the park?

Yes

Not quite — Adorn requires purposeful decoration meant to enhance beauty, and windblown trash is accidental and unattractive, not adornment.

No

Correct — Scattered trash is unintended and ugly, so it cannot adorn the park; adorning means deliberately adding ornaments to make something more beautiful.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
decorate

The plain word for adding decoration; adorn is more formal and specifically emphasizes making something beautiful.

embellish

To add details or enhancements; embellish often means adding details to make something richer, not necessarily for beauty.

garnish

To add trimmings or small decorative touches; garnish is narrower and often used for food, while adorn applies more broadly.

Antonyms · opposites

strip — To remove decorations or coverings—the opposite action and intention of adorn.

How Strong Is It?
bare→decorated→adorned→ornate

Adorn represents the intentional beautification: from bare to decorated to adorned (intentionally beautified) to ornate. (our word)

Build the Word

ad- + ornare Latin: 'to + furnish or equip'

The root 'ornare' means to furnish or ornament, so adorn literally means 'to add ornaments to' something in order to enhance it.

She hung a crystal ornament on the tree as part of the holiday decoration.

Same root, new word — what does ornament mean here?

a warning label or identifying tag

Not quite — Small things hung on items can be tags, which makes this tempting, but the ornare root means to furnish or decorate, not to warn or label.

a tool used for trimming branches

Not quite — The tree in the sentence makes a trimming tool sound plausible, but ornare points to ornamenting and decorating, not cutting.

a decorative object placed on something to beautify it

Correct — Adorn and ornament both come from Latin ornare, 'to furnish or equip.' Adorn (ad- plus ornare) means to add ornaments to something, and an ornament is one of those decorative pieces you add.

The root connection →

The shared root is ornare, 'to furnish or decorate.' To adorn is literally to put ornaments onto something, so the taught word and its transfer word name the same act of decorating from opposite ends: the verb and the object.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Use adorn for formal, intentional decoration meant to enhance beauty or for aesthetic effect. The simpler 'decorate' works fine for everyday contexts.
