Corrosive

/kuh-ROH-siv/ — stress on ROH · adjective

PREVIEW — taught in depth later in Harm, Illness & Destruction

Hear It
What It Means

Something corrosive slowly eats away at or destroys whatever it touches, whether that is metal, tissue, or something more abstract like trust.

Show it in context

Road salt is corrosive, which is why winter cars rust from the bottom up.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when corrosive is the exact word.

Worked Example

The constant criticism had a corrosive effect on his confidence; by semester's end, he barely believed in his own abilities.

Corrode (eat away) + -ive (having the quality of): the criticism has the quality of gradually eating away at his confidence. More precise than 'damaging' because corrosive emphasizes the slow, gradual wearing-away rather than sudden harm.

Your Turn

The encouraging feedback was corrosive to her self-doubt.

Does corrosive correctly describe how the feedback affects her self-doubt?

Yes

Not quite — It is tempting as a strong word paired with self-doubt, but 'corrosive' means eating away and destroying, and encouraging feedback does the opposite.

No

Correct — Encouraging feedback builds her up rather than eroding anything, so 'healing' or 'restorative' fits, not corrosive.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
destructive

Causing damage or ruin. Destructive is broader and can happen suddenly, while corrosive emphasizes gradual eating-away over time.

caustic

Biting, sarcastic, or chemically burning. Caustic emphasizes harsh and sharp qualities, while corrosive emphasizes the slow erosion process.

erosive

Wearing away over time. Erosive and corrosive are similar, but erosive often applies to natural gradual wearing, while corrosive works for both literal chemicals and metaphorical damage.

Antonyms · opposites

constructive — Building up or improving rather than tearing down. The opposite quality — creating or repairing rather than gradually eating away and destroying.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

corros (corrode) + -ive

corrode → corros- + -ive. The -de ending swaps to -s-: having the quality of eating away gradually — at metal, at tissue, or at something abstract like trust.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Reach for corrosive when something gradually destroys or wears away, whether it's a physical substance (like acid on metal) or something abstract (like mistrust in a relationship). The key is the slow, incremental damage.
