Temper

/TEM-per/ — rhymes with remember noun

Hear It
What It Means

A person's temper is their tendency to become angry or lose control of their emotions quickly.

Show it in context

He lost his temper when the referee made an unfair call.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

A coach is known for shouting at players, slamming clipboards, and nearly getting ejected from games whenever calls go against his team.

Temper captures not just the coach's anger but his characteristic quickness to lose emotional control. We're seeing his tendency to explode — that's his nature. Temper means this is who he is, not just a one-time angry outburst. That's more precise than simply saying 'his anger.'

Roottemperto mix in due proportion; to moderate, restrain (Latin (temperare, borrowed into Old English as temprian))

The whole word is the Latin root temperare, 'to mix in the right proportion; to moderate or restrain.' Your temper was originally your inner 'mixture' of moods kept in balance — so when that balance tips and anger takes over, we say you lose your temper.

Use the root in a new word: temperate

The temperate climate stayed mild and moderate all year, never swinging to extremes of heat or cold.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / everyday

Temper describes a characteristic tendency to lose emotional control, while anger describes the feeling itself. When someone 'has a bad temper,' you're describing their nature; when they 'feel anger,' you're describing a momentary emotion. Use temper when talking about a person's emotional pattern or how they typically respond when provoked.
