Listless

/LIST-lis/ — rhymes with this is · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Listless describes a lack of energy or enthusiasm, as if someone is too tired or bored to care about anything.

Show it in context

Jet-lagged and listless, she stared at the menu without reading it.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when listless is the exact word.

Worked Example

During the family vacation, the teenager was listless—refusing to leave his room, showing no interest in the beach or nearby attractions, and responding to questions with empty stares.

Listless nails the specific combination here: lack of energy combined with lack of interest. It's not just 'tired'—it's tired AND apathetic, AND nothing can stir enthusiasm. The plain word 'bored' would miss this deeper disengagement from both feeling and doing.

Your Turn

The student yawned through most of the movie.

Should we say the student was listless or just tired?

Tired

Correct — Yawning through a movie points to plain sleepiness, and tired captures that without claiming the student was also bored or uninterested.

Listless

Not quite — Listless means having no energy and no interest, but a single yawn only shows sleepiness; we cannot tell the student had actually lost interest in the movie.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
apathetic

Lacking interest or emotion about something. Apathetic is purely about not caring; listless adds physical tiredness to the emotional disengagement.

indifferent

Not caring one way or the other. Indifferent is about the attitude alone; listless combines that with exhaustion.

unmotivated

Lacking drive or desire to act. Unmotivated focuses on the lack of drive; listless includes both energy loss and emotional withdrawal.

Antonyms · opposites

enthusiastic — Eager and excited about something. The opposite of listless's tired disengagement.

How Strong Is It?
unmotivated→indifferent→apathetic→listless

Listless — the strongest: lacking both energy and interest, combining physical exhaustion with emotional disengagement. (our word)

Build the Word

list Old English: 'to listen; to pay attention'

The root 'list' means 'to listen' or 'to pay attention,' and the suffix '-less' means 'without'—so listless literally means 'without attention or desire.'

Lusty for adventure, she hiked a brand-new trail every single weekend.

Same root, new word — what does lusty mean here?

a strong desire or craving for something

Correct — Correct. Lusty and the list buried inside listless both come from Old English lystan, to please or desire, so lusty describes someone full of eager desire.

a feeling of dull boredom or dislike

Not quite — The root list, from Old English lystan, means to desire or please, so lusty describes a powerful, eager wanting, which is the opposite of boredom.

The root connection →

Here is the honest surprise: the list in listless is not the list you write or the word listen. It comes from an old word for desire, the same root as lusty, so listless literally means lacking desire or interest.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Listless is the word for that specific kind of exhaustion where someone has checked out both mentally and physically—too tired and too uninterested to engage. Use it when both energy and enthusiasm are absent. For just boredom or just tiredness, a plainer word is better.
