Witty

/WIT-ee/ — rhymes with pretty · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Witty describes someone who is clever and quick at making amusing remarks.

Show it in context

Her witty acceptance speech had the audience laughing within seconds.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when witty is the exact word.

Worked Example

During the quiz bowl, the team's best player cracked jokes so cleverly timed and unexpectedly funny that judges actually paused to laugh.

This captures more than just being funny—it's being quick, clever, and making amusing remarks all at once. The sharpness and speed of the quips matter as much as the humor. 'Funny' wouldn't capture this precision; witty does.

Your Turn

A child giggles at a silly face their parent makes.

Is witty the right word here, or just silly?

Silly

Correct — The parent is just being playful and goofy to make the child giggle, which is exactly what silly means, without the sharp cleverness witty demands.

Witty

Not quite — A goofy face gets a laugh through pure silliness, but nobody made a quick, clever remark here, so there is no wit to point to in this moment.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
clever

Clever is smart thinking in general; witty is specifically quick, amusing, and clever all at once.

humorous

Humorous just means funny; witty adds the sharpness and quickness of clever remarks.

sharp-witted

Sharp-witted emphasizes the mental quickness; witty emphasizes the amusing remarks that come from that quickness.

Antonyms · opposites

dull — Dull means lacking cleverness and amusement; witty is full of clever, amusing remarks.

Build the Word

wit Old English: 'mind, knowledge, wisdom'

Wit originally meant having a sharp mind and keen intellect; over time it narrowed to mean the clever, quick remarks that come from that sharp mind.

The witness was smart enough to notice the tiny detail.

Same root, new word — what does witness mean here?

a person who sees or knows something

Correct — Correct. Witness shares the Old English root witan, to know, so a witness is someone who knows or has seen something firsthand.

a written record of an official event

Not quite — The root witan means to know, not to write, so witness points to a person who knows what happened, not to a document.

The root connection →

Both wit and witness trace back to witan, to know. A witty person shows a quick, knowing mind, and a witness is someone who knows what happened.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Witty is lighter and faster than 'clever'—it's about quick, amusing remarks rather than deep thinking. Reach for witty when the cleverness shines through jokes or quick comebacks, not just smart problem-solving.
