Proximity

/prok-SIM-uh-tee/ — stress on second syllable · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Proximity is the state of being close to something in distance or position.

Show it in context

The hotel's proximity to the station let us walk to every train.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when proximity is the exact word.

Worked Example

The young lawyer was thrilled to find an apartment in the same block as the courthouse; the proximity to work meant she could walk home in eight minutes during her lunch break.

Proximity is more precise than 'closeness' here because it emphasizes not just the distance itself, but the *advantage* of being near—that being close makes the commute easy and practical. A plain word like 'closeness' would miss that sense of useful benefit.

Your Turn

Two houses sit on opposite ends of a quiet suburban street, maybe a quarter-mile apart.

If someone just wants to describe how far apart they are, is 'proximity' the right word, or should they use something simpler?

Proximity

Not quite — The two houses are just a quarter-mile apart with no practical consequence to being near or far. Proximity hints that closeness matters in some real way, so it overcomplicates a plain fact about spacing.

Distance

Correct — If you only want to say how far apart the houses sit, plain distance reports the gap directly, without suggesting that being near carries any practical weight.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
closeness

General state of being near; lacks proximity's sense of practical advantage or convenience.

nearness

Similar meaning to proximity, but softer and less formal; doesn't convey the consequence of being close.

adjacency

Technical term for being next to something; precise about position but indifferent to practical advantage.

Build the Word

proximus Latin: 'nearest'

Proximus comes from prope, meaning 'near'—the Romans built the sense of *nearness that matters* into this root.

The teacher gave an approximate figure for the class size, close to the real number but not exact.

Same root, new word — what does approximate mean here?

measured with perfect precision down to the last unit

Not quite — Perfect precision is the opposite idea. The prope and proximus root family means near, so approximate means close to the true value, not exactly on it.

nearly but not exactly right; close to the actual value

Correct — Correct. Approximate belongs to the same prope and proximus family (Latin for near), which is why it means close to the true value rather than exact.

The root connection →

Proximity and approximate both come from the Latin root for near (prope, superlative proximus). Proximity is nearness in space, while approximate is nearness to a true value.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Proximity is most useful when being near carries practical significance—a benefit, a convenience, or a logical connection. For a simple statement of spatial distance, plain words like 'distance' or 'being near' usually work better. Reach for proximity when closeness *matters*.
