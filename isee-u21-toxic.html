Toxic

/TAHk-sik/ — rhymes with logic · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Toxic describes something poisonous or harmful to living things.

Show it in context

Keep the toxic cleaning fluids locked away from children and pets.

Here's how to decide when toxic is the exact word.

Worked Example

A teenager opens an old storage shed and finds a pile of unlabeled bottles left by a previous owner. The fumes that leak out make her dizzy and her eyes water. She slams the door shut and runs outside, recognizing at once that whatever is inside that shed is toxic and unsafe to handle.

Toxic is more precise than 'dangerous' here because it specifically names the chemical or biological threat—the harm comes from poison or toxins, not from a general hazard like a sharp edge or slippery surface.

Your Turn

A student stays up too late every night playing video games, and his grades drop as a result.

Is 'toxic' the right word to describe his late-night gaming habit?

toxic

Not quite — His late-night gaming is not a poison or a biological hazard; it is a habit that slowly drags his grades down, and that kind of gradual harm is not what toxic describes.

detrimental

Correct — The habit chips away at his grades over time, which is exactly the slow, building damage the word detrimental captures.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
harmful

Broader term for any kind of damage; toxic specifies chemical or biological harm.

poisonous

Nearly identical meaning; poisonous is more clinical or scientific, toxic more everyday.

noxious

Means foul-smelling or disgusting; toxic adds specificity about poisonous danger.

Antonyms · opposites

benign — Harmless and mild; opposite of toxic's aggressive chemical or biological danger.

Build the Word

toxikon Greek: 'relating to poison' or literally 'relating to arrows' (because poison was used on arrows)

Toxic roots back to Greek words for bow and arrow—poison was smeared on arrowtips, so 'toxikon' came to mean 'poisonous.'

Same root, new word — what does intoxicate mean here?

to poison or stupefy

Correct — Correct. Intoxicate keeps the Greek toxikon (poison) root, so to intoxicate is to poison or stupefy.

to refresh and cleanse the body

Not quite — The toxikon root means poison, so intoxicate cannot mean to refresh; it means to fill someone with a poisoning effect.

The root connection →

Toxic and intoxicate both come from the Greek toxikon, relating to poison. The prefix in- here means to put that poisoning effect into someone.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral to formal

Toxic names poisonous or chemically harmful substances and their effects—a direct, serious word rooted in chemistry. Use it when describing poisons, contaminated air, or water. For general harm that isn't chemical or biological in nature, 'harmful' is simpler and more precise.
