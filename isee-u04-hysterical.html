Hysterical

/hih-STER-uh-kul/ — stress on second syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is hysterical is overwhelmed by extreme emotion, especially fear or distress, and loses control of their reactions.

Show it in context

The toddler grew hysterical when his balloon slipped away into the sky.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when hysterical is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the doctor announces that the emergency surgery was a success, the patient's wife breaks down—laughing and crying at once, unable to form words—completely hysterical with relief and gratitude.

The wife is overwhelmed by emotion so extreme that she's lost control of her reactions; she's laughing and weeping at the same time, unable to compose herself. That total loss of emotional control in the face of overwhelming feeling is what hysterical means. 'Emotional' is far too mild.

Your Turn

A girl is worried about her math test tomorrow and can't sleep.

Is hysterical the right word for her state of mind?

Yes

Not quite — 'Yes' is tempting because she is clearly upset and losing sleep, but hysterical means a total loss of emotional control. Ordinary worry, even strong worry, does not rise to completely falling apart.

No

Correct — She is anxious or nervous, not hysterical. Hysterical is for moments when someone loses all emotional control, and a sleepless night before a test is normal worry, not that.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
emotional

Showing feeling and reacting to emotions. Hysterical is emotional taken to an extreme—complete loss of composure.

panicked

Overwhelmed by fear and losing some control. Hysterical goes further; it's extreme emotion of any kind, not just fear.

frantic

Desperate, rushed, chaotic action. Hysterical is the emotional state; frantic describes movement and urgency.

Antonyms · opposites

composed — Calm and in control—the opposite of the falling-apart that hysterical describes.

How Strong Is It?
upset→panicked→frantic→hysterical

Hysterical—the strongest: complete loss of emotional control under extreme stress or emotion. (our word)

Build the Word

hystera Greek: womb, uterus

Historically, doctors wrongly believed that extreme emotional outbursts came from disturbance in the womb, so the word 'hysterical' was born—a false belief, but the word stuck to describe losing control emotionally.

The surgeon performed a hysterectomy to address the patient's reproductive health.

Same root, new word — what does hysterectomy mean here?

surgical repair of the heart

Not quite — This sounds like a serious operation, which makes it tempting, but the root hystera means 'womb, uterus,' not heart. The procedure concerns the uterus.

an examination of the stomach

Not quite — Many medical terms sound alike, so this seems possible, but hystera ('womb') tells you the operation involves the uterus, not the stomach.

surgical removal of the uterus

Correct — Hysterical and hysterectomy both contain the Greek root hystera, 'womb, uterus.' A hysterectomy literally removes the hystera, while hysterical carries the same root from an old, mistaken belief that the womb caused emotional outbursts.

The root connection →

The root hystera ('womb, uterus') ties them together: a hysterectomy is surgery on the hystera itself, and hysterical inherited the same root from a discredited old theory that blamed the womb for loss of emotional control.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / informal

Hysterical describes someone in the grip of such extreme emotion—fear, grief, joy, relief—that they lose their composure entirely. Don't use it for ordinary worry, sadness, or excitement; save it for moments when someone completely breaks down or loses emotional control.
