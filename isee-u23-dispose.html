Dispose

/dih-SPOHZ/ — rhymes with "rose" · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To dispose of something is to throw it away or get rid of it for good.

Show it in context

Please dispose of the batteries at the recycling center, not in the trash.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when dispose is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the art project ended, the teacher helped students dispose of the toxic paint cans at the hazardous waste facility, ensuring they would never harm the environment.

Dispose means more than just throwing away — it emphasizes responsible, proper removal. The word carries the sense of handling something in a careful, lawful way, which is exactly why the hazardous waste facility is mentioned rather than a regular trash bin.

Your Turn

Sarah threw her old notebook in the recycling bin.

Is dispose the precise word here, or should it just say 'threw'?

dispose

Not quite — Tossing an old notebook into the recycling bin is a quick, casual act. Dispose suggests formal, careful handling, which overstates such an ordinary throwaway.

threw

Correct — Sarah just tosses the notebook in the bin, and 'threw' matches that simple, offhand action without adding a false sense of formality or procedure.

Build the Word

disponere Latin: "to put away"

The prefix dis- means "away," and ponere means "to place" — so dispose literally means "to place away" or "put at a distance."

The concert was postponed because of bad weather.

to cancel something permanently

Not quite — Not quite. The ponere root means 'to put,' and post- means 'after,' so postpone means to put the concert off to a later time, not to call it off for good.

to put off to a later time

Correct — Correct. Postpone shares the ponere root ('to put') with dispose's source word disponere, so to postpone is to put an event off until after, at a later time.

The root connection →

Dispose comes from Latin disponere (dis- plus ponere 'to put'), and postpone keeps that same ponere root with post- 'after,' so it literally means to put something off to a later time.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / neutral

Dispose suggests a formal, responsible, and often lawful process — especially for waste, hazardous materials, or official handling. For everyday throwing away, "throw away" is simpler and more natural.
