Impertinent

/im-PER-tuh-nent/ — stress on second syllable; rhymes with pertinent · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is impertinent is rude or disrespectful, especially by speaking or acting toward others with inappropriate boldness.

Show it in context

Asking the judge her age was an impertinent question that silenced the courtroom.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when impertinent is the exact word.

Worked Example

A student stands up in a job interview and asks the CEO why the company pays its executives so much more than minimum-wage workers.

The question is not just rude—it crosses a line. The student is inappropriately bold, asking something that oversteps the bounds of what is fitting for a job interview. Impertinent captures the rudeness combined with the sense of 'you should not have said that.' A word like 'rude' would miss the 'out of place' part that makes impertinence distinct.

Your Turn

At a family dinner, a guest quietly declines the dessert and says it looks delicious.

Is impertinent the right word to describe the guest's behavior?

Yes

Not quite — Impertinent requires inappropriate boldness or overstepping a boundary. The guest declined politely and complimented the dessert, so there is nothing rude to describe.

No

Correct — Polite or courteous fits. The guest is not being rude or overstepping any boundary, and impertinent requires inappropriate boldness, not graceful politeness.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
rude

General lack of politeness. Impertinent adds the sense of overstepping bounds or being out of place.

disrespectful

Shows lack of respect, but may be subtle or contextually understandable. Impertinent emphasizes inappropriate boldness that crosses a line.

presumptuous

Assumes unwarranted authority or familiarity. Impertinent focuses on rudeness and overstepping, not just unfounded assumptions.

Antonyms · opposites

courteous — Showing genuine respect and good manners — the opposite of impertinent overstepping.

How Strong Is It?
thoughtless→rude→impertinent→insulting

Impertinent — rude and inappropriately bold; stronger than basic rudeness but focused on crossing boundaries rather than pure harm. (our word)

Build the Word

impertinens (in- + pertinens) Latin: 'in-' (not) + 'pertinens' (relevant, appropriate, fitting)

Impertinent behavior is inappropriate and out of place — you are saying or doing something that does not fit the situation.

Her suggestion for the project was pertinent and helped the team make a better plan.

Same root, new word — what does pertinent mean here?

relevant and appropriate to the matter at hand

Correct — Impertinent and pertinent share the Latin pertinere, 'to relate to or belong.' A pertinent idea belongs to the topic, while impertinent adds in- ('not') to mark something that does not belong or fit.

off-topic and unrelated

Not quite — This is actually what impertinent leans toward; pertinent, without the in- ('not'), means the reverse, relevant and fitting.

confusing and hard to follow

Not quite — Pertinent comes from pertinere, 'to relate to'; it is about relevance, not about whether something is clear or confusing.

The root connection →

The shared root is pertinere, 'to relate to': pertinent means fitting and on point, and impertinent is that same root with in- ('not') in front, so it means out of place, the exact opposite.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Impertinent is stronger than rude because it emphasizes that someone is being disrespectful in a way that is inappropriate for the situation—overstepping boundaries. Use it when someone speaks or acts boldly out of place. For basic rudeness, rude is the simpler choice.
