Culpable

/KUL-puh-bul/ — rhymes with bull · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Culpable describes someone who deserves blame for having done something wrong.

Show it in context

The inspector found the factory culpable for the polluted river.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when culpable is the exact word.

Worked Example

An oil company ignored environmental warnings and spilled toxic waste into the sea — they were culpable for the ecological disaster.

Culpable means deserving blame for wrongdoing. It's more specific than just 'responsible' because it clearly assigns moral or legal fault for doing something wrong, not merely being involved in a cause.

Your Turn

A construction worker's nail gun malfunctioned and struck a bystander, but the worker had followed all safety procedures correctly.

Is culpable the right word here, or should it just say 'responsible'?

culpable

Not quite — 'Culpable' means someone deserves blame for doing something wrong, but the worker did nothing wrong — he followed every safety procedure and the tool malfunctioned on its own.

responsible

Correct — Because the worker acted correctly, he caused harm only by accident. 'Responsible' notes that he was involved without accusing him of the blame that 'culpable' requires.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
responsible

Can mean simply 'involved in a cause'; culpable always means 'deserving blame for wrongdoing.'

guilty

Proven wrongdoing in court; culpable can apply before formal conviction or judgment.

blameworthy

Nearly synonymous but more formal and abstract; culpable is the term used in law and formal writing.

Antonyms · opposites

innocent — Free from blame or guilt — the opposite state of culpability.

How Strong Is It?
innocent→responsible→guilty→culpable

Culpable — the strongest form of moral or legal fault: deserving serious blame for wrongdoing. (our word)

Build the Word

culpa Latin: "fault, blame"

Culpable comes from Latin culpa, meaning fault or blame — when you're culpable, it's your culpa.

The police identified the culprit in the robbery.

Same root, new word — what does culprit mean here?

the person who is guilty of a crime or wrongdoing

Correct — Correct. Culpable and culprit both grow from the Latin root culpa, 'fault' or 'blame,' so a culprit is the person whose culpa the crime is.

the detective assigned to solve the case

Not quite — The sentence is about police work, so this feels close, but a culprit is not the investigator. The root culpa means 'fault' or 'blame,' pointing to the guilty party rather than the person solving the crime.

the witness who reported the crime

Not quite — A witness stands near the crime, which is tempting, but culpa means 'fault,' so a culprit is the one at fault, not the one who merely saw or reported it.

The root connection →

Culpable and culprit both trace back to culpa, Latin for 'fault' or 'blame.' If you are culpable you deserve the blame, and the culprit is the very person that blame lands on.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / legal

Culpable is the word for serious legal or moral fault — when someone deserves blame for wrongdoing. For general responsibility or involvement without blame, use 'responsible.' Reserve culpable for situations where fault is the key point.
