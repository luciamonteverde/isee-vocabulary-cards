Obstruct

ub-STRUKT -- Latin ob- ('in the way') plus struere ('to build, pile up'): to build something in the way. verb

Hear It
What It Means

To obstruct to block or stand in the way of movement or progress.

Show it in context

A jackknifed truck managed to obstruct all three lanes of the highway for hours.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Overnight, a rockslide dumps a wall of boulders across the only road into the valley, and no vehicle can get in or out until crews clear it.

That complete blocking of the way is exactly what it means to obstruct. Unlike impede, which merely slows progress down, obstruct means the path is closed off entirely.

prefixob-against, in the way (Latin)
root-struct-to build, pile up (Latin (struere))

Latin obstruere meant 'to build against' -- to obstruct is literally to pile something up in the way, from the same root struere that gives us construct and structure.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Obstruct means to block something outright, physically or procedurally -- 'obstruct a doorway,' 'obstruct justice.' Compare it with this unit's impede, which means to slow progress without necessarily blocking it completely.
