Colossal

/kuh-LAH-sul/ — stress on LAH · adjective

NEW (morpheme-only word)

Hear It
What It Means

Something colossal is so large in size or scale that it stands out as far bigger than normal.

Show it in context

A colossal balloon shaped like a dragon floated over the parade.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when colossal is the exact word.

Worked Example

The ancient Roman aqueduct stretched for 60 kilometers across mountains and valleys, its colossal stone arches soaring so high that construction required scaffolding 40 meters tall.

Coloss (from colossus: a giant statue, like the Colossus of Rhodes striding over the harbor) + -al (characteristic of) = characteristic of a colossus—giant-statue huge. The arches are so enormous they rival the scale of monumental ancient structures, worthy of comparison to the Colossus itself.

Your Turn

A beetle carried a crumb across the laboratory floor and disappeared under the bench.

Is colossal the precise word for what the beetle accomplished?

Yes

Not quite — The beetle's feat may seem impressive or remarkable for its effort, but that describes difficulty, not physical size.

No

Correct — 'Colossal' means enormous in physical size, like the Colossus of Rhodes, and a beetle carrying a crumb is tiny rather than giant-sized.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
massive

Very large. Massive is neutral and straightforward; colossal carries emphasis and excitement about the sheer impressive scale.

tremendous

Extraordinarily large. Tremendous can also mean 'wonderful' or 'excellent,' creating ambiguity; colossal specifically means enormous in physical size.

titanic

Giant-sized, from Greek mythology. Titanic suggests immense scale like colossal, but is more literary and less common in everyday speech.

Antonyms · opposites

tiny — Extremely small. Tiny is the direct opposite of colossal—while colossal describes something as remarkably huge, tiny describes something as remarkably small.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

coloss(us) + -al

colossus (a giant statue — like the Colossus of Rhodes, a bronze giant striding over a harbor) + -al (characteristic of) = characteristic of a colossus: giant-statue huge. Note: colossus is a NOUN; only colossal is the adjective — colossus never contains the -ous suffix, whatever it looks like.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

enthusiastic

Reach for colossal when size is not merely large but impressively, strikingly so—something that stands out and dominates your awareness through sheer magnitude. Unlike massive (neutral) or enormous (straightforward), colossal conveys drama and visual impact. Use it when describing monuments, natural wonders, or structures where the scale itself is the point.
