Boast

/bōst/ — rhymes with toast · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To boast is to talk proudly, and often at length, about your own achievements or possessions.

Show it in context

He loves to boast about catching the biggest fish at camp.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when boast is the exact word.

Worked Example

After winning the chess tournament, Maria spent the entire lunch period boasting about her victory to anyone who would listen, describing every move she'd made.

Boast captures exactly what's happening: she's talking proudly AND at length about her achievement in a way that gets annoying. Plain 'brag' is close, but boast specifically includes that sense of going on and on about it.

Your Turn

After making a basket in basketball, Jake ran over and told his friend, 'I just scored!'

Is boast the precise word here, or something simpler?

mentioned

Correct — Jake is just briefly reporting what happened, which is what mentioned names. A quick 'I just scored!' is a single remark, not showing off.

boast

Not quite — Boast requires the prolonged, excessive quality of talking at length about your achievement to impress people. One short line is far too brief for that.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
brag

Any talk about your own good qualities; boast adds the sense of talking at length and often annoyingly.

trumpet

To publicly proclaim or announce; boast is specifically about talking proudly about YOUR achievements.

vaunt

To boast in a formal or poetic way; boast is more everyday and often conversational.

Antonyms · opposites

humility — Being modest about your achievements; boast is the loud, proud opposite.

How Strong Is It?
keep quiet→mention accomplishment→brag→boast

Boast — the loudest and longest: talking extensively and proudly about your own achievements. (our word)

Build the Word

bōst Old English: origin uncertain, possibly related to puffing or swelling

The word likely captures the image of puffing yourself up — making yourself bigger and louder to show off your importance.

The athlete's chest burst with pride as she held the trophy high.

Same root, new word — what does burst mean here?

to swell and overflow, unable to hold a feeling in

Correct — The tie here is one of imagery rather than a proven shared root, since boast's own origin is uncertain: boast likely pictures puffing yourself up, and to burst with pride captures that same swelling until you cannot hold it in.

to shrink and grow smaller

Not quite — Burst pictures swelling until something breaks open, the opposite of shrinking, which matches the puffing-up image loosely linked to boast.

to quietly hide every feeling

Not quite — To burst with pride is to overflow with it, not to conceal it, so hiding feeling is the reverse of what the word suggests.

The root connection →

Instead of a documented root, boast and burst share a picture: both suggest swelling with feeling until it spills out, though boast's true origin remains unproven.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral to informal

Boast means to talk proudly and at length about your achievements — there's usually an excessive or annoying quality to how much you talk. Use brag for casual talk about your own good qualities; use boast when someone is talking a LOT and in a way others find irritating.
