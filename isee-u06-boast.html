Boast

/bōst/ — rhymes with toast verb

Hear It
What It Means

To boast is to talk proudly, and often at length, about your own achievements or possessions.

Show it in context

He loves to boast about catching the biggest fish at camp.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

After winning the chess tournament, Maria spent the entire lunch period boasting about her victory to anyone who would listen, describing every move she'd made.

Boast captures exactly what's happening: she's talking proudly AND at length about her achievement in a way that gets annoying. Plain 'brag' is close, but boast specifically includes that sense of going on and on about it.

Rootbostpuffed up, swollen with pride (Middle English, probably from a Scandinavian (North Germanic) source)

The root idea behind boast is being 'puffed up' or swollen with pride. To boast is to let that inflated pride escape as talk — going on and on about your own achievements and possessions.

Use the root in a new word: boastful

Marco's boastful speech about his three trophies made the audience roll their eyes — boastful is simply boast wearing its adjective ending, describing someone always puffed up with pride.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral to informal

Boast means to talk proudly and at length about your achievements — there's usually an excessive or annoying quality to how much you talk. Use brag for casual talk about your own good qualities; use boast when someone is talking a LOT and in a way others find irritating.
