Nuisance

/NOO-sens/ — rhymes with goose + sense · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A nuisance is a person or thing that causes annoyance, trouble, or mild inconvenience.

Show it in context

The dripping faucet was a small nuisance that kept everyone awake.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when nuisance is the exact word.

Worked Example

Every morning at 7 a.m., construction workers outside the school made a terrible racket right during first period, and even though classes weren't canceled, everyone found it difficult to concentrate.

Nuisance captures the mild-to-moderate annoyance perfectly here — the construction isn't a disaster or a health hazard, just an ongoing source of trouble and inconvenience. A word like 'problem' would be too strong; 'annoyance' is close but lacks the sense of ongoing inconvenience.

Your Turn

The local factory suddenly exploded, forcing everyone to evacuate within minutes to avoid toxic gas.

Is nuisance the right word here, or something much more serious?

emergency

Correct — An explosion with toxic gas that forces an evacuation is serious and dangerous, which is exactly what emergency names.

nuisance

Not quite — Nuisance is strictly for mild, ongoing annoyance or inconvenience. This situation is far too serious and life-threatening for such a weak word.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
annoyance

The feeling of being irritated; nuisance is the person or thing that causes that feeling.

inconvenience

Something that causes difficulty or trouble; inconvenience is broader and doesn't always imply annoyance.

impediment

Something that blocks or hinders progress; impediment is more formal and serious than nuisance.

Antonyms · opposites

blessing — Something wonderful and welcome; a nuisance is annoying and unwanted.

Nuisance — on the mild side: a person or thing that causes ongoing annoyance and trouble, but not a serious problem. (our word)

Build the Word

nuire Old French: 'to harm; to injure'

The word originally meant genuine harm or injury, but over time it weakened to mean any annoying thing that bothers you — now it describes only mild, ongoing annoyance.

The loud machinery at the factory released noxious smoke that stung the residents' eyes.

Same root, new word — what does noxious mean here?

harmful, poisonous, or deeply unpleasant

Correct — Nuisance and noxious both reach back to the Latin nocere, 'to harm.' Noxious smoke does real harm to the lungs, and nuisance once meant genuine harm too before it softened into mere annoyance.

fresh and pleasant to breathe

Not quite — Noxious traces to the Latin nocere, 'to harm,' so it means harmful, the opposite of pleasant.

merely loud and noisy

Not quite — This is tempting because the sentence mentions loud machinery, but noxious describes harm, from nocere, not the volume of the sound.

The root connection →

The deeper root is nocere, 'to harm': noxious keeps that harmful sense strong, while nuisance (through Old French nuire) has weakened from real injury down to everyday annoyance.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Nuisance describes something annoying and inconvenient, but definitely not serious — it's the dripping faucet, not the flooded basement. Use inconvenience for practical difficulty or disruption; use nuisance when there's emotional annoyance attached to ongoing trouble.
