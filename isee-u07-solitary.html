Solitary

/SAH-luh-ter-ee/ — stress on first syllable, rhymes with 'gotta carry' · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Solitary describes something done alone, or someone who prefers to be by themselves rather than with others.

Show it in context

Lighthouse keeping is a solitary job suited to people who enjoy their own company.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when solitary is the exact word.

Worked Example

After weeks of noisy family gatherings, Emma disappeared into her study for a solitary afternoon, finally finding peace.

Solitary is more precise than just 'alone' because it emphasizes the PREFERENCE or NATURE of being alone, not just the circumstance. It carries a tone of choice and suitedness.

Your Turn

During lunch period, Marcus sat at a table by himself because all his friends were in a different lunch hour.

Is solitary the right word for Marcus's situation?

Yes

Not quite — Solitary implies a preference or suitedness to being alone. Marcus would rather be with his friends if he could; he's alone by circumstance, not by nature.

No

Correct — He's simply alone. Solitary suggests being inclined or suited to being alone, but Marcus would probably prefer his friends' company.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
alone

Simply being without others; alone doesn't imply preference or suitedness like solitary does.

isolated

Separated from others, often involuntarily or due to circumstance; solitary implies choice or comfort.

secluded

In a quiet, remote place; emphasizes the physical location or environment rather than personal preference.

Antonyms · opposites

social — Preferring the company of others; a social person enjoys groups, while a solitary person prefers their own company.

How Strong Is It?

Solitary—comfort with being alone, but without the active withdrawal that reclusiveness implies. (our word)

Build the Word

solus Latin: alone

Solus means alone in Latin; -ary makes it an adjective describing something that is alone or suited to being alone.

Alone on the darkened stage, the prince paused, then began a soliloquy, voicing his private thoughts aloud to the empty theater.

Same root, new word — what does soliloquy mean here?

a speech in which a character alone voices their thoughts aloud

Correct — The root 'solus' means 'alone,' the same root that makes a solitary person keep to themselves. A soliloquy carries that 'alone' too: it is a speech a character delivers while alone on stage, voicing private thoughts as the prince does.

a spirited exchange between two rival characters onstage

Not quite — An exchange needs two speakers going back and forth, but the sentence shows the prince completely alone. The root 'solus' means 'alone,' so the answer should involve one person speaking by themselves, not two.

a short summary announced to the audience before a play begins

Not quite — That is a scene-setting announcement, not a private speech spoken while alone. The root 'solus' means 'alone,' pointing toward a speech given in solitude rather than an introduction to the audience.

The root connection →

Solitary comes from Latin 'solus,' meaning 'alone,' describing someone who is by themselves. A soliloquy shares that root: it is a speech in which a character, alone on stage, says their thoughts aloud.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Use solitary when describing something intrinsically done alone or someone who prefers solitude. It's more precise than 'alone,' which just describes a physical state, and less extreme than 'reclusive,' which implies actively avoiding others. Solitary carries a tone of calm choice and suitedness.
