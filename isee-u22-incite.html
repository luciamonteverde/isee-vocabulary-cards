Incite

Hear It
What It Means

To incite someone is to stir them up or push them into a strong reaction or action.

Show it in context

The fiery speech incited the crowd to march on city hall.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when incite is the exact word.

Worked Example

The fiery speech incited the crowd to march on city hall, with thousands of people surging forward in unified action toward the government building.

Incite perfectly captures stirring people up and PUSHING THEM INTO specific action. The speaker didn't just trigger anger (provoke); they actively pushed the crowd to march. Incite emphasizes this aggressive push toward ACTION, not just emotional reaction.

Your Turn

The comedian incited a few scattered laughs with her jokes.

Is incite the precise word here, or would something milder work better?

Incited

Not quite — Incite means to stir people up and push them toward strong, often aggressive action. A few scattered laughs at a comedian's jokes are a mild, passive response, far too gentle for a word that implies whipping a crowd into action.

Amused

Correct — This matches the light, harmless reaction of scattered laughter at some jokes, without implying the audience was driven toward any intense action.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
encourage

To urge on positively; incite implies stirring people up toward aggressive or forceful action, which encourage doesn't capture.

arouse

To awaken or stir up emotion; incite is more specifically about pushing toward action, whereas arouse focuses on awakening feeling.

rouse

To stir up or wake up; similar to incite but less focused on pushing people toward explicit action or aggressive response.

Antonyms · opposites

dissuade — To talk someone out of something; the opposite of incite, which is to stir someone up and push them toward action.

Build the Word

citāre Latin: 'to set in motion, to summon, to call'

Incite combines in- (into) with citāre (to set in motion) — to push someone into motion or action, often strong or aggressive action.

When the journalist cited her sources, she gave proper credit to all her research.

Same root, new word — what does cite mean here?

to hide or leave out

Not quite — Not quite. 'Cite' comes straight from citare, 'to call or summon,' so it means to call up and name a source, the opposite of hiding it.

to quote or refer to a source

Correct — Correct. 'Cite' comes directly from the Latin citare, 'to set in motion, summon, call,' the same root inside 'incite': to cite a source is to call it up by name.

The root connection →

Both 'incite' and 'cite' come from the Latin citare, 'to call or summon.' To incite is to call people into action, while to cite is to call up a source and name it.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / serious

Use incite when you want to show someone stirring people up and pushing them toward significant action (usually aggressive or dangerous). For encouraging something positive, use encourage. For awakening emotion without action, use arouse.
