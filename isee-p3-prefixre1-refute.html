Refute

rih-FYOOT verb

Hear It
What It Means

To refute to prove that something is false or incorrect.

Show it in context

The debate team's closing argument managed to refute nearly every point their opponents had raised.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Detailed satellite images were enough to refute the company's claim that it had stopped dumping waste into the river.

To refute a claim is to prove, with solid evidence or argument, that it is false -- not just to disagree with it.

Refute comes from Latin re- ('back') + futare, an old verb meaning 'to beat down' or 'strike,' related to the rare word confute ('to prove wrong'). That root never became common on its own, so there's no quiz here -- just this one little-known cousin worth knowing about.

Use the root in a new word: confute

The lawyer's precise timeline was enough to confute the witness's entire story, though most people today would simply say 'refute.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Refute means to actually prove something false with evidence or logic -- many people misuse it to mean simply 'deny' or 'disagree with,' but careful writers reserve it for a successful, evidence-backed counter-argument.
