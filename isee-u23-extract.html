Extract

/ik-STRAKT/ — rhymes with "attract" · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To extract something is to carefully pull or draw it out from the place where it is embedded or contained.

Show it in context

The dentist extracted the cracked tooth in minutes.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when extract is the exact word.

Worked Example

The archaeologist carefully extracted the fossilized bone from the rock surrounding it using delicate tools and hours of patience.

Extract implies careful, deliberate removal from something that holds it tightly. Just saying "dug out" or "removed" wouldn't capture the precision needed when an item is embedded in another material.

Your Turn

Maria pulled the wrench from her toolbag.

Is extracted the precise word here, or just pulled?

extracted

Not quite — A wrench sits loosely in a toolbag and lifts right out. Extract suggests forcing out something tightly held or embedded, which does not fit an object resting freely in a bag.

pulled

Correct — Maria just lifts the wrench out of the bag, and 'pulled' describes that easy retrieval without implying the wrench was stuck or wedged in place.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
pull

Simple and direct. Extract suggests careful removal from something that holds it tightly.

remove

To take away generally. Extract emphasizes careful pulling from something specific that holds it.

withdraw

To take back or draw away. Extract suggests pulling from something specific that contains it.

Antonyms · opposites

insert — To put something in. Extract means to pull it out deliberately.

Build the Word

extrahere Latin: "to pull out"

The prefix ex- means "out," and trahere means "to pull or drag" — so extract literally means "to pull out" or "draw forth."

The athlete signed a long-term contract with the team.

Same root, new word — what does contract mean here?

an agreement that pulls parties together

Correct — Correct. Contract shares the trahere root ('to pull') with extract's source word extrahere, so a contract is an agreement that pulls the parties together.

a decision to release someone from all duties

Not quite — This misses the root. The trahere root means 'to pull' and con- means 'together,' so a contract pulls the parties into a binding agreement rather than freeing anyone from obligations.

The root connection →

Extract comes from Latin extrahere (ex- 'out' plus trahere 'to pull'), and contract keeps that same trahere root with con- 'together,' so it names an agreement that pulls parties together.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Extract suggests careful, deliberate removal of something embedded or contained. Use it when precision and tightness matter — like removing teeth, fossils, or specific information. For simple pulling, "pull" is clearer.
