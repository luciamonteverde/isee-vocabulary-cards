Frugal

/FROO-gul/ — stress on the first syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is frugal is careful and economical with money, spending only what is necessary and avoiding waste.

Show it in context

A frugal traveler, he cooked hostel dinners instead of eating out.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when frugal is the exact word.

Worked Example

On a backpacking trip across Southeast Asia, Marcus ate street food, took overnight buses to save on hotels, and wore the same clothes repeatedly rather than buying new items.

Frugal is more precise than 'careful with money' here because it captures not just being careful but actively choosing the cheaper option and avoiding waste — Marcus isn't just budgeting; he's committed to spending only what is necessary.

Your Turn

After saving money carefully for three years, Jordan spent $1,200 on a weekend getaway at a nice resort, staying in a comfortable room and dining well.

Is Jordan being frugal?

Frugal

Not quite — Frugal means avoiding unnecessary spending, but Jordan deliberately chose a comfortable resort, a nice room, and fine dining, so this weekend is not frugal.

Responsible

Correct — Jordan saved carefully for three years before spending, which is financially responsible planning, even though the resort splurge itself is anything but frugal.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
economical

Economical means efficient use of resources, but doesn't necessarily mean avoiding expense — you can be economical with expensive materials. Frugal is about avoiding unnecessary spending.

thrifty

Thrifty means careful with money, similar to frugal, but thrifty is more about a habit of saving rather than a principle of avoiding waste.

stingy

Stingy means unwilling to spend money, which overlaps with frugal, but stingy carries a negative tone of being miserly, while frugal is neutral and practical.

Antonyms · opposites

extravagant — Extravagant means spending freely and wastefully without concern for cost — the opposite of frugal's careful, waste-avoiding approach.

Build the Word

fruges Latin: 'fruits, produce'

Frugal comes from the Latin word for fruit and crops — it originally meant making the most of what the earth provides, not wasting food or resources.

The fruit trees bore an abundant harvest, and the farmers preserved every bit to last through winter.

Same root, new word — what does fruit mean here?

a tool used for planting crops

Not quite — The root fruges means fruits or produce, so fruit is what a plant yields, not a farming tool.

the product of a plant

Correct — Correct. Fruit and frugal both trace back to Latin fruges, fruits or produce, so fruit is the harvest a plant provides.

The root connection →

Frugal and fruit both trace back to Latin fruges, fruits or produce. In old farming life, being frugal meant making the most of your fruits and harvest so nothing went to waste.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Use frugal when someone is genuinely committed to avoiding unnecessary spending and waste — it's a lifestyle choice, not just a temporary measure. For simple efficiency with resources, economical is the clearer choice.
