Guerrilla

/guh-RILL-uh/ — rhymes with gorilla (but different meaning) noun

Hear It
What It Means

A guerrilla is a fighter who belongs to a small, independent band that attacks a larger, official army through ambushes and surprise raids instead of open battles.

Show it in context

The guerrillas ambushed supply trucks and vanished back into the jungle.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

A small group of rebels in mountainous terrain cannot face the national army in open combat, so they hide in caves and attack supply convoys at night, then vanish into the jungle before reinforcements arrive.

Guerrilla captures both the unconventional tactic and the small-band structure—surprise attacks instead of formal battle, independence from official armies. 'Soldier' would miss what makes them different: their methods and scale.

Rootguerr(a)war (Spanish, from a Germanic root (werra, 'strife, war'))
Suffix-illalittle (diminutive) (Spanish)

Spanish guerra ('war') plus the diminutive suffix -illa makes 'little war' — guerrilla warfare is a small, scrappy war of ambushes and raids, and a guerrilla is a fighter who wages one.

Use the root in a new word: war

English war and Spanish guerra both grew from the same old Germanic root meaning 'strife' — which is why a guerrilla is literally a fighter in a 'little war.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / military

Guerrilla is a military and historical term—reach for it when describing irregular fighters or unconventional warfare tactics. For a general fighter, use 'soldier' or 'fighter'; for a protester, use 'activist' or 'protester'.
