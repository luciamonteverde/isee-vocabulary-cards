Pontificate

pon-TIF-ih-kayt verb

Hear It
What It Means

To pontificate pompously express opinions.

Show it in context

Every Sunday dinner, Uncle Ray would pontificate about politics as though his opinion were simply beyond question.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

At every family dinner, Grandpa would pontificate about the state of modern music as though no one could possibly disagree.

It wasn't just that he shared an opinion -- it was the pompous certainty and length of it, as if disagreement were unthinkable, that makes this pontificating.

Pontific- (from pontifex, the Latin title for Rome's high priest) plus the verb-forming -ate gives 'to act the high priest' -- to pontificate is to talk as if you held that same unquestionable religious authority.

Use the root in a new word: pontiff

Pontiff is the modern English title drawn from the very same Latin word for a high priest, now used for the pope.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Pontificate is almost always critical -- it mocks the speaker's excessive self-importance. Compare it with opine, this unit's more neutral word for simply stating an opinion.
