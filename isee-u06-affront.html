Affront

/ə-FRONT/ — stress on second syllable, rhymes with font · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To affront someone is to insult them or offend their dignity, usually through disrespectful words or behavior.

Show it in context

Serving the guest of honor last was taken as a deliberate affront.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when affront is the exact word.

Worked Example

A diplomat was seated below junior staff members at an official state dinner — a calculated slight intended to diminish her nation's standing.

Affront captures both the deliberate nature and the targeting of someone's dignity or honor. It is more precise than 'insult' (which could be accidental) or 'embarrassment' (which lacks the intentionality aimed at status).

Your Turn

When the teacher called on Marcus without raising his hand first, he felt overlooked.

Is affront the right word here, or would something else fit better?

overlooked

Correct — Marcus felt unnoticed and passed over, which is what overlooked names. There was no deliberate attack on his dignity.

affront

Not quite — An affront requires intentional disrespect aimed at someone's status or honor. Being called on without raising a hand is not a deliberate insult, so affront is too strong.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
insult

Any rude or disrespectful remark; can be accidental. Affront is always calculated and purposeful.

snub

A deliberate social rejection or cold shoulder; narrower in scope. Affront is broader and more about attacking dignity.

disrespect

Rude or uncivil behavior in general. Affront targets someone's honor or standing specifically.

Antonyms · opposites

honor — To treat with respect and dignity — the opposite of deliberately attacking someone's standing.

Build the Word

ad- + frontum Latin — 'to the face; against the forehead'

Affront originally meant to face someone boldly or defiantly — later it came to mean confronting someone with deliberate disrespect aimed at their dignity.

The speaker was confronted by an angry crowd of protesters.

Same root, new word — what does confront mean here?

to face or stand up to someone directly

Correct — Affront and confront both build on the Latin frontum, 'forehead or face.' To confront a crowd is to meet it face-to-face, just as an affront throws disrespect straight in someone's face.

to comfort or reassure someone in distress

Not quite — This sounds a little like confront, but the root frontum ('forehead, face') plus con- ('against') means to meet someone face-to-face in challenge, not to soothe them.

to slip away from a problem without facing it

Not quite — The root frontum means 'the face,' and confront means putting yourself face-to-face with something, which is the opposite of avoiding it.

The root connection →

Both words trace to frontum, 'the face': you confront someone by turning toward them in challenge, and you affront someone by aiming an insult right at their face.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Affront is a formal word for calculated, intentional insult aimed at someone's honor or standing. Use it when the disrespect is clearly deliberate and touches on status or dignity. For everyday rudeness, 'insult' is simpler and sufficient.
