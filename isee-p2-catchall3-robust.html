Robust

roh-BUST — stress the BUST; something robust is so strong it won't bust. adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Robust is strong, healthy, and unlikely to fail or break down.

Show it in context

The bridge's robust design let it withstand decades of storms and heavy traffic.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

One laptop in the office has survived three drops down the stairs, a full cup of spilled coffee, and five years of daily travel. It still boots instantly and runs every program. Its owner has never once taken it in for repair.

Something built so strong and reliable that it resists failure is robust. Unlike 'big,' robustness is about strength and durability, not size.

rootrobur-oak; strength (Latin)

Latin robur named the oak tree, the ancient symbol of toughness. Something robust is literally 'as strong as oak' — solid, healthy, and hard to break.
