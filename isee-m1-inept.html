Inept

/in-EPT/ — stress on EPT · adjective

PREVIEW — taught in depth later in Skill, Competence & Judgment

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is inept lacks the skill or ability needed to do something well, so their attempts usually fail.

Show it in context

His inept attempt at whistling sounded like a leaking balloon.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when inept is the exact word.

Worked Example

The new intern's inept handling of the database corrupted three months of financial records.

In (not) + aptus (fitted, suited) = not fitted for the task. The intern lacked the skill to manage the system. Inept is more precise than 'careless' because it names the fundamental lack of capability, not just a one-time mistake.

Your Turn

The surgeon moved with practiced precision, never hesitating, each motion exact.

Is inept the word for this surgeon?

Yes

Not quite — Surgery is high-stakes and difficult, so any word about a surgeon's skill might feel plausible. But this surgeon works with practiced precision and never hesitates, which is the exact opposite of inept.

No

Correct — The surgeon is apt, fitted and suited to the task, moving with exact, confident precision. Remove in- and you are left with apt, the antonym, so inept is wrong here.

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

in + ept

in (not) + Latin aptus ('fitted, suited') = not suited to the task. Its skilled twin adept comes from the same root — inept and adept are opposites by construction.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Reach for inept when someone fundamentally lacks the skill or ability for a task—not just making a one-time mistake, but demonstrating deep incompetence. It works equally well in casual contexts ('an inept dancer') and formal writing.
