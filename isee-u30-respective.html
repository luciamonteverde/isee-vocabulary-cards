Respective

/rih-SPEK-tiv/ — stress on second syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Respective describes something that belongs individually to each particular person or thing in a group, rather than being shared.

Show it in context

After the ceremony, the teams returned to their respective locker rooms.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when respective is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the championship debate, the three teams collected their respective trophies—Team A took the gold, Team B took the silver, and Team C took the bronze.

Respective is more precise than just 'their own' because it emphasizes that each trophy is *individually assigned* to its particular team, not mixed or shared. The word clarifies the one-to-one matching: each team gets its *specific trophy*. Plain 'their own' would work, but respective highlights the individual *assignment*.

Your Turn

Three friends were all wearing jackets after soccer practice, and each friend had to pick up her own jacket from the pile.

Would 'their respective jackets' be the natural way to describe this situation?

Respective

Not quite — Friends casually grabbing their own jackets from a pile after practice is an informal moment. Respective fits formal or official matching, so 'their respective jackets' sounds too stiff for something this ordinary.

Their own

Correct — Saying 'their own jackets' naturally means each friend picks up the one belonging to her, in a relaxed setting, without the formal assignment that respective would signal.

Emphasizes 'specific' or 'individual', but doesn't carry the formal, assigned quality that respective does.

Emphasizes 'separate' or 'distinct', but is less formal and doesn't suggest the structured matching or designation that respective implies.

Build the Word

respectivus Latin: 'relating to each separately' (from respicere 'to look back at' or 'to regard')

Respective comes from the idea of looking back at each thing individually—each member of a group is 'regarded' in relation to their own assigned piece.

The detective chose to respect the evidence by examining each item separately rather than jumping to conclusions.

Same root, new word — what does respect mean here?

to ignore something as unimportant

Not quite — Ignoring is the opposite of regarding. The respicere root means to look back at or regard, so respecting the evidence means giving it careful attention, not brushing it aside.

to regard with attention or consideration

Correct — Correct. Respect comes from respicere, to look back at or regard, the same root behind respective, which is why it means to give something careful consideration.

The root connection →

Respective and respect both come from respicere, to look back at or regard. Respect is regarding something with attention, while respective is regarding each thing separately.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Respective is useful in formal or structured situations where each person or thing is being individually assigned or matched to something specific—think team assignments, seating charts, job titles, or official categories. In everyday contexts where people simply have their own stuff, plain 'own' or 'their own' is usually the simpler, better choice. Respective sounds formal on purpose; use it when the situation *is* formal or when the individual matching *is* the point.
