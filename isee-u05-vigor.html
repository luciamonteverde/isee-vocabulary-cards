What It Means

Vigor is physical strength and energetic force.

After a good night's sleep, she attacked the project with renewed vigor.

Here's how to decide when vigor is the exact word.

Worked Example

The young athlete bounded up the stadium steps two at a time, her vigor evident in every powerful leap.

Vigor captures both the physical strength AND the energetic force in action. A plain word like 'strength' misses the dynamic, driving quality; 'energy' alone misses the physical power. Vigor names the combination precisely.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
strength

Physical power — but strength doesn't capture the energetic, driving force that vigor includes.

energy

Active force and liveliness — close to vigor, but energy is more about spirit and enthusiasm; vigor is more about physical force in action.

vitality

The power to sustain life and growth — similar to vigor, but vitality is more about wellness and lasting power; vigor is more about forceful action.

Build the Word

vigere Latin: 'to be lively, thrive, be strong'

Vigor comes from the Latin root meaning 'to thrive' — when you have vigor, you're literally thriving with physical strength and energetic force.

The cold plunge of water invigorated her and made her feel ready for the day.

Same root, new word — what does invigorate mean here?

to make calm and sleepy

Not quite — A cold plunge might sound shocking, but the root vigere means 'to be lively and strong,' so invigorated means energized and ready, not calmed or made sleepy.

to fill someone with fresh energy and strength

Correct — Vigor and invigorate both spring from the Latin vigere ('to be lively, to thrive'). The cold plunge fills her with lively energy, the same thriving strength that vigor names.

to make cold and numb

Not quite — The cold water is the cause, but invigorate describes the result of feeling lively and strong, matching the root vigere, not the sensation of numbness.

The root connection →

The shared root is vigere ('to be lively and strong'): to invigorate is to pour that liveliness into someone, and vigor is the state of thriving with that very strength and energy.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Vigor captures both physical strength and dynamic energy — use it when describing active, forceful effort or movement. For just physical power, 'strength' is simpler; for just liveliness, 'energy' works better.
