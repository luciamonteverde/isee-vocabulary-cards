Altruistic

al-troo-ISS-tik -- from Latin alter, 'other': an altruistic person acts for the other person. adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Altruistic is unselfish and generous toward others.

Show it in context

Donating a kidney to a stranger is about as altruistic as a person can get.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

A student who has been saving all year for new sneakers quietly hands the money to a classmate whose family lost everything in a fire, and never mentions it to anyone.

Acting for someone else's good at real cost to yourself, with nothing to gain in return, is exactly what altruistic means. Unlike 'kind,' which covers any pleasant act, altruistic stresses that your own interest was set aside.

The French philosopher Auguste Comte coined altruisme in the 1830s from Italian altrui, 'somebody else,' which traces back to Latin alter, 'other' -- an altruistic person is literally 'other-centered,' from the same root that gives us alternative and alter ego.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Altruistic is a compliment, and it usually describes people, motives, or acts -- 'altruistic motives,' 'a purely altruistic gesture.' The matching noun is altruism.
