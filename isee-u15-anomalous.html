Anomalous

/uh-NOM-uh-lus/ — stress on second syllable, similar to marvelous · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something that is anomalous is different from what is normal or expected, standing out as strange or irregular.

Show it in context

The scientists rechecked the anomalous reading; no other sensor showed it.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when anomalous is the exact word.

Worked Example

The biologist discovered an anomalous species of frog with six legs instead of four, forcing her to completely reconsider her data and field observations.

Anomalous is more precise than 'unusual' because it means something breaks a system's rules or expected pattern—not just different, but wrong according to the established system. 'Unusual' just means surprising.

Your Turn

The traffic was heavier than expected during lunch hour.

Is anomalous the right word here, or would unusual work better?

unusual

Correct — The traffic is heavier than normal but breaks no system or rule, which is exactly what 'unusual' describes.

anomalous

Not quite — 'Anomalous' would fit only if the traffic contradicted all known patterns, such as vanishing entirely, not just running a little heavy.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
strange

Surprising and hard to explain, but doesn't indicate deviation from a scientific system or rule.

abnormal

Not normal or typical, but often used in medical contexts without the sense of breaking a scientific pattern.

irregular

Not following a pattern or schedule, but emphasizes inconsistency rather than deviation from a rule.

Antonyms · opposites

normal — Following expected rules and patterns—the opposite of anomalous, which breaks the system.

How Strong Is It?
normal→strange→irregular→anomalous

Anomalous — the strongest of the group: breaks the system's rules or expected pattern. (our word)

Build the Word

anomalos Greek: 'a-' (not) + 'nomos' (law, rule) = 'not according to law'

Anomalous literally means 'not according to law or rule.' It comes from Greek roots emphasizing that something deviates from the system's established rules.

The scientist discovered an anomaly in the test results that required a complete investigation.

Same root, new word — what does anomaly mean here?

a result that matched every prediction perfectly

Not quite — Tempting because scientists hope for clean, expected results, but an anomaly is exactly the thing that does not match what was predicted.

a tiny error too small to bother with

Not quite — Tempting because odd data is sometimes brushed aside, but the sentence says it 'required a complete investigation,' so it clearly mattered.

something that doesn't fit the established system or pattern

Correct — Anomaly and anomalous share the Greek roots a- ('not') and nomos ('law, rule'), so an anomaly is literally a thing that does not follow the rule, a result that breaks the expected pattern in the data.

The root connection →

Anomalous is the adjective and anomaly is the noun, but both are built from Greek a- plus nomos, 'not according to rule.' Something anomalous behaves against the rules, while an anomaly is the out-of-rule thing itself.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / technical

Anomalous is the right choice when something breaks a system's rules or pattern in a technically significant way. For everyday strangeness, 'unusual' or 'strange' is simpler and better.
