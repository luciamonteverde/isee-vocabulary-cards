Impertinent

/im-PER-tuh-nent/ — stress on PER · adjective

PREVIEW — taught in depth later in Rudeness, Annoyance & Disrespect

Hear It
What It Means

Someone who is impertinent is rude or disrespectful, especially by speaking or acting toward someone senior as if they were an equal or inferior.

Show it in context

Asking the bride how much the wedding cost was an impertinent question.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when impertinent is the exact word.

Worked Example

During the board meeting, the new intern interrupted the CEO mid-sentence and said, 'That's not how we should do this.' His tone was impertinent.

Im (not) + pertinent (appropriate, fitting). The intern's interruption and manner were not appropriate—he treated a senior executive as an equal rather than with the respect the position demands. Impertinent captures both rudeness and the violation of social hierarchy.

Your Turn

The employee respectfully raised her hand, waited to be called on, and then asked the manager, 'Could you clarify the deadline for this project?'

Was she being impertinent?

Yes

Not quite — She spoke up to a manager, and challenging or questioning a superior can seem bold or out of place. But she raised her hand, waited, and asked with deference, which is appropriate rather than rude.

No

Correct — Her approach was pertinent: she followed protocol and addressed a senior person respectfully. Impertinent means the opposite, an inappropriate rudeness toward someone of higher status, preserving an older sense of pertinent as 'appropriate' rather than today's 'relevant.'

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
rude

Impolite or discourteous. Impertinent is more formal and emphasizes disrespect toward someone of higher status or authority.

cheeky

Boldly disrespectful in a playful or cocky way. Impertinent is more serious and formal, stressing a breach of social propriety.

presumptuous

Overstepping one's proper place or role. Impertinent stresses inappropriate rudeness; presumptuous stresses arrogance about one's standing.

Antonyms · opposites

pertinent

— The bare base word—appropriate and to the point (in its older sense). Remove the prefix and you have the antonym. Beware: 'pertinent' today usually means 'relevant,' but impertinent preserves the historical meaning of 'appropriate.'

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

im + pertinent (meaning drifted)

im (not) + pertinent ('appropriate, to the point'). Centuries ago it meant simply 'not appropriate'; the meaning drifted to 'rude.' Careful: pertinent today means 'relevant' — so impertinent is NOT just 'irrelevant.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Reach for impertinent when someone is being rude or disrespectful, especially to a social superior or in a formal setting. The word implies not just rudeness but a violation of social protocol and hierarchy. Don't assume the prefix simply means 'not relevant'—the base word's meaning has drifted over centuries.
