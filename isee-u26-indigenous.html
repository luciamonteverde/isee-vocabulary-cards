Indigenous

/in-DIJ-uh-nus/ — stress on the 'DIJ' syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Indigenous describes people, animals, or plants that originated in a particular place rather than arriving there from somewhere else.

Show it in context

The kangaroo is indigenous to Australia and found wild nowhere else.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when indigenous is the exact word.

Worked Example

The koalas living in the eucalyptus forests of Australia had never spread to any other continent, making them truly indigenous to that island continent alone.

The koalas originated in Australia and are found wild nowhere else on Earth. Indigenous is more precise than 'native' because it emphasizes that they naturally belong there from their origins, not just that they live there now or were introduced there.

Your Turn

After moving to California five years ago and settling down, Maya began to think of herself as indigenous to the state.

Is indigenous the precise word here, or should it be something else?

indigenous

Not quite — Maya moved to California as an adult only five years ago, but indigenous describes peoples and species that originated in a place over generations, so it does not fit her.

resident

Correct — Since Maya settled in California recently rather than originating there, resident accurately describes someone who lives in a place they moved to.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
native

Originally from a place. Indigenous emphasizes group origin and deep historical roots more strongly than native does.

endemic

Found nowhere else but in one region. Indigenous includes geographic exclusivity but emphasizes natural origins and belonging.

original

First in existence. Indigenous means naturally present since origins, not just first within a limited timeframe.

Antonyms · opposites

introduced — Brought in from elsewhere. Indigenous describes what originated and naturally belongs in a place.

Build the Word

indigēna Latin: 'generated within' (in- 'within' + -gena 'born of')

Indigenous comes from the Latin word for 'generated within' — those beings born and created within a place, not brought in from outside.

The research was designed to generate new understanding of the indigenous communities' history.

Same root, new word — what does generate mean here?

to gather or collect together

Not quite — That describes collecting, not creating. Generate traces back to the ancient root meaning 'to beget' or 'produce,' so the research was meant to produce new understanding, not merely gather it.

The root connection →

Indigenous and generate both trace back to the ancient root gen-, 'to beget' or 'produce,' though by different paths. Indigenous comes from indigena, 'born within a place,' while generate comes from generare, 'to bring forth.' They are deep root relatives, not direct derivatives of each other.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / scientific

Indigenous is the precise term for something that originated naturally in a place, especially over long periods and in cultural or ecological contexts. Use it when referring to the deep roots and original presence of a species or people. For something just currently living in an area, 'local' or 'native' may be simpler, but indigenous carries the sense of long-standing, natural origin.
