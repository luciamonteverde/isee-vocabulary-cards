Culpable

/KUL-puh-bul/ — stress on KUL · adjective

REVIEW (Crime & Punishment)

Hear It
What It Means

Culpable describes someone who deserves blame for having done something wrong.

Show it in context

The report found the shipping company culpable for the oil spill.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when culpable is the exact word.

Worked Example

The principal determined that the student was culpable for the incident, despite his insistence that he was just following orders.

Culpa is Latin for blame or fault (as in 'mea culpa,' my fault). Culpa + -able (worthy of) = worthy of blame, deserving censure. This is a rule-breaker: Latin culpabilis already ended in -abilis, and English kept the -a instead of switching to -ible. The scenario shows the student bears fault (culpa) for the wrongdoing—he cannot escape culpability by blaming obedience.

Your Turn

The employee entered the wrong data into the system, but her supervisor never trained her on the software and ignored her questions.

Should we say the employee is culpable?

solely culpable

Not quite — Being solely culpable would place the entire fault on the employee, but her supervisor never trained her and ignored her questions. Since those failures contributed to the mistake, the blame cannot rest on the employee alone.

partly responsible

Correct — The employee did enter the wrong data, so she carries some fault, but the untrained conditions the supervisor created share it too. Partly responsible fits this split, where both people contributed to the error rather than one bearing all the blame.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
responsible

Means answerable or in charge. Responsible can be neutral ('Who's responsible for snacks?'); culpable is always negative—it means deserving blame specifically for a wrong.

liable

Means legally responsible or obligated. Liable focuses on legal consequence and obligation; culpable focuses on moral blame and blameworthiness.

guilty

Means having committed a wrong or crime. Culpable is the judgment that someone *deserves* blame; guilty is the verdict after proving culpability.

Antonyms · opposites

innocent — Culpable means worthy of blame; innocent means free from blame—direct opposites. (The morpheme connection: culpa = blame; innocent = no fault.)

Build the Word · the heart of this unit

culp(a) + able

Latin culpa = blame, fault — the same culpa in the classic apology 'mea culpa,' literally 'my fault.' culpa + -able = deserving blame, worthy of blame. Watch the spelling: 'culp' is a Latin stem, so the rule predicts -ible — but Latin culpabilis already ended in -abilis, and English kept the a. A famous rule-breaker.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Reach for culpable when assigning blame for a specific wrong. Unlike 'responsible' (which can be neutral), culpable always carries a moral judgment of wrongdoing. Use it in legal, ethical, or serious interpersonal contexts where fault and blameworthiness are at issue.
