Dormant

/DOR-muhnt/ — rhymes with torment · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Something dormant is temporarily inactive or resting, but still capable of becoming active again later.

Show it in context

The volcano has been dormant for four centuries, quiet but not extinct.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when dormant is the exact word.

Worked Example

A perennial garden seems dead each winter, but the roots lie dormant beneath the frozen soil, waiting to surge back in spring.

The precise power of dormant is that it means temporary inactivity with the capacity to reawaken—unlike "inactive" or "quiet," which don't promise that return. For a root system, this distinction is crucial: dormant captures both the rest and the future awakening.

Your Turn

A senior takes a break from the debate team for the fall, planning to compete again in spring.

Is dormant the right word here, or would 'paused' work better?

paused

Correct — Taking a break by choice and planning to resume is exactly a pause, a deliberate stop the person controls.

dormant

Not quite — The senior deliberately chooses to step away and return in spring; dormant is reserved for natural things like volcanoes or plants that reawaken on their own biological cycle, not a planned human decision.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
inactive

Simply not active. Inactive doesn't imply the thing will ever wake up—a broken machine is inactive, but not dormant.

latent

Present but hidden or unreleased. Latent emphasizes what's underneath; dormant emphasizes the coming awakening.

suppressed

Held down by external force. Suppressed suggests someone is keeping something quiet; dormant is natural rest.

Antonyms · opposites

active — Fully engaged and awake—the opposite of the resting state dormant names.

Build the Word

dormīre Latin: "to sleep"

Same family as dormitory — dormant means in a sleep-like state of inactivity, from the root for sleep itself.

College dormitories are buildings where students sleep and rest during the school year.

Same root, new word — what does dormitory mean here?

a building for sleeping and resting

Correct — Correct. Dormitory shares the Latin root dormire (to sleep) with dormant, so a dormitory is a place for sleeping and resting.

a building used for classes and lectures

Not quite — Dormitory comes from dormire (to sleep): it names where people sleep, not where they attend lectures.

The root connection →

Dormant and dormitory both come from Latin dormire, meaning to sleep. Dormant describes something in a sleeping, inactive state, and a dormitory is a place built for sleeping.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / scientific

Dormant is technical, used for natural phenomena with the capacity to reawaken—volcanoes, seeds, diseases, seasonal cycles. For human decisions or simple inactivity, 'inactive' or 'paused' is clearer and more natural.
