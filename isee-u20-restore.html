Restore

/ri-STOR/ — rhymes with door, stress on second syllable · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To restore something is to repair it and bring it back to its original, good condition.

Show it in context

Volunteers restored the mural to its original brilliance.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when restore is the exact word.

Worked Example

Volunteers spent months carefully restoring the deteriorated library mural to its original vivid colors and detailed brushwork.

Restore captures the idea of returning something to its original good condition, not just repairing it to function again. Repair alone would miss the sense of returning it to former beauty.

Your Turn

The contractor renovated the kitchen by installing modern stainless-steel appliances and granite countertops.

Is restore the right word here, or should it say something else?

restore

Not quite — The kitchen is getting brand-new stainless-steel appliances and granite countertops it never had before, so the contractor is moving it forward to something new, not bringing it back to any earlier condition. Restore would only fit if the goal were to return the kitchen to how it originally looked.

renovate

Correct — Installing modern upgrades to modernize the space is exactly what this scenario describes, since the work updates the kitchen rather than recovering an older version of it.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
repair

Fix something to working order, but doesn't necessarily mean returning to original condition.

renovate

Update and improve a space, but may add new elements rather than returning to the original state.

refurbish

Intensively clean and update something to look new, but less specific about returning to its original design.

Antonyms · opposites

demolish — Tear down or destroy completely—the opposite action of bringing something back to good condition.

How Strong Is It?

Build the Word

restaurare Latin: 're-' (again) + 'statuere' (to set up, establish)

Restore literally means to set something back up again, to return it to its original standing and good condition.

Her status as a respected leader was built on years of honest work.

Same root, new word — what does status mean here?

standing, position, or condition someone holds

Correct — Correct. Status keeps the Latin sta- root shared with restore (from statuere, to set up or establish): restore sets something back in its place, and status is the standing or position someone holds.

a sudden burst of forward movement

Not quite — That describes momentum, not status. Status traces to the Latin sta- family (about standing), so it names the position or standing someone holds, not motion.

The root connection →

Restore and status both trace back to the Latin sta- family (restore from statuere, to set up or establish; status from stare, to stand). The shared idea is something being set firmly in place or standing in a position.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Restore is about bringing something back to its original good condition. Use it when repair alone doesn't capture the idea of returning to the former state. For something brand new being made beautiful for the first time, create or build works better.
