Coma

/KO-muh/ — rhymes with quota · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A coma is a deep, prolonged state of unconsciousness in which a person cannot be woken up and does not respond to anything around them.

Show it in context

Doctors kept him in a medically induced coma while the swelling healed.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when coma is the exact word.

Worked Example

After the car accident, the driver's brain began to swell. Doctors induced a deep coma, keeping him completely unresponsive and unaware for five days while the swelling went down.

Coma precisely captures a medical emergency: a prolonged, unresponsive state where the patient cannot be roused. The word 'unconsciousness' alone is too vague — it could mean a brief blackout — but coma conveys the severity and duration.

Your Turn

A teenager is exhausted after staying up all night studying and jokes, 'I'm in a coma and need to sleep.'

Is the teenager using coma precisely, or just as an exaggeration?

coma

Not quite — The teen just needs sleep after an all-nighter, but a real coma is a deep, prolonged unconsciousness a person cannot simply wake from, so this is only exaggeration.

exhausted

Correct — He is exhausted, and a night's sleep will fix it, unlike a true coma, so exhausted describes his state precisely.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
sleep

Normal rest from which you can be easily awakened. A coma is involuntary and unbreakable — you cannot wake from it.

unconsciousness

A general state of not being aware. Unconsciousness can be brief; a coma is specifically deep and prolonged.

stupor

A state of dazed confusion or drowsiness. Stupor allows some awareness; a coma is total unresponsiveness.

Antonyms · opposites

consciousness — The state of being awake and aware — the opposite of a coma's complete unresponsiveness.

Build the Word

koma Greek: deep sleep or stupor

The Greek word koma means a state of deep, unresponsive sleep — which is exactly what happens in a medical coma.

The root connection →

Coma and comatose both come from the Greek koma, deep sleep or stupor. A coma is that deep sleep, and comatose describes a person in it.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / medical

Coma is the precise term for a serious medical emergency where a patient is deeply unconscious and unresponsive. Reserve it for genuine medical situations; using it casually for sleep or tiredness waters down its gravity and misleads listeners about the severity.
