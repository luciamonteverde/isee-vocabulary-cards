Copious

/KOH-pee-us/ — three syllables: CO-pi-ous · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Copious describes something that exists in large, plentiful amounts, more than enough.

Show it in context

She took copious notes, filling two notebooks in a single lecture.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when copious is the exact word.

Worked Example

She took copious notes throughout the semester, filling three thick folders with detailed observations and cross-references.

Copious captures the overflowing abundance of notes—not just many pages, but an outpouring and profusion. It is more precise than "many" or "lots of" because it conveys the sheer volume and sense of things flowing forth in great supply.

Your Turn

He drank one large glass of water before the run.

Is copious the right word for his water intake?

Yes

Not quite — One glass is a single, ordinary serving. Copious describes an overflowing, large quantity, not one normal portion.

No

Correct — One glass is a single serving, not copious. Copious means an overflowing, large quantity in abundance, not a modest single portion.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
plentiful

Means plenty of something; similar to copious but without the sense of overflow or profusion.

profuse

Means growing or produced in great quantities; overlaps with copious but emphasizes lavish, unrestrained abundance.

voluminous

Means large in size, quantity, or extent; focuses on sheer bulk rather than the flowing abundance that copious conveys.

Antonyms · opposites

sparse — Scattered or thin in quantity; the opposite of copious abundance.

Build the Word

copia Latin: 'abundance, plenty, supply'

Copia originally meant pooled wealth or shared resources; when something is copious, it is an abundance overflowing as if from a shared store.

The banquet featured a cornucopia of fresh fruits and delicacies.

Same root, new word — what does cornucopia mean here?

a small, carefully chosen sample

Not quite — A fancy banquet spread makes this tempting, but copia means 'abundance, plenty,' so a cornucopia is an overflowing supply, not a small sample.

a horn of plenty; an overflowing abundance of good things

Correct — Copious and cornucopia both carry the Latin copia, 'abundance, plenty.' Copious names an overflowing quantity, and a cornucopia is literally a horn spilling over with that same plenty.

a single rare treat

Not quite — A banquet might include one special dish, but the root copia means 'plenty,' so cornucopia names full bounty, not one item.

The root connection →

Copia meant a pooled store of wealth or supply. Copious describes anything overflowing from such a store, and cornucopia, the 'horn of plenty,' is the classic image of that abundance pouring out.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal

Copious is the word when you want to emphasize an outpouring or profusion—abundance that seems to flow forth generously. For everyday quantities, 'plenty' or 'plentiful' is simpler and clear.
