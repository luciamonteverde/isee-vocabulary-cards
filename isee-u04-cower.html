Cower

/COW-er/ — rhymes with tower · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To cower is to crouch down or shrink back in fear, making yourself smaller to avoid a threat.

Show it in context

The puppy cowered behind the sofa during the thunderstorm.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when cower is the exact word.

Worked Example

When the bully approached with an angry expression, the smaller student cowered against the lockers, hunching down and turning away to appear smaller.

Cower precisely names the physical act of crouching down and shrinking back in fear, which is far more vivid and specific than simply saying 'was afraid.'

Your Turn

During the fire alarm, a student cowers under her desk.

Is cower the right word for this action, or would 'takes shelter' be more accurate?

takes shelter

Correct — During a fire alarm the point is safety, so ducking under a desk is best read as 'takes shelter,' a protective action rather than a fear response.

cower

Not quite — 'Cower' is tempting because she is low and small under the desk, but cower means shrinking back in fear. The alarm calls for a safety action, and nothing says she is frozen by terror rather than protecting herself.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
flinch

Flinch is a quick, reflexive reaction to pain or threat. Cower is sustained crouching from fear.

crouch

Crouch just describes the physical position of bending low. Cower includes that position plus the emotion of fear driving it.

tremble

Tremble is shaking from fear or cold. Cower includes the physical retreat and shrinking, not just shaking.

Antonyms · opposites

confront — To confront means to face something bravely; to cower means to shrink back and hide from threat.

Build the Word

kūra Old Norse: to crouch, squat, huddle

Cower comes from the same root as crouch — the physical position of bending low and pulling yourself small from fear.

The hiker had to crouch beneath the overhanging rocks to pass through the cave.

Same root, new word — what does crouch mean here?

to run away quickly from danger

Not quite — Fleeing and cowering are both fear reactions, which makes this tempting, but the root kra means 'to crouch, squat, huddle.' That is about lowering the body in place, not running.

to shout or cry out in alarm

Not quite — The dark cave feels dangerous, so a cry seems to fit, but kra points to a body position of huddling low, not to making a sound.

to bend very low, bringing the body close to the ground

Correct — Cower and crouch are usually linked to the same Norse root kra, 'to crouch, squat, huddle.' Cower adds fear to that low, huddled posture, while crouch keeps just the physical bending-low the hiker needs.

The root connection →

The root kra ('to crouch, squat, huddle') is the shared thread: cower is huddling low out of fear, and crouch is that same close-to-the-ground posture with the fear stripped away. This is a historically debated link, since crouch's exact origin is treated as uncertain.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / literary

Cower is the vivid word for someone physically retreating in fear—crouching down and shrinking. Use it when you need to show fear as an action, not just an emotion. 'Hide' or 'crouch' alone are simpler; 'cower' adds the layer of fear driving the movement.
