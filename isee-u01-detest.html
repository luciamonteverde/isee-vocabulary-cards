Detest

/dih-TEST/ — rhymes with protest · verb

Hear It
What It Means

To detest something is to feel intense dislike or hatred toward it.

Show it in context

He detested being talked down to in front of his coworkers.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when detest is the exact word.

Worked Example

After years of office politics, she detested the very sound of her boss's voice.

Detest shows not just disagreement or mild dislike, but intense, visceral hatred. The word captures an emotional strength and often a sense of disgust that simple 'dislike' cannot express. Detest makes the strength of her feelings precise and powerful.

Your Turn

I don't really like cold pizza.

Should we say 'I detest cold pizza'?

Detest

Not quite — Detest is far too strong and intense for a mild food preference.

Dislike

Correct — Not really liking cold pizza is just a preference, so the plainer word fits.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
hate

Strong dislike. Detest is even more intense and often includes a flavor of disgust or revulsion.

abhor

To hate with strong disgust. Very close to detest, though abhor may focus more on the disgust element.

loathe

To hate with contempt and disgust. Nearly identical to detest — both express deep, visceral hatred.

Antonyms · opposites

cherish — To hold dear and value — the opposite of detest's intense hatred and revulsion.

How Strong Is It?
dislike→despise→abhor→detest

Detest — the strongest: intense hatred with a flavor of disgust and revulsion. (our word)

Build the Word

detestari Latin: 'to curse, call as a witness against' (de- 'away' + testari 'to witness')

Detest comes from Latin testari, meaning 'to call as a witness.' When you detest something, it's as if you're calling upon all witnesses to confirm how much you hate it. The same root gives us testify.

Several coworkers who had known her for years were willing to attest to her honesty in court.

Same root, new word — what does attest mean here?

to strongly object to or argue against a claim

Not quite — This sounds like 'protest,' so it is a natural guess, but the root testari means 'to witness.' To attest is to bear witness in favor of something, not to argue against it.

to test or examine something carefully before approving it

Not quite — The visible letters 'test' tempt you toward 'examine,' but the Latin root testari means 'to witness,' not 'to inspect.' Attesting is vouching that something is true, not checking it over.

to declare or confirm that something is true, especially in a formal way

Correct — You traced the Latin root testari ('to witness') from detest into attest. Where detest is like calling every witness to confirm your hatred, attest is bearing witness that something, here her honesty, is true.

The root connection →

Detest and attest both come from the Latin root testari, 'to witness' (the same root as testify). Detest is like summoning witnesses to how much you hate something, while attest is formally witnessing that something is true.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Detest is stronger and more visceral than 'dislike' or even 'hate.' It often includes a sense of disgust or revulsion. Save it for genuine, intense aversion — not casual preferences.
